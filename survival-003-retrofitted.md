<div class="quip">Finders keepers is not a legal doctrine. It is a nursery rhyme that can put you in prison.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The common law categories (still good law in Georgia)</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lost property:</strong> The owner unintentionally parted with it. The finder has rights against everyone <em>except</em> the true owner. Example: a wallet falls out of a pocket onto the Walmart floor.</li>
<li><strong>Mislaid property:</strong> The owner intentionally placed it somewhere and forgot. The owner of the premises (Walmart) has a superior claim to the finder. Example: setting a phone on a shelf and walking away.</li>
<li><strong>Abandoned property:</strong> The owner intentionally discarded it. The finder can keep it. Example: trash in a dumpster.</li>
</ul>
<p>The distinction matters. If the money on the floor is <em>lost</em>, your common-law claim is better than Walmart's. If it is <em>mislaid</em>, Walmart's claim is better than yours. But either way, the statute overrides both.</p>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>What Georgia actually says: O.C.G.A. 16-8-6</b>
<p>Georgia does not care whether you call it lost, mislaid, or abandoned. The statute says: if you come into control of property that you know or learn has been lost or mislaid, and you <strong>appropriate it to your own use without first taking reasonable measures to restore the property to the owner</strong>, you have committed theft.</p>
<p><strong>The penalty depends on value:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>$1,500 or less:</strong> Misdemeanor. Up to 12 months in jail and a $1,000 fine.</li>
<li><strong>More than $1,500:</strong> Felony. One to ten years in prison.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What counts as "reasonable measures"?</strong> The statute does not define it. It is a fact question for the jury. Georgia courts have held that waiting around for a minute to see if the owner returns is <em>not</em> enough. Turning the money in to store management or the police generally is.</p>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>What people get wrong</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"Finders keepers, losers weepers."</strong> Not the law in Georgia. It is a defense that will get you convicted.</li>
<li><strong>"If I found it in the parking lot, it's different."</strong> The statute applies regardless of location. The only question is whether you knew or learned the property was lost and failed to take reasonable measures.</li>
<li><strong>"I didn't know whose it was, so I couldn't return it."</strong> Ignorance of the owner's identity is not a defense. The statute requires you to take reasonable measures to restore it - turning it in to the store or police satisfies this even if the owner is never found.</li>
<li><strong>"The store has cameras, they'll find the owner."</strong> That is the store's problem, not yours. Your obligation is to take reasonable measures, not to solve the case.</li>
<li><strong>"It's only theft if I take it from a person."</strong> No. Theft of lost or mislaid property is a separate offense from theft by taking. You do not have to steal it from anyone's hand. Picking it up and walking out is enough.</li>
</ul>
</div>
