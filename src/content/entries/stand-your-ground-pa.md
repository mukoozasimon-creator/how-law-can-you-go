## The Short Answer

Pennsylvania is a **stand-your-ground state** (since 2011 amendment). You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you have a legal right to be, are not engaged in criminal activity, and are not in illegal possession of a firearm (18 Pa.C.S. 505(b)(2.3)). Pennsylvania's **Castle Doctrine** (18 Pa.C.S. 505) creates a presumption of reasonable fear when someone unlawfully enters your home, vehicle, or workplace. However, the assailant must display a firearm, replica firearm, or other lethal weapon for the stand-your-ground provision to apply outside the home.

## Key Statutes

### 18 Pa.C.S. 505 - Use of Force in Self-Protection

> "(b)(2.3) An actor who is not engaged in a criminal activity, who is not in illegal possession of a firearm and who is attacked in any place where the actor would have a duty to retreat under paragraph (2)(ii) has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his ground and use force, including deadly force, if: (i) the actor has a right to be in the place where he was attacked; (ii) the actor believes it is immediately necessary to do so to protect himself against death, serious bodily injury, kidnapping or sexual intercourse by force or threat; and (iii) the person against whom the force is used displays or otherwise uses a firearm, replica of a firearm, or other lethal weapon."

### 18 Pa.C.S. 505(b)(2)(ii) - Castle Doctrine

> "The use of deadly force is not justifiable under this section unless the actor believes that such force is necessary to protect himself against death, serious bodily injury, kidnapping or sexual intercourse by force or threat; nor is it justifiable if: (i) the actor, with the intent of causing death or serious bodily injury, provoked the use of force against himself in the same encounter; or (ii) the actor knows that he can avoid the necessity of using such force with complete safety by retreating, except the actor is not obliged to retreat from his dwelling or place of work, unless he was the initial aggressor..."

### 18 Pa.C.S. 506 - Use of Force for the Protection of Other Persons

> "The use of force upon or toward the person of another is justifiable to protect a third person when: (1) the actor would be justified under section 505 in using such force to protect himself against the injury he believes to be threatened to the person whom he seeks to protect; and (2) under the circumstances as the actor believes them to be, the person whom he seeks to protect would be justified in using such protective force..."

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent deadly threat |
| In your vehicle | No | Yes, under Castle Doctrine |
| In your workplace | No | Yes, under Castle Doctrine |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if assailant displays lethal weapon |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Stand-your-ground since 2011:** Pennsylvania amended its self-defense law in 2011 to remove the duty to retreat in public places where the actor has a legal right to be.
- **Weapon display requirement:** For the stand-your-ground provision to apply outside the home, the assailant must display or use a firearm, replica firearm, or other lethal weapon. This is a unique limitation.
- **No illegal firearm possession:** The stand-your-ground provision does not apply if you are in illegal possession of a firearm.
- **Castle Doctrine:** No duty to retreat from your dwelling or place of work (unless you were the initial aggressor).
- **Civil immunity:** 18 Pa.C.S. 8340.1 provides civil immunity for justified use of force in self-defense.
- **Recording:** Pennsylvania is a two-party consent state for recordings (18 Pa.C.S. 5703). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** NY requires retreat in public; PA does not (since 2011).
- **vs. Ohio:** Both are stand-your-ground states. OH removed duty to retreat in 2021; PA did so in 2011.
- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states. FL has a pre-trial immunity hearing; PA does not.
