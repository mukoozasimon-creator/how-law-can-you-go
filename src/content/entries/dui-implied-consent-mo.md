<div class="quip">Missouri: where the BBQ is Kansas City-style and you get 20 minutes to call a lawyer before deciding whether to blow.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Missouri</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> RSMo 577.020. By driving in Missouri, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, saliva, or urine testing if lawfully arrested for DWI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 1-year revocation. Second = 5-year denial.</strong> RSMo 577.041. The officer issues a notice of revocation and takes your license on the spot.</li>
<li><strong>UNIQUE: You get 20 minutes to call a lawyer.</strong> RSMo 577.041.1. Missouri is one of the few states that gives you up to 20 minutes to contact an attorney before deciding. The conversation must be private. <em>Roesing v. Director of Revenue</em> held that the conversation must be private for the right to be meaningful.</li>
<li><strong>You are entitled to an independent test.</strong> RSMo 577.020.2. After submitting to the state's test, you can have an independent test by a qualified person of your choosing at your own expense. But the officer does not have to tell you this unless you ask.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Missouri is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have the right to contact an attorney for up to 20 minutes. But if you cannot reach one within 20 minutes, the officer can proceed. The conversation must be private.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't give me my 20 minutes."</strong> If the officer fails to give you a reasonable opportunity to contact an attorney, the refusal may be invalid.</li>
<li><strong>"I wasn't driving, I was sleeping in the car."</strong> Missouri uses 'operating' standard. If you have the keys and the ability to operate the vehicle, you can be arrested for DWI.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic revocation AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have unlimited time to call a lawyer." Wrong. You have up to 20 minutes. After that, the officer can proceed.</li>
<li>"The revocation only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The revocation is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a limited driving privilege right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you must serve a portion of the revocation before becoming eligible for a limited driving privilege.</li>
</ul>
</div>
