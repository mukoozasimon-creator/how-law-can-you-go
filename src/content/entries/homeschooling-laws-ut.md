## Summary

Utah is one of the most homeschool-friendly states in the nation. Under Utah Code § 53G-6-204 (amended by HB 209 in May 2025), parents must provide only a one-time initial notification to the local school board when beginning to homeschool. As of the 2025 amendment, the previous affidavit requirement was replaced with a simple notice, and the criminal background check requirement was removed. Utah has no parent qualification requirements, no mandated subjects, no standardized testing, no minimum instructional hours, and no curriculum approval. Parents receive an excuse certificate within 30 days of notification. Utah also offers robust dual enrollment rights and the Utah Fits All (UFA) scholarship program.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**Utah Code § 53G-6-204** - Excuse from attendance - Home school

> "When a parent withdraws an enrolled school-age child from school for the purpose of home-schooling, or chooses not to enroll a school-age child, the parent shall provide a one-time initial notification, that may include a letter of intent, to the local school board of the school-age child's district of residence."

- **Instrument**: Utah Code § 53G-6-204
- **Live Link**: https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title53G/Chapter6/53G-6-S204.html
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; amended by HB 209 (2025) - replaced affidavit with notice, removed background check requirement
- **Verdict**: One-time notification to the local school board is required; no annual renewals.

### Dual Enrollment Statute

**Utah Code § 53G-6-702** - Dual enrollment

> Defines dual enrollment and establishes rights for parents to enroll their children in public school courses while homeschooling.

- **Instrument**: Utah Code § 53G-6-702
- **Live Link**: https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title53G/Chapter6/53G-6-S702.html
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026
- **Verdict**: Homeschool students have statutory dual enrollment rights.

## Notification

**One-Time Initial Notification** must be provided to the local school board of the child's district of residence. The notification may include a letter of intent and should include:

- Parent/guardian name
- Current address (to verify residency)
- Names of students who will be homeschooling
- Dates of birth and/or current grade of students

After notification, the school board must send an **excuse certificate** acknowledging the homeschool program within **30 days**. The certificate is sent only once and should be kept for all remaining school years.

**Important**: Parents who submitted an affidavit prior to May 7, 2025 do not need to resubmit notice.

**Kindergarten Note**: Kindergarten for 5-year-olds is optional in Utah. A parent does not need to submit a homeschool notice for a 5-year-old not participating in school.

## Required Subjects

No subjects are mandated by Utah law for homeschoolers. Complete curriculum freedom is granted.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required. Utah law explicitly states that homeschool families are not obligated to submit test scores, progress reports, or attendance records.

## Record-Keeping

No records are required by law. School districts may not require parents to keep attendance records or records of instruction.

## Special Provisions

- **Dual Enrollment (§ 53G-6-702)**: Homeschool students have a statutory right to dual enrollment - taking individual courses at public schools while maintaining homeschool status
- **Utah Fits All (UFA) Scholarship**: Utah's universal school choice program provides scholarship funding that can be used for homeschool expenses. Students utilizing UFA funding must identify themselves as UFA students to the school/district for tracking purposes
- **Excuse Certificate**: Parents receive a formal excuse certificate within 30 days of notification, which serves as legal documentation of homeschool status
- **No Background Checks**: As of HB 209 (2025), parents are no longer required to provide criminal background information when notifying the district
- **Sports/Activities**: No specific statutory right to public school extracurricular participation, but dual enrollment may provide access
- **Single Family Unit**: An instructional program provided to more than one family unit does not constitute a homeschool (similar to Wisconsin)

## Distinctive Features

- **One-time notification only** (no annual renewals)
- No parent qualifications, no testing, no required subjects, no minimum hours
- **Excuse certificate** issued within 30 days
- **Dual enrollment rights** enshrined in statute
- Utah Fits All (UFA) scholarship available for homeschool expenses
- **No background check requirement** (removed by HB 209, 2025)
- Previous affidavit requirement replaced with simple notice (HB 209, 2025)
- Kindergarten is optional - no notice needed for 5-year-olds
