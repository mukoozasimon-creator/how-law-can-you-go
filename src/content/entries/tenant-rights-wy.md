## The Short Answer

In Wyoming, your landlord must give you a **3-day written notice** to quit for nonpayment of rent or holdover (Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1002). For lease violations, the landlord may terminate the rental agreement and provide **3 days' notice** to vacate. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1002). Wyoming has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1002 - 3-Day Notice to Quit

> "A landlord may serve a 3-day notice to quit for nonpayment of rent, holdover after lease expiration, or breach of the rental agreement. The notice must state that the tenant has 3 days to vacate or legal action will be commenced."

**Live link:** https://wyoleg.gov/laws/statutes/title1/chapter21/section1002
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice for nonpayment and lease violations. Tenant can cure nonpayment by paying within 3 days.

### Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1002 - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "A month-to-month tenancy may be terminated by either party upon 30 days' written notice."

**Live link:** https://wyoleg.gov/laws/statutes/title1/chapter21/section1002
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### Wyo. Stat. 1-21-1003 - Forcible Entry and Detainer

> "If the tenant does not vacate after the notice period, the landlord may file a forcible entry and detainer action in circuit court."

**Live link:** https://wyoleg.gov/laws/statutes/title1/chapter21/section1003
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Court action required. Self-help eviction is prohibited.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 3 days to quit | N/A |
| Lease violation | 3 days to quit | N/A |
| Holdover | 3 days to quit | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Wyoming landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Wyoming does not have statewide rent control.
- **Uniform 3-day notice:** Wyoming uses a uniform 3-day notice for nonpayment, violations, and holdover.
- **Entry notice:** Landlords must provide 48 hours' notice before entry (non-emergency).
- **Recording:** Wyoming is a one-party consent state for recordings (Wyo. Stat. 7-3-702). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.
