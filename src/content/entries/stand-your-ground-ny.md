## The Short Answer

New York is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly physical force in public if you can do so with complete personal safety (N.Y. Penal Law 35.15(2)(a)). The two exceptions: (1) you are in your own dwelling and were not the initial aggressor, or (2) you are a police officer or acting at their direction. New York's **Castle Doctrine** (Penal Law 35.20(3)) removes the duty to retreat inside your dwelling. You may use deadly physical force only if you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent imminent death, serious physical injury, kidnapping, forcible rape, forcible criminal sexual act, or robbery.

## Key Statutes

### N.Y. Penal Law 35.15 - Justification; Use of Physical Force in Defense of a Person

> "(2) A person may not use deadly physical force upon another person under circumstances specified in subdivision one unless: (a) The actor reasonably believes that such other person is using or about to use deadly physical force. Even in such case, however, the actor may not use deadly physical force if he or she knows that with complete personal safety, to oneself and others he or she may avoid the necessity of so doing by retreating; except that the actor is under no duty to retreat if he or she is: (i) in his or her dwelling and not the initial aggressor; or (ii) a police officer or peace officer or a person assisting a police officer or peace officer at the latter's direction..."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/penal-law/pen-sect-35-15/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat before deadly force in public. No duty to retreat inside your own dwelling if not the initial aggressor.

### N.Y. Penal Law 35.20 - Justification; Use of Physical Force in Defense of Premises and in Defense of a Person in the Course of Burglary

> "(3) A person in possession or control of any premises, or a person licensed or privileged to be thereon or therein, may use physical force upon another person when he or she reasonably believes such to be necessary to prevent or terminate what he or she reasonably believes to be the commission or attempted commission by such other person of a criminal trespass upon such premises..."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/penal-law/pen-sect-35-20/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified to prevent criminal trespass. Deadly force only to prevent arson or burglary under specific circumstances.

### N.Y. Penal Law 35.05 - Justification; A Defense

> "In any prosecution for an offense, justification, as defined in sections 35.15 through 35.30, is a defense."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/penal-law/pen-sect-35-05/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Self-defense is an affirmative defense; defendant must produce some evidence, then prosecution must disprove beyond reasonable doubt.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder, not aggressor) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent deadly threat |
| In public | Yes, if can retreat with complete safety | Yes, only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if can retreat with complete safety | Same standard as self-defense |
| In your workplace | Yes, if can retreat with complete safety | Same duty to retreat applies |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** New York imposes a duty to retreat before using deadly force in public if retreat is possible with complete personal safety. This is a key distinction from stand-your-ground states.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to dwelling:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception applies only inside your own dwelling. It does not extend to your vehicle or workplace.
- **Reasonable belief standard:** Both your belief in the threat and your belief that deadly force is necessary must be objectively reasonable.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** New York is a one-party consent state for recordings (N.Y. Penal Law 250.00). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** CA has no duty to retreat in public; NY requires retreat if safe.
- **vs. Florida:** FL has no duty to retreat anywhere; NY requires retreat in public.
- **vs. Pennsylvania:** PA is a stand-your-ground state (2011 amendment); NY is not.
