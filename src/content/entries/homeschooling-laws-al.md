## Summary

Alabama does not have a specific homeschool statute. Instead, families operate under one of three legal options: (1) Church School (the most popular, under Code § 16-28-1), (2) Private School, or (3) Private Tutor. The Church School option provides the least state oversight - families enroll in a church-affiliated school that handles all state reporting. There are no parent qualification requirements, no standardized testing mandates, no curriculum approval, and no minimum instructional hours for homeschoolers under any option. Alabama is one of the most homeschool-friendly states in the Southeast.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**Code of Alabama § 16-28-1** - School attendance required; child to attend public or private school, etc.

> Establishes compulsory attendance for children ages 6-17 and provides exemptions for children attending church schools, private schools, or receiving instruction from a private tutor.

- **Instrument**: Code of Alabama § 16-28-1 et seq.
- **Live Link**: https://alisondb.legislature.state.al.us/alison/codeofalabama/1975/coatoc.htm
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Homeschooling is permitted through church school, private school, or private tutor exemptions.

## Three Legal Options

### Option 1: Church School (Most Popular)

- Enroll the child in a church school (a school affiliated with a church that meets the definition under Alabama law)
- The church school handles all state reporting and compliance
- Parents teach at home under the church school's umbrella
- No state-mandated subjects, testing, or record-keeping for the parent
- Church schools are not required to seek state accreditation or approval

### Option 2: Private School

- Establish or enroll in a private school
- The principal teacher must keep a daily attendance roster throughout the calendar year
- By the fifth day after the public school year begins, the school must report names and addresses of enrolled children to the county or city superintendent
- New enrollments and unexcused absences reported weekly
- Students must provide proof of immunization or valid medical/religious exemptions
- Physical education must be included in the program

### Option 3: Private Tutor

- Notify the local superintendent within 5 days of the public school year's start
- Provide names and addresses of enrolled students
- Maintain a daily attendance roster throughout the year
- Require proof of immunization or valid exemptions
- Include physical education in the program
- The tutor must hold a valid Alabama teaching certificate

## Notification

- **Church School**: No direct notification to the state by parents. The church school handles all reporting.
- **Private School**: School reports to superintendent by 5th day after school year begins.
- **Private Tutor**: Tutor notifies superintendent within 5 days.

## Required Subjects

No subjects are mandated by Alabama state law for homeschoolers. Church schools and private schools set their own curriculum standards.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required for homeschoolers under any option.

## Record-Keeping

- **Church School**: The church school maintains records. Parents should keep personal records for transcripts.
- **Private School**: Daily attendance roster required; immunization records.
- **Private Tutor**: Daily attendance roster required; immunization records.

## Special Provisions

- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Funding**: No state-level financial programs for homeschoolers
- **Driver's License**: No specific homeschool provisions
- **High School Diplomas**: Church schools and private schools may issue diplomas

## Distinctive Features

- **No dedicated homeschool statute** - operates through church school, private school, or private tutor exemptions
- Church school option is the most popular and involves the least state oversight
- No parent qualifications, no testing, no curriculum approval, no minimum hours
- One of the most deregulated states in the Southeast
- Private tutor option requires a valid Alabama teaching certificate
- Church schools are not required to be accredited or state-approved
