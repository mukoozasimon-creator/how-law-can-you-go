# Marijuana Laws - Washington

## One-Sentence Verdict

Washington legalized recreational marijuana for adults 21+ in 2012 via Initiative 502, but remains the only recreational-legal state where home cultivation is a Class C felony for non-medical users; adults may possess up to 1 oz of flower, 16 oz of solid edibles, 72 oz of liquid edibles, and 7g of concentrate, while medical patients may grow up to 6 plants (15 with provider authorization).

## Home Cultivation Rules

- **Recreational users**: NOT permitted. Growing even one plant without medical authorization is a **Class C felony** punishable by up to 5 years in prison and $10,000 in fine. Washington is the only recreational-legal state with felony penalties for unauthorized home cultivation.
- **Medical patients**: Up to 6 plants standard; up to 15 with health care provider authorization.
- **Housing unit cap**: No more than 15 plants per housing unit regardless of number of patients.
- **Registration**: Voluntary but recommended. Registered patients receive a recognition card, tax exemption on dispensary purchases, and stronger legal protections.
- **Non-registered patients**: May grow up to 4 plants.
- **Security**: Must be in an enclosed, locked facility not visible from public spaces.
- **Pending legislation**: SB 6204 would have allowed 6 plants per adult (15 per household) for recreational users. It was shelved in the Senate Rules Committee in February 2026 - the 11th consecutive year home grow legislation has failed.

---

## Purchase and Retail

- **Age**: 21+ for recreational; 18+ with medical authorization.
- **Licensed retailers**: Washington State Liquor and Cannabis Board (LCB) licenses required.
- **Taxes**: 37% excise tax (one of the highest in the nation) + standard sales tax.
- **Medical exemption**: Registered medical patients are exempt from sales tax.
- **Delivery**: Available in some jurisdictions.

---

## Consumption Rules

- **Private property**: Legal.
- **Public spaces**: Illegal.
- **Vehicles**: Illegal to consume or possess open container while driving.
- **Workplaces**: Employers may prohibit cannabis use and maintain drug-free workplace policies.

---

## DUI / Impairment

- **Standard**: It is illegal to operate a motor vehicle with a THC concentration of 5.00 ng/mL or higher within 2 hours of driving (RCW 46.61.502).
- **Per se limit**: 5 ng/mL THC blood concentration (one of the few states with a per se limit).
- **Penalties**: First offense - minimum 1 day jail, $350-$5,000 fine, license suspension 90 days-2 years.

---

## Employment Protections

- **No comprehensive statutory protection**: Washington does not have broad employment protections for off-duty recreational cannabis use.
- **Medical patients**: Some protections under the Medical Use of Cannabis Act.
- **Federal contractors**: Subject to federal Drug-Free Workplace Act.

---

## Federal Conflict Notes

- **Schedule I federally**: Recreational cannabis remains Schedule I under federal law.
- **Federal property**: Illegal on federal land, including national parks and military bases.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Only rec-legal state with felony home grow**: Washington is unique among the ~24 recreational-legal states in criminalizing home cultivation as a Class C felony.
2. **Highest excise tax**: 37% excise tax is among the highest in the nation.
3. **Per se DUI limit**: 5 ng/mL THC blood concentration is one of the few explicit per se limits.
4. **11 years of failed home grow bills**: Every year since 2015, legislators have introduced home grow bills. All have failed, primarily due to industry lobbying to protect tax revenue.
5. **Medical registration voluntary**: Patients may grow without registering, but registration provides significant benefits.

## Recording Consent Rules

Washington is a **two-party consent** state for audio recordings. RCW 9.73.030 makes it a crime to record a private communication without the consent of all parties.

---

## Practical Notes

- Do NOT grow cannabis at home in Washington without a medical authorization - it is a felony.
- The 37% excise tax makes Washington cannabis among the most expensive in the nation.
- Medical patients should consider registering for the tax exemption and legal protections.
- The 5 ng/mL DUI limit means even moderate use the night before could result in a DUI charge the next morning.
- Home grow bills have failed 11 years in a row; do not expect this to change soon.
