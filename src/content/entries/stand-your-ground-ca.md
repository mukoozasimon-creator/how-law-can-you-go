## The Short Answer

California is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense, whether in public or in your home (Cal. Penal Code 197; jury instructions). California's **Castle Doctrine** (Penal Code 198.5) creates a rebuttable presumption of reasonable fear of imminent harm when an intruder forcibly enters your home. You may use deadly force only if you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily injury.

## Key Statutes

### Cal. Penal Code 197 - Justifiable Homicide

> "Homicide is also justifiable when committed by any person in any of the following cases: 1. When resisting any attempt to murder any person, or to commit a felony, or to do some great bodily injury upon any person; 2. When committed in defense of habitation, property, or person, against one who manifestly intends or endeavors, by violence or surprise, to commit a felony..."

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=197
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified to prevent imminent death, great bodily injury, or certain felonies. No duty to retreat.

### Cal. Penal Code 198.5 - Castle Doctrine

> "Any person using force intended or likely to cause death or great bodily injury within his or her residence shall be presumed to have held a reasonable fear of imminent peril of death or great bodily injury to self, family, or a member of the household when that force is used against another person, not a member of the family or household, who unlawfully and forcibly enters or has unlawfully and forcibly entered the residence and the person using the force knew or had reason to believe that an unlawful and forcible entry occurred."

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=198.5
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Rebuttable presumption of reasonable fear when intruder forcibly enters home. Does not apply to cohabitants or lawful residents.

### Cal. Penal Code 692 - Defense of Self or Another

> "Lawful resistance to the commission of a public offense may be made: 1. By the party about to be injured; 2. By other parties."

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=692

### CALCRIM 505 / 506 - Jury Instructions

**CALCRIM 505 (Justifiable Homicide: Self-Defense or Defense of Another):**
- Defendant must have reasonably believed they were in imminent danger.
- Force used must be no more than reasonably necessary.
- **No duty to retreat** - California courts have held there is no duty to retreat before using force in self-defense.

**Live link:** https://www.courts.ca.gov/partners/documents/CALCRIM_505.pdf

**Verdict:** Case law and jury instructions confirm no duty to retreat in California.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable fear of death/great bodily injury |
| In public | No | Yes, if imminent threat of death/great bodily injury |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard as self-defense |
| Defending property only | No | No - deadly force not justified solely for property |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** California courts have consistently held there is no duty to retreat before using force in self-defense, even in public spaces.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to home:** The statutory Castle Doctrine (PC 198.5) applies only to your residence. Stand-your-ground principles apply anywhere you have a legal right to be.
- **Reasonable belief standard:** Both your belief in the threat and your belief that force is necessary must be objectively reasonable.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat. Deadly force is only justified against imminent threat of death or great bodily injury.
- **Recording:** California is a two-party consent state for recordings (Cal. Penal Code 632). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with no duty to retreat. TX extends Castle Doctrine to vehicle and workplace; CA limits statutory Castle Doctrine to home.
- **vs. New York:** NY imposes a duty to retreat before using deadly force in public; CA does not.
- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states. FL has broader civil immunity protections; CA does not have a specific civil immunity statute for self-defense.
