## The Short Answer

In Massachusetts, your landlord must give you a **14-day written notice to quit** for nonpayment of rent (M.G.L. c. 186, § 11). For no-fault termination of a tenancy at will, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (or one full rental period, whichever is longer). Massachusetts has no statewide just-cause requirement, but several cities (including Boston, Cambridge, and Somerville) have **rent control or just-cause protections**. Security deposits are capped at **1 month's rent** and must be held in a **separate interest-bearing account** at a Massachusetts bank. Self-help eviction is illegal and exposes the landlord to damages of **3 months' rent** or actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney's fees.

### M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum security deposit: **1 month's rent**
- Must be held in **separate interest-bearing account** at Massachusetts bank
- Annual interest payment to tenant (5% or bank passbook rate, whichever is lower)
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement
- Last month's rent: If collected, must also earn interest
- Wrongful withholding: **3x the deposit** plus attorney's fees and interest

**Live link:** https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 1-month cap. Interest-bearing account required. Triple damages for wrongful withholding.

## Important Notes

- **Rent control:** Boston, Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline, and other municipalities have rent control or just-cause protections. Check local ordinances.
- **Housing Court:** Massachusetts has a specialized Housing Court system that handles most residential evictions. Some cases go to District Court.
- **Accepting rent after notice:** Under M.G.L. c. 186, § 11, a landlord who accepts rent after delivering a notice to quit may be deemed to have waived the notice, effectively restarting the process.
- **Renters' Rights and Stabilization Act (effective Oct. 1, 2024):** Requires landlords to attach the current Maryland Tenants' Bill of Rights to every residential lease.
- **Recording:** Massachusetts is a two-party consent state for recordings (M.G.L. c. 272, § 99). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Both have strong tenant protections and rent control in major cities, but MA uses Housing Court while NY uses Housing Court (NYC) or District Court (upstate).
- **vs. Connecticut:** Both have similar New England-style protections with strong security deposit rules and specialized housing courts.
- **vs. Texas:** MA is far more tenant-friendly with deposit interest requirements, Housing Court, and rent control in some cities.
