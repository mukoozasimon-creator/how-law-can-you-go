## The Short Answer

Virginia is a **stand-your-ground state by case law**, not by statute. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are **without fault** in provoking the confrontation (Bailey v. Commonwealth). If you were partly at fault, you must retreat as far as safely possible before using force (excusable self-defense). Virginia's **Castle Doctrine** removes any duty to retreat inside your home and its curtilage (porch, yard) and gives you the strongest legal footing. Deadly force is only justified to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm.

## Key Case Law

### Bailey v. Commonwealth - Justifiable Self-Defense (No Duty to Retreat)

> "Justifiable homicide occurs where a person, without any fault on his part in provoking or bringing on the difficulty, kills another under reasonable apprehension of death or great bodily harm. When without fault, the defender need not retreat, but may stand his ground and repel the attack by force, including deadly force, if it is necessary."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/cases/virginia/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat if entirely without fault in provoking the encounter.

### McCoy v. Commonwealth (1919) - Stand Your Ground Principle

> "A defender who is without fault need not retreat, but is permitted to stand his ground and repel the attack by force, including deadly force, if it is necessary."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/cases/virginia/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Virginia courts have recognized the no-duty-to-retreat principle since 1919.

### Castle Doctrine in Virginia

> "Inside your own home, Virginia gives you the strongest legal position. If an intruder forces his way in, you have no duty to retreat and may use all needful force, including deadly force, to keep the aggressor out or expel him, when it is necessary to prevent a felony inside the home or a felonious attack on the people in it."

**Live link:** https://milesfranklinlaw.com/self-defense-laws-homicide-virginia/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies to home and curtilage. Does not apply to lawful guests.

### Excusable Self-Defense (Partly at Fault)

> "If you were partly at fault, Virginia law still allows a self-defense claim, but only if you first retreated as far as you safely could, made your desire for peace known, and then used force out of a reasonably apparent necessity to save your own life or avoid serious harm."

**Live link:** https://milesfranklinlaw.com/self-defense-laws-homicide-virginia/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** If partly at fault, must retreat before using force.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, if necessary to prevent felony or felonious attack |
| In your curtilage (porch, yard) | No (if without fault) | Yes, same standard |
| In public (without fault) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| In public (partly at fault) | Yes, as far as safely possible | Only after retreating |
| Defending another | No (if without fault) | Same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Stand-your-ground by case law:** Virginia has no stand-your-ground statute. The principle comes entirely from common law and case decisions dating back over a century.
- **Fault is the key distinction:** The critical question in Virginia is whether you were at fault in provoking or bringing on the difficulty. If entirely without fault, no duty to retreat. If partly at fault, must retreat first.
- **Castle Doctrine does not cover guests:** The Castle Doctrine does not apply when the other person was peaceably present in the home with your permission, express or implied. If a dinner guest becomes violent, ordinary self-defense rules apply.
- **Proportionality still applies:** Even inside your home, the force used must be needful and reasonably necessary under the circumstances.
- **Recording:** Virginia is a one-party consent state for recordings (Va. Code 19.2-62). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are stand-your-ground states, but NC has statutory SYG while VA relies on case law.
- **vs. West Virginia:** WV has a statutory stand-your-ground law; VA relies on common law.
- **vs. Maryland:** MD imposes a duty to retreat in public; VA does not (if without fault).
