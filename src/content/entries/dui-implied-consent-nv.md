# DUI / Implied Consent - Nevada

## Verdict

Nevada's implied consent law (NRS 484.382 et seq.) deems that any person driving or in actual physical control of a vehicle on Nevada highways has automatically consented to preliminary breath testing (PBT) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe impairment. For evidentiary testing after arrest, Nevada has made refusal **virtually impossible** through legislative amendments: blood will be drawn by force if necessary, particularly for second or subsequent offenses within 7 years, or when death or substantial bodily injury is involved. A first refusal of an evidentiary test triggers a **1-year license revocation**; a second refusal triggers **3 years**. Refusal of a preliminary breath test results in **90-day license seizure**. Refusal is admissible as evidence of consciousness of guilt.

> -- NCDD, Nevada DUI Laws, [source](https://www.ncdd.com/nevada-dui-laws)

> "Because of legislative amendments in recent years, it is virtually impossible for a driver to refuse to take either preliminary or evidentiary tests. There are no penalties, because in the event of a refusal, blood will be drawn by force, if necessary."
> -- NCDD, Nevada DUI Laws, [source](https://www.ncdd.com/nevada-dui-laws)

> "For a suspected second or subsequent offense within 7 years, however, or if there is death or substantial bodily injury which the officer reasonably believes was caused by a person under the influence, then the officer may order a blood test. Reasonable force may be used to perform the blood test."
> -- NRS 484.383(4), [source](https://www.ncdd.com/nevada-dui-laws)

> "Refusing a test can lead to several penalties: License suspension - Typically, one year for a first refusal, and three years for repeat refusals."

> "A driver's statement that he is unwilling to take such a test, however, is generally considered to be an admission pursuant to NRS 51.035. As such, it likely admissible in any subsequent proceeding."
> -- NCDD, Nevada DUI Laws, [source](https://www.ncdd.com/nevada-dui-laws)

| Factor | Blood Test | Breath Test |
|--------|-----------|-------------|

| Driver Choice (2nd+ offense) | **No** - blood may be ordered regardless of preference | Officer directs |
| Officer Choice | Officer directs; blood mandatory for 2nd+/injury cases | Officer may request first |

## Distinctive State Features

1. **"Virtually Impossible to Refuse"**: Nevada has legislated away the practical ability to refuse evidentiary testing. Blood will be drawn by force if necessary, making Nevada one of the most aggressive states on test refusal.

2. **Forced Blood Draws**: For second or subsequent offenses within 7 years, or cases involving death or substantial bodily injury, officers may order a blood test and use reasonable force to perform it.

3. **PBT vs. Evidentiary Distinction**: Nevada has separate implied consent rules for preliminary breath tests (roadside) and evidentiary tests (post-arrest). PBT refusal triggers 90-day seizure; evidentiary refusal triggers 1-3 year revocation.

4. **PBT Results Inadmissible in Criminal Court**: NRS 484.382(3) expressly prohibits PBT results from being admitted in criminal proceedings. PBT is for probable cause only.

5. **7-Year Lookback**: Nevada uses a 7-year lookback period for both administrative revocations and criminal penalty enhancements.

6. **Restricted License Available**: After half the revocation period for a first offense, a restricted license may be issued for work, medical care, and essential errands.

7. **Refusal as Admission**: Under NRS 51.035, a driver's statement of unwillingness to take a test is treated as an admission and is admissible in subsequent proceedings.

## Practical Notes

- **Two Testing Phases**: Nevada DUI stops involve (1) a voluntary PBT at the roadside, and (2) a mandatory evidentiary test after arrest. Refusing the PBT leads to arrest and license seizure; refusing the evidentiary test leads to revocation and forced blood draw.
- **Administrative Per Se**: A breath test showing .08+ triggers immediate license seizure and 90-day revocation by the officer. Blood test cases involve DMV notification and similar revocation.
- **Treatment Can Reduce Suspension**: First offense revocations can be reduced by half if the driver completes a 6-month treatment program.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Nevada's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Nevada highways, including out-of-state residents.
- **Felony DUI**: A third DUI within 7 years is a felony with 1-6 years prison and 3-year license revocation.
