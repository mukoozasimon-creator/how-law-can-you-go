## Summary

Arizona is one of the most homeschool-friendly states in the nation. Under A.R.S. §15-802, parents need only file a **one-time** notarized affidavit of intent with the county school superintendent within 30 days of beginning to homeschool. There are no annual renewals, no required parent qualifications, no standardized testing, no curriculum approval, and no minimum instructional hours. Arizona also offers the Empowerment Scholarship Account (ESA) program, the most generous universal school choice program in the country, though ESA recipients are not legally classified as homeschoolers.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**A.R.S. §15-802** - School instruction; exceptions; violations

> "If the child will attend a private school or homeschool, file an affidavit of intent with the county school superintendent stating that the child is attending a regularly organized private school or is being provided with instruction in a homeschool. The affidavit of intent shall include: (a) The child's name. (b) The child's date of birth. (c) The current address of the school the child is attending. (d) The names, telephone numbers and addresses of the persons who currently have custody of the child."

- **Instrument**: A.R.S. §15-802
- **Live Link**: https://www.azleg.gov/ars/15/00802.htm
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Homeschooling is permitted with a one-time notarized affidavit. No annual filing required unless instruction is terminated and resumed.

## Notification

**One-Time Filing**: Parents must complete, notarize, and file an affidavit of intent with the **county school superintendent** within 30 days of beginning to homeschool.

- If instruction is **terminated**, the parent must notify the county superintendent within 30 days
- If instruction is **resumed**, a new affidavit must be filed within 30 days
- If moving to a **new county**, file a new affidavit with the new county superintendent

The parent must also submit proof of the child's identity and age (birth certificate or equivalent) within 30 days of starting home instruction (A.R.S. §15-828(B)).

## Required Subjects (5)

Under A.R.S. §15-802(A), home-school instruction at minimum includes:

1. Reading
2. Grammar
3. Mathematics
4. Social Studies
5. Science

There is no curriculum approval process, no required lesson plans, and no specified hours for each subject.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required. Homeschooled students are explicitly exempt from statewide assessments (A.R.S. §15-745).

## Record-Keeping

No records are required by law. However, keeping basic documentation is recommended.

## Special Provisions

- **Sports Access**: Arizona requires public schools to allow homeschooler participation in sports and other interscholastic activities (A.R.S. §15-802.01)
- **Age 8 Delay**: Parents may delay formal instruction until the child reaches 8 years of age by September 1 (A.R.S. §15-802(B)(3))
- **ESA Program**: Arizona's Empowerment Scholarship Account (A.R.S. §15-2401 et seq.) provides formula-based funding for all students, but ESA recipients are not legally "homeschoolers"
- **Special Education**: If a school district receives federal special education funding, it must provide services to homeschool students in the same manner as private school students (A.R.S. §15-763)
- **Credit Transfer**: School districts must provide a list of accepted/denied credits for transfer students; students may test out of denied courses (A.R.S. §15-701.01(G))

## Distinctive Features

- **One-time filing** - no annual renewals (most states require annual notification)
- No parent qualifications, no testing, no curriculum approval
- Universal ESA program available (though it changes legal classification)
- Statutory right to public school sports participation
- Ability to delay instruction until age 8
- One of the most homeschool-friendly states in the U.S.
