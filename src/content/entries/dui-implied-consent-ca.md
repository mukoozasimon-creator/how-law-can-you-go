<div class="quip">California: where your avocado toast costs $18 and your DUI refusal costs you a year of Uber rides.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in California</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> Cal. Veh. Code 23612. By driving in California, you are deemed to have given consent to a blood or breath test if lawfully arrested for DUI. The officer chooses which test - you do not.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse, but your license is gone.</strong> Cal. Veh. Code 13353. First refusal = 1-year suspension. Second refusal within 10 years = 2-year revocation. Third = 3 years. This is administrative, not criminal - it happens even if you beat the DUI charge.</li>
<li><strong>Blood test preserves evidence; breath test does not.</strong> Cal. Veh. Code 23614. If you choose breath, the officer must warn you that no sample is retained for independent testing. Blood tests preserve a sample you can have retested.</li>
<li><strong>Breath test = no warrant needed. Blood = warrant required.</strong> <em>Birchfield v. North Dakota</em> (2016). Warrantless breath tests are permitted as a search incident to arrest. Warrantless blood draws are not. If you refuse blood, the officer needs a warrant or genuine exigent circumstances.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> California is a two-party consent state for confidential communications, but courts have consistently held that recording police in public is protected by the First Amendment. Do not interfere with the investigation.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want my lawyer before I decide."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing under implied consent. The officer does not have to wait for your lawyer.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request a blood test, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"I have a medical condition that prevents me from blowing."</strong> Documented medical conditions may be a defense, but you need actual medical documentation. "I have asthma" without a doctor's note will not help you.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't read me my Miranda rights."</strong> Miranda does not apply to the implied consent warning. The officer must advise you of the consequences of refusal under 23612, but that is not a Miranda warning.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse the test, they have no evidence and you walk." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic license suspension AND the prosecutor can use your refusal as evidence of consciousness of guilt. Plus they can still convict you on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an independent test before the state's test." Wrong. You are entitled to an independent test AFTER submitting to the state's test, not before. Cal. Veh. Code 23614.</li>
<li>"They can't suspend your license without a conviction." Wrong. The suspension is administrative, not criminal. DMV suspends your license independently of the court case.</li>
<li>"If the officer didn't have probable cause, the refusal doesn't count." Partially true - but you have to prove the arrest was unlawful at a DMV hearing, which is harder than it sounds.</li>
</ul>
</div>
