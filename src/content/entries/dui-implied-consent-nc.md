<div class="quip">North Carolina: where the barbecue is vinegar-based and your license is revoked for a year if you refuse the breath test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in North Carolina</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> N.C.G.S. 20-16.2. By driving in North Carolina, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, or urine testing if charged with an implied-consent offense. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal = 1-year revocation.</strong> N.C.G.S. 20-16.2(a)(1). First refusal = 1-year revocation. The officer must inform you orally and in writing of your rights before testing.</li>
<li><strong>You have 30 minutes to contact an attorney or witness.</strong> N.C.G.S. 20-16.2(a)(6). You may call an attorney for advice and select a witness to view the testing procedures, but the testing may not be delayed more than 30 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal does not stop them from getting a warrant.</strong> N.C.G.S. 20-16.2(i). If you willfully refuse, the officer can obtain a search warrant to compel a blood draw.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse the roadside PBT without penalty.</strong> North Carolina has no penalty for refusing the preliminary breath test at the roadside. The implied consent law only applies to the post-arrest chemical test.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> North Carolina is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have the right to call an attorney, but the test cannot be delayed more than 30 minutes. The officer does not have to wait beyond 30 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't read me my rights."</strong> The officer must inform you of your rights both orally and in writing. Failure to do so properly can invalidate the refusal or test results.</li>
<li><strong>"If I refuse, they can't test me."</strong> Wrong. The officer can obtain a search warrant to compel a blood draw even if you refuse.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic revocation AND can be used as evidence of guilt. Plus the officer can get a warrant for your blood.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You can call an attorney, but the test cannot be delayed more than 30 minutes.</li>
<li>"The revocation only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The revocation is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a limited driving privilege right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you must wait 6 months and meet strict requirements before becoming eligible for a limited privilege.</li>
</ul>
</div>
