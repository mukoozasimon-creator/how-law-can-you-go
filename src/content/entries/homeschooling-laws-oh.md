## Citation Layer

### 1. Legal Framework - HB 33 (2023)

**Instrument:** Ohio Revised Code Section 3321.042 (as amended by HB 33, effective October 2023)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042)

**Verbatim Quote (offset ~char 100-400):**
> "A parent who intends to provide home education to a child of compulsory school age shall provide notice to the superintendent of the school district in which the child resides."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending. HB 33 was a major deregulation. No bills to reimpose testing or hour requirements have advanced.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Ohio moved from moderate to low regulation in October 2023. Annual notification is now essentially the only requirement.

---

### 2. What Was Eliminated by HB 33

**Instrument:** Ohio Revised Code Section 3321.042 (pre- and post-HB 33)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "What was ELIMINATED: Annual assessment requirements (testing or portfolio review); Teacher qualifications (previously required high school diploma/GED); Minimum hour requirements (previously 900 hours annually); Curriculum outline submission to district; Detailed record-keeping mandates."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. HB 33 eliminated testing, teacher qualifications, 900-hour minimums, curriculum outlines, and detailed record-keeping. Ohio went from moderate to one of the least regulated states.

---

### 3. Notification Requirements

**Instrument:** Ohio Revised Code Section 3321.042(B)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The notification shall include: the parent's name and address; the child's name; and an assurance that the child will receive education in the required subject areas."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Notification due by August 30 annually (or within 5 days of starting). Only requires parent's name/address, child's name, and subject assurance. No curriculum, no diplomas, no birth certificates, no assessment results.

---

### 4. Superintendent Acknowledgment

**Instrument:** Ohio Revised Code Section 3321.042(C)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The superintendent shall acknowledge receipt of the notification within fourteen calendar days."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Superintendent must acknowledge receipt within 14 days. This is NOT an approval - the superintendent has no authority to deny the filing or require additional information.

---

### 5. Jon Peterson Special Needs Scholarship

**Instrument:** Ohio Department of Education; Ohio Revised Code Chapter 3310

**Live Link:** [https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/Other-Resources/Scholarships/Jon-Peterson-Special-Needs-Scholarship](https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/Other-Resources/Scholarships/Jon-Peterson-Special-Needs-Scholarship)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The Jon Peterson Special Needs Scholarship provides $10,045 to $34,000 per year for students with disabilities."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Ohio offers substantial funding for homeschool students with disabilities through the Jon Peterson Scholarship, ranging from $10,045 to $34,000 annually depending on disability category.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Most Dramatic Deregulation in US History:** HB 33 (October 2023) eliminated testing, teacher qualifications, 900-hour minimums, and curriculum outlines in a single bill - the most significant homeschool deregulation ever enacted.

2. **Notification Only:** Post-HB 33, Ohio requires only a simple annual notice with parent's name, child's name, and subject assurance.

3. **Jon Peterson Scholarship:** Up to $34,000/year for special needs homeschool students - among the most generous special education funding programs for homeschoolers.

4. **College Credit Plus:** Free college courses at Ohio public institutions for homeschool students.

5. **No Approval Process:** The superintendent's acknowledgment is merely a receipt, not an approval. Parents do not need permission to homeschool.
