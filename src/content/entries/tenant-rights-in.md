## The Short Answer

In Indiana, your landlord must give you a **10-day written notice** to pay rent or quit for nonpayment (IC 32-31-1-6). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **reasonable notice** to cure (typically interpreted as the time remaining in the rental period). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (IC 32-31-1-1). Indiana has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### IC 32-31-3-12 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **45 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist** if deposit exceeds one month's rent.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **actual damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://iga.in.gov/legislative/laws/2025/ic/titles/032#32-31-3-12
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 45-day return. Move-in checklist required for deposits over one month's rent.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 10 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation | Reasonable notice to cure | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Indiana landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Indiana preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery or certified/registered mail. Posting on door is NOT authorized for pre-suit notice.
- **Recording:** Indiana is a one-party consent state for recordings (IC 35-33.5-1-5). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Ohio:** Both have moderate protections, but IN has a longer 10-day notice vs. OH's 3-day notice.
- **vs. Illinois:** IN is more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no Chicago-style RLTO.
- **vs. Michigan:** Both have similar 10-day/30-day notice frameworks, but MI has stricter deposit rules.
