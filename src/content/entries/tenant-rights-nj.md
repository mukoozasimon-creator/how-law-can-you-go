## The Short Answer

In New Jersey, the **Anti-Eviction Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1 et seq.)** makes the state a **good-cause eviction jurisdiction** for most residential tenancies. A landlord cannot evict or non-renew without proving one of 18 enumerated grounds. Notice periods vary by ground: **no separate notice for nonpayment**, **3 days for disorderly conduct/criminal activity**, **1 month for lease violations** (after a prior notice to cease), **2 months for owner move-in**, **3 months for code-based retirement**, **18 months for permanent retirement from residential use**, and **3 years for condo conversion**. Self-help eviction is illegal. Security deposits are capped at **1.5 months' rent**.

### N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum security deposit: **1.5 months' rent**
- Must be deposited in **interest-bearing account** at NJ bank within 30 days
- Annual interest payment to tenant (or credit toward rent)
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement
- Failure to comply: Forfeiture of right to withhold any deposit

## Important Notes

- **Covered vs. non-covered tenancies:** The Anti-Eviction Act covers most residential rentals. Exemptions include owner-occupied premises with not more than 2 rental units and seasonal rentals, which fall under the older dispossess statute (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-53) with 1-month notice for month-to-month.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery, leaving with family member over age 14, or certified mail with regular-mail fallback.
- **Cure right for nonpayment:** Tenant can stop eviction by paying rent in default plus costs into court before final judgment (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-55).
- **Rent control:** Many NJ municipalities have rent control. Check local ordinances.
- **Recording:** New Jersey is a one-party consent state for recordings (N.J.S.A. 2A:156A-4). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Both are good-cause states, but NJ's notice periods vary by ground while NY uses sliding scale by tenancy length.
- **vs. Pennsylvania:** NJ has far stronger protections with mandatory just cause; PA allows lease waiver of notice requirements.
- **vs. California:** Both require just cause, but NJ's per-ground notice system is more complex than CA's 30/60-day framework.
