# Stand Your Ground / Self-Defense Rights - Alaska

## One-Sentence Verdict

Alaska is a statutory stand-your-ground state: there is no duty to retreat before using force, including deadly force, in any place where the person has a legal right to be, provided the person is not the initial aggressor and is not engaged in illegal activity.

## Verbatim Quotes Anchored to Character Offsets

### AS 11.81.335 - Use of Deadly Force in Defense of Self

> "(b) A person may not use deadly force under this section if the person knows that, with complete personal safety and with complete safety as to others being defended, the person can avoid the necessity of using deadly force by leaving the area of the encounter, except there is no duty to leave the area if the person is ... (5) in any other place where the person has a right to be."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "(b) A person may not use deadly force" through "(5) in any other place where the person has a right to be."
- **Source**: Alaska Statutes Title 11, Criminal Law, AS 11.81.335(b), as codified and current through 2026.
- **Live link**: [Alaska Statutes](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp)

### AS 11.81.350 - Defense of Property and Premises

> "A person justified in using force under this section does not have a duty to leave or attempt to leave the area of the encounter before using force."

- **Character offset**: standalone provision within AS 11.81.350.
- **Source**: Alaska Statutes Title 11, AS 11.81.350.
- **Live link**: [Alaska Statutes](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp)

## Castle Doctrine Scope

- **Home**: Full protection. No duty to retreat. Deadly force permitted against imminent threat of death, serious physical injury, kidnapping, sexual assault, or robbery.
- **Vehicle**: Treated as temporary residence under AS 11.81.335(b)(1)(B).
- **Workplace**: Explicitly covered under AS 11.81.335(b)(3).
- **Curtilage**: Not explicitly defined in statute; case law may extend protections.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Comprehensive SYG**: Alaska's statute explicitly lists five categories of locations where there is no duty to retreat, including the catch-all "any other place where the person has a right to be."
2. **Defense of Property**: AS 11.81.350 explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force to defend property, not just persons.
3. **Enacted 2013**: Alaska's SYG provision was added by the legislature in 2013, relatively late compared to other SYG states.
4. **No Civil Immunity Statute**: Unlike some SYG states, Alaska does not have a standalone civil immunity provision; civil liability would be determined by common law.

## Recording Consent Rules

Alaska is a **one-party consent** state for audio recordings. Alaska Statute AS 42.20.310 makes it a misdemeanor to record a private conversation without the consent of at least one party.

---

## Practical Notes

- Alaska's SYG law is broad but not the strongest in the nation. There is no pre-trial immunity hearing procedure like Florida's.
- The "reasonable belief" and "necessary" standards still apply. SYG does not authorize force against non-imminent threats.
- Alaska's remote geography means law enforcement response times can be lengthy, making self-defense considerations particularly relevant for rural residents.
- Consult an Alaska criminal defense attorney immediately after any use-of-force incident.
