# Homeschooling Laws - Iowa

## Statutory Framework

Iowa offers **five distinct private instruction options** under Iowa Code Chapter 299A, providing families with a spectrum of regulatory paths from zero oversight to full dual enrollment.

### Option 1: Independent Private Instruction (IPI)

Established in 2013 under **Iowa Code 299A.1(2)(b)**.

> "Independent Private Instruction (IPI) was added into the Iowa Code in 2013 and is defined as instruction that is: not accredited, enrolls not more than four unrelated students, does not charge tuition, and provides private or religious-based instruction in mathematics, reading, language arts, science, and social studies."
> - Source: [Homeschool Iowa - Know the Law](https://homeschooliowa.org/start-homeschooling/know-the-law-rules/) (2025-08-10)

**IPI Requirements:**
- **No Form A filing** and no advance notice to the district
- Must teach **5 subjects**: math, reading and language arts, science, social studies
- No minimum instructional days or daily hour requirements
- No assessment required unless requested by the district superintendent or Iowa Department of Education
- Max **4 unrelated students**; cannot charge tuition or remuneration
- **No dual enrollment** in public school classes, extracurriculars, or HSAP services

> "IPI has no initial reporting requirements, but school district officials may request (in written form only) specific details about the IPI program."
> - Source: [Homeschool Iowa - Know the Law](https://homeschooliowa.org/start-homeschooling/know-the-law-rules/)

### Option 2: Competent Private Instruction (CPI) with Opt-Out Reporting

Also added in 2013. Allows parents to choose **not** to report to the public school.

- No submission of CPI report form at the beginning of the school year
- No submission of annual assessment results at the end of the school year
- Provides more flexibility than traditional CPI while maintaining legal compliance

### Option 3: Competent Private Instruction (CPI) Option 2 (Supervised by Non-Licensed Parent)

The most commonly used CPI option for families wanting some public school resource access.

> "CPI Option 2 is supervised by a parent, guardian, or custodian who does not hold an Iowa teaching license."
> - Source: [The Schoolhouse - Iowa Homeschool Curriculum Guide](https://www.theschoolhouse.org/post/iowa-homeschool-curriculum-guide) (2026-03-30)

**Requirements:**
- **Form A filing is optional** unless the family wants dual enrollment, extracurricular participation, or special education services
- Instruction must be provided daily for at least **148 days per year**, with a minimum of **37 days per quarter**
- Annual assessment reporting is **optional**
- Assessment options (if opted in): standardized tests, portfolio review by a licensed teacher, or evaluation by an accredited correspondence school
- "Adequate progress" defined as scores above the **30th percentile** based on national norms, plus at least six months of progress from the previous evaluation

### Option 4: Competent Private Instruction (CPI) by Licensed Iowa Teacher

Requires instruction by a person holding a valid Iowa teaching license.
- Form A filing required
- 148 days / 37 days per quarter
- No assessment requirement (the licensed teacher's supervision satisfies this)
- Full dual enrollment access

### Option 5: Enrollment in Accredited Correspondence School

The child is enrolled in an accredited correspondence school that is recognized by the Iowa Department of Education.
- Form A filing typically required
- Compliance with the correspondence school's requirements satisfies Iowa law

### Teacher Qualifications

- **IPI:** None
- **CPI Option 2 (parent-supervised):** None
- **CPI by licensed teacher:** Valid Iowa teaching license required
- **Correspondence school:** Accreditation of the school satisfies this

### Testing Requirements

- **IPI:** None (unless district requests information)
- **CPI with opt-out:** None
- **CPI Option 2:** Optional; if opted in, must complete by May 31 and submit by August 1
- **CPI by licensed teacher:** None

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 16 (or until high school diploma).

### Public School Access

Dual enrollment and extracurricular access depend on the option chosen:
- **IPI:** No access to dual enrollment or extracurriculars
- **CPI Option 2 (with Form A):** Access to dual enrollment, extracurriculars, and special education services
- **CPI by licensed teacher:** Full access

### Distinctive Features

| **Subjects** | 5 required | 5 tested areas | 5 tested areas | 5 tested areas | Per school |

### Verdict

Iowa is a **multi-path, low-to-moderate regulation** state. The IPI option offers near-zero oversight comparable to Idaho or Oklahoma, while the CPI paths provide structured options for families seeking public school resource access. The 2013 reforms dramatically reduced reporting burdens, making Iowa one of the more flexible Midwestern states for homeschooling.
