# Homeschooling Laws - Maine

## Statutory Framework

Maine homeschools operate under **M.R.S. 20-A 5001-A(3)(A)(4)**, which provides a dedicated home instruction program within the compulsory attendance framework.

### Primary Instrument: M.R.S. 20-A 5001-A(3)(A)(4)

> "The student's parent or guardian shall provide a written notice of intent to provide home instruction simultaneously to the school officials of the administrative unit in which the student resides and to the commissioner within 10 calendar days of the beginning of home instruction."
> - Source: [Maine Legislature - Title 20-A, 5001-A](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/20-a/title20-asec5001-a.html) (Authority: NA)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Notice of Intent - First Year (5001-A(4)(a))

The notice must contain:
1. Name, signature, and address of the student's parent or guardian
2. Name and age of the student
3. Date the home instruction program will begin
4. A statement of assurance that the program will provide at least **175 days annually** of instruction in: English and language arts, math, science, social studies, physical education, health education, library skills, fine arts, and in at least one grade from grade 6 to 12, **Maine studies**
5. A statement of assurance that at one grade level from grade 7 to 12, the student will demonstrate proficiency in the use of **computers**
6. A statement of assurance that the program will include an **annual assessment** of academic progress

> "A statement of assurance that indicates the home instruction program will provide at least 175 days annually of instruction and will provide instruction in the following subject areas: English and language arts, math, science, social studies, physical education, health education, library skills, fine arts and, in at least one grade from grade 6 to 12, Maine studies. At one grade level from grade 7 to 12, the student will demonstrate proficiency in the use of computers."
> - Source: [Maine Department of Education - Home Instruction Requirements](http://www.maine.gov/doe/schools/schoolops/homeinstruction/requirements) (Authority: A)

### Annual Continuation - Subsequent Years (5001-A(4)(b))

On or before **September 1** of each subsequent year, the parent must file a letter with the school officials and commissioner stating the intention to continue home instruction and enclose a copy of one of the following forms of annual assessment:

1. A **standardized achievement test** administered through the administrative unit or other arrangements approved by the commissioner
2. A **test developed by school officials** appropriate to the home instruction program (must be agreed to prior to submission of notice)
3. A review and acceptance of progress by an individual holding a **current Maine teacher's certificate**
4. A review and acceptance based on a presentation of an **educational portfolio** to a local homeschooling support group whose membership includes a currently certified Maine teacher or administrator
5. A review and acceptance by a **local advisory board** selected by the superintendent (one administrative unit employee and two home instruction tutors)

> "On or before September 1st of each subsequent year of home instruction, the student's parent or guardian shall file a letter with the school officials of the administrative unit in which the student resides and the commissioner stating the intention to continue providing home instruction and enclose a copy of one of the following forms of annual assessment."
> - Source: [Maine Department of Education - Home Instruction Requirements](http://www.maine.gov/doe/schools/schoolops/homeinstruction/requirements) (Authority: A)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Maine statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for homeschool parents.

### Testing Requirements

Annual assessment is required, but parents may choose from **five assessment options**, including portfolio review and support group evaluation - not limited to standardized testing.

### Instructional Time

At least **175 days** annually.

### Required Subjects

- English and language arts
- Math
- Science
- Social studies
- Physical education
- Health education
- Library skills
- Fine arts
- Maine studies (at one grade, 6-12)
- Computer proficiency (at one grade, 7-12)

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 17.

### Truancy

Home instruction lies within the compulsory attendance law, so truancy laws apply if the program is not properly established.

> "As home instruction lies within the compulsory attendance law, truancy laws apply."
> - Source: [Maine Department of Education - Home Instruction Requirements](http://www.maine.gov/doe/schools/schoolops/homeinstruction/requirements) (Authority: A)

### Public School Access

Maine does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school classes, sports, or extracurricular activities. District policies vary.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Notice of intent within 10 days of beginning; annual letter by Sep 1 |
| **Testing** | Annual assessment (5 options including portfolio) |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | 9 subjects + Maine studies + computer proficiency |
| **Instructional Time** | 175 days minimum |
| **Assessment Options** | Test, district-developed test, certified teacher review, portfolio to support group, or advisory board |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 6-17 |
| **Truancy** | Applies if program not properly established |

### Verdict

Maine is a **moderate-regulation** state. The 175-day requirement, 9+ subject mandates, and annual assessment create a meaningful compliance burden. However, the five assessment options (including portfolio and support group review) provide flexibility that pure testing states lack. The Maine studies and computer proficiency requirements are distinctive state-specific mandates.
