## The Short Answer

Iowa is a **stand-your-ground state** (since 2017). You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not engaged in illegal activity and are in a place where you have a right to be (Iowa Code 704.1(3)). Iowa's **Castle Doctrine** (704.1) creates a presumption that you are justified in using deadly force when someone unlawfully enters your home, place of employment, or occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, serious injury, or the commission of a forcible felony. Iowa also provides **civil and criminal immunity** under 704.13.

## Key Statutes

### Iowa Code 704.1 - Reasonable Force

> "(3) A person who is not engaged in illegal activity has no duty to retreat from any place where the person is lawfully present before using force as specified in this chapter. (4) A person who is lawfully present in the person's dwelling, place of employment, or occupied vehicle is presumed to be justified in using reasonable force, including deadly force, against another person if the person reasonably believes that the other person is committing or attempting to commit a felony involving force or violence, or is committing or attempting to commit unlawful entry into the dwelling, place of employment, or occupied vehicle."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.iowa.gov/law/statutoryCode/sections?code=704&section=1
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat. Presumption of justification in dwelling, place of employment, or occupied vehicle.

### Iowa Code 704.3 - Reasonable Force

> "A person is justified in the use of reasonable force when the person reasonably believes such force is necessary to defend oneself or another from any imminent use of unlawful force."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.iowa.gov/law/statutoryCode/sections?code=704&section=3
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified against imminent unlawful force.

### Iowa Code 704.13 - Immunity

> "A person who uses reasonable force, including deadly force, in accordance with this chapter is immune from criminal prosecution and civil liability for any damages caused to the person against whom the force was used."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.iowa.gov/law/statutoryCode/sections?code=704&section=13
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil liability.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In your place of employment | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Stand-your-ground since 2017:** Iowa enacted its stand-your-ground law in 2017, removing the previous duty to retreat.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to employment:** The presumption applies to your dwelling, place of employment, and occupied vehicle.
- **Civil and criminal immunity:** Iowa provides broad immunity for justified use of force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Iowa is a one-party consent state for recordings (Iowa Code 808B.2). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Minnesota:** MN imposes a duty to retreat; IA does not.
- **vs. Missouri:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Midwest.
- **vs. Nebraska:** NE imposes a duty to retreat; IA does not.
