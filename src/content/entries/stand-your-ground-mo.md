## The Short Answer

Missouri is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not the initial aggressor and are in a place where you have a right to be (RSMo 563.031.3). Missouri's **Castle Doctrine** (RSMo 563.031.2(2)) creates a presumption that you held a reasonable belief in the necessity of force when someone unlawfully enters or attempts to enter your dwelling, residence, or lawfully occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent death, serious physical injury, or any forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### RSMo 563.031 - Use of Force in Defense of Persons

> "(1) A person may, subject to the provisions of subsection 2 of this section, use physical force upon another person when and to the extent he or she reasonably believes such force to be necessary to defend himself or herself or a third person from what he or she reasonably believes to be the use or imminent use of unlawful force by such other person. (2) A person may not use deadly force upon another person under the circumstances specified in subsection 1 of this section unless: (a) He or she reasonably believes that such deadly force is necessary to protect himself or herself or another against death, serious physical injury, or any forcible felony..."

**Live link:** https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=563.031
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified against death, serious physical injury, or any forcible felony.

### RSMo 563.031.2(2) - Castle Doctrine Presumption

> "A person does not have a duty to retreat: (a) From a dwelling, residence, or vehicle where the person is not unlawfully entering or unlawfully remaining; (b) From private property that is owned or leased by such individual; or (c) If the person is in any other location such person has the right to be."

**Live link:** https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=563.031
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat from dwelling, residence, vehicle, or any place you have a right to be.

### RSMo 563.031.3 - Stand Your Ground

> "A person who is not the initial aggressor and is in a place he or she has a right to be has no duty to retreat before using force, including deadly force, to defend against an imminent threat."

**Live link:** https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=563.031
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Stand-your-ground explicitly codified. No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be.

### RSMo 563.031.5 - Burden of Proof

> "The defendant shall have the burden of injecting the issue of justification under this section. If the defendant satisfies that burden, the state must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant did not act in self-defense."

**Live link:** https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=563.031
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Defendant must raise the issue; then state must disprove beyond reasonable doubt.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your residence | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Missouri explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine presumption:** The presumption of reasonable fear is significant. The state must overcome this presumption with evidence.
- **Forcible felony exception:** Missouri allows deadly force to prevent "any forcible felony," which is broader than many states that limit it to enumerated felonies.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Civil immunity:** Missouri provides civil immunity for justified use of force.
- **Recording:** Missouri is a one-party consent state for recordings (RSMo 542.402). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Kansas:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Midwest.
- **vs. Iowa:** IA enacted stand-your-ground in 2017; MO has had it longer.
- **vs. Illinois:** IN has explicit statutory SYG; IL relies on case law.
