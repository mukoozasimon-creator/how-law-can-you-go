## The Short Answer

In Nevada, your landlord must give you a **7-day written notice** (judicial days, excluding weekends and holidays) to pay rent or surrender the premises for nonpayment (NRS 40.2512, 40.253). For lease violations other than nonpayment, a **5-day cure-or-quit notice** applies (NRS 40.2516). For substantial violations (nuisance, waste, unlawful business, controlled substances), a **3-day no-cure notice** applies (NRS 40.2514). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (NRS 40.251). Nevada has no statewide just-cause requirement and no rent control.

## Key Statutes

### NRS 40.2512 & 40.253 - 7-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may give the tenant a written notice to pay the rent or surrender the premises within seven judicial days... The notice must advise the tenant of the right to contest by filing an affidavit with the court."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-040.html#NRS40Sec2512
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7 judicial days (excluding weekends/holidays) to pay or quit. Tenant can cure by paying in full. Mobile home lots get 10 days.

### NRS 40.2516 - 5-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "For a material noncompliance with the rental agreement other than nonpayment, the landlord must deliver a written notice specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than five days after receipt of the notice if the breach is not remedied."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-040.html#NRS40Sec2516
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 5-day cure-or-quit for lease violations. Second breach within 6 months = no cure right.

### NRS 40.2514 - 3-Day Notice for Substantial Violations

> "For nuisance, waste, unlawful business, or controlled-substance violations, the landlord may deliver a written notice specifying the time, place, and nature of the act and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than three days after receipt of the notice."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-040.html#NRS40Sec2514
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day no-cure notice for serious violations. No opportunity to remedy.

### NRS 40.251 - 30-Day No-Cause Termination

> "A landlord may terminate a periodic tenancy by giving written notice: 30 days for a monthly tenancy, 7 days for a weekly tenancy, 5 days for a tenancy at will."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-040.html#NRS40Sec251
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days for month-to-month. Tenants 60+ or with disability may request additional 30 days.

### NRS 40.280 - Service Requirements

> "Notice must be served by a sheriff, constable, licensed process server, or attorney's agent. The landlord may not serve the notice personally."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-040.html#NRS40Sec280
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Landlord cannot serve notice personally. Must use authorized server.

### NRS 118A.510 - Anti-Retaliation

> "A landlord may not retaliate against a tenant for complaining about habitability, filing a complaint with a government agency, or exercising any right under the law."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-118A.html#NRS118ASec510
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Retaliatory eviction is prohibited. Rebuttable presumption if action within 90 days of protected activity.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 7 judicial days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 5 days (cure or quit) | N/A |
| Substantial violation | 3 days (no cure) | N/A |
| Weekly tenancy termination | 7 days written | No |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Nevada landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Nevada preempts local rent control under NRS 118.165.
- **Summary eviction:** Nevada uses an affidavit-driven summary eviction process. If tenant does not file an affidavit contesting the notice, court can order removal without a hearing.
- **Service:** Only authorized servers (sheriff, constable, process server, attorney's agent) may serve notice. Landlord cannot do it personally.
- **Senior/disability extension:** Tenants 60+ or with disability may request additional 30 days for no-cause termination.
- **Recording:** Nevada is a one-party consent state for recordings (NRS 200.620). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** NV is far more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no rent control.
- **vs. Arizona:** Both have similar landlord-friendly frameworks with no just-cause requirements.
- **vs. Utah:** NV has longer notice periods (7 days vs. UT's 3 days for nonpayment) and requires authorized service.
