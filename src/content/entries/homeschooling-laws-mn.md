## Summary

Minnesota requires moderate regulation for homeschooling. Under Minn. Stat. 120A.24, parents must file an annual Compulsory Instruction Report with the local superintendent by October 1 (or within 15 days of beginning mid-year). Parents teaching their own children face no qualification requirements, but those teaching other people's children need a bachelor's degree, teaching license, or competency exam. Minnesota mandates 4 broad subject areas and requires annual standardized testing with no minimum score. The state offers robust PSEO (dual enrollment) and a K-12 education tax deduction.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**Minn. Stat. 120A.24** - Reporting

> "A person who is providing instruction to a child... shall submit the following information to the superintendent of the school district in which the child resides..."

- **Instrument**: Minn. Stat. 120A.24
- **Live Link**: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/120A.24
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Annual reporting to the local superintendent is required with specific information.

### Compulsory Instruction Statute

**Minn. Stat. 120A.22** - Compulsory instruction

> Establishes that children ages 7-17 must receive instruction and defines the homeschool exemption.

- **Instrument**: Minn. Stat. 120A.22
- **Live Link**: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/120A.22
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026
- **Verdict**: Establishes compulsory instruction ages and the homeschool alternative.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual Compulsory Instruction Report by October 1 |
| **Teacher Qualifications** | None for own children; bachelor's/teacher license/competency exam for others' children |
| **Required Subjects** | 4 broad areas (see below) |
| **Testing/Assessment** | Annual nationally norm-referenced standardized test (no minimum score) |
| **Record-Keeping** | Test results retained by family; immunization records submitted |
| **Attendance** | Not specified (approx. 170-180 days typical) |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 7-17 |

## Notification

**Annual Compulsory Instruction Report** must be filed with the local school district superintendent by **October 1** each year. For mid-year starts, file within **15 days** of beginning home instruction.

The report must include:
- Child's name, birth date, and address
- Annual tests intended to be used to meet assessment requirements
- Confirmation that the form is submitted by the parent
- Immunization records (per Minn. Stat. 121A.15, subdivision 15)

## Required Subjects (4 Broad Areas)

Under Minn. Stat. 120A.22, subdivision 9, instruction must include:

1. **Basic communication skills** including Reading, Writing, Literature, and Fine Arts
2. **Mathematics and Science**
3. **Social Studies** including History, Geography, Economics, Government, and Citizenship
4. **Health and Physical Education**

The state does not approve curricula or evaluate teaching quality.

## Assessment & Testing

Annual testing is required for students who:
- Were at least 7 on October 1 of the current school year and have not yet turned 16, OR
- Began homeschooling after their 16th birthday and have not yet turned 17

Requirements:
- Must be a **nationally norm-referenced standardized achievement test**
- **No minimum score** is required
- Results are **retained by the family**, not submitted to the district
- If a student scores at or below the **30th percentile** or one grade level below age level, the parent must obtain additional evaluation for learning disabilities

## Record-Keeping

- Test results kept at home (not submitted)
- Immunization records submitted with annual report
- Recommended: curriculum records, work samples, attendance documentation

## Special Provisions

- **PSEO (Post-Secondary Enrollment Options)**: High school students may take college courses for free at Minnesota public colleges and universities
- **K-12 Education Tax Deduction**: Up to $1,625 per child for qualifying educational expenses (books, tutoring, academic summer camps, etc.)
- **Textbook Lending**: School districts may (but are not required to) lend textbooks to homeschool families
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Special Education**: Districts must evaluate suspected disabilities under Child Find; some services may be available

## Distinctive Features

- Annual testing with **no minimum score** (results private)
- Parent qualifications only apply when teaching **other people's children**
- Strong PSEO dual enrollment program
- K-12 tax deduction for educational expenses
- 30th percentile trigger for learning disability evaluation
- Immunization records required with annual report
