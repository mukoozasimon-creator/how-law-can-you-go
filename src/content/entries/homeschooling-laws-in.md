## Summary

Indiana is a no-notice, low-regulation state for homeschooling. Under IC 20-33-2-28, parents must simply provide an "equivalent education" to that offered in public schools and teach in English. There is no registration, no notification, no curriculum approval, no standardized testing, and no parent qualifications. Homeschools are classified as "non-accredited private schools." Indiana also offers a $1,000 tax deduction per student for educational expenses and one of the nation's most generous special needs ESA programs (up to $20,000 annually).

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**IC 20-33-2-28** - Equivalent education required

> "A parent who provides a child with an equivalent education that is provided in the public schools is not required to send the child to a public or nonpublic school."

- **Instrument**: IC 20-33-2-28
- **Live Link**: http://iga.in.gov/legislative/laws/2026/ic/titles/020#20-33-2-28
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Parents providing equivalent education are exempt from compulsory school attendance requirements.

### Compulsory Attendance Statute

**IC 20-33-2-6** - Compulsory school attendance

> Establishes that children ages 7-18 must attend school or receive equivalent instruction.

- **Instrument**: IC 20-33-2-6
- **Live Link**: http://iga.in.gov/legislative/laws/2026/ic/titles/020#20-33-2-6
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026
- **Verdict**: Establishes compulsory attendance ages and the homeschool exemption.

| Requirement | Detail |

| **Teacher Qualifications** | None required |
| **Required Subjects** | None mandated ("equivalent education") |
| **Testing** | None required |

## Notification

No notification or registration is required to begin homeschooling in Indiana. The Indiana Department of Education offers a **voluntary** enrollment reporting form (IC 20-33-2-21), but submission is explicitly optional.

**Important**: If withdrawing a **high school student** from public school, parents MUST sign the "Withdrawal to Non-Accredited Non-public School Located in Indiana" form. This prevents the student from being classified as a dropout, which affects driver's license eligibility.

## Required Subjects

No subjects are mandated by law. The standard is simply "equivalent education." The Indiana Department of Education publishes Indiana Academic Standards as a voluntary benchmark, but homeschoolers are not required to follow them.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required. Homeschoolers are explicitly exempt from state standardized testing.

## Record-Keeping

Indiana law requires only **attendance records**, and only upon request from the state superintendent or local superintendent (IC 20-33-2-20).

- Track dates of instruction to document 180 days
- No specific format required
- Superintendents cannot request curriculum details, work samples, or anything beyond attendance verification

**Recommended** (not required):
- Curriculum descriptions and materials used
- Work samples across subjects
- Test results (if administered)
- Course descriptions for high school (essential for transcripts)

## Special Provisions

- **Tax Deduction**: Up to $1,000 per student for educational expenses (curriculum, software, textbooks, instructional materials)
- **INESA (Indiana ESA)**: Up to $20,000 annually for students with disabilities; up to $8,000 for siblings without disabilities. Requires IEP/service plan and household income below 400% of free/reduced lunch threshold. **Note**: Accepting INESA means the student is no longer an independent homeschooler and must participate in statewide assessments.
- **Parent-Issued Diplomas**: HEA 1348 (2025) explicitly recognizes homeschool diplomas as meeting state high school completion requirements - state agencies and colleges cannot reject students solely for having a parent-issued diploma.
- **Work Permits**: As of April 2020, homeschoolers do NOT need work permits for employment
- **Driver's License**: Learner's permit at age 15 with driver's ed, or 16 without; license at 16+90 days (with ed) or 16+270 days (without)
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation

## Distinctive Features

- **No-notice state** - one of the most deregulated homeschool environments
- $1,000 tax deduction per student
- Generous special needs ESA (up to $20,000)
- State law now explicitly recognizes parent-issued diplomas (HEA 1348, 2025)
- High school withdrawal form is critical for driver's license eligibility
- "Equivalent education" standard is intentionally undefined by law
