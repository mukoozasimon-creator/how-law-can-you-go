## The Short Answer

New Mexico does **not have a formal stand-your-ground statute**, but **case law supports the principle that there is no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense when faced with an imminent threat (State v. Horton, 1982). New Mexico's **Castle Doctrine** (NMSA 30-2-7) allows the use of deadly force inside your home against an intruder who unlawfully and forcibly enters. Deadly force is only justified when you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm.

## Key Statutes

### NMSA 30-2-7 - Justifiable Homicide by Public Officer or Party Acting in Aid of Public Officer

> "Homicide is justifiable when committed by any person in any of the following cases: A. when committed in the lawful defense of the person committing the homicide or of another when there is reasonable ground to apprehend a design to commit a felony or to do some great personal injury to the person committing the homicide or to another, and there is imminent danger of such design being accomplished; or B. when necessarily committed in attempting, by lawful ways and means, to apprehend any person for any felony committed..."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-30/article-2/section-30-2-7/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified against imminent felony or great personal injury.

### State v. Horton (1982) - No Duty to Retreat

> New Mexico case law has established that a person has no duty to retreat before using force in self-defense when faced with an imminent threat of death or great bodily harm.

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat by case law, even without a formal SYG statute.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/great bodily harm |
| In public | No (case law) | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No (case law) | Same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No formal SYG statute:** New Mexico does not have a statute explicitly removing the duty to retreat. The principle comes from case law.
- **Castle Doctrine by case law:** While not as formalized as in some states, New Mexico recognizes the right to defend your home without retreating.
- **Reasonable belief standard:** Both your belief in the threat and your belief that force is necessary must be objectively reasonable.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** New Mexico is a one-party consent state for recordings (NMSA 30-12-1). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Arizona:** AZ has explicit statutory SYG; NM relies on case law.
- **vs. Texas:** Both allow no duty to retreat, but TX has explicit statutory protections.
- **vs. Colorado:** CO has the Make My Day law; NM has less formalized protections.
