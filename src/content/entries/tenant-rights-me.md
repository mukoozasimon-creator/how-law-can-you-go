## The Short Answer

In Maine, your landlord must give you a **7-day written notice** to pay rent or face eviction (14 MRSA 6002(1)). For material lease violations, a **7-day notice** to quit applies (14 MRSA 6002). To end a tenancy at will (month-to-month), the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (14 MRSA 6002). Maine has no statewide just-cause requirement (except in Portland, which has local rent control). Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### 14 MRSA 6002(1) - 7-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "Unless the lease states otherwise, if a tenant is at least 7 days late paying rent, a 7-day Notice to quit gives them 7 days to pay, warning that if they don't pay what they owe, you will take them to court to evict."

**Live link:** https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6002.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 7 days. Notice voids if tenant pays amount due before expiration. After expiration, tenant can still reinstate by paying all arrears, rent due, and filing fees before writ of possession issues.

### 14 MRSA 6002 - 7-Day Notice for Cause

> "For substantial damage, nuisance, unfitness, legal violation, or perpetrator of domestic violence, a 7-day notice to quit applies."

**Live link:** https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6002.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day notice for enumerated causes. Some causes (substantial damage, nuisance) may not have a statutory right to cure.

### 14 MRSA 6002 - 30-Day Tenancy-at-Will Termination

> "A tenancy at will may be terminated by either party by giving at least 30 days' written notice."

**Live link:** https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6002.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for tenancy-at-will termination.

### 14 MRSA 6032 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **2 months' rent** (includes all refundable deposits).
- Return deadline: **30 days** after tenancy terminates. If itemized statement provided within 21 days, remaining balance must accompany it.

**Live link:** https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6032.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. 2-month cap.

### 14 MRSA 6004 - Information Sheet Required

> "The 'Residential Forcible Entry and Detainer (Eviction) Information Sheet and Mediation Request' must be served with each Notice to Quit in a residential tenancy."

**Live link:** https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/14/title14sec6004.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Landlord must serve official information sheet (Form CV-256) with every eviction notice.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Tenancy-at-will termination | 30 days written | No (statewide) |
| Nonpayment of rent | 7 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation / cause | 7 days to quit | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No statewide just-cause requirement:** Maine landlords can terminate tenancies at will for any reason with proper notice, except in Portland which has local rent control requiring longer no-cause notice.
- **Portland rent control:** Portland's ordinance (Code of Ordinances Chapter 6) requires longer no-cause notice than the 30-day state minimum and ties shorter options to the landlord paying stated months' rent.
- **Reinstatement right:** Tenant can reinstate tenancy after 7-day notice expires by paying all rental arrears, rent due as of payment date, and filing fees before writ of possession issues.
- **Service:** In-hand personal service is default. After 3 good-faith efforts, service by first-class mail plus leaving at abode is permitted.
- **Recording:** Maine is a one-party consent state for recordings (14 MRSA 710). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Vermont:** Both have longer no-cause notice periods (VT: 60-90 days) and tenant-friendly frameworks.
- **vs. New Hampshire:** NH requires good cause for most evictions; ME does not statewide (except Portland).
- **vs. Massachusetts:** MA has just-cause requirements and rent control in some cities; ME does not statewide.
