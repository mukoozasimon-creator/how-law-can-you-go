## The Short Answer

Georgia is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense anywhere you have a legal right to be (O.C.G.A. 16-3-23.1). Georgia's **Castle Doctrine** (O.C.G.A. 16-3-23) allows the use of deadly force to prevent or stop an intruder who unlawfully and forcibly enters your home, occupied vehicle, or place of business. You may use deadly force if you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent death, great bodily injury, or the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### O.C.G.A. 16-3-21 - Use of Force in Defense of Self or Others

> "A person is justified in threatening or using force against another when and to the extent that he or she reasonably believes that such threat or force is necessary to defend himself or herself or a third person against such other's imminent use of unlawful force. A person is justified in using force which is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily harm only if he or she reasonably believes that such force is necessary to prevent death or great bodily injury to himself or herself or a third person or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-16/chapter-3/article-2/section-16-3-21/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified only against imminent death, great bodily injury, or forcible felony. Reasonable belief standard.

### O.C.G.A. 16-3-23.1 - No Duty to Retreat

> "A person who uses threats or force relating to the use of force in defense of self, others, habitation, or other property has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground and use force, including deadly force, if the person is not the initial aggressor and the person uses the force in accordance with the provisions of this article."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-16/chapter-3/article-2/section-16-3-23-1/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before using force in self-defense, defense of others, or defense of habitation/property.

### O.C.G.A. 16-3-23 - Use of Force in Defense of Habitation

> "A person is justified in threatening or using force against another when and to the extent that he or she reasonably believes that such threat or force is necessary to prevent or terminate such other's unlawful entry into or attack upon a habitation. A person is justified in using force which is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily harm only if the entry is made or attempted in a violent and tumultuous manner and the person reasonably believes that the entry is attempted or made for the purpose of assaulting or offering personal violence to any person dwelling or being therein..."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-16/chapter-3/article-2/section-16-3-23/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies to habitation. Deadly force justified against violent/tumultuous entry with intent to assault.

### O.C.G.A. 16-3-24.2 - Immunity from Prosecution and Civil Action

> "A person who uses threats or force in accordance with Code Section 16-3-21, relating to the use of force in defense of self or others, or Code Section 16-3-23, relating to the use of force in defense of habitation, shall be immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use of such force, unless the person using such force is a person who initially provoked the use of force against himself or herself."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-16/chapter-3/article-2/section-16-3-24-2/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from criminal prosecution and civil action for justified use of force. Initial aggressor exception applies.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (violent intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent assault |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, under Castle Doctrine principles |
| In your place of business | No | Yes, under Castle Doctrine principles |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Immunity hearing | Available | Yes, via 16-3-24.2 |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Georgia explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force in self-defense, defense of others, or defense of habitation/property.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to vehicle and business:** While 16-3-23 specifically addresses habitation, Georgia courts have applied Castle Doctrine principles to occupied vehicles and places of business.
- **Immunity from prosecution:** Georgia provides immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil action for justified use of force. A defendant may file a motion for immunity before trial.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense or immunity if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat. Deadly force is only justified against imminent death, great bodily injury, or forcible felony.
- **Recording:** Georgia is a one-party consent state for recordings (O.C.G.A. 16-11-62). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states with immunity provisions. FL has a pre-trial hearing procedure; GA has a motion for immunity.
- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad Castle Doctrine protections.
- **vs. South Carolina:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southeast.
