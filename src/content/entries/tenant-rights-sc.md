## The Short Answer

In South Carolina, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or quit for nonpayment (S.C. Code § 27-40-710(B)). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **14-day notice** to cure or quit. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (§ 27-40-770). South Carolina has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### S.C. Code § 27-40-710(A) - 14-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If there is a material noncompliance by the tenant with the rental agreement or a noncompliance with Section 27-40-510 materially affecting health and safety, the landlord may deliver a written notice to the tenant specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than fourteen days after receipt of the notice, if the breach is not remedied in fourteen days." cite🛠web_search:42#1:~:text=If there is a material noncompliance by the tenant with the rental agreement... the landlord may deliver a written notice to the tenant specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than fourteen days after receipt of the notice, if the breach is not remedied in fourteen days.

**Verdict:** 14-day notice for lease violations. Tenant has opportunity to cure.

### S.C. Code § 27-40-770 - Termination of Month-to-Month Tenancy

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least thirty days before the termination date specified in the notice." cite🛠web_search:42#1:~:text=The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least thirty days before the termination date specified in the notice.

**Live link:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### S.C. Code § 27-40-410 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **actual damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. Move-in checklist required.

### S.C. Code § 27-40-760 - Self-Help Eviction Prohibited

> "A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks." cite🛠web_search:42#1:~:text=A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks.

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** South Carolina landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** South Carolina preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery or certified/registered mail.
- **Recording:** South Carolina is a one-party consent state for recordings (S.C. Code § 17-30-30). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are landlord-friendly Southern states, but SC has a shorter 5-day notice vs. NC's 10-day demand.
- **vs. Georgia:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but GA has longer termination notice (60 days vs. SC's 30 days).
- **vs. Florida:** Both have fast eviction timelines, but FL has stricter notice-counting rules (business days only).
