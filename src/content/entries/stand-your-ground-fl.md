## The Short Answer

Florida is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you have a legal right to be and are not engaged in criminal activity (Fla. Stat. 776.012). Florida's **Castle Doctrine** (Fla. Stat. 776.013) creates a presumption of reasonable fear of imminent death or great bodily harm when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle. Florida also provides **criminal and civil immunity** through a pre-trial hearing procedure (Fla. Stat. 776.032).

## Key Statutes

### Fla. Stat. 776.012 - Use or Threatened Use of Force in Defense of Person

> "(1) A person is justified in using or threatening to use force, except deadly force, against another when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes that such conduct is necessary to defend himself or herself or another against the other's imminent use of unlawful force. A person who uses or threatens to use force in accordance with this subsection does not have a duty to retreat before using or threatening to use such force. (2) A person is justified in using or threatening to use deadly force if he or she reasonably believes that using or threatening to use such force is necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another or to prevent the imminent commission of a forcible felony. A person who uses or threatens to use deadly force in accordance with this subsection does not have a duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground if the person using or threatening to use the deadly force is not engaged in a criminal activity and is in a place where he or she has a right to be."

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter776/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before using force or deadly force if lawfully present and not engaged in criminal activity.

### Fla. Stat. 776.013 - Home Protection; Use or Threatened Use of Deadly Force

> "A person who is in a dwelling or residence in which the person has a right to be has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground and use or threaten to use: (a) Nondeadly force against another when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes that such conduct is necessary to defend himself or herself or another against the other's imminent use of unlawful force; or (b) Deadly force if he or she reasonably believes that using or threatening to use such force is necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another or to prevent the imminent commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter776/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies to dwelling, residence, and occupied vehicle. Presumption of reasonable fear during unlawful and forcible entry.

### Fla. Stat. 776.032 - Immunity from Criminal Prosecution and Civil Action

> "A person who uses or threatens to use force as permitted in 776.012, 776.013, or 776.031 is justified in such conduct and is immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use or threatened use of such force."

**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil lawsuits for justified use of force. Defendant may request a pre-trial immunity hearing.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Pre-trial immunity | Available | Yes, via 776.032 hearing |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Florida was the first state to enact a modern stand-your-ground law (2005). No duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a legal right to be.
- **Pre-trial immunity hearing:** Florida allows defendants to request a hearing before trial to establish immunity under 776.032. If successful, the case is dismissed.
- **Civil immunity:** Justified use of force provides immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil lawsuits.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you provoked the encounter, unless you withdrew and communicated that withdrawal.
- **Recording:** Florida is a two-party consent state for recordings (Fla. Stat. 934.03). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections. FL has a pre-trial immunity hearing; TX does not.
- **vs. California:** Both are stand-your-ground states. FL has broader civil immunity and a pre-trial hearing procedure.
- **vs. New York:** NY imposes a duty to retreat in public; FL does not.
