<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Massachusetts</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You must show your license, registration, and proof of insurance.</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, secs. 24, 9, and 34J. All three. The officer can demand them on the spot. Driving without a license is a fine of $500-$1,000. Up to 10 days in jail for repeat offenses.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the officer.</strong> Massachusetts is technically a two-party consent state (M.G.L. c. 272, sec. 99), but the Supreme Judicial Court has upheld the right to record police performing public duties. Police have no reasonable expectation of privacy in public traffic stops. Just do not interfere.</li>
<li><strong>They cannot make you wait for a drug dog.</strong> <em>Rodriguez v. United States</em> (2015). Once the ticket is written, the stop is over. Massachusetts courts follow this.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse to consent to a search.</strong> Fourth Amendment. Say "I do not consent to a search." If they have probable cause (smell, plain view), they can search anyway. But your refusal matters in court.</li>
<li><strong>Passengers do not have to show ID.</strong> Massachusetts has no stop-and-identify statute for mere passengers. But if the officer has reasonable suspicion that the passenger committed a crime, that changes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I forgot my license at home."</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, sec. 24 is a fine offense for first-timers, but you can be arrested. If you produce a valid license in court, the charge may be reduced.</li>
<li><strong>"I do not answer questions."</strong> True for most questions, but c. 90, sec. 24 requires you to display your license. You cannot refuse that one.</li>
<li><strong>"You cannot search my car."</strong> Correct, but Massachusetts has a broad plain-view doctrine. If the officer sees contraband, they do not need consent. Also, the smell of marijuana alone is not probable cause in Massachusetts after legalization, but the officer may claim other factors.</li>
<li><strong>"I am driving to a safer location."</strong> No Massachusetts statute gives you this right. The officer may interpret it as evading. Use your judgment, but know the law does not have your back on this.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"Massachusetts is a sanctuary state so they cannot ask for ID." Wrong. M.G.L. c. 90, sec. 24 applies to everyone driving a motor vehicle. Immigration status is irrelevant to a traffic stop.</li>
<li>"If the officer takes longer than 10 minutes, you can leave." There is no magic number. <em>Rodriguez</em> says "reasonably required to complete the mission."</li>
<li>"You have to let them search if they say they smell weed." Not anymore. Marijuana is legal in Massachusetts. Smell alone is not probable cause. But if they claim to see contraband in plain view, that is different.</li>
<li>"If you do not roll down your window, they cannot ticket you." Wrong. You must display your license. Shoving it through a cracked window satisfies the statute but makes the officer suspicious, which is not illegal but is also not smart.</li>
</ul>
</div>
