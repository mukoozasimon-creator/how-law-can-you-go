<div class="quip">Virginia: where the history is rich and your license is gone for a year if you refuse the breath test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Virginia</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> Va. Code 18.2-268.2. By driving in Virginia, you are deemed to have consented to blood, breath, or both if lawfully arrested for DUI. The officer chooses which test. Test must be within 3 hours of arrest.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 1-year suspension. Second = Class 1 misdemeanor + 3 years.</strong> Va. Code 18.2-268.3. First refusal is a civil offense. Second refusal within 10 years is a Class 1 misdemeanor with 3-year suspension.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must advise you of the consequences.</strong> Va. Code 18.2-268.3. Before a refusal is valid, the officer must advise you that refusal will result in a 1-year revocation.</li>
<li><strong>Breath tests are preferred; blood for drugs.</strong> Va. Code 18.2-268.2(B). Unless breath is unavailable, officers must administer breath tests. Blood tests are used for drug DUIs or when breath is unavailable.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Virginia is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it unless breath is unavailable.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the implied consent suspension can be challenged. But the standard is low - the officer only needs probable cause to believe you violated 18.2-266.</li>
<li><strong>"The 3-hour rule means they can't test me after 3 hours."</strong> Wrong. The test must be administered within 3 hours to be admissible under the per se statute, but it can still be admissible under other theories.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic suspension AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You have no right to counsel before the implied consent decision.</li>
<li>"The suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a restricted license right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you must serve a portion of the suspension before becoming eligible for a restricted license.</li>
</ul>
</div>
