## The Short Answer

In Delaware, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or the rental agreement terminates (25 Del. Code 5502). For material lease violations, a **7-day notice** to remedy applies (25 Del. Code 5513). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **60 days' written notice** (25 Del. Code 5106). Delaware has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### 25 Del. Code 5502 - 5-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may give the tenant a written 5-day notice to pay or the rental agreement will terminate."

**Live link:** https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c055/index.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 5-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 5 days.

### 25 Del. Code 5513 - 7-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If the tenant breaches any rule or covenant material to the rental agreement, the landlord shall notify the tenant in writing and allow at least 7 days for remedy. If the violation continues after 7 days, the landlord may terminate and bring an action for summary possession."

**Live link:** https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c055/index.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day cure period for lease violations. If same breach recurs within 1 year, landlord may rely on prior notice as grounds for eviction.

### 25 Del. Code 5106 - 60-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least 60 days before the termination date."

**Live link:** https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c051/index.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 60 days written notice for month-to-month termination. This is longer than the 30-day standard in most states.

### 25 Del. Code 5514 - Summary Possession

> "For immediate termination without notice: if the tenant causes or threatens to cause irreparable harm to person or property, the landlord may immediately terminate and file for summary possession."

**Live link:** https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c055/index.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No notice required for irreparable harm to person or property.

### 25 Del. Code 5314 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **1 month's rent** for leases 1 year or longer; no cap for shorter leases.
- Return deadline: **20 days** after lease termination.
- Must provide itemized statement if deductions made.

**Live link:** https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c053/index.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 20-day return. 1-month cap for year+ leases.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 60 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 5 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 7 days (cure) | N/A |
| Irreparable harm | No notice required | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Delaware landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Delaware does not have statewide rent control.
- **Long no-cause notice:** Delaware's 60-day month-to-month notice is longer than the 30-day standard in most states.
- **Entry notice:** Landlords must give at least 48 hours' notice before entry, between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. (25 Del. Code 5509).
- **Recording:** Delaware is a two-party consent state for recordings (11 Del. Code 1335). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Maryland:** MD requires good cause in some jurisdictions; DE does not statewide.
- **vs. New Jersey:** NJ has extensive just-cause and rent control laws; DE does not.
- **vs. Pennsylvania:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but PA has 30-day notice vs. DE's 60-day.
