# Homeschooling Laws - South Dakota

## Statutory Framework

South Dakota recognizes homeschooling as **"alternative instruction"** under **SDCL 13-27-3** and **SDCL 13-27-7**.

### Primary Instrument: SDCL 13-27-3

> "A child shall be excused from school attendance because the child is otherwise provided with alternative instruction for an equivalent period of time, as in the public schools, in the basic skills of language arts and mathematics."
> - Source: [SD Legislature - Codified Law 13-27-3](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/13-27-3) (Authority: NA)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Alternative Instruction Notification (AIN): SDCL 13-27-7

Parents must file a **one-time** Alternative Instruction Notification.

> "Each notification of alternative instruction pursuant to 13-27-3 shall be on a standard form that declares that the child will be provided with alternative instruction in the basic skills of language arts and mathematics. No person may instruct more than twenty-two children."
> - Source: [SD Legislature - Codified Law 13-27-7](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/13-27-7) (Authority: NA)

**Key details:**
- **One-time filing** - no annual renewal required
- Filed on a standard form
- Must declare that the child will receive instruction in **language arts and mathematics**
- No person may instruct more than **22 children**
- The notification is filed with the local school district

> "Parents who choose to homeschool must file a one-time Alternative Instruction Notification (AIN)."
> - Source: [Homeschool South Dakota - Getting Started](https://www.homeschoolsd.org/get-started/) (Authority: NA)

### Required Subjects

The statute specifies only two required subject areas:
- **Language arts**
- **Mathematics**

No other subjects are mandated by the alternative instruction statute.

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** South Dakota statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for parents providing alternative instruction.

### Testing Requirements

**None.** South Dakota does not mandate standardized testing, portfolio review, or annual assessment for alternative instruction students.

> "South Dakota does not require any standardized testing for homeschoolers."
> - Source: [Blue Folder - South Dakota Homeschool Requirements](https://bluefolder.app/states/south-dakota) (2026)

### Instructional Time

The statute requires instruction for "an equivalent period of time, as in the public schools." Public schools in South Dakota typically operate for approximately **180 days** per year. However, the statute does not specify exact hour or day minimums for homeschools.

### Record-Keeping

Not statutorily mandated, but maintaining records of instruction and progress is recommended.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 18.

### Public School Sports Access: SDCL 13-36-7

South Dakota law guarantees homeschoolers access to interscholastic athletics and activities.

> "Each public-school district shall allow participation in athletics, fine arts, or activities for a child being provided alternative instruction pursuant to 13-27-3 within the district in which the child resides. This includes school-sponsored activities and those sanctioned by the South Dakota High School Activities Association. Alternative instruction students must meet the same requirements as publicly enrolled students to participate in activities."
> - Source: [SD Department of Education - Homeschooling](https://doe.sd.gov/oatq/homeschooling.aspx) (Authority: NA)

**Participation requirements:**
- Proof of age (birth certificate or affidavit)
- Copy of transcript from previous semester
- Completion of SDHSAA Eligibility Checklist for Alternative Instruction Students
- Athletic physical form (if applicable)
- Compliance with all district and SDHSAA rules

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | One-time AIN (no annual renewal) |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | Language arts and mathematics only |
| **Instructional Time** | Equivalent to public schools (~180 days) |
| **Class Size** | Max 22 children per instructor |
| **Sports Access** | Statutory guarantee for athletics, fine arts, and activities |
| **Record-Keeping** | Not statutorily required |

### Verdict

South Dakota is a **low-regulation** state with minimal bureaucratic burden. The one-time notification, absence of testing and qualifications, and restriction to only two required subjects (language arts and math) make it one of the more permissive states. The 22-student cap per instructor is notable for co-op arrangements, and the statutory guarantee of sports and activities access is a significant benefit. The "equivalent period of time" standard is vague but rarely enforced against genuine homeschoolers.
