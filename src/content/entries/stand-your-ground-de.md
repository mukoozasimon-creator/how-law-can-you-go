# Stand Your Ground / Self-Defense Rights - Delaware

## One-Sentence Verdict

Delaware is a duty-to-retreat state outside the home and workplace: a person must retreat if they can do so with complete safety before using deadly force in public, but there is no duty to retreat inside one's own dwelling or place of work, and there is a specific statutory defense for occupants who use force against intruders unlawfully in their dwelling.

### 11 Del. C. Sec. 464(e)(2) - Duty to Retreat with Dwelling and Workplace Exceptions

> "(e) The use of deadly force is not justifiable under this section if: ... (2) The defendant knows that the necessity of using deadly force can be avoided with complete safety by retreating, by surrendering possession of a thing to a person asserting a claim of right thereto or by complying with a demand that the defendant abstain from performing an act which the defendant is not legally obligated to perform except that: a. The defendant is not obliged to retreat in or from the defendant's dwelling; and b. The defendant is not obliged to retreat in or from the defendant's place of work, unless the defendant was the initial aggressor; and c. A public officer justified in using force in the performance of the officer's duties ... need not desist ..."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "(e) The use of deadly force is not justifiable" through "need not desist ..."
- **Source**: Delaware Code, Title 11, Chapter 4, Section 464(e)(2), current through 2026.
- **Live link**: [Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c004/)

### 11 Del. C. Sec. 469 - Defense for Occupant Against Intruder

> "In the prosecution of an occupant of a dwelling charged with killing or injuring an intruder who was unlawfully in said dwelling, it shall be a defense that the occupant was in the occupant's own dwelling at the time of the offense, and: (1) The encounter between the occupant and intruder was sudden and unexpected, compelling the occupant to act instantly; or (2) The occupant reasonably believed that the intruder would inflict personal injury upon the occupant or others in the dwelling; or (3) The occupant demanded that the intruder disarm or surrender, and the intruder refused to do so."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "In the prosecution of an occupant of a dwelling" through "the intruder refused to do so."
- **Source**: Delaware Code, Title 11, Chapter 4, Section 469.
- **Live link**: [Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c004/)

### 11 Del. C. Sec. 464(b) - No Retreat Required for Non-Deadly Force

> "(b) Except as otherwise provided in subsections (d) and (e) of this section, a person employing protective force may estimate the necessity thereof under the circumstances as the person reasonably believes them to be when the force is used, without retreating, surrendering possession, doing any other act which the person has no legal duty to do or abstaining from any lawful action."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "(b) Except as otherwise provided" through "abstaining from any lawful action."
- **Source**: Delaware Code, Title 11, Chapter 4, Section 464(b).
- **Live link**: [Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c004/)

## Castle Doctrine Scope

- **Dwelling**: Full Castle Doctrine. No duty to retreat. Additionally, Sec. 469 provides a specific defense for occupants who kill or injure intruders unlawfully in their dwelling if the encounter was sudden, the occupant reasonably believed injury would occur, or the occupant demanded surrender and the intruder refused.
- **Place of Work**: Explicitly included. No duty to retreat in the workplace unless the defendant was the initial aggressor.
- **Vehicle**: Not explicitly covered. General duty to retreat would apply.
- **Curtilage**: Not explicitly defined in statute.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Workplace Exception**: Delaware is one of the few duty-to-retreat states that explicitly extends the no-retreat rule to the workplace (alongside Connecticut, Hawaii, Nebraska, and Wisconsin).
2. **Specific Intruder Defense (Sec. 469)**: Delaware has a unique statute (Sec. 469) that creates a specific defense for occupants who use force against intruders unlawfully in their dwelling, with three alternative conditions for the defense.
3. **No Retreat for Non-Deadly Force**: Sec. 464(b) explicitly states that non-deadly force may be used "without retreating" - a broader protection than many states provide.
4. **Model Penal Code Based**: Delaware's self-defense statutes are based on the Model Penal Code, making them more structured than common-law states.

## Recording Consent Rules

Delaware is a **two-party consent** state for audio recordings. 11 Del. C. Sec. 1335 makes it a misdemeanor to record a private conversation without the consent of all parties.

---

## Practical Notes

- Delaware's workplace exception is significant for employees who may face threats at work. However, the initial aggressor exception means that if you started the confrontation, you cannot claim the workplace no-retreat rule.
- Sec. 469 provides a powerful defense for home invasions, but one of its three conditions must be met: sudden encounter, reasonable belief of injury, or demand to surrender that was refused.
- Delaware's duty to retreat in public is real and enforceable. The standard is "complete safety" - if any risk attends retreat, the duty is discharged.
- Delaware has relatively strict gun laws, including a concealed carry permit requirement and background checks for private sales.
