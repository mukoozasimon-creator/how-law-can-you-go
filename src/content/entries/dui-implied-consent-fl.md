<div class="quip">Florida: where the alligators are friendlier than the DMV after a DUI refusal.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Florida</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> Fla. Stat. 316.1932. By driving in Florida, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, or urine testing if lawfully arrested for DUI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 1-year suspension. Second = 18 months.</strong> Fla. Stat. 322.2615. Administrative suspension by DHSMV, separate from criminal case.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal is now a crime.</strong> Fla. Stat. 316.1939 (effective Oct. 1, 2025). First refusal = second-degree misdemeanor. Second or subsequent refusal = first-degree misdemeanor. This is NEW - it used to be administrative only for first refusals.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal can be used against you in court.</strong> The fact of your refusal is admissible as evidence of consciousness of guilt.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Florida is a two-party consent state, but recording police in public is generally protected. Do not interfere.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to a lawyer first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't read me the implied consent warning."</strong> If the officer fails to read the statutory warning, the refusal may be invalid. This is a common defense at formal review hearings.</li>
<li><strong>"I wasn't driving, I was sleeping in the car."</strong> Florida uses 'actual physical control' standard. If you have the keys and the ability to operate the vehicle, you can be arrested for DUI even if the car is not moving.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers criminal charges (as of Oct. 2025), automatic license suspension, AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations.</li>
<li>"First refusal is just a civil penalty." Wrong. As of October 1, 2025, a first refusal is a second-degree misdemeanor under 316.1939.</li>
<li>"You have 30 days to request a formal review hearing." Wrong. You have 10 days. Miss it and the suspension is automatic.</li>
<li>"They can't suspend your license if you beat the DUI." Wrong. The administrative suspension is independent of the criminal case.</li>
</ul>
</div>
