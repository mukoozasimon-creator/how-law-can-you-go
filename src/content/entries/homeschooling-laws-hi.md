# Homeschooling Laws - Hawaii

## Statutory Framework

Hawaii homeschools operate under **HRS § 302A-1132** and implementing administrative rules, which require parents to provide a structured home education program with annual assessment.

### Primary Instrument: HRS § 302A-1132

> "The parent shall provide the local public school principal with a notice of intent to home educate the child before ..."
> - Source: [Christian Homeschoolers of Hawaii - Hawaii Homeschooling Rule](https://www.christianhomeschoolersofhawaii.org/hawaii-homeschooling-rule/)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Notice of Intent

Parents must submit a **Notice of Intent** to the **principal of the local public school** in their geographic area before beginning homeschooling.

> "Notification Required: Yes, Notice of Intent to principal."
> - Source: [Numa School - Hawaii Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/hawaii/homeschool-laws) (2026-07-30)

**Key details:**
- Filed with the **local public school principal** (not a state agency)
- Must be submitted before beginning instruction
- If homeschooling is terminated, the parent must notify the principal; the child must be re-enrolled in public or licensed private school within **five school days** unless a new alternative educational program is presented

> "The parent shall notify the principal if home schooling is terminated. A child shall be re-enrolled in the local public school or licensed private school unless a new alternative educational program is presented within five school days after the termination of home schooling."
> - Source: [Cornell Law School - Haw. Code R. § 8-12-16](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-8-12-16) (Authority: S)

### Structured Curriculum Requirement

Hawaii law does not mandate specific subjects, but the curriculum must be **structured** according to educational objectives and tailored to the child's needs.

> "Hawaii law does not mandate specific subjects that must be taught, but your homeschool curriculum must be structured according to certain educational objectives and tailored to meet your child's needs. The curriculum should be cumulative, sequential, and designed to provide a comprehensive range of knowledge and skills."
> - Source: [Student Discipline Defense - Homeschooling Defense in Hawaii](https://www.studentdisciplinedefense.com/homeschooling-defense-in-hawaii)

**Guideline subjects (not statutory mandates):**
- **Elementary:** Language arts, math, social studies, science, art, music, health, physical education
- **Secondary:** Social studies, English, mathematics, science, health, physical education, guidance

Note: These are administrative guidelines, not statutory requirements. Failure to meet curriculum requirements can result in state intervention.

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Hawaii statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for homeschool parents.

### Annual Assessment

Parents must obtain an **annual assessment** of the child's educational progress. The assessment may take the form of:
- A standardized achievement test
- An evaluation by a qualified person
- A portfolio review

> "Testing Required: Annual assessment (test, evaluation, or portfolio)."
> - Source: [Numa School - Hawaii Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/hawaii/homeschool-laws)

The specific requirements for the assessment (timing, submission, standards) are governed by administrative rule and may vary by district interpretation.

### Instructional Time

No minimum hours or days are specified in statute.

### Record-Keeping

Not extensively mandated by statute, but maintaining records of the curriculum, assessments, and student progress is strongly recommended to demonstrate compliance if questioned.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 5 through 18.

> "Compulsory Ages: 5-18 years old."
> - Source: [Numa School - Hawaii Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/hawaii/homeschool-laws)

### Immunization

Hawaii requires immunization records or a waiver for homeschool students, though enforcement mechanisms vary.

### Public School Access

Hawaii does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school classes, sports, or extracurricular activities. District policies vary.

### State Oversight

Hawaii retains more oversight authority than the most deregulated states. The state has the authority to review homeschooling setups to ensure compliance with educational standards, and may intervene if the curriculum is found lacking.

> "If your curriculum is found lacking, the state may intervene, which could include requiring you to make changes to your program."
> - Source: [Student Discipline Defense - Homeschooling Defense in Hawaii](https://www.studentdisciplinedefense.com/homeschooling-defense-in-hawaii)

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Notice of Intent to local public school principal |
| **Testing** | Annual assessment (test, evaluation, or portfolio) |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | None statutorily mandated; structured curriculum required |
| **Instructional Time** | None specified |
| **Record-Keeping** | Recommended but not statutorily detailed |
| **Termination** | Must notify principal; re-enroll within 5 school days |
| **State Oversight** | State may review and intervene if curriculum lacking |

### Verdict

Hawaii is a **low-to-moderate regulation** state. The notice of intent and annual assessment requirements place it above the most deregulated states, but the absence of teacher qualifications, specific subject mandates, and hour requirements preserves substantial parental autonomy. The "structured curriculum" standard is vague enough to allow flexibility while providing the state with intervention authority in egregious cases. The principal-based notification system (rather than a state agency) is administratively lightweight.
