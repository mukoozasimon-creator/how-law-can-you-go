## The Short Answer

Minnesota is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you can do so safely (Minn. Stat. 609.06). Minnesota's **Castle Doctrine** (Minn. Stat. 609.065) removes the duty to retreat inside your home or occupied vehicle and presumes reasonable fear of death or great bodily harm when someone unlawfully or forcibly enters. Deadly force is only justified to prevent imminent death, great bodily harm, or a forcible felony. Note: HF3130 (introduced April 2025) would establish a Stand Your Ground Act, but it has not passed as of August 2026.

## Key Statutes

### Minn. Stat. 609.06 - Authorized Use of Force

> "(1) the absence of aggression or provocation on the part of the defendant; (2) the defendant's actual and honest belief that he or she was in imminent danger of bodily harm; (3) the existence of reasonable grounds for that belief; and (4) the absence of a reasonable possibility of retreat to avoid the danger."

**Live link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609.06
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Four-part test for self-defense outside the home. Duty to retreat if safe retreat is possible.

### Minn. Stat. 609.065 - Justifiable Taking of Life

> "The intentional taking of the life of another is not authorized by section 609.06, except when necessary in resisting or preventing an offense which the actor reasonably believes exposes the actor or another to great bodily harm or death, or preventing the commission of a felony in the actor's place of abode."

**Live link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609.065
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies in place of abode (dwelling). No duty to retreat inside home.

### Minnesota Case Law - Castle Doctrine

> "Under the so-called Castle Doctrine, a person need not retreat from his home before acting in self-defense. The home is a place critical for the protection of the family. Requiring retreat from the home before acting in self-defense would require one to leave one's safest place." (State v. Johnson, 719 N.W.2d 619 (Minn. 2006))

**Live link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat inside the home. Castle Doctrine is well-established in Minnesota case law.

### State v. Valdez (Minn. 2024) - Defense of Another

> "The right to defend another is logically incompatible with imposing a duty to retreat on the defendant."

**Live link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat when acting in defense of another, even outside the home.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/great bodily harm |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, under Castle Doctrine |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | No | No duty to retreat when defending others |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** Minnesota explicitly requires retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. This is a key distinction from stand-your-ground states.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to home/vehicle:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception applies inside your dwelling and occupied vehicle. It does not extend to your yard, driveway, or porch.
- **Defense of another exception:** The Minnesota Supreme Court held in State v. Valdez (2024) that imposing a duty to retreat when defending another would effectively negate the right to defend another.
- **Pending legislation:** HF3130 (introduced April 2025) would establish a Minnesota Stand Your Ground Act, eliminating the duty to retreat and creating presumptions similar to other SYG states. As of August 2026, it has not passed.
- **Four-part test:** Self-defense outside the home requires: (1) no aggression/provocation, (2) actual honest belief of imminent danger, (3) reasonable grounds for that belief, and (4) no safe possibility of retreat.
- **Recording:** Minnesota is a one-party consent state for recordings (Minn. Stat. 626A.02). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Wisconsin:** WI has no formal SYG statute but no statutory duty to retreat; MN explicitly requires retreat.
- **vs. Iowa:** IA enacted stand-your-ground in 2017; MN has not.
- **vs. North Dakota:** ND has no formal SYG statute but no duty to retreat by case law; MN explicitly requires retreat.
