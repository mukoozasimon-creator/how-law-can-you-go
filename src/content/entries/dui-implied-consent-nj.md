<div class="quip">New Jersey: where the Turnpike never ends and your license is gone for a year if you refuse the Alcotest.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in New Jersey</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to breath testing.</strong> N.J.S.A. 39:4-50.2. By driving in New Jersey, you are deemed to have consented to breath testing if lawfully arrested for DWI. Blood and urine require separate consent under 39:4-50.2a.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 7 months to 1-year suspension.</strong> N.J.S.A. 39:4-50.4a. The length depends on your prior record. Second refusal = 2 years. Third refusal = 10 years. All carry fines of $300-$2,000.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must read the Standard Statement.</strong> <em>State v. Marquez</em> (2010). The officer must read the Attorney General's Standard Statement in full and give you two clear opportunities to provide the sample. If you cannot understand the language, the refusal conviction cannot stand.</li>
<li><strong>You are entitled to an independent test.</strong> N.J.S.A. 39:4-50.2(c). After submitting to the state's test, you can have an independent test by a person or physician of your own selection at your own expense.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> New Jersey is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test. For standard DWI, the officer will request breath. Blood/urine require separate consent under 39:4-50.2a.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't read me the Standard Statement."</strong> If the officer fails to read the Standard Statement or does not give two clear opportunities, the refusal may be invalid.</li>
<li><strong>"I wasn't driving, I was sleeping in the car."</strong> New Jersey uses 'operating' standard. If you have the keys and the ability to operate the vehicle, you can be arrested for DWI.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic suspension AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You have no right to counsel before the implied consent decision.</li>
<li>"The suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a conditional license right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you must serve a portion of the suspension before becoming eligible for a conditional license.</li>
</ul>
</div>
