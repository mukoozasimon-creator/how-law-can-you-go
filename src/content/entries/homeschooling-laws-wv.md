# Homeschooling Laws - West Virginia

## Statutory Framework

West Virginia provides a dedicated **Home Instruction Option** under **WV Code §18-8-1(c)**, alongside other exemption paths including private schools and learning pods/microschools (added in recent amendments).

### Primary Instrument: WV Code §18-8-1(c)(2)

> "The child meets the requirements set forth in this subdivision: Provided, That the county superintendent may, after a showing of probable cause, seek from the circuit court of the county an order denying home instruction of the child. The order may be granted upon a showing of clear and convincing evidence that the child will suffer neglect in his or her education or that there are other compelling reasons to deny home instruction."
> - Source: [FindLaw - WV Code Chapter 18. Education § 18-8-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/wv/chapter-18-education/wv-code-sect-18-8-1/) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Notice of Intent: §18-8-1(c)(2)(A)

> "Upon commencing home instruction under this section the parent of a child receiving home instruction shall present to the county superintendent or county board a notice of intent to provide home instruction that includes the name, address, and age of any child of compulsory school age to be instructed and assurance that the child shall receive instruction in reading, language, mathematics, science, and social studies, and that the child shall be assessed annually in accordance with this subdivision."
> - Source: [FindLaw - WV Code §18-8-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/wv/chapter-18-education/wv-code-sect-18-8-1/)

**Key details:**
- The notice is effectively **one-time** per child, but the parent must notify the county superintendent upon **termination** of home instruction
- Upon establishing residence in a **new county**, the parent must notify the previous county superintendent and submit a new notice to the new county
- If the child is enrolled in public school, notice must be given **on or before the date home instruction is to begin**

### Teacher Qualifications: §18-8-1(c)(2)(B)

> "The person or persons providing home instruction shall submit satisfactory evidence of a high school diploma or equivalent, or a post-secondary degree or certificate from a regionally accredited institution, or from an institution of higher education that has been authorized to confer a post-secondary degree or certificate in West Virginia by the West Virginia Council for Community and Technical College Education or by the West Virginia Higher Education Policy Commission."
> - Source: [FindLaw - WV Code §18-8-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/wv/chapter-18-education/wv-code-sect-18-8-1/)

### Required Subjects

The notice must assure instruction in:
- Reading
- Language
- Mathematics
- Science
- Social studies

### Annual Assessment: §18-8-1(c)(2)(C)

The parent must obtain an academic assessment of the child for the previous school year, choosing **one** of four options:

1. **Nationally normed standardized achievement test** - published or normed not more than 10 years from administration date; administered under published guidelines by a qualified person; in reading, language, mathematics, science, and social studies. "Acceptable progress" = mean within or above the **fourth stanine**, or if below, showing improvement from previous year.

2. **Participation in the state's testing program**

3. **Written narrative** - portfolio of samples reviewed by a certified teacher, indicating academic progress "in accordance with the child's abilities"

4. **Alternative assessment** - mutually agreed upon by parent and county superintendent

> "The person or persons providing the instruction shall annually obtain an academic assessment of the child for the previous school year. This can be satisfied by 1) a nationally normed standardized achievement test; 2) participating in the state's testing program; 3) a written narrative indicating that a portfolio of samples of the child's work has been reviewed by a certified teacher; or 4) evidence of an alternative assessment of the child's proficiency mutually agreed upon by the parent and the superintendent."
> - Source: [U.S. Department of Education - West Virginia State Regulation](http://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools/west-virginia-state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools) (Authority: S)

### Assessment Submission and Remedial Requirements: §18-8-1(c)(2)(D)-(E)

- Parents must **maintain copies** of each assessment for **three years**
- Results must be submitted to the county superintendent at grade levels **3, 5, 8, and 11** by **June 30**
- If assessment fails to show acceptable progress, parent must initiate a **remedial program**
- If no acceptable progress for a **second consecutive year**, parent must submit additional evidence that appropriate instruction is being provided
- The county board must notify parents of available services to assist in assessing the child's eligibility for **special education services**
- "Identification of a disability does not preclude the continuation of home schooling."

### Public School Access: §18-8-1(c)(2)(E)(3)

> "Any child receiving home instruction may upon approval of the county board exercise the option to attend any class offered by the county board as the person or persons providing home instruction may consider appropriate subject to normal registration and attendance requirements."
> - Source: [U.S. Department of Education - West Virginia State Regulation](http://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools/west-virginia-state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools) (Authority: S)

**Sports note:** A homeschooled student prohibited from participating in interscholastic athletics does not violate equal protection under Article III, §10 of the West Virginia Constitution. However, many districts allow participation under local policy or through the Hope Scholarship program.

### Hope Scholarship

West Virginia's **Hope Scholarship** program provides ESA funding. For recipients choosing an individualized instructional program, annual test results or progress determinations must be submitted.

### Learning Pods / Microschools: §18-8-1(n)

Recent amendments added a learning pod/microschool exemption, which operates with similar assessment requirements to homeschooling.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 17 (or until high school diploma).

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | One-time notice of intent (notify upon termination or moving) |
| **Testing** | Annual assessment (4 options); submit at grades 3, 5, 8, 11 |
| **Qualifications** | High school diploma or equivalent (or post-secondary degree) |
| **Required Subjects** | 5 subjects (reading, language, math, science, social studies) |
| **Progress Standard** | 4th stanine or improvement |
| **Remedial Trigger** | Required if no acceptable progress; additional evidence if 2nd consecutive year |
| **Record-Keeping** | Maintain assessments for 3 years |
| **Public School Classes** | Statutory right with county board approval |

### Verdict

West Virginia is a **moderate-regulation** state. The dedicated homeschool statute provides clear structure, but the annual assessment requirement (with the 4th stanine benchmark), grade-level submission mandates, and remedial program triggers create a meaningful compliance burden. The four assessment options provide flexibility, and the statutory right to public school classes is a notable benefit. The Hope Scholarship adds a funding dimension for eligible families.
