# Marijuana Laws - Kentucky

## Verdict

Kentucky legalized medical cannabis via SB 47 in 2023, with the program becoming operational in 2025. Recreational marijuana remains illegal. Home cultivation is not permitted. Smoking is prohibited as a method of administration. THC potency caps apply: 35% for raw cannabis, 70% for concentrates, and 10 mg per serving for edibles. Governor Beshear expanded qualifying conditions by executive order in 2026.

## Named Instruments

- **KRS Chapter 218B** - Medical Cannabis Program (SB 47, 2023)
- **KRS 218B.010(26)** - Definition of qualifying medical conditions
- **KRS 218A.1422** - Possession of marijuana (criminal penalties)
- **Governor's Executive Order (2026)** - Expansion of qualifying conditions

## Verbatim Quotes

> "Kentucky's medical cannabis law limits certification for a medical cannabis card to a relatively narrow set of enumerated 'qualifying medical conditions,' including cancer, chronic pain, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, muscle spasms, spasticity, chronic nausea, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)."
> - KRS 218B.010(26)

> "The only limitation on the methods of administration is that smoking is not allowed and cannabis for vaporization can only be sold to patients who are 21 or under. Raw cannabis is allowed."
> - KRS 218B.170

> "There is a 35% THC cap for raw cannabis, edibles cannot exceed 10 mg of THC per serving, and concentrates are capped at 70% THC."
> - KRS 218B.140

> "The Governor's executive order explains that some of the statutorily enumerated conditions - such as chronic nausea, muscle spasms, and chronic pain - can themselves be symptoms of other serious medical conditions."
> - Governor's Executive Order, 2026

## Possession Limits (Medical)

| Product Type | Limit |
|--------------|-------|
| Raw cannabis | 30-day supply as determined by physician |
| THC potency (raw) | 35% maximum |
| THC potency (concentrates) | 70% maximum |
| Edibles | 10 mg THC per serving maximum |
| Home possession | 30-day supply (no home grow) |

## Penalty Tiers (Non-Medical Possession)

| Amount | Classification | Penalty |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| <8 oz (first offense) | Class B misdemeanor | Up to 45 days jail; up to $250 fine |
| <8 oz (subsequent) | Class A misdemeanor | Up to 12 months jail; up to $500 fine |
| 8 oz - 5 lbs | Class D felony | 1-5 years prison |
| >5 lbs | Class C felony | 5-10 years prison |

## Medical Program

- **Status:** Operational since 2025
- **Enabling law:** SB 47 (2023), codified as KRS Chapter 218B
- **Regulator:** Kentucky Cabinet for Health and Family Services
- **Qualifying conditions (original):** Cancer, chronic pain, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, muscle spasms, spasticity, chronic nausea, PTSD
- **Expanded conditions (2026 EO):** Terminal illness, sickle cell anemia, ALS, Parkinson's disease, HIV/AIDS, Huntington's disease, muscular dystrophy, cachexia, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, neuropathies, severe arthritis, fibromyalgia, glaucoma
- **Administration methods:** NO smoking permitted. Raw cannabis allowed (for vaporization). Edibles, tinctures, topicals, capsules permitted.
- **Home grow:** Not allowed
- **Caregivers:** Permitted; must be 21+ (or parent/guardian); max 3 patients per caregiver
- **Local opt-out:** Local governments may opt out of cannabis businesses; citizens may petition for ballot measure to opt back in

## Home Cultivation

- **Status:** Illegal for all purposes.
- Neither patients nor caregivers may cultivate cannabis at home.

## DUI / Impairment

- Kentucky prohibits operating a motor vehicle under the influence of cannabis.
- No per se THC limit is established.
- Medical cannabis card is not a defense to DUI.
- Cannabis must be kept in original container and not within driver's reach unless it requires at least a two-step process to open.

## Employment Protections

- Employers may retain drug-free workplace policies.
- No statutory employment protections for medical cannabis patients.
- Health insurance, workers compensation, and governmental medical assistance are not required to reimburse for medical cannabis costs.

## Federal Conflict

- Cannabis remains Schedule I under federal law.
- Interstate transport is prohibited.
- Federal property in Kentucky prohibits cannabis possession.
