## The Short Answer

New Jersey is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you know you can do so with complete safety (N.J.S.A. 2C:3-4(b)(2)(b)). New Jersey's **Castle Doctrine** removes the duty to retreat inside your own dwelling (N.J.S.A. 2C:3-4(b)(2)(b)(i)). Deadly force is only justified when you reasonably believe it is immediately necessary to prevent death or serious bodily injury.

## Key Statutes

### N.J.S.A. 2C:3-4 - Use of Force in Self-Protection

> "(a) Use of force justifiable for protection of the person. Subject to the provisions of this section and of section 2C:3-9, the use of force upon or toward another person is justifiable when the actor reasonably believes that such force is immediately necessary for the purpose of protecting himself against the use of unlawful force by such other person on the present occasion."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2c/section-2c-3-4/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified when reasonably believed immediately necessary against unlawful force.

### N.J.S.A. 2C:3-4(b)(2)(b) - Duty to Retreat

> "The use of deadly force is not justifiable under this section unless the actor reasonably believes that such force is necessary to protect himself against death or serious bodily injury; nor is it justifiable if: (b) the actor knows that he can avoid the necessity of using such force with complete safety by retreating or by surrendering possession of a thing to a person asserting a claim of right thereto..."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2c/section-2c-3-4/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is possible.

### N.J.S.A. 2C:3-4(b)(2)(b)(i) - Dwelling Exception

> "The actor is not obliged to retreat from his dwelling, unless he was the initial aggressor..."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2c/section-2c-3-4/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat inside your own dwelling. Initial aggressor exception applies.

### N.J.S.A. 2C:3-6 - Use of Force for Protection of Other Property

> "A person in possession or control of a dwelling or a person who is licensed or privileged to be therein is justified in using force upon another person when he reasonably believes such force is necessary to prevent or terminate what he reasonably believes to be an unlawful entry into the dwelling."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-2c/section-2c-3-6/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified to prevent unlawful entry into dwelling. Deadly force only in limited circumstances.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/serious bodily injury |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Yes, only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same standard as self-defense |
| In your vehicle | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same duty to retreat applies |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** New Jersey explicitly requires retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is known to be available.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to dwelling:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception applies only inside your own dwelling. It does not extend to your vehicle or workplace.
- **Reasonable belief standard:** Both your belief in the threat and your belief that force is necessary must be objectively reasonable.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat. Deadly force only justified against imminent death or serious bodily injury.
- **Recording:** New Jersey is a one-party consent state for recordings (N.J.S.A. 2A:156A-4). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. NY limits Castle Doctrine to dwelling; NJ does the same.
- **vs. Pennsylvania:** PA is a stand-your-ground state; NJ is not.
- **vs. Delaware:** DE imposes a duty to retreat in public; NJ does the same.
