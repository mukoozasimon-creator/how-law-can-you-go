## The Short Answer

Oklahoma is a **stand-your-ground state** with three overlapping self-defense doctrines codified in a single statute: **Castle Doctrine**, **"Make My Day"**, and **Stand Your Ground** (21 O.S. 1289.25). You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not engaged in an unlawful activity and are in a place where you have a right to be. Oklahoma creates a presumption of reasonable fear when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling, residence, occupied vehicle, place of business, or place of worship. Oklahoma also provides **criminal and civil immunity** and limits law enforcement's ability to arrest someone claiming self-defense.

## Key Statutes

### 21 O.S. 1289.25(B) - Castle Doctrine / Make My Day Presumption

> "A person who is not engaged in an unlawful activity and who is attacked in any place where he or she has a right to be has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground and meet force with force, including deadly force, if he or she reasonably believes it is necessary to do so to prevent death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-21/section-21-1289-25/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be. Deadly force justified against imminent death, great bodily harm, or forcible felony.

### 21 O.S. 1289.25(A) - Presumption of Reasonable Fear

> "A person who uses defensive force that is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily harm is presumed to have held a reasonable fear of imminent peril of death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another if the person against whom the defensive force was used: (1) Was in the process of unlawfully and forcefully entering, or had unlawfully and forcibly entered, a dwelling, residence, occupied vehicle, place of business, or place of worship, or if that person had removed or was attempting to remove another against that person's will from the dwelling, residence, occupied vehicle, place of business, or place of worship; and (2) The person who uses defensive force knew or had reason to believe that an unlawful and forcible entry or unlawful and forcible act was occurring or had occurred."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-21/section-21-1289-25/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters dwelling, residence, vehicle, business, or place of worship.

### 21 O.S. 1289.25(F) - Immunity from Criminal Prosecution and Civil Action

> "A person who uses defensive force, as permitted pursuant to the provisions of subsections A, B, D and E of this section, is justified in using such defensive force and is immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use of such defensive force. As used in this subsection, the term 'criminal prosecution' includes charging or prosecuting the defendant."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-21/section-21-1289-25/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil action for justified use of force.

### 21 O.S. 1289.25(G) - Arrest Limitation

> "A law enforcement agency may use standard procedures for investigating the use of defensive force, but the law enforcement agency may not arrest the person for using defensive force unless it determines that there is probable cause that the defensive force that was used was unlawful."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-21/section-21-1289-25/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Police cannot arrest someone claiming self-defense without probable cause that the force was unlawful.

### 21 O.S. 1289.25(E) - Presumption of Intent

> "A person who unlawfully and by force enters or attempts to enter the dwelling, residence, occupied vehicle of another person, place of business or place of worship is presumed to be doing so with the intent to commit an unlawful act involving force or violence."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-21/section-21-1289-25/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption that unlawful forcible entry is with intent to commit violent crime.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your place of business | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your place of worship | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Three doctrines in one statute:** Oklahoma uniquely codifies Castle Doctrine, Make My Day, and Stand Your Ground in a single statute (21 O.S. 1289.25).
- **Castle Doctrine extends to place of worship:** Oklahoma is one of the few states that explicitly extends Castle Doctrine protections to places of worship.
- **Arrest limitation:** Like Kentucky, Oklahoma limits law enforcement's ability to arrest someone claiming self-defense. Police must have probable cause that the force was unlawful.
- **Domestic violence limitation:** In domestic abuse cases, the person invoking self-defense must first attempt to retreat before responding with deadly force if the aggressor is a lawful resident.
- **Civil and criminal immunity:** Oklahoma provides broad immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil action.
- **Recording:** Oklahoma is a one-party consent state for recordings (21 O.S. 1172). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections. OK extends Castle Doctrine to place of worship; TX does not.
- **vs. Kansas:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Midwest/South.
- **vs. Arkansas:** AR has no formal SYG statute; OK has explicit statutory SYG.
