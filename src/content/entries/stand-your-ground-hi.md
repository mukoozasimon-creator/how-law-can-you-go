## The Short Answer

Hawaii is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you know you can do so with complete safety (HRS 703-304(5)(b)). Hawaii's **Castle Doctrine** (703-304(5)(b)(i)) removes the duty to retreat inside your **dwelling** or **place of work** if you were not the initial aggressor. Deadly force is only justified when you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent death, serious bodily injury, kidnapping, rape, or forcible sodomy. Note: HB795 (2023) would have repealed the duty to retreat but did not pass.

## Key Statutes

### HRS 703-304 - Use of Force in Self-Protection

> "(5) The use of deadly force is not justifiable under this section if: (b) The actor knows that he can avoid the necessity of using such force with complete safety by retreating or by surrendering possession of a thing to a person asserting a claim of right thereto or by complying with a demand that he abstain from any action which he has no duty to take, except that: (i) The actor is not obliged to retreat from his dwelling or place of work, unless he was the initial aggressor or is assailed in his place of work by another person whose place of work the actor knows it to be..."

**Live link:** https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol14_Ch0701-0853/HRS0703/HRS_0703-0304/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is known to be available. No duty to retreat in dwelling or place of work.

### HRS 703-305 - Use of Force for the Protection of Other Persons

> "The use of force upon or toward the person of another is justifiable to protect a third person when: (1) Under the circumstances as the actor believes them to be, the person whom he seeks to protect would be justified in using such protective force; and (2) The actor believes that his intervention is necessary for the protection of the other person."

**Live link:** https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol14_Ch0701-0853/HRS0703/HRS_0703-0305/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Same standard as self-defense applies when defending others.

### HRS 703-304 Commentary - Model Penal Code Basis

> "This section substantially adopts the Model Penal Code rules on justification of the use of force in self-protection."

**Live link:** https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol14_Ch0701-0853/HRS0703/HRS_0703-0304/

**Verdict:** Hawaii's self-defense law is based on the Model Penal Code.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (not initial aggressor) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/serious bodily injury |
| In your place of work (not initial aggressor) | No | Yes, same standard |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** Hawaii explicitly requires retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. This is based on the Model Penal Code.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to workplace:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception applies to both your dwelling and your place of work.
- **Subjective knowledge standard:** The duty to retreat requires that you "know" you can retreat with complete safety.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Pending legislation:** HB795 (2023) would have repealed the duty to retreat but did not pass as of August 2026.
- **Recording:** Hawaii is a one-party consent state for recordings (HRS 803-42). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** CA has no formal SYG statute but case law confirms no duty to retreat; HI explicitly requires retreat.
- **vs. New York:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. Both extend Castle Doctrine to dwelling and workplace.
- **vs. Connecticut:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. Both extend Castle Doctrine to dwelling and workplace.
