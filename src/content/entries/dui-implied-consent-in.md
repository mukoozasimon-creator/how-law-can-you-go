<div class="quip">Indiana: where the racing is Indy and your license is gone for a year if you refuse the chemical test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Indiana</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> I.C. 9-30-6-1. By driving in Indiana, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, or urine testing if lawfully arrested for OWI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 1-year suspension. Second = 2 years.</strong> I.C. 9-30-6-3. Administrative suspension by BMV, separate from criminal case.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must inform you of the consequences.</strong> I.C. 9-30-6-3. Before a refusal is valid, the officer must inform you that refusal will result in a 1-year suspension.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal is admissible in court.</strong> I.C. 9-30-6-4. The prosecutor can use your refusal as evidence of consciousness of guilt.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Indiana is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the implied consent suspension can be challenged. But the standard is low - the officer only needs probable cause to believe you violated I.C. 9-30-5.</li>
<li><strong>"The 3-hour rule means they can't test me after 3 hours."</strong> Wrong. The test must be offered within 3 hours to be admissible under the per se statute, but it can still be admissible under other theories.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic suspension AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You have no right to counsel before the implied consent decision.</li>
<li>"The suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a hardship license right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you must serve a portion of the suspension before becoming eligible for a hardship license.</li>
</ul>
</div>
