## The Short Answer

In Tennessee, your landlord must give you a **14-day written notice** for nonpayment of rent (T.C.A. § 66-28-505). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **30-day notice** to cure or vacate. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (§ 66-28-512). Tennessee has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) applies in counties with populations over 75,000; outside those counties, common law governs with weaker protections. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### T.C.A. § 66-28-512 - Termination of Month-to-Month Tenancy

### T.C.A. § 66-28-301 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist** if deposit exceeds one month's rent.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/112/pub/pc0654.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. Move-in checklist required for deposits over one month's rent.

## Important Notes

- **URLTA coverage:** Applies in counties with populations over 75,000 (Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Montgomery, Rutherford, Shelby, Sullivan, Williamson, Wilson). Outside these counties, common law applies with fewer protections.
- **No just-cause requirement:** Tennessee landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Tennessee preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Recording:** Tennessee is a one-party consent state for recordings (T.C.A. § 39-13-601). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are landlord-friendly Southern states, but TN has longer notice periods (14 days vs. NC's 10-day demand).
- **vs. Georgia:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but GA has longer termination notice (60 days vs. TN's 30 days).
- **vs. Washington:** TN is far more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no rent control.
