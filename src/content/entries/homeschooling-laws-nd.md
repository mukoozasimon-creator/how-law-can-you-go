# Homeschooling Laws - North Dakota

## Statutory Framework

North Dakota has one of the most detailed and structured homeschool statutes in the nation. Home education is governed by **NDCC Chapter 15.1-23**, which covers every aspect from notification to testing to remediation.

### Primary Instrument: NDCC 15.1-23-01

> "'Home education' means a program of education supervised by a child's parent in accordance with the requirements of this chapter. 'Parent' includes a child's legal guardian. 'Supervise' means the selection of materials, determination of an educational philosophy, and oversight of the method, manner, and delivery of instruction."
> - Source: [Justia - North Dakota Century Code Title 15.1, Chapter 15.1-23](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Statement of Intent: NDCC 15.1-23-02

> "At least five days before beginning home education or within fourteen days of establishing a child's residence in a school district, and once each year thereafter, a parent intending to supervise or supervising home education shall file a statement, reflecting that intent or fact, with the superintendent of the child's school district of residence."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-02](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

**Statement must include:**
- Name, address, date of birth, and grade level of the child
- Name, address, and **qualifications** of the parent who will supervise
- Any public school courses in which the child intends to participate
- Any extracurricular activities in which the child intends to participate

**Must be accompanied by:**
- Copy of the child's **immunization record**
- **Proof of identity** as required by NDCC 12-60-26

### Parental Qualifications: NDCC 15.1-23-03

> "A parent may supervise home education if the parent: 1. Holds a high school diploma or a general educational development diploma; or 2. Meets the requirements of section 15.1-23-06."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-03](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

**Key point:** A parent without a high school diploma or GED may still supervise home education but must be **monitored** for the first two years under NDCC 15.1-23-06.

### Required Subjects and Instructional Time: NDCC 15.1-23-04

> "A parent supervising home education shall include instruction in those subjects required by law to be taught to public school students. The instruction must have a duration of at least four hours each day for a minimum of one hundred seventy-five days each year."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-04](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

**Requirements:**
- **4 hours per day**
- **175 days per year**
- Subjects required by law for public school students

### Academic Records: NDCC 15.1-23-05

Parents must maintain an **annual record** of courses taken and academic progress assessments, including standardized achievement test results. If the child transfers to a public school, the parent must furnish the record upon request.

> "A parent supervising home education shall maintain an annual record of courses taken by the child and the child's academic progress assessments, including any standardized achievement test results."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-05](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

### Monitoring for Unqualified Parents: NDCC 15.1-23-06

A parent without a high school diploma or GED must be monitored for the **first two years**.

> "A parent who does not meet the qualifications provided in section 15.1-23-03 may supervise home education but must be monitored in accordance with section 15.1-23-07 for the first two years."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-06](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

**Monitoring extension:** If a child scores below the **50th percentile** nationally on a standardized test during monitoring, the parent must be monitored for at least **one additional school year** until the child scores at or above the 50th percentile.

### Monitoring Requirements: NDCC 15.1-23-07

- A licensed teacher monitors the child
- **Twice per school year**, the monitor reports progress to the superintendent
- **1 hour per week** in contact with the child and parent (for one child)
- **1/2 hour per month** for each additional child

> "Twice during each school year, the individual shall report the child's progress to the school district superintendent. If one child receives home education, the individual shall spend an average of one hour per week in contact with the child and the child's parent."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-07](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

### Standardized Testing: NDCC 15.1-23-09

**Grades 4, 6, 8, and 10:** Each child must take a standardized achievement test.

> "While in grades four, six, eight, and ten, each child receiving home education shall take: (1) A standardized achievement test used by the school district in which the child resides; or (2) A nationally normed standardized achievement test if requested by the child's parent."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-09](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

**Test administration:** Must be administered by an individual licensed to teach by the education standards and practices board.

**Exemption:** Parents may be exempted from testing if they:
- Have a philosophical, moral, or religious objection to standardized tests, OR
- Are licensed to teach, OR
- Hold a baccalaureate degree, OR
- Have met the cutoff score of a national teacher examination

### Test Results and Remediation: NDCC 15.1-23-11

Parents must file test results with the superintendent.

**30th percentile trigger:** If the child's basic composite score is less than the **30th percentile** nationally, a multidisciplinary assessment team shall assess the child for a potential learning problem.

**Remediation plan:** If the child is not disabled and the parent wishes to continue home education, the parent (with advice from a licensed teacher) must prepare a **remediation plan** and file it with the superintendent. Failure to file a remediation plan means the parent is deemed in violation of compulsory attendance and may no longer supervise home education.

> "If the multidisciplinary assessment team determines that the child is not disabled and the child's parent wishes to continue home education, the parent, with the advice and consent of an individual who is licensed to teach ... shall prepare a remediation plan to address the child's academic deficiencies and file the plan with the superintendent."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-11](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

### Remediation Plan Continuation: NDCC 15.1-23-12

The remediation plan remains in effect until the child achieves a basic composite score at or above the **30th percentile** or demonstrates **one year of academic progress** compared to the previous year's test score.

### Special Education: NDCC 15.1-23-13

If a multidisciplinary team determines the child is disabled and requires specially designed instruction, the parent may continue home education if they file a **services plan** demonstrating that special needs are being addressed by qualified persons.

### Developmental Disability Provisions: NDCC 15.1-23-14 through 15.1-23-15

Special rules apply for children with developmental disabilities, including:
- Diagnosis by a licensed psychologist
- Filing of a services plan
- Progress reports due November 1, February 1, and May 1 each year

### Extracurricular Activities: NDCC 15.1-23-16

Homeschooled students may participate in extracurricular activities:
- Under the auspices of the child's school district of residence, OR
- Under the auspices of an approved nonpublic school (if permitted)

They are subject to the same participation standards as full-time students.

> "A child receiving home education may participate in extracurricular activities either: Under the auspices of the child's school district of residence; or Under the auspices of an approved nonpublic school, if permitted by the administrator of the school."
> - Source: [Justia - NDCC 15.1-23-16](https://law.justia.com/codes/north-dakota/title-15-1/chapter-15-1-23/) (Authority: A)

### High School Diplomas: NDCC 15.1-23-17

A school district, approved nonpublic high school, or the Center for Distance Education may issue a high school diploma to a homeschooled student who has met graduation requirements. The parent must submit:
- Description of course material covered in each subject
- Description of course objectives and how they were met
- Transcript of performance in grades 9-12

Alternatively, the student may complete at least **22 units** of high school coursework from the minimum required curriculum.

### Liability Protection: NDCC 15.1-23-18

No state agency, school district, or county superintendent may be held liable for accepting as correct the information on the statement of intent or for any damages resulting from a parent's failure to educate the child.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 7 through 16.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual statement of intent (5 days before start) |
| **Testing** | Grades 4, 6, 8, 10 (exemption available for qualified parents) |
| **Qualifications** | HS diploma/GED required (monitoring for 2 years if not) |
| **Required Subjects** | Same as public schools |
| **Instructional Time** | 4 hours/day, 175 days/year |
| **Record-Keeping** | Annual records required; must furnish on transfer |
| **30th Percentile** | Trigger for multidisciplinary assessment and remediation plan |
| **Remediation** | Mandatory if below 30th percentile; failure = loss of homeschool right |
| **Monitoring** | Licensed teacher, 1 hr/week, twice-yearly reports (unqualified parents) |
| **Diplomas** | District or Center for Distance Education may issue |
| **Sports Access** | Statutory right |
| **Liability** | State/district shielded from liability for parent failure |

### Verdict

North Dakota is a **high-regulation** homeschool state with the most detailed statutory framework in the nation. The annual notification, 4-hour/175-day instructional mandate, grade-level testing (4, 6, 8, 10), 30th percentile remediation trigger, and 2-year monitoring for unqualified parents create a substantial compliance burden. However, the statute provides clear procedures, exemption pathways for qualified parents, diploma issuance authority, and strong liability protections for the state. For families meeting the qualification and progress standards, the framework is predictable; for those who struggle academically, the remediation and monitoring provisions are among the most stringent in the U.S.
