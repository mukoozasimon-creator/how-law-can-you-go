# DUI / Implied Consent - South Carolina

## Verdict

South Carolina's implied consent law (SC Code § 56-5-2950 and § 56-5-2951) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if lawfully arrested for DUI. A first refusal results in a **6-month license suspension** (or ignition interlock device for 6 months). Refusal is admissible as evidence of consciousness of guilt in criminal proceedings. As of early 2026, legislation is pending that would increase refusal penalties to **1-4 years** depending on prior convictions, but current law remains 6-15 months. Drivers have **30 days** to request an administrative hearing with the Office of Motor Vehicle Hearings (OMVH) to challenge the suspension.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Videotape Requirement**: South Carolina uniquely requires that the police officer videotape the reading of implied consent rights and any refusal. The officer must explain rights and penalties on video.

2. **Temporary Alcohol License (TAL)**: Drivers who request an administrative hearing within 30 days may receive a TAL, allowing them to drive while awaiting the hearing outcome. Cost: $100.

3. **Route Restricted License**: If suspension is upheld, drivers are eligible (once per lifetime) for a route-restricted license to drive to work, school, and ADSAP. Cost: $100.

4. **ADSAP Requirement**: All refusal cases require enrollment in the Alcohol and Drug Safety Action Program (ADSAP), costing $500-$2,500.

5. **Pending 2026 Enhancement**: Legislation passed the Senate in February 2026 that would increase refusal suspensions from 6-15 months to 1-4 years depending on prior convictions. Status: Pending House action as of August 2026.

6. **Refusal as Evidence**: Prosecutors may argue refusal demonstrates consciousness of guilt. The refusal itself is admissible in criminal proceedings.

7. **Almost 50% Refusal Rate**: According to 2026 legislative testimony, nearly half of all DUI arrests in South Carolina result in refusal, prompting the penalty enhancement push.

## Practical Notes

- **DataMaster Breath Test**: South Carolina uses the DataMaster breath testing device. Results from this device are commonly challenged on calibration and maintenance grounds.
- **30-Day Deadline is Strict**: The 30-day window to request an OMVH hearing is jurisdictional. Missing it waives the right to challenge the suspension.
- **Two Separate Tracks**: Like most states, South Carolina has separate administrative (license) and criminal (DUI) proceedings. A driver can win the criminal case but still lose their license administratively.
- **BAC .15+ Trigger**: Even if a driver submits to testing, a BAC of .15 or higher triggers a 1-month minimum suspension and IID requirement, separate from refusal penalties.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face suspension at .02 BAC or higher.
