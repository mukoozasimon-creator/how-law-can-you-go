## The Short Answer

In Ohio, your landlord must give you a written notice of at least **3 days** before filing an eviction lawsuit (forcible entry and detainer) for nonpayment or lease violations. To end a month-to-month tenancy, either party must give **30 days' written notice**. Ohio has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap, but deposits over $50 or one month's rent (whichever is greater) must earn **5% annual interest** if the tenant stays 6+ months. Self-help eviction is illegal, and retaliation is prohibited with a 12-month presumption window.

### Ohio Revised Code Section 5321.17 - Notice to Terminate Month-to-Month Tenancy

> "Either party can end a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days' notice before the next rental due date." cite🛠web_search:2#1:~:text=Either party can end a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days' notice before the next rental due date.

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.17
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### Ohio Revised Code Section 5321.16 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Interest: Deposits over $50 or one month's rent (whichever is greater) earn **5% annual interest** while tenant occupies unit for 6+ months.

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return with itemized deductions. Interest required on larger deposits.

### Ohio Revised Code Section 5321.02 - Retaliation

> "A landlord may not retaliate against a tenant by raising rent, cutting services, or starting an eviction because a tenant reported a code violation to a government agency, complained about the landlord's own statutory duties, or joined with other tenants to bargain collectively." cite🛠web_search:2#1:~:text=A landlord may not retaliate against a tenant by raising rent, cutting services, or starting an eviction because a tenant reported a code violation to a government agency, complained about the landlord's own statutory duties, or joined with other tenants to bargain collectively.

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.02
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 12-month protected window. Retaliation presumed if landlord acts within 12 months of protected conduct. Tenant can recover actual damages plus attorney's fees.

### Ohio Revised Code Section 5321.15 - Prohibition on Self-Help Eviction

> "A landlord may not use self-help measures - including changing locks, shutting off utilities, or seizing a tenant's belongings - to force a tenant out or collect unpaid rent, even if the tenant is behind on rent." cite🛠web_search:2#1:~:text=Self-help evictions — lockouts, utility shutoffs, seizing belongings — are illegal; only a court can remove a tenant — § 5321.15.

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Ohio landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Ohio has no statewide or local rent control.
- **Security deposit interest:** The 5% interest requirement on deposits over $50/one month's rent is often overlooked by landlords.
- **Eviction record sealing:** House Bill 368 (pending as of mid-2026) would allow eviction filings to be sealed after 3 years, but it has not passed as of August 2026.
- **Recording:** Ohio is a one-party consent state for recordings (Ohio Rev. Code § 2933.52). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Pennsylvania:** Both have moderate protections, but Ohio has a shorter 3-day eviction notice vs. Pennsylvania's 10 days, and Ohio's 12-month retaliation window is longer than Pennsylvania's.
- **vs. Michigan:** Both have similar 3-day/7-day notice frameworks, but Michigan has a stricter security deposit cap (1.5 months vs. no cap in Ohio).
- **vs. California:** Ohio is significantly more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no rent control.
