## Summary

Missouri is one of the most homeschool-friendly states in America. Under RSMo § 167.031, parents are not required to notify anyone, submit to testing, or seek approval to homeschool. The requirements are minimal: provide 1,000 hours of instruction per year (with at least 600 hours in core subjects), teach 5 required subjects, and maintain basic records at home. Homeschools in Missouri operate under their own separate statutory framework and are not classified as private schools. Records are private and never submitted to authorities unless a formal truancy investigation occurs.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**RSMo § 167.031** - Compulsory attendance, home school, definition

> "1. (1) Every parent, guardian, or other person in this state having charge, control, or custody of a child is responsible for enrolling the child in a program of academic instruction in a public, private, parochial, parish school, home school, FPE school, or full time equivalent attendance in a combination of such schools between the ages of seven years and the compulsory attendance age for the district."

- **Instrument**: RSMo § 167.031
- **Live Link**: https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=167.031
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Homeschooling is a legal option with minimal regulatory requirements.

### Home School Definition

**RSMo § 167.012** - Definition of "home school"

> "For purposes of state law... a 'home school' is a school, whether incorporated or unincorporated, that: (1) Has as its primary purpose the provision of private or religious-based instruction; (2) Enrolls children between the ages of seven years and the compulsory attendance age... of which no more than four are unrelated by affinity or consanguinity in the third degree; (3) Does not charge or receive consideration in the form of tuition, fees, or other remuneration... (4) Does not enroll children who participate in the [MOScholars] program... (5) Is not an FPE school."

- **Instrument**: RSMo § 167.012
- **Live Link**: https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=167.012
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026
- **Verdict**: Defines homeschools as distinct from private schools with enrollment and compensation restrictions.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Notification** | None required |
| **Teacher Qualifications** | None required |
| **Required Subjects** | 5 core subjects (see below) |
| **Testing** | None required |
| **Record-Keeping** | Plan book/diary/log, portfolio, evaluation record (kept at home) |
| **Attendance** | 1,000 hours/year (600 in core subjects; 400 of 600 at home location) |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 7-17 |

## Notification

No notification is required. If a child is currently enrolled in public school, a withdrawal letter is strongly recommended (but not legally required) to prevent truancy inquiries.

## Required Subjects (5 Core)

Under RSMo § 167.031, at least 600 of the 1,000 hours must be in the "basics":

1. Reading
2. Language Arts
3. Mathematics
4. Social Studies
5. Science

At least 400 of the 600 core-subject hours must be taught in the home location.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required.

## Record-Keeping

Parents must maintain the following records (kept at home, never submitted):

1. **Plan book, diary, daily log, or other written record** indicating subjects taught and activities engaged in
2. **Portfolio** containing samples of the student's academic work
3. **Record of evaluation** of the student's academic progress

**Important**: The production of a daily log showing compliance is a defense to any truancy prosecution or educational neglect charge (RSMo § 167.012(4)). Records are subject to review **only by the local prosecuting attorney** - not by school districts or social workers.

## Special Provisions

- **Age 16+ Exemption**: The 1,000-hour and recordkeeping requirements do not apply to pupils 16 years of age or older
- **Combination Attendance**: Students may attend a combination of public/private school and homeschool to satisfy compulsory attendance
- **MOScholars**: Missouri's tax-credit scholarship program; homeschoolers are explicitly excluded from participating (RSMo § 167.012(2)(4))
- **FPE Schools**: "Family Paced Education" schools are a separate category with different requirements
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Truancy Defense**: A daily log showing compliance is an affirmative defense to truancy charges

## Distinctive Features

- **No notification at all** - among the most deregulated states
- 1,000-hour requirement with 600-hour core subject minimum
- Records are **private** - kept at home, reviewed only by prosecuting attorney
- Homeschools are **not classified as private schools** - separate statutory category
- No more than 4 unrelated students per homeschool
- Cannot charge tuition or fees
- Strong legal protections against home inspections and record demands
