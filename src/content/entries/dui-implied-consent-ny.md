<div class="quip">New York: where the bagels are fresh, the subway is delayed, and your license is revoked for a year if you refuse the breath test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in New York</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> N.Y. Veh. & Traf. Law 1194(2)(a). By driving in New York, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, urine, or saliva testing if lawfully arrested for DWI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal = revocation, not just suspension.</strong> N.Y. Veh. & Traf. Law 1194(2)(b). First refusal = 1-year revocation. Second refusal within 5 years = 18-month revocation. This is harsher than most states.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must warn you of the consequences.</strong> N.Y. Veh. & Traf. Law 1194(2)(c). Before a refusal is valid, the officer must inform you that refusal will result in revocation. Failure to give proper warning is a strong defense at the DMV refusal hearing.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal is admissible in court.</strong> N.Y. CPLR 4506. The prosecutor can use your refusal as evidence of consciousness of guilt.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> New York is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait. However, you DO have the right to call an attorney and select a witness to observe testing - but the test cannot be delayed more than 30 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the refusal can be challenged at a DMV refusal hearing. But the standard is low - the officer only needs reasonable grounds to believe you violated VTL 1192.</li>
<li><strong>"I have a conditional license, so the refusal doesn't matter."</strong> Wrong. A refusal while driving with a conditional license can result in permanent revocation of your driving privileges.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic revocation AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You have no right to counsel before the implied consent decision. You can call an attorney, but the test cannot be delayed more than 30 minutes.</li>
<li>"The revocation only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The revocation is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get a conditional license right away after refusal." Wrong. You must wait out the revocation period or win the refusal hearing. No hardship privilege for refusal cases.</li>
</ul>
</div>
