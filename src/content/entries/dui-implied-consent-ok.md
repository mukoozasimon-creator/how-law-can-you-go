# DUI / Implied Consent - Oklahoma

## Verdict

Oklahoma's implied consent law (47 O.S. § 753) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if lawfully arrested for DUI or DWI. The arresting officer must inform the subject of the implied consent requirements, including that they are under arrest, the type of test requested, and the penalty for refusing. A first refusal results in a **180-day license revocation** (or 6 months). A second refusal within 10 years triggers a **1-year revocation**, and a third refusal within 10 years triggers a **3-year revocation**. Oklahoma has separate administrative hearing proceedings for test failure (47 O.S. § 754) and refusal cases (47 O.S. § 753). Refusal is admissible in criminal prosecution. Unconscious drivers are deemed not to have withdrawn consent.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Separate Hearing Tracks for Failure vs. Refusal**: Oklahoma uniquely has two separate administrative hearing statutes: 47 O.S. § 754 for drivers who took and failed a test, and 47 O.S. § 753 for drivers who refused. The issues and procedures differ between the two.

2. **Unconscious = Presumed Consent**: Oklahoma explicitly states that any person who is unconscious or otherwise incapable of refusing is "deemed not to have withdrawn the consent," and testing may proceed without a warrant.

3. **Erin Swezey Act IID Requirements**: Under the Erin Swezey Act, refusal or BAC .15+ triggers mandatory ignition interlock for 18 months (1st offense) or 4 years (2nd offense) after the revocation period is completed.

4. **Officer Must Specify Test Type**: The arresting officer must inform the subject of the specific type of test or tests requested. The driver does not get to choose which test to take.

5. **Up to Two Samples**: Oklahoma permits officers to request up to two samples in any combination (blood, breath, or urine) as directed by law enforcement.

6. **Zero Tolerance for Under 21**: Drivers under 21 are subject to zero-tolerance rules - any measurable BAC (deemed .02 or higher) triggers suspension under the same timeframes.

7. **30-Day Effective Date**: The revocation or denial becomes effective 30 days after the arrested person is given written notice.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative Proceedings Are Civil**: Oklahoma's administrative license proceedings are civil matters, separate from the criminal DUI/DWI case. The burden of proof is lower than in criminal court.
- **Modified License with IID**: After serving the revocation period, drivers may be eligible for a modified license with an IID installed.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Non-residents who refuse face revocation of their Oklahoma operating privilege and possible reporting to their home state.
- **Field Sobriety Test Refusal**: Refusal to perform field sobriety tests is also admissible in criminal prosecution under 47 O.S. § 756(A).
- **Request Additional Test**: Drivers have the right to request an additional independent test at their own expense, but the officer's test proceeds regardless.
