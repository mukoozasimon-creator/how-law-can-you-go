## The Short Answer

West Virginia is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using deadly force in self-defense if you are not the initial aggressor and are in a place where you have a legal right to be (WV Code 55-7-22(c)). West Virginia's **Castle Doctrine** (55-7-22) creates a presumption that you reasonably believed deadly force was necessary when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling or occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, serious bodily injury, or the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### WV Code 55-7-22 - Civil Immunity for Justified Use of Force

> "(a) A lawful occupant within a home or other place of residence is justified in using reasonable and proportionate force, including deadly force, against an intruder or attacker to prevent a forcible entry into the home or residence or to terminate the intruder's or attacker's unlawful entry if the occupant reasonably apprehends that the intruder or attacker may kill or inflict serious bodily harm upon the occupant or another person lawfully in the home or residence. (b) A lawful occupant within a home or other place of residence does not have a duty to retreat from an intruder or attacker in the circumstances described in subsection (a) of this section. (c) A person not engaged in unlawful activity who is attacked in any place he or she has a legal right to be outside of his or her home or residence may use reasonable and proportionate force against an intruder or attacker: Provided, That such person may use deadly force against an intruder or attacker in such place only if he or she reasonably believes that he or another person is in imminent danger of death or serious bodily harm from which he or another can only be saved by the use of deadly force against the intruder or attacker."

**Live link:** https://code.wvlegislature.gov/55-7-22/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have a legal right to be. Castle Doctrine applies to dwelling and occupied vehicle.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable apprehension of death/serious bodily harm |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, same standard |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** West Virginia explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a legal right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine applies to vehicle:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception extends to your occupied vehicle.
- **Civil immunity:** West Virginia provides civil immunity for justified use of force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** West Virginia is a one-party consent state for recordings (WV Code 62-1D-3). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Virginia:** VA is a stand-your-ground state by case law; WV has explicit statutory SYG.
- **vs. Kentucky:** Both are stand-your-ground states in Appalachia.
- **vs. Ohio:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Ohio River Valley.
