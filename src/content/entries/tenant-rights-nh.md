## The Short Answer

In New Hampshire, your landlord must give you a **7-day written demand for rent**, followed by a **30-day notice to quit** if rent is not paid (RSA 540:2, 540:3). For lease violations or "other good cause," a **30-day notice to quit** applies (RSA 540:3). For criminal conduct or significant damage, a **7-day notice to quit** may apply (RSA 540:2, II(d)). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (RSA 540:3). New Hampshire requires **statutory good cause** for most evictions. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### RSA 540:2, II(a) & 540:3 - 7-Day Demand for Nonpayment

> "A landlord may terminate for neglect or refusal to pay rent due and in arrears, upon demand. A 7-day notice period applies for the grounds listed in RSA 540:2, II(a), (b), (d), or (h)."

**Live link:** https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/nhtoc.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 7 days. Notice must state with specificity the reason for eviction.

### RSA 540:3 - 30-Day Notice to Quit

> "For residential tenants, 30 days' notice shall be sufficient in all cases of termination not otherwise specified."

**Live link:** https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/nhtoc.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day notice for lease violations, other good cause, and month-to-month termination.

### RSA 540:2, II(e) - "Other Good Cause"

> "Other good cause includes any legitimate business or economic reason and need not be based on the action or inaction of the tenant."

**Live link:** https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/nhtoc.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** New Hampshire requires statutory good cause for most evictions, but "other good cause" is broadly defined and includes economic reasons.

### RSA 540:2, II(i) - Non-Renewal of Long-Term Leases (Effective July 1, 2026)

> "For a lease of 12 months or longer, the landlord may terminate at expiration by giving at least 60 days' written notice that the lease will not be renewed, provided the possessory action is filed within 6 months of termination."

**Live link:** https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/nhtoc.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. Effective July 1, 2026.
**Verdict:** New ground for non-renewal of long-term leases with 60-day notice.

### RSA 540-B:3 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **1 month's rent or $100, whichever is greater**.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after tenant vacates.
- Must provide itemized statement if deductions made.

**Live link:** https://www.gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/nhtoc.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. 1-month cap (or $100 minimum).

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | Yes (good cause) |
| Nonpayment of rent | 7 days (demand) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 30 days to quit | Yes |
| Criminal conduct / significant damage | 7 days to quit | N/A |
| Non-renewal (12+ month lease) | 60 days written | Yes (new ground) |

## Important Notes

- **Good cause required:** New Hampshire is one of the few states requiring statutory good cause for most evictions, even from month-to-month tenancies. However, "other good cause" is broadly defined.
- **No rent control:** New Hampshire preempts local rent control.
- **Notice specificity:** The eviction notice must "state with specificity the reason for the eviction."
- **Service:** Notice may be served by any person, personally or left at last and usual place of abode.
- **Retaliation protection:** RSA 540-A:4 protects tenants from retaliation within 6 months of protected activity.
- **Recording:** New Hampshire is a two-party consent state for recordings (RSA 570-A:2). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Maine:** ME does not require good cause statewide (except Portland); NH requires good cause for most evictions.
- **vs. Vermont:** VT has longer no-cause notice periods (60-90 days) but does not require good cause statewide.
- **vs. Massachusetts:** MA has just-cause requirements in some cities; NH requires good cause statewide.
