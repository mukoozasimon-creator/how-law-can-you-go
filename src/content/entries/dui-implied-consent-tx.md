<div class="quip">Texas: where the stars at night are big and bright, and your license is gone for 180 days if you refuse to blow.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Texas</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> Tex. Transp. Code 724.011. By driving in Texas, you are deemed to have consented to a breath or blood test if lawfully arrested for DWI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal = 180-day suspension.</strong> Tex. Transp. Code 724.035. First refusal = 180 days. Second or subsequent refusal = 2 years. This is an administrative suspension by DPS, separate from any criminal case.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must read you the DIC-24 warning.</strong> Before a refusal is valid, the officer must read a statutory warning informing you of the consequences of refusal and testing over the legal limit.</li>
<li><strong>Breath test = no warrant needed. Blood = warrant required.</strong> <em>Birchfield v. North Dakota</em> (2016). Warrantless breath tests are permitted. Warrantless blood draws are not permitted without consent or exigent circumstances. Texas courts have held that the natural dissipation of alcohol does NOT create per se exigent circumstances (<em>Missouri v. McNeely</em>).</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Texas is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the implied consent suspension can be challenged at an ALR hearing. But you have only 15 days after arrest to request that hearing.</li>
<li><strong>"I was already at the hospital when they took blood."</strong> If you were unconscious or incapacitated, you are deemed not to have withdrawn consent under 724.014. The officer can take blood without a warrant in some circumstances.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic license suspension AND can be used as evidence of consciousness of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"They can't take blood without a warrant." Wrong. If you consent, or if there are exigent circumstances, or if you are unconscious, blood can be drawn without a warrant.</li>
<li>"The 180-day suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The ALR suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You have 30 days to request an ALR hearing." Wrong. You have 15 days. Miss it and the suspension is automatic.</li>
</ul>
</div>
