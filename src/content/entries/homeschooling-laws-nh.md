# Homeschooling Laws - New Hampshire

## Statutory Framework

New Hampshire homeschools operate under **RSA 193-A** (in effect since 1991, most recently amended June 2, 2022) and administrative rules **Ed 315**.

### Primary Instrument: RSA 193-A:4

> "Instruction shall be deemed home education if it consists of instruction in science, mathematics, language, government, history, health, reading, writing, spelling, the history of the constitutions of New Hampshire and the United States, and an exposure to and appreciation of art and music. Home education shall be provided, coordinated, or directed by a parent for his or her own child."
> - Source: [Justia - New Hampshire Revised Statutes Section 193-A:4](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-hampshire/title-xv/chapter-193-a/section-193-a-4/) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

**Key flexibility:** "You do NOT need to teach each of the subjects required in the law every year."

### Notification: RSA 193-A:5

Parents must send a **letter of notification** to a **participating agency**:
- Local district superintendent, OR
- Commissioner of Education at the NH Department of Education, OR
- Principal of a participating nonpublic school

> "When you start home educating, you must send in your letter of notification by the time school starts in your district and must notify within 5 days of withdrawing a child from school."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/) (Authority: NA)

The notification letter must contain:
- Children's names, addresses, birth dates
- Parent(s)' name(s)
- Date the home education program will begin
- Signature of the parent(s)

**As of May 2006, curriculum or scope and sequence information is no longer required.**

> "As of May 2006, it is no longer required to include curriculum or scope and sequence information. It is also not required to provide the child's grade level, language spoken at home, local district assigned school, or race. Also, districts cannot require families to use their forms."
> - Source: [Granite State Home Educators - Where to Begin](https://gshenh.org/where-to-begin/) (2016-10-15)

### Portfolio Requirement

Parents must keep a **portfolio** of the homeschooled child's work for at least **two years**.

> "Keep a portfolio of the homeschooled child's work (for at least two years)."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

### Annual Evaluation: RSA 193-A:6

Parents must complete an **annual evaluation** demonstrating educational progress commensurate with the child's age, ability, and/or disability.

**Evaluation options:**
1. A **nationally normed standardized achievement test** administered by a qualified person
2. An **evaluation by a certified teacher** (current NH certificate or neighboring state)
3. An **evaluation by a teacher currently teaching in a nonpublic school**
4. A **portfolio review** by a certified teacher

> "Complete an annual evaluation demonstrating educational progress commensurate with the child's age, ability, and/or disability."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

**Important:** Parents do **not** need to submit the evaluation for review unless specifically requested.

> "You do NOT need to submit your child's annual evaluation for review."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** No degree, diploma, or certification is required for the parent providing home instruction.

### Testing Requirements

Annual evaluation is required, but parents may choose from **four options** including portfolio review. The evaluation is not submitted unless requested.

### Instructional Time

**No minimum** days or hours are specified in statute.

> "You do NOT need to teach a certain number of days in a year or a certain number of hours in a day."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

### Required Subjects

- Science
- Mathematics
- Language
- Government
- History
- Health
- Reading
- Writing
- Spelling
- History of the constitutions of NH and the United States
- Exposure to and appreciation of art and music

Note: Not all subjects must be taught every year.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 (by September 30) through 18, or high school graduation.

> "In NH, children are required to follow compulsory attendance requirements by the age of six and until the age of eighteen or high school graduation."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

### Public School Access: RSA 193:1-c

New Hampshire homeschoolers have a statutory right to access public school programs.

> "Parents should be aware of their legal rights to access public school programs (193:1-c)."
> - Source: [NH Homeschooling Coalition - Legal Requirements](https://nhhomeschooling.org/legal-requirements/)

### Education Freedom Accounts (EFA)

New Hampshire operates the **Education Freedom Account** program, which provides ESA funding that homeschoolers can access. Families using an EFA are governed by a different legal statute than RSA 193-A.

### Self-Certification of High School Completion

Parents may self-certify secondary school completion for their homeschooled students.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | One-time letter to participating agency (update if moving) |
| **Testing** | Annual evaluation (4 options); not submitted unless requested |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | 11 subjects (not all required every year) |
| **Instructional Time** | None specified |
| **Portfolio** | Must maintain for 2 years |
| **Public School Access** | Statutory right (RSA 193:1-c) |
| **Funding** | Education Freedom Accounts available |
| **Flexibility** | High - no days/hours, no curriculum submission |

### Verdict

New Hampshire is a **low-to-moderate regulation** state with significant flexibility. The one-time notification, absence of instructional time requirements, and four evaluation options (with no mandatory submission) create a parent-friendly framework. The 2-year portfolio requirement and 11-subject list are the primary compliance obligations. The 2022 amendments and EFA program continue to expand homeschool options.
