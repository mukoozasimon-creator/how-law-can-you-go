## The Short Answer

Tennessee is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not engaged in unlawful activity and are in a place where you have a legal right to be (TCA 39-11-611). Tennessee's **Castle Doctrine** extends to your home, business, and vehicle, allowing you to use force - including deadly force - if you reasonably believe you or another are in imminent danger of death or serious bodily injury. You may also use deadly force to prevent certain enumerated felonies.

## Key Statutes

### TCA 39-11-611 - Self-Defense

> "(b)(1) A person who is not engaged in unlawful activity and is in a place where the person has a right to be has no duty to retreat before threatening or using force against another person when and to the degree the person reasonably believes the force is immediately necessary to protect against the other's use or attempted use of unlawful force. (2) A person who is not engaged in unlawful activity and is in a place where the person has a right to be has no duty to retreat before threatening or using force intended or likely to cause death or serious bodily injury, if: (A) The person has a reasonable belief that there is an imminent danger of death or serious bodily injury; (B) The danger creating the belief of imminent death or serious bodily injury is real, or honestly believed to be real at the time; and (C) The belief of danger is founded upon reasonable grounds."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-39/chapter-11/part-2/section-39-11-611/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before force or deadly force if lawfully present and not engaged in unlawful activity.

### TCA 39-11-611(c) - Castle Doctrine

> "Any person using force intended or likely to cause death or serious bodily injury within a residence, business, dwelling or vehicle is presumed to have held a reasonable belief of imminent death or serious bodily injury to self, family, a member of the household or a person visiting as an invited guest, when the force is used against another person, who is not a member of the family or household, and who unlawfully and forcibly entered or has unlawfully and forcibly entered the residence, business, dwelling or vehicle."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-39/chapter-11/part-2/section-39-11-611/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters residence, business, dwelling, or vehicle.

### TCA 39-11-621 - Defense of Third Person

> "A person is justified in threatening or using force against another to protect a third person if: (1) The person reasonably believes the force is immediately necessary to protect the third person against the other's use or attempted use of unlawful force; and (2) Under the circumstances as the person reasonably believes them to be, the person whom he seeks to protect would be justified in using such protective force."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-39/chapter-11/part-2/section-39-11-621/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Same standard as self-defense applies when defending others.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your residence (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your business | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Tennessee explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force or deadly force if you are lawfully present and not engaged in unlawful activity.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to business and vehicle:** Unlike many states that limit Castle Doctrine to the home, Tennessee extends the presumption to your business and vehicle.
- **Civil immunity:** Tennessee provides civil immunity for justified use of force under TCA 39-11-622.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Tennessee is a one-party consent state for recordings (TCA 39-13-601). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are stand-your-ground states with Castle Doctrine protections in the Southeast.
- **vs. Georgia:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections.
- **vs. Kentucky:** KY is a stand-your-ground state; similar to TN.
