<div class="quip">Tennessee: where the music is country and your license is suspended for 18 months if you refuse the blood test (as of 2026).</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Tennessee</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> T.C.A. 55-10-406. By driving in Tennessee, you are deemed to have consented to breath, blood, or both if lawfully arrested for DUI. The officer chooses which test. You must sign a standardized waiver.</li>
<li><strong>NEW: First refusal = up to 18 months (as of Jan. 1, 2026).</strong> T.C.A. 55-10-406(d)(2). First refusal (blood test, no prior DUI within 10 years) = 18-month suspension. Second = 2 years. Third = 5 years. This is a major increase from the previous 12-month standard.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must advise you of consequences.</strong> T.C.A. 55-10-406(d)(2). Before a refusal is valid, the officer must advise you that refusal will result in suspension and possible ignition interlock requirement.</li>
<li><strong>Officer can get a warrant after refusal.</strong> T.C.A. 55-10-406(b)(2)(D). Even if you refuse, the officer can obtain a search warrant or claim exigent circumstances to draw blood.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Tennessee is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the implied consent suspension can be challenged. But the standard is low - the officer only needs probable cause to believe you violated 55-10-401.</li>
<li><strong>"If they get a warrant, the refusal charge goes away."</strong> Wrong. The refusal is a separate civil violation. Even if police later obtain a warrant and draw blood, the refusal suspension still applies.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic suspension AND can be used as evidence of guilt. Plus the officer can get a warrant for your blood.</li>
<li>"First refusal is only 12 months." Wrong. As of January 1, 2026, certain first refusals = 18 months.</li>
<li>"You have a right to an attorney before deciding." Wrong. You have no right to counsel before the implied consent decision.</li>
<li>"The suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
</ul>
</div>
