# DUI / Implied Consent - Mississippi

## Verdict

Mississippi's implied consent law (MCA 63-11-23, 63-11-30) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle on public highways, roads, or streets automatically consents to chemical testing (breath, blood, or urine) if a law enforcement officer has reasonable grounds and probable cause to believe they are driving under the influence. A first refusal results in a **90-day license suspension** for a Class R (regular) license. If the driver has a prior DUI conviction or non-adjudication, the suspension increases to **1 year**. CDL holders face **1-year disqualification** for a first refusal. Mississippi does NOT criminalize refusal; penalties are purely administrative. Courts may issue an order for an IID-restricted license to avoid suspension. Refusal is admissible as evidence of consciousness of guilt.

## Verbatim Quotes

> "When a person refuses to submit to the chemical testing of that person's breath, blood, or urine upon request of a law enforcement officer who has reasonable grounds and probable cause to believe a person has been operating a motor vehicle upon the public highways, public roads, and streets of Mississippi while under the influence of intoxicating liquor or any other substance that may impair a person's mental or physical ability, the person's Class R license shall be suspended for 90 days. CDL's shall be suspended for 1 year for a refusal of chemical testing."
> -- MS Driver Service Bureau, DUI Department FAQ, [source](https://www.driverservicebureau.dps.ms.gov/DriverRecords/DUI_Department)

> "If the person has previously been convicted or non-adjudicated for a violation of 63-11-30, the person's Class R license shall be suspended for 1 year."

> "To avoid a suspension of a Class R license, courts may issue an order to the driver for the ignition interlock-restricted license."

## Distinctive State Features

1. **No Criminal Penalty for Refusal**: Mississippi is one of the states that does NOT criminalize test refusal. The penalties are purely administrative (license suspension).

2. **Court-Issued IID License to Avoid Suspension**: Courts may issue an order directing the driver to obtain an IID-restricted license, which can prevent or reduce the administrative suspension period.

3. **Non-Adjudication Available**: First-time offenders may be eligible for non-adjudication, where the court withholds adjudication of guilt and places the defendant in a program conditioned upon successful completion. This avoids a conviction on the record.

4. **Hardship Reduction for Non-Refusers**: First offense license suspension can be reduced from 90 days to 30 days if the driver did NOT refuse the chemical test and qualifies under hardship provisions.

5. **Warrant Required for Blood Draw**: If a driver refuses a blood test, Mississippi requires a judicial search warrant to authorize the draw.

6. **MASEP Required**: All DUI offenders must attend the Mississippi Alcohol Safety Education Program (MASEP).

7. **5-Year Lookback**: Mississippi uses a 5-year lookback period for penalty enhancements.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative Only**: Refusal penalties in Mississippi are civil/administrative only. There are no criminal penalties for refusal itself.
- **Intoxilyzer 8000**: Mississippi uses the Intoxilyzer 8000 for breath testing. Results are commonly challenged on maintenance and calibration grounds.
- **Vehicle Forfeiture**: Third and subsequent offenses within 5 years can result in mandatory seizure and forfeiture of the vehicle used.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face penalties at .02 BAC or higher, with license suspension of 90 days to 2 years depending on prior offenses.
- **Victim Impact Panel**: Courts may require attendance at a victim impact panel as part of sentencing.
