<div class="quip">Washington: where the coffee is strong, the rain is constant, and your license is revoked for a year if you refuse the breath test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Washington</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> RCW 46.20.308. By driving in Washington, you are deemed to have consented to breath and blood testing if lawfully arrested for DUI. The officer chooses which test. Blood tests require a warrant unless consent or exigent circumstances exist.</li>
<li><strong>First refusal = 1-year revocation. Second = 2 years. Third = 3 years.</strong> RCW 46.20.308(2). Administrative revocation by DOL, separate from criminal case.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must inform you of your rights and consequences.</strong> RCW 46.20.308(1). Before a refusal is valid, the officer must inform you of your right to refuse, right to additional tests, and consequences of refusal.</li>
<li><strong>You have 20 days to request a DOL hearing.</strong> RCW 46.20.308(9). Miss it and the revocation is automatic.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Washington is a two-party consent state, but recording police in public is generally protected. Do not interfere.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have the right to consult with an attorney before deciding, but the officer does not have to wait indefinitely. The test must be administered within a reasonable time.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't have probable cause."</strong> If the arrest was unlawful, the revocation can be challenged at a DOL hearing. But the standard is low - the officer only needs reasonable grounds to believe you were DUI.</li>
<li><strong>"I have a medical marijuana card."</strong> Medical marijuana does not exempt you from implied consent. If the officer has reasonable grounds to believe you are impaired by marijuana, you must submit to testing.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic revocation AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You have 30 days to request a DOL hearing." Wrong. You have 20 days. Miss it and the revocation is automatic.</li>
<li>"The revocation only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The revocation is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You can get an ignition interlock license right away." Wrong. You must serve a portion of the revocation before becoming eligible for an ignition interlock license.</li>
</ul>
</div>
