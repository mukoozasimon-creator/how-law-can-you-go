# Homeschooling Laws - Rhode Island

## Statutory Framework

Rhode Island does not have a dedicated homeschool statute. Homeschooling operates under the general compulsory attendance framework with local district approval required.

### Primary Instrument: R.I. Gen. Laws 16-19-1

> "Every child shall attend a public school or a private school approved by the commissioner of elementary and secondary education, or shall be taught by an approved private tutor, or shall be home instructed in accordance with the requirements set forth in regulations of the board of regents for elementary and secondary education."
> - Source: [FindLaw - Rhode Island General Laws 16-19-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-16-education/ri-gen-laws-sect-16-19-1/) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Local District Approval

Rhode Island is unique in that **local school district approval** is required to homeschool. The process and requirements vary by district, as they are governed by local school committee policies rather than a uniform state statute.

> "A waiver to the compulsory attendance requirement may be granted by the superintendent only upon proof that the pupil has an alternative learning plan."
> - Source: [FindLaw - Rhode Island General Laws 16-19-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-16-education/ri-gen-laws-sect-16-19-1/) (Authority: A)

**Typical district requirements include:**
- Written notice of intent to homeschool
- Submission of a curriculum plan or course of study
- Evidence that instruction will be provided in required subjects
- Annual assessment or evaluation of student progress
- Attendance records

### Required Subjects

While not codified in a single state statute, districts typically require instruction in:
- Reading
- Writing
- Mathematics
- Science
- Social studies
- Physical education
- Health

### Teacher Qualifications

**None at the state level.** Individual districts may have their own policies, but state law does not mandate credentials.

### Testing Requirements

Varies by district. Some districts require annual standardized testing; others accept portfolio reviews or teacher evaluations.

### Instructional Time

Typically **180 days** per year, matching the public school calendar, though this varies by district.

### Record-Keeping

Parents must maintain attendance records and evidence of instruction, subject to district review.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 18.

### Public School Access

Rhode Island does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school classes, sports, or extracurricular activities. District policies vary.

### District Variation

Because approval is local, requirements can differ significantly between districts. Some districts are highly permissive; others impose substantial oversight. The Rhode Island Department of Education does not standardize homeschool policy across districts.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal Framework** | No dedicated homeschool statute; operates under compulsory attendance waiver |
| **Notification** | Local district approval required (varies by district) |
| **Testing** | Varies by district |
| **Qualifications** | None at state level |
| **Required Subjects** | Typically 6-7 subjects (district-dependent) |
| **Instructional Time** | Typically 180 days (district-dependent) |
| **Record-Keeping** | Attendance and progress records (district-dependent) |
| **Key Risk** | District variation creates unequal treatment |

### Verdict

Rhode Island is a **moderate-to-high regulation** state due to its **decentralized, district-dependent approval system**. The absence of a uniform state homeschool statute means families face different requirements depending on their residence. Some districts operate with minimal oversight; others impose testing, curriculum review, and record-keeping requirements. This creates a compliance landscape that is unpredictable and potentially burdensome depending on the local school committee's disposition toward homeschooling.
