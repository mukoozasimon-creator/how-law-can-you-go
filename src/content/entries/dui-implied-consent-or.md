# DUI / Implied Consent - Oregon

## Verdict

Oregon's implied consent law (ORS 813.100) deems that any person driving on a public highway or area open to the public has automatically agreed to submit to a breath test if arrested by a police officer on probable cause for driving under the influence of intoxicants (DUII). Refusal to submit to a breath or blood test results in a **1-year license suspension** (or 3 years if the driver has prior DUII convictions or diversion history). Refusal is also a **specific fine traffic violation** under ORS 813.095 with a presumptive fine of **$650**. Oregon requires officers to inform drivers of their rights and consequences before requesting a test. Drivers have **10 days** to request a DMV Implied Consent Hearing to challenge the suspension.

## Verbatim Quotes

> "Whenever you drive on a public highway or an area that is open to the public, you are subject to the 'implied consent law' found in the Oregon Vehicle Code. This means that just by the act of driving, you automatically agree to take a breath test if you are arrested by a police officer on probable cause for driving under the influence of intoxicants."
> -- Oregon State Bar, Must I Take a Chemical Breath Test?, [source](https://www.osbar.org/public/legalinfo/1271_BreathTest.htm)

> "If a person refuses to physically submit to a chemical test under ORS 813.100 or 813.131, evidence of the person's refusal is admissible in any civil or criminal action, suit or proceeding arising out of acts alleged to have been committed while the person was driving a motor vehicle on premises open to the public or the highways while under the influence of intoxicants."
> -- ORS 813.310, [source](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors813.html)

> "A person commits the offense of refusal to take a test for intoxicants if the person refuses to: (a) Take a breath test when requested to do so in accordance with the provisions of ORS 813.100; or (b) Take a urine test when requested to do so in accordance with the provisions of ORS 813.131 and 813.132."
> -- ORS 813.095, [source](https://www.oregonduii.com/what_happens_after_you_refuse_to_take_breath_test.html)

> "The offense described in this section, refusal to take a test for intoxicants, is a specific fine traffic violation. The presumptive fine for refusal to take a test for intoxicants is $650."
> -- ORS 813.095(2), [source](https://www.oregonduii.com/what_happens_after_you_refuse_to_take_breath_test.html)

> "Oregon will suspend your license for at least one year if you refuse to submit to breath or blood testing. The suspension may be increased to multiple years if you: Were enrolled in a DUII diversion program at the time of refusal; Had previously completed the DUII diversion program; Have been convicted of DUII."
> -- Southern Oregon Defense, What You Need To Know: Implied Consent, [source](https://www.southernoregondefense.com/blog/what-you-need-to-know-implied-consent/)

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal Is a Traffic Violation with $650 Fine**: Oregon uniquely classifies refusal to take a chemical test as a "specific fine traffic violation" under ORS 813.095, carrying a presumptive fine of $650 - separate from the administrative license suspension.

2. **10-Day Hearing Deadline**: Oregon has one of the shortest hearing request windows in the country - just 10 days from arrest to request a DMV Implied Consent Hearing.

3. **30-Day Temporary Permit**: Upon refusal, the officer immediately confiscates the license and issues a 30-day temporary permit. The suspension begins after the 30 days expire.

4. **Hardship Permit Restrictions**: Drivers who refuse are NOT eligible for a hardship permit for at least 90 days, and possibly for three years depending on prior DUII history.

5. **DUII Diversion Impact**: Drivers enrolled in or who have completed DUII diversion face enhanced suspension periods (3 years instead of 1 year) for refusal.

6. **Lifetime Revocation for Third Conviction**: A third DUII conviction results in lifetime license revocation, with the possibility of obtaining a license after 10 years.

7. **"Driving" Requires Movement**: Oregon defines driving as requiring vehicle movement. Sleeping in a parked car is not "driving," though driving to the location while impaired can support a charge.

## Practical Notes

- **Two Separate Proceedings**: Oregon has separate criminal (DUII) and administrative (implied consent) proceedings. A driver can be acquitted of DUII but still lose their license for refusal.
- **Felony DUII**: Three convictions within 10 years makes DUII a felony. A third conviction overall (regardless of timing) triggers lifetime revocation.
- **DUII Diversion Program**: First-time offenders may be eligible for diversion, which requires treatment and results in dismissal upon completion. However, diversion history affects future refusal penalties.
- **Officer Must Read Rights**: Officers must inform drivers of their rights and consequences under ORS 813.130 before requesting a test. Failure to do so may invalidate the suspension.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Oregon's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Oregon roadways, including out-of-state residents.
