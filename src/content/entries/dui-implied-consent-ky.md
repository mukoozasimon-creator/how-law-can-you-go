# DUI / Implied Consent - Kentucky

## Verdict

Kentucky's implied consent law (KRS 189A.103) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (breath, blood, or urine) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are impaired. Refusal triggers an **automatic pre-trial license suspension** that is separate from any suspension imposed after a DUI conviction. For a first offense, the suspension is **30 days** (per some sources) or pre-trial suspension until case resolution. Refusal is also one of six aggravating factors under KRS 189A.010(11) that **doubles mandatory minimum jail time and community service**. Kentucky does NOT grant a right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit to testing. As of July 15, 2026, new DUI legislation took effect with changes to chemical test refusals and implied consent warnings.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal as Aggravating Factor**: Kentucky uniquely treats refusal as one of six aggravating factors that automatically doubles mandatory minimum jail time and community service upon conviction. The other five are: BAC .15+, excessive speed (30+ mph over), wrong-way driving, accident causing serious injury/death, and minor passenger under 12.

2. **No Right to Counsel Before Testing**: Kentucky explicitly does NOT grant drivers the right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing.

3. **Pre-Trial Suspension**: Refusal triggers an automatic pre-trial license suspension that begins immediately and runs separately from any post-conviction suspension.

4. **New 2026 Law Changes**: Effective July 15, 2026, Kentucky amended its implied consent law with changes to chemical test refusals and implied consent warnings. Drivers should verify whether their arrest occurred before or after this date.

5. **Admissible as Evidence**: Refusal is admissible in court as evidence against the driver.

6. **Medical Exception**: If a driver cannot physically provide a sample due to injury or medical conditions, law enforcement will not penalize them for refusal.

## Practical Notes

- **Two Tracks**: Kentucky, like most states, has separate administrative and criminal proceedings. The pre-trial suspension for refusal is administrative and immediate.
- **Mandatory Minimums Double**: Because refusal is an aggravating factor, mandatory minimum jail sentences are doubled. For example, a first offense with refusal requires a minimum of 4 days in jail instead of 2.
- **Community Service Doubles**: Mandatory community service hours also double when refusal is present as an aggravating factor.
- **Ignition Interlock**: Kentucky has an ignition interlock program, but specifics vary by offense number and circumstances.
- **Lookback Period**: Kentucky uses a 10-year lookback period for prior DUI offenses.
- **Effective Date Matters**: For arrests on or after July 15, 2026, the amended implied consent law applies. For arrests before that date, the prior version controls.
