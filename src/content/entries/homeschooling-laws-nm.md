# Homeschooling Laws - New Mexico

## Statutory Framework

New Mexico homeschools operate under **Section 22-1-2(E) and Section 22-1-2.1 NMSA 1978**, which define homeschooling and set forth its legal requirements.

### Definition: Section 22-1-2(E) NMSA 1978

> "'Home school' means the operation by the parent of a school-age person of a home study program of instruction that provides a basic academic educational program, including reading, language arts, mathematics, social studies and science."
> - Source: [New Mexico Public Education Department - Home Schooling](https://web.ped.nm.gov/bureaus/options-for-parents-and-families/home-schooling/)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Legal Requirements: Section 22-1-2.1 NMSA 1978

> "Any person operating or intending to operate a home school shall:
> A. submit a home school registration form made available by the department and posted on the department's web site to notify the department within thirty days of the establishment of the home school and to notify the department on or before August 1 of each subsequent year of operation of the home school;
> B. maintain records of student disease immunization or a waiver of that requirement; and
> C. provide instruction by a person possessing at least a high school diploma or its equivalent."
> - Source: [New Mexico Public Education Department - Home Schooling](https://web.ped.nm.gov/bureaus/options-for-parents-and-families/home-schooling/)
> - Repeal check: Active; last reviewed 2026.

### Notification

- **Initial:** Within 30 days of establishing the homeschool
- **Annual renewal:** On or before **August 1** of each subsequent year
- Filed with the **New Mexico Public Education Department (NMPED)**
- Can be submitted online via the NMPED portal or by mail

> "State law requires you to submit a Notice of Intent to the New Mexico Public Education Department within 30 days of beginning to homeschool. This can be done either by mail or online, and it needs to be submitted annually by August 1st for each year you continue to homeschool."
> - Source: [Miacademy - How to Start Homeschooling in New Mexico](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/by-state/homeschooling-in-new-mexico/) (2026-08-05)

### Teacher Qualifications

The person providing instruction must possess at least a **high school diploma or its equivalent (GED)**.

> "In order to homeschool in New Mexico, you must have a high school diploma or its equivalent (i.e., a GED)."
> - Source: [HSLDA - How to Comply with New Mexico's Homeschool Law](https://hslda.org/post/how-to-comply-with-new-mexicos-homeschool-law) (2026-02-24)

### Required Subjects

The curriculum must provide a "basic academic educational program" including:
- Reading
- Language arts
- Mathematics
- Social studies
- Science

However, the curriculum **does not require NMPED approval**.

> "Your curriculum doesn't have to be approved by the New Mexico Public Education Department (NMPED), either. You just have to provide instruction in the five primary subjects mentioned previously."
> - Source: [Student Discipline Defense - Homeschooling in New Mexico](https://www.studentdisciplinedefense.com/homeschooling-in-new-mexico-rights-and-responsibilities)

### Instructional Time

- **1,140 hours per year** for full-day students
- **550 hours per year** for half-day kindergarten students
- Generally equivalent to **180 days** of instruction

> "All students need to receive 1,140 hours of instruction per year, except half-day kindergarten students, who should receive 550 hours per year."
> - Source: [Miacademy - How to Start Homeschooling in New Mexico](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/by-state/homeschooling-in-new-mexico/)

### Testing Requirements

**None.** New Mexico does not mandate standardized testing, portfolio review, or annual assessment for homeschool students.

> "New Mexico homeschool laws do not mandate any form of testing for homeschooled students."
> - Source: [SVHS - Homeschooling in New Mexico](https://svhs.co/homeschooling-new-mexico-laws/) (2026-01-16)

### Immunization

Parents must maintain records of student disease immunization or a valid waiver.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Children who are at least 5 years old before September 1 of the school year must attend school. They must continue until they receive a high school diploma or equivalent, or reach their 22nd birthday on the first day of the school year.

> "All children who are at least five years old before September 1 of the school year must attend school in New Mexico. They must also attend if they haven't received a high school diploma or equivalent or they haven't reached their twenty-second birthday on the first day of the school year."
> - Source: [Student Discipline Defense - Homeschooling in New Mexico](https://www.studentdisciplinedefense.com/homeschooling-in-new-mexico-rights-and-responsibilities)

### Public School Access

New Mexico does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school sports or classes. District policies vary.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Initial within 30 days; annual renewal by Aug 1 |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | High school diploma or GED |
| **Required Subjects** | 5 core subjects (no approval needed) |
| **Instructional Time** | 1,140 hours/year (or 180 days) |
| **Immunization** | Records or waiver required |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 5-22 (broadest in the nation) |

### Verdict

New Mexico is a **low-to-moderate regulation** state. The annual notification, high school diploma requirement, and 1,140-hour instruction standard place it slightly above the most deregulated states, but the absence of testing, curriculum approval, and broad compulsory age range (through age 22) provide substantial flexibility. The online NMPED portal streamlines compliance.
