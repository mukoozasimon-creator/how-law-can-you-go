## The Short Answer

Kansas is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not engaged in unlawful activity and are in a place where you have a right to be (KSA 21-5222(c)). Kansas's **Castle Doctrine** (KSA 21-5222) creates a presumption that you reasonably believed deadly force was necessary when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling, occupied vehicle, or place of work. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm, or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### KSA 21-5222 - Same; Defense of a Person

> "(a) A person is justified in the use of force against another when and to the extent it appears to such person and such person reasonably believes that such force is necessary to defend such person or a third person against such other's imminent use of unlawful force. (b) A person is justified in the use of deadly force under circumstances described in subsection (a) if such person reasonably believes that such force is necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm to such person or a third person. (c) A person who is not engaged in an unlawful activity and who is attacked in any place where such person has a right to be has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand such person's ground and to meet force with force, including deadly force, if such person reasonably believes it is necessary to do so to prevent death or great bodily harm to such person or another or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** http://www.kslegislature.org/li/statute/021_000_0000_chapter/021_052_0000_article/021_052_0022_section/021_052_0022_k/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be. Deadly force justified against imminent death, great bodily harm, or forcible felony.

### KSA 21-5230 - Presumption of Reasonableness

> "A person who is lawfully present in a dwelling, occupied vehicle or place of work is presumed to have reasonably believed that the use of force, including deadly force, was necessary to prevent imminent death or great bodily harm if the person against whom the force was used was unlawfully and forcibly entering, or had unlawfully and forcibly entered, the dwelling, occupied vehicle or place of work."

**Live link:** http://www.kslegislature.org/li/statute/021_000_0000_chapter/021_052_0000_article/021_052_0030_section/021_052_0030_k/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters dwelling, vehicle, or place of work.

### KSA 21-5231 - Immunity from Criminal Prosecution and Civil Action

> "A person who uses force which is justified pursuant to K.S.A. 21-5222 is immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use of such force."

**Verdict:** Immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil action.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your place of work | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Kansas explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to workplace:** The presumption applies to your dwelling, vehicle, and place of work.
- **Civil and criminal immunity:** Kansas provides broad immunity for justified use of force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Kansas is a one-party consent state for recordings (KSA 22-2517). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Missouri:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Midwest.
- **vs. Oklahoma:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections.
- **vs. Nebraska:** NE imposes a duty to retreat; KS does not.
