## The Short Answer

In Oregon, your landlord must have **just cause** to evict or refuse to renew your tenancy (ORS § 90.394). Oregon is a **statewide just-cause state** with enumerated grounds. For nonpayment, the landlord must serve a **10-day written notice** to pay or quit (§ 90.394(2)(a)). For lease violations, a **30-day notice** to cure or quit applies (14 days for repeat violations). For no-cause terminations, the landlord must give **30 days' notice** for tenancies under 1 year, **60 days** for 1+ years, or **90 days** for 3+ years (§ 90.427). Some no-fault terminations require **relocation assistance**. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### ORS § 90.394(2)(a) - 10-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "For nonpayment of rent, the landlord must give the tenant a written notice that the rent is due and that the rental agreement will terminate if the rent is not paid within 10 days." cite🛠web_search:42#4:~:text=For nonpayment of rent, the landlord must give the tenant a written notice that the rent is due and that the rental agreement will terminate if the rent is not paid within 10 days.

**Live link:** https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/90.394
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 10-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying rent within 10 days.

### ORS § 90.392 - 30-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "For a material violation of the rental agreement, the landlord must give the tenant a written notice specifying the violation and stating that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than 30 days after receipt of the notice, if the violation is not remedied within 14 days." cite🛠web_search:42#4:~:text=For a material violation of the rental agreement, the landlord must give the tenant a written notice specifying the violation and stating that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than 30 days after receipt of the notice, if the violation is not remedied within 14 days.

**Verdict:** 30-day notice for lease violations (14 days to cure). 10-day notice for repeat violations.

### ORS § 90.427 - No-Cause Termination Notice Periods

> "For no-cause terminations, the landlord must give: 30 days' notice for tenancies under 1 year; 60 days' notice for tenancies of 1 year or more; 90 days' notice for tenancies of 3 years or more." cite🛠web_search:42#4:~:text=For no-cause terminations, the landlord must give: 30 days' notice for tenancies under 1 year; 60 days' notice for tenancies of 1 year or more; 90 days' notice for tenancies of 3 years or more.

**Live link:** https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/90.427
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Sliding-scale notice based on tenancy duration for no-cause terminations.

### ORS § 90.300 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **31 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **2x the amount wrongfully withheld** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/90.300
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 31-day return. Move-in checklist required. Double damages for wrongful withholding.

### ORS § 90.375 - Self-Help Eviction Prohibited

> "A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks." cite🛠web_search:42#4:~:text=A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks.

**Live link:** https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/90.375
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Self-help eviction is illegal. Only court order can remove tenant.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Nonpayment of rent | 10 days (pay or quit) | Yes |
| Lease violation | 30 days (14 days to cure) | Yes |
| Repeat lease violation | 10 days (no cure) | Yes |
| No-cause termination, < 1 year | 30 days | Yes (for covered tenancies) |
| No-cause termination, 1-3 years | 60 days | Yes |
| No-cause termination, 3+ years | 90 days | Yes |

## Important Notes

- **Just-cause coverage:** Applies to most residential tenancies. Exemptions include owner-occupied buildings with 4 or fewer units and certain short-term rentals.
- **Relocation assistance:** Required for some no-fault terminations (owner move-in, demolition, substantial repairs). Amount varies by jurisdiction.
- **Rent control:** Oregon has statewide rent control under SB 608 (2019). Annual rent increase capped at 7% plus CPI (with exemptions for newer buildings and certain subsidized housing).
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Recording:** Oregon is a one-party consent state for recordings (ORS § 165.540). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Washington:** Both are statewide just-cause states in the Pacific Northwest with similar protections. OR has rent control; WA does not.
- **vs. California:** Both require just cause and have rent control, but OR's framework is newer (SB 608, 2019).
- **vs. Idaho:** OR is far more tenant-friendly with just-cause requirements and rent control.
