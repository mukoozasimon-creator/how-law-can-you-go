## The Short Answer

Indiana is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using deadly force in self-defense if you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent serious bodily injury or the commission of a forcible felony (IC 35-41-3-2). Indiana's **Castle Doctrine** is explicitly codified in the same statute and extends to your dwelling, curtilage, and occupied vehicle. Indiana also provides **civil immunity** for justified use of force (IC 34-30-31) and uniquely allows reasonable force against public servants under certain circumstances.

## Key Statutes

### IC 35-41-3-2 - Use of Force to Protect Person or Property

> "(a) A person is justified in using reasonable force against another person to protect the person or a third person from what the person reasonably believes to be the imminent use of unlawful force. (b) A person: (1) is justified in using deadly force; and (2) does not have a duty to retreat; if the person reasonably believes that deadly force is necessary to prevent serious bodily injury to the person or a third person or the commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** https://iga.in.gov/laws/2025/ic/titles/35#35-41-3-2
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before deadly force if reasonably believed necessary to prevent serious bodily injury or forcible felony.

### IC 35-41-3-2 - Castle Doctrine Policy Statement

> "It is the policy of this state to recognize the unique character of a citizen's home and to ensure that a citizen feels secure in his or her own home against unlawful intrusion by another individual or a public servant."

**Live link:** https://iga.in.gov/laws/2025/ic/titles/35#35-41-3-2
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Explicit state policy recognizing the unique character of the home.

### IC 35-41-3-2(j) - Force Against Public Servants

> "A person is justified in using reasonable force against a public servant if the person reasonably believes the force is necessary to: (1) protect the person or a third person from what the person reasonably believes to be the imminent use of unlawful force; (2) prevent or terminate the public servant's unlawful entry of or attack on the person's dwelling, curtilage, or occupied motor vehicle; or (3) prevent or terminate the public servant's unlawful trespass on or criminal interference with property lawfully in the person's possession."

**Live link:** https://iga.in.gov/laws/2025/ic/titles/35#35-41-3-2
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Unique provision allowing reasonable force against public servants under specific circumstances.

### IC 34-30-31 - Civil Immunity

> "A person is immune from civil liability for any injury or damage caused to another person if the person using force was justified in using the force under IC 35-41-3-2."

**Live link:** https://iga.in.gov/laws/2025/ic/titles/34#34-30-31
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Civil immunity for justified use of force.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of serious bodily injury/forcible felony |
| In your curtilage | No | Yes, same standard |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, same standard |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Against public servant (unlawful entry) | No | Reasonable force justified |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Indiana explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using deadly force.
- **Castle Doctrine includes curtilage:** Indiana extends Castle Doctrine protections to the curtilage (area immediately surrounding the dwelling), not just the dwelling itself.
- **Civil immunity:** Indiana provides explicit civil immunity for justified use of force.
- **Unique public servant provision:** Indiana is one of the few states that explicitly addresses the use of force against public servants, allowing reasonable force to prevent unlawful entry by law enforcement.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Recording:** Indiana is a one-party consent state for recordings (IC 35-33.5-5-5). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Ohio:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Midwest. OH enacted SYG in 2021; IN has had it longer.
- **vs. Illinois:** IN has explicit statutory SYG; IL relies on case law.
- **vs. Michigan:** Both are stand-your-ground states with civil immunity.
