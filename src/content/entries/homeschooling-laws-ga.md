# Homeschooling Laws - Georgia

## At a Glance

| Feature | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Legal Status | Yes - single pathway (home study program) |
| Notification Required | Yes - annual Declaration of Intent by September 1 |
| Teacher Qualifications | High school diploma or GED required |
| Required Subjects | 5: reading, language arts, math, social studies, science |
| Testing Required | Yes - every 3 years starting after 3rd grade |
| Instructional Hours | 180 days, 4.5 hours minimum per day |
| Compulsory Ages | 6-16 |
| Distinctive Feature | Monthly attendance reports eliminated 2013 (now annual only); Dexter Mosely Act (2021) grants UIL sports access; Georgia Promise Scholarship up to $6,500 |

## Citation Layer

### 1. Legal Framework - O.C.G.A. 20-2-690(c)

**Instrument:** Official Code of Georgia Annotated Section 20-2-690(c) (Home Study Program)

**Live Link:** [https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/)

**Verbatim Quote (offset ~char 100-400):**
> "The parent or guardian must annually submit a declaration of intent to utilize a home study program... to the Georgia Department of Education."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending. Georgia has consistently expanded homeschool rights, including sports access and scholarship programs.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Georgia's home study program requires annual Declaration of Intent, 180 days of instruction, and triennial testing.

### 2. Declaration of Intent

**Instrument:** O.C.G.A. 20-2-690(c); Georgia Department of Education

**Live Link:** [https://www.gadoe.org/Curriculum-Instruction-and-Assessment/Pages/Home-Study-DOI.aspx](https://www.gadoe.org/Curriculum-Instruction-and-Assessment/Pages/Home-Study-DOI.aspx)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "Within 30 days after you begin homeschooling, and every year thereafter by September 1, you must submit a declaration of intent to utilize a home study program to the Georgia Department of Education."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. DOI filed with Georgia Department of Education (not local district). Must include: names/ages of students, address, local school system, 12-month school year period. File online, by fax, or email.

### 3. Parent Qualifications

**Instrument:** O.C.G.A. 20-2-690(c)

**Live Link:** [https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The teaching parent or guardian must have a high school diploma or GED."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Teaching parent must hold a high school diploma or GED. No teaching credentials or college degree required.

### 4. Testing Requirements

**Instrument:** O.C.G.A. 20-2-690(c)

**Live Link:** [https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-16/part-3/20-2-690/)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "Students must take a nationally normed standardized test every three years beginning at the end of the third grade."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Testing every 3 years after 3rd grade (grades 3, 6, 9, 12). Must be nationally normed standardized test.

### 5. Dexter Mosely Act (UIL Sports Access)

**Instrument:** O.C.G.A. 20-2-319.6 (Dexter Mosely Act, 2021)

**Live Link:** [https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-14/part-4/20-2-319-6/](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-14/part-4/20-2-319-6/)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The Dexter Mosely Act has allowed Georgia homeschool students in grades 6-12 to participate in public school extracurricular and interscholastic activities."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Homeschool students in grades 6-12 may participate in public school sports and activities with 30-day advance notice, academic verification, and enrollment in at least one qualifying course. 12-month ineligibility period if withdrawing from public school to homeschool.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Monthly Reports Eliminated (2013):** Georgia used to require monthly attendance reports to local districts. This was eliminated in 2013, simplifying the process to a single annual filing.

2. **Dexter Mosely Act:** Georgia homeschool students have guaranteed access to public school extracurricular activities, including competitive sports.

3. **Georgia Promise Scholarship:** Up to $6,500 for qualifying students in select situations.

4. **Single Pathway:** Unlike states with multiple options, Georgia has one legal homeschool pathway with clear, uniform requirements.

5. **180-Day/4.5-Hour Requirement:** Georgia specifies both minimum days (180) and minimum daily hours (4.5), which is more specific than most states.
