## The Short Answer

In Rhode Island, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or quit after rent is 15 days in arrears (R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-35(a)). For material lease violations affecting health and safety, a **20-day notice** to cure or quit applies (34-18-36(a-d)). For repeat violations within 6 months, a **20-day unconditional notice** applies (34-18-36(e)). For drug-related or violent crimes, **no notice** is required (34-18-36(f)). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (34-18-37). Rhode Island has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-35(a) - 5-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If any part of the stipulated rent is due and in arrears for fifteen days, the landlord shall send a written notice, and the tenant has 5 days from the date of mailing to cure."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-35/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Rent must be 15 days late before notice. Tenant then has 5 days from mailing to cure. 15-day grace period before notice can be sent.

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-36(a-d) - 20-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "For noncompliance with the rental agreement that materially affects health and safety, the landlord shall deliver a written notice specifying the acts and omissions and that the rental agreement will terminate in 20 days if the breach is not remedied."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-36/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 20-day cure-or-quit for material lease violations.

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-36(e) - 20-Day Notice for Repeat Violations

> "If the tenant substantially commits the same noncompliance within six months, the landlord may deliver a 20-day notice to quit with no opportunity to cure."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-36/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No cure right for repeat violations within 6 months.

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-36(f) - No Notice for Drug/Violent Crimes

> "No notice is required before filing for eviction when the tenant uses the premises as a narcotics nuisance, manufactures/sells/delivers controlled substances, or commits any crime of violence on or near the premises."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-36/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immediate filing permitted for drug-related or violent crimes.

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-37 - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least 30 days before the periodic rental date."

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-37/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination.

### R.I. Gen. Laws 34-18-19 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **1 month's rent**, plus furniture deposit up to 1 month's rent.
- Return deadline: **20 days** after lease termination.
- Must provide itemized statement if deductions made.

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ri/title-34-property/ri-gen-laws-sect-34-18-19/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 20-day return. 1-month cap (plus furniture deposit).

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 5 days (after 15-day grace) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 20 days (cure or quit) | N/A |
| Repeat violation (within 6 months) | 20 days (no cure) | N/A |
| Drug/violent crime | No notice required | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Rhode Island landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Rhode Island does not have statewide rent control.
- **15-day grace period:** Rent must be 15 days late before the 5-day notice can be sent.
- **Housing code disclosure:** Landlords must inform tenants of outstanding housing code violations before entering a rental agreement.
- **Recording:** Rhode Island is a two-party consent state for recordings (R.I. Gen. Laws 11-35-21). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Connecticut:** CT requires good cause for evictions in some circumstances; RI does not.
- **vs. Massachusetts:** MA has just-cause requirements in some cities; RI does not statewide.
- **vs. New York:** NY has extensive just-cause and rent stabilization laws; RI does not.
