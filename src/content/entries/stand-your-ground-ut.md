## The Short Answer

Utah is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense, and the **failure to retreat is not a relevant factor** in determining whether your use of force was reasonable (Utah Code 76-2-402(3)). You may use force when you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent unlawful force against yourself or another. Deadly force is only justified when you reasonably believe it is necessary to prevent imminent death or serious bodily injury, or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### Utah Code 76-2-402 - Force in Defense of Person

> "(1) A person is justified in threatening or using force against another when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes that force or a threat of force is necessary to defend the person or a third person against another person's imminent use of unlawful force. (2) A person is justified in using force intended or likely to cause death or serious bodily injury only if the person reasonably believes that force is necessary to prevent death or serious bodily injury to the person or a third person as a result of another person's imminent use of unlawful force, or to prevent the commission of a forcible felony."

**Live link:** https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter2/76-2-S402.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force only justified against imminent death, serious bodily injury, or forcible felony.

### Utah Code 76-2-402(3) - No Duty to Retreat

> "A person does not have a duty to retreat from the force or threatened force described in Subsection (1) or (2) in a place where that person has lawfully entered or remained, except as provided in Subsection (4)(a)(ii). (4)(a)(ii) A person does not have a duty to retreat under Subsection (3) if the person is in a place where the person has lawfully entered or remained."

**Live link:** https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter2/76-2-S402.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have lawfully entered or remained.

### Utah Code 76-2-405 - Force in Defense of Habitation

> "(1) A person is justified in using force against another when and to the extent that he reasonably believes that the force is necessary to prevent or terminate the other's unlawful entry into or attack upon his habitation. (2) A person is justified in using force intended or likely to cause death or serious bodily injury only if: (a) the entry is made or attempted in a violent and tumultuous manner, and he reasonably believes that the entry is attempted or made for the purpose of assaulting or offering personal violence to any person, dwelling, or being in the habitation..."

**Live link:** https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title76/Chapter2/76-2-S405.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies to habitation. Deadly force only against violent/tumultuous entry with intent to assault.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your habitation (violent intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent assault |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Failure to retreat | Not relevant | Cannot be used against you |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Utah explicitly states that a person does not have a duty to retreat from force anywhere they have lawfully entered or remained.
- **Failure to retreat is not relevant:** Utah Code 76-2-402(3) specifically provides that the failure to retreat is not a relevant factor in determining whether the use of force was reasonable. This is a stronger protection than many SYG states.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to habitation:** The statutory Castle Doctrine (76-2-405) applies to your habitation. Deadly force is only justified against violent/tumultuous entry.
- **Civil immunity:** Utah provides civil immunity for justified use of force under Utah Code 78B-3-110.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Utah is a one-party consent state for recordings (Utah Code 77-23a-4). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Arizona:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southwest. AZ has a Castle Doctrine presumption; UT does not.
- **vs. Colorado:** Both are stand-your-ground states. CO has Make My Day for dwelling; UT has broader no-duty-to-retreat language.
- **vs. Idaho:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Mountain West.
