<div class="quip">Massachusetts: where the clam chowder is thick and your license is gone for 180 days if you refuse the breath test.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Massachusetts</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, 24. By driving in Massachusetts, you are deemed to have consented to breath or blood testing if lawfully arrested for OUI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal suspension escalates with priors.</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, 24(1)(f)(1). No prior = 180 days. 1 prior = 3 years. 2 priors = 5 years. 3+ priors = lifetime. Refusal suspension runs back-to-back with any OUI-related suspension.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal is admissible in court.</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, 24(1)(f)(1). The prosecutor can use your refusal as evidence of consciousness of guilt. <em>Commonwealth v. Blais</em> (1998) requires the officer to inform you of consequences first.</li>
<li><strong>You have the right to an independent test.</strong> M.G.L. c. 90, 24(1)(f)(2). You can have a physician of your own choosing administer an independent test at your own expense.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Massachusetts is a two-party consent state, but recording police in public is generally protected. Do not interfere.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't inform me of the consequences."</strong> <em>Commonwealth v. Blais</em> requires the officer to inform you of the consequences of refusal. Failure to do so can make the refusal inadmissible.</li>
<li><strong>"I wasn't driving, I was sleeping in the car."</strong> Massachusetts uses 'operating' standard. If you have the keys and the ability to operate the vehicle, you can be arrested for OUI.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers automatic suspension AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"You can get a hardship license right away." Wrong. After a refusal, you are ineligible for any hardship license during the suspension period.</li>
<li>"The suspension only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. The suspension is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You have 30 days to appeal." Wrong. You have 15 days to request a hearing with the RMV.</li>
</ul>
</div>
