## Summary

Virginia offers three legal pathways for home education: (1) Home Instruction under Va. Code § 22.1-254.1 (most common), (2) Certified Tutor under § 22.1-254, and (3) Religious Exemption under § 22.1-254(B)(1) - the only such statutory religious exemption in the United States. The Home Instruction path requires annual notification, parent qualifications, 180 days/990 hours of instruction, and annual evidence of progress.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute - Home Instruction

**Va. Code § 22.1-254.1** - Declaration of policy; requirements for home instruction

> "When the requirements of this section have been satisfied, instruction of children by their parents is an acceptable alternative form of education under the policy of the Commonwealth of Virginia."

- **Instrument**: Va. Code § 22.1-254.1
- **Live Link**: https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title22.1/chapter14/section22.1-254.1/
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Home instruction is an acceptable alternative when statutory requirements are met.

### Religious Exemption Statute

**Va. Code § 22.1-254(B)(1)**

> "Any pupil who, together with his parents, by reason of bona fide religious training or belief is conscientiously opposed to attendance at school. For purposes of this subdivision, 'bona fide religious training or belief' does not include essentially political, sociological or philosophical views or a merely personal moral code."

- **Instrument**: Va. Code § 22.1-254(B)(1)
- **Live Link**: https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title22.1/chapter14/section22.1-254/
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Virginia is the **only state** with a specific statutory religious exemption from compulsory school attendance. No education requirements apply to exempted students.

## Requirements - Home Instruction Path

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual Notice of Intent by August 15 |
| **Teacher Qualifications** | Must meet 1 of 4 options (see below) |
| **Required Subjects** | None mandated |
| **Testing/Assessment** | Annual evidence of progress by August 1 |
| **Attendance** | 180 days or 990 hours |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 5-18 (child who will have reached 5th birthday by Sept 30) |

### Parent Qualification Options (§ 22.1-254.1(A))

Parents must meet **one** of the following:

1. **Option I**: Hold a high school diploma or higher credential
2. **Option II**: Be a teacher of qualifications prescribed by the Virginia Board of Education
3. **Option III**: Provide a program of study or curriculum through correspondence course, distance learning, or any other manner
4. **Option IV**: Provide evidence of ability to provide an adequate education for the child

## Notification

**Annual Notice of Intent (NOI)** must be filed with the division superintendent by **August 15** each school year. The NOI must include:

- Child's name
- Parent's name and address
- Which qualification option is being used (I, II, III, or IV)
- Description of curriculum (limited to a list of subjects to be studied)

If starting mid-year, file immediately and comply within 30 days.

## Required Subjects

Virginia does **not** mandate any specific subjects under the Home Instruction statute. Complete curriculum freedom is granted.

## Assessment & Testing

**Evidence of progress** must be submitted to the division superintendent by **August 1** each year:

- **Option A**: Evidence that the child attained a composite score in or above the fourth stanine on any nationally normed standardized achievement test, or equivalent score on ACT, SAT, or PSAT
- **Option B**: An evaluation or assessment that the division superintendent determines indicates adequate educational growth and progress, including:
  - Evaluation letter from a person licensed to teach in any state, or person with a master's degree or higher in an academic discipline
  - Report card or transcript from an institution of higher education, college distance learning program, or home-education correspondence school

**Note**: Certified Tutor and Religious Exemption paths have **no** testing requirements.

## Record-Keeping

Keep copies of:
- Annual NOI submissions
- Evidence of progress submissions
- Curriculum descriptions
- Proof of parent qualification

## Special Provisions

- **AP/PSAT Access**: School boards must make AP, PSAT/NMSQT, and PreACT examinations available to homeschool students (§ 22.1-254.1(F))
- **Appeals**: Any party aggrieved by a superintendent's decision may appeal within 30 days to an independent hearing officer (§ 22.1-254.1(E))
- **Privacy**: No division superintendent or school board shall disclose homeschool information to anyone outside the local division without written parental consent (§ 22.1-254.1(G))
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation

## Distinctive Features

- **Only state with a statutory religious exemption** from compulsory attendance (§ 22.1-254(B)(1))
- Three distinct legal pathways with very different requirements
- No required subjects - complete curriculum freedom
- August 1 assessment deadline comes **before** the August 15 NOI deadline
- Strong privacy protections for homeschool records
