## Summary

Connecticut is one of the most homeschool-friendly states in the nation. Under CGS § 10-184, parents are required only to provide "equivalent instruction" in specified subjects to children ages 5-18. There is no mandatory notification, no registration, no standardized testing, no curriculum approval, no parent qualifications, and no minimum instructional hours. The Connecticut State Department of Education's official homeschool guidance page literally returns "No Results Found" - an unintentionally accurate summary of the state's hands-off approach. The C-4 and C-14 guidelines are voluntary policy documents, not statutes, and filing a Notice of Intent or undergoing a portfolio review is entirely optional.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**CGS § 10-184** - Duties of parents

> "All parents and those who have the care of children shall bring them up in some lawful and honest employment and instruct them or cause them to be instructed in reading, writing, spelling, English grammar, geography, arithmetic, U.S. history, and in citizenship, including a study of the town, state and federal governments."

- **Instrument**: CGS § 10-184
- **Live Link**: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_168.htm#sec_10-184
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Parents providing equivalent instruction in specified subjects satisfy compulsory education requirements.

## Notification

**No notification is legally required.** Connecticut does not require parents to file any paperwork with the state, school district, or town to begin homeschooling.

**Voluntary Best Practice**: Many families choose to send a voluntary Letter of Withdrawal to their child's school to prevent truancy concerns. Some districts may request a voluntary Notice of Intent, but parents are not legally obligated to provide one.

**Important**: The C-4 and C-14 guidelines (created in 1990 and revised in 1994) are policy documents that suggest procedures for districts. They do **not** create legal obligations beyond the statute. Filing a Notice of Intent or undergoing a portfolio review under these guidelines is entirely voluntary.

## Required Subjects (8)

Under CGS § 10-184, instruction must include:

1. Reading
2. Writing
3. Spelling
4. English Grammar
5. Geography
6. Arithmetic (Mathematics)
7. U.S. History
8. Citizenship (including study of town, state, and federal governments)

The statute does not specify how these subjects must be taught, what curriculum must be used, or how progress should be assessed.

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required. Connecticut does not mandate any testing for homeschoolers.

## Record-Keeping

No records are required by law. The Connecticut State Department of Education suggests as "best practice" that parents:
- Keep a log of attendance reflecting days and hours of instruction
- Maintain a portfolio for each child containing samples of activities, assignments, projects, and assessments
- Keep a log of books and materials used
- Include results of any national assessments

However, these are **suggestions**, not legal requirements.

## Special Provisions

- **Returning to Public School**: A district is not required to award graduation credit for work completed during homeschooling in grades 9-12 (CGS § 10-221a). Students may be placed based on age and district assessment.
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Funding**: No state-level financial programs for homeschoolers
- **Special Education**: If a child is not enrolled in public or private school, the school district does not owe services, including special education services. Parents may seek services independently.

## Distinctive Features

- **No government oversight** - no registration, notification, testing, or approval required
- One of the broadest compulsory age ranges (5-18)
- C-4/C-14 guidelines are voluntary, not statutory
- Filing Notice of Intent is optional, not mandatory
- Portfolio reviews are optional, not mandatory
- "Equivalent instruction" standard with no enforcement mechanism
- State DOE homeschool page returns "No Results Found"
- Strong homeschool community with decades of successful operation under this framework
