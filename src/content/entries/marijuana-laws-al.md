# Marijuana Laws - Alabama

## Verdict

Alabama permits medical cannabis only. The Darren Wesley "Ato" Hall Compassion Act (SB 46, signed May 17, 2021) created a tightly regulated medical program. After years of licensing litigation, the first dispensary (Callie's Apothecary in Montgomery) opened on June 4, 2026. Recreational marijuana remains illegal and possession is not decriminalized. Home cultivation is prohibited. Approved medical products exclude smokable flower, vape products, and traditional food edibles.

## Named Instruments

- **Ala. Code Title 20, Chapter 2A** - Darren Wesley "Ato" Hall Compassion Act (SB 46, 2021)
- **Ala. Code Section 13A-12-214** - Unlawful possession of marijuana (criminal penalties)
- **SB 285 (2026 Regular Session)** - Decriminalization bill (died in Senate Judiciary Committee)

## Verbatim Quotes

> "Recreational marijuana is not legal in Alabama in 2026. The state operates a medical-cannabis-only program that, after roughly five years of licensing litigation, finally opened to patients when Alabama's first dispensary began sales in Montgomery on June 4, 2026."
> - Alabama Medical Cannabis Commission (AMCC), June 2026

> "A first offense for personal-use possession is charged as a misdemeanor that can carry up to a year in jail and fines up to $6,000. A second or subsequent possession for personal use is more serious: it is charged as Unlawful Possession of Marijuana in the First Degree, a Class D felony punishable by one year and one day to five years in prison and a fine up to $7,500."
> - Ala. Code Section 13A-12-214

> "Allowed medical product forms in Alabama include tablets and capsules, tinctures, gels and oils, topical creams, transdermal patches, suppositories, lozenges, gelatinous cubes, and inhalable liquids or oils delivered through a nebulizer or inhaler. Prohibited forms include smokable flower, vaping products, and raw plant material."
> - AMCC Program Rules; Ala. Code Title 20, Chapter 2A

## Penalty Tiers (Non-Medical Possession)

| Offense | Classification | Penalty |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| First offense, personal use | Class A misdemeanor | Up to 1 year jail; up to $6,000 fine |
| Second/subsequent, personal use | Class D felony | 1 year 1 day - 5 years prison; up to $7,500 fine |
| Possession for other than personal use | Class C felony | 1-10 years prison; up to $15,000 fine |

## Medical Program

- **Status:** Operational as of June 2026
- **Enabling law:** Darren Wesley "Ato" Hall Compassion Act (SB 46, 2021)
- **Regulator:** Alabama Medical Cannabis Commission (AMCC)
- **Qualifying conditions:** 16 conditions including cancer, epilepsy, chronic pain, PTSD, Crohn's disease, sickle cell anemia, panic disorder, and others
- **Patient requirements:** Alabama resident, age 19+ (minors with caregiver), physician recommendation from AMCC-certified physician, AMCC registration
- **Product restrictions:** NO smokable flower, NO vape products, NO raw plant material, NO food/baked edibles
- **Allowed forms:** Tablets, capsules, tinctures, gels, oils, creams, patches, suppositories, lozenges, gelatinous cubes, nebulizer/inhaler liquids
- **Dispensaries:** Limited licenses; first opened June 4, 2026

## Home Cultivation

- **Status:** Illegal for all purposes - medical patients, caregivers, and recreational users.
- Alabama does not permit any form of home cannabis cultivation.

## DUI / Impairment

- Alabama prohibits driving under the influence of any controlled substance, including cannabis.
- No specific per se THC limit is established.
- Medical cannabis card does not provide a defense to DUI.

## Employment Protections

- No employment protections for medical cannabis patients exist under Alabama law.
- Employers may maintain drug-free workplace policies and terminate for positive cannabis tests.
- The medical program does not create anti-discrimination rights in employment.

## Federal Conflict

- Cannabis remains Schedule I under federal law (21 U.S.C. Section 812).
- Interstate transport of medical cannabis products is a federal offense.
- Federal property in Alabama prohibits cannabis possession regardless of state medical authorization.
