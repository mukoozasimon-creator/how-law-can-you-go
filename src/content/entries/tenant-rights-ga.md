## The Short Answer

In Georgia, your landlord must give you **60 days' written notice** to terminate a month-to-month tenancy (tenancy at will). For nonpayment or lease violations, the landlord must make a **demand for possession** before filing a dispossessory action - there is no fixed statutory waiting period between the demand and filing for fixed-term leases, but for leases entered or renewed on or after July 1, 2024, the Safe at Home Act requires a **3-business-day cure period** for nonpayment before filing. Georgia has no just-cause requirement, no rent control (statewide preemption), and no statewide security deposit cap for leases before July 1, 2024. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### Official Code of Georgia Annotated Section 44-7-7 - Termination of Tenancy at Will

> "OCGA § 44-7-7 requires 60 days written notice from the landlord to terminate a tenancy at will (month-to-month or no specified term)... The 60-day notice ends the tenancy itself. After the tenancy ends, the landlord still must demand possession under § 44-7-50 and, if the tenant refuses to deliver possession, file the dispossessory affidavit." cite🛠web_search:2#3:~:text=OCGA § 44-7-7 requires 60 days written notice from the landlord to terminate a tenancy at will (month-to-month or no specified term)

### Safe at Home Act (Effective July 1, 2024)

> "Effective July 1, 2024, for leases entered or renewed on or after that date, the Act introduced four key changes: a warranty that premises must be fit for human habitation, a cap on security deposits at two months' rent, a mandatory 3-business-day cure period before filing for eviction for non-payment, and the addition of cooling to the list of utilities a landlord cannot shut off during eviction proceedings." cite🛠web_search:2#7:~:text=Effective July 1, 2024, for leases entered or renewed on or after that date, the Act introduced four key changes: a warranty that premises must be fit for human habitation, a cap on security deposits at two months' rent, a mandatory 3-business-day cure period before filing for eviction for non-payment

## Important Notes

- **Safe at Home Act applies to leases entered/renewed on or after July 1, 2024.** Older leases are not covered by the 3-day cure period or deposit cap.
- **No just-cause requirement:** Georgia landlords can terminate tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **Dispossessory timeline:** Tenant has 7 days to answer after service. Writ of possession becomes effective 7 days after judgment. Uncontested evictions typically take 2-3 weeks.
- **Security deposits (post-July 2024):** Capped at 2 months' rent including all refundable deposits. Formal escrow requirements apply to landlords with 10+ units or third-party management.
- **Recording:** Georgia is a one-party consent state for recordings (OCGA § 16-11-62). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are landlord-friendly with no just-cause or rent control, but Georgia has longer termination notice (60 days vs. 30 days).
- **vs. Florida:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but Florida has stricter notice-counting rules (business days only).
- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are landlord-friendly Southern states, but Georgia's 60-day notice is longer than NC's 7-day demand for nonpayment.
