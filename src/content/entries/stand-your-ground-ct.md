## The Short Answer

Connecticut is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you know you can do so with complete safety (C.G.S. 53a-19(b)). Connecticut's **Castle Doctrine** removes the duty to retreat inside your **dwelling** or **place of work** if you were not the initial aggressor (C.G.S. 53a-19(b)(1)). Deadly force is only justified when you reasonably believe the other person is using or about to use deadly physical force, or is inflicting or about to inflict great bodily harm. Connecticut courts have recognized the common law privilege to challenge unlawful entry into one's home.

## Key Statutes

### C.G.S. 53a-19 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Person

> "(a) Except as provided in subsections (b) and (c) of this section, a person is justified in using reasonable physical force upon another person to defend himself or a third person from what he reasonably believes to be the use or imminent use of physical force, and he may use such degree of force which he reasonably believes to be necessary for such purpose; except that deadly physical force may not be used unless the actor reasonably believes that such other person is (1) using or about to use deadly physical force, or (2) inflicting or about to inflict great bodily harm."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-53a/chapter-951/section-53a-19/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force only against imminent deadly physical force or great bodily harm.

### C.G.S. 53a-19(b) - Duty to Retreat

> "Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (a) of this section, a person is not justified in using deadly physical force upon another person if he or she knows that he or she can avoid the necessity of using such force with complete safety (1) by retreating, except that the actor shall not be required to retreat if he or she is in his or her dwelling, as defined in section 53a-100, or place of work and was not the initial aggressor, or if he or she is a peace officer or a private person assisting such peace officer at his or her direction, and acting pursuant to section 53a-22, or (2) by surrendering possession of property to a person asserting a claim of right thereto, or (3) by complying with a demand that he or she abstain from performing an act which he or she is not obliged to perform."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-53a/chapter-951/section-53a-19/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is known to be available. No duty to retreat in dwelling or place of work if not initial aggressor.

### C.G.S. 53a-20 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Premises

> "A person in possession or control of premises, or a person who is licensed or privileged to be in or upon such premises, is justified in using reasonable physical force upon another person when and to the extent that he reasonably believes such to be necessary to prevent or terminate the commission or attempted commission of a criminal trespass by such other person in or upon such premises..."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-53a/chapter-951/section-53a-20/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified to prevent criminal trespass. Deadly force only to prevent arson or crime of violence.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (not initial aggressor) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent deadly force/great bodily harm |
| In your place of work (not initial aggressor) | No | Yes, same standard |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** Connecticut explicitly requires retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. This is a key distinction from stand-your-ground states.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to workplace:** The no-duty-to-retreat exception applies to both your dwelling and your place of work, provided you were not the initial aggressor.
- **Subjective knowledge standard:** The duty to retreat requires that you "know" you can retreat with complete safety. This is a subjective standard - your actual knowledge matters, not what a reasonable person would know.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat. Deadly force is never justified solely to protect property.
- **No stand-your-ground legislation:** Bills to enact stand-your-ground legislation have been introduced (most recently in 2007) but have not passed.
- **Recording:** Connecticut is a two-party consent state for recordings (C.G.S. 53a-189). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. Both extend Castle Doctrine to dwelling.
- **vs. Massachusetts:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. MA limits Castle Doctrine to unlawful intruders; CT extends to workplace.
- **vs. Rhode Island:** RI is a stand-your-ground state; CT is not.
