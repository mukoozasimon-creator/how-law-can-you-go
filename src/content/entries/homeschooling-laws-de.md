# Homeschooling Laws - Delaware

## Statutory Framework

Delaware homeschools are defined as **nonpublic schools** under **14 Del. C. 2703A**, with three distinct statutory types. All nonpublic schools, including homeschools, must register with the state.

### Primary Instrument: 14 Del. C. 2703A

> "For purposes of this chapter, a 'homeschool' is considered a nonpublic school, except that students enrolled in homeschools are deemed to be parentally-placed private school children for purposes of providing equitable services using proportionate share funds under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, 20 U.S.C. 1400, et. seq."
> - Source: [Justia - 14 Delaware Code 2703A](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-14/chapter-27/subchapter-i/section-2703a/) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Three Types of Homeschools

**1. Single-Family Homeschool**
> "'Single-family homeschool' means the education of an individual's own child, primarily by the parent or legal guardian of the child, mainly in their own residence."
> - Source: [Delaware Code Title 14 Chapter 27](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title14/c027/sc01/) (Authority: NA)

**2. Multi-Family Homeschool**
> "'Multi-family homeschool' means the education of children, primarily by the parents or legal guardians of the children mainly in 1 or more residences, or other facilities, when the children are not all related to each other as brother or sister. A person shall act as a liaison to the Department of Education for reporting enrollment and attendance information for all families involved."
> - Source: [Delaware Code Title 14 Chapter 27](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title14/c027/sc01/)

**3. Single-Family Homeschool Coordinated with Local School District**
> "'Single-family homeschool coordinated with the local school district' means the education of a child primarily by the parent or legal guardian of the child, mainly in their own residence, using a curriculum approved by the local superintendent or the local superintendent's designee. The local superintendent shall determine in writing that the student is or will be provided with regular and thorough instruction by the student's parent or legal guardian in the subjects prescribed for the public schools of the State and in a manner suitable to children of the same age and stage of advancement."
> - Source: [Delaware Code Title 14 Chapter 27](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title14/c027/sc01/)

### Registration and Reporting: 14 Del. C. 2704

All nonpublic schools, including homeschools, must:
- **Register students** with the state to be exempt from compulsory attendance
- Report **attendance** on or before **July 31** of each year
- Submit a statement of **enrollment** no later than **October 5** on forms prescribed by the Department of Education

> "All persons conducting private schools and/or home schools shall report to the Department of Education the attendance on or before July 31 of each year. All persons conducting private schools and/or home schools must also submit a statement annually of enrollment no later than October 5 on such forms and in such manner as may be prescribed by the Department of Education."
> - Source: [U.S. Department of Education - Delaware State Regulation](http://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools/delaware-state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools) (Authority: S)

### EdAccess Registration Process

1. Apply for an EdAccess Account and select Nonpublic School Account
2. Log into EdAccess, complete the Nonpublic School Application
3. Obtain the Acknowledgment Letter once approved
4. Access the NPS homepage via EdAccess for ongoing reporting

> "Apply for an EdAccess Account and select Nonpublic School Account. Log into EdAccess, complete the Nonpublic School Application. Obtain the Acknowledgment Letter once your NPS is approved."
> - Source: [SVHS - Homeschooling in Delaware](https://svhs.co/homeschooling-delaware-laws/) (2026-01-16)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Delaware statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for homeschool parents.

> "Legislation offers three distinct pathways for homeschooling in Delaware, each without mandatory teacher qualifications or standardized testing prerequisites."
> - Source: [SVHS - Homeschooling in Delaware](https://svhs.co/homeschooling-delaware-laws/)

### Testing Requirements

**None at the state level.** Delaware does not mandate standardized testing for homeschool students.

### Required Subjects

No state curriculum policy currently exists for homeschools. However, single-family homeschools coordinated with the district must use a curriculum approved by the local superintendent covering subjects "prescribed for the public schools of the State."

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 5 through 16.

### Special Education

Homeschool students are deemed "parentally-placed private school children" for purposes of equitable services under IDEA.

### Public School Access

Delaware does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school classes, sports, or extracurricular activities. District policies vary.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal Classification** | Nonpublic school (3 homeschool types defined) |
| **Notification** | Register with DDOE via EdAccess; annual enrollment (by Oct 5) and attendance (by Jul 31) reports |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | None (unless using district-coordinated option) |
| **Multi-Family** | Permitted with designated liaison for reporting |
| **District-Coordinated** | Optional path with superintendent-approved curriculum |
| **Special Ed** | Treated as parentally-placed private school under IDEA |

### Verdict

Delaware is a **low-regulation** state with a unique three-type classification system. The registration and annual reporting requirements (enrollment and attendance) create a light administrative burden, but the absence of testing, qualifications, and curriculum mandates (for non-coordinated options) preserves significant flexibility. The multi-family homeschool option with a liaison is distinctive, and the IDEA equitable services treatment is a notable benefit for special needs families.
