# DUI / Implied Consent - Utah

## Verdict

Utah's implied consent law (Utah Code § 41-6a-520, effective May 6, 2026) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (breath, blood, saliva, or urine) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are driving under the influence. Utah has the nation's lowest BAC limit at **.05** (not .08). A first refusal results in an **18-month license revocation**. A second or subsequent refusal within a 10-year period triggers a **3-year revocation**. Blood tests require a search warrant; breath tests do not. Drivers are entitled to know test results and may request an additional independent test. Refusal is admissible as evidence in criminal proceedings.

## Blood vs. Breath Distinctions

| Factor | Blood Test | Breath Test | Saliva/Urine Test |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Warrant Required | **Yes** - Blood test requires a search warrant | No (implied consent) | No (implied consent) |
| Drug Detection | Yes (all substances) | No (alcohol only) | Yes (drugs) |
| Officer Choice | Officer may request; must obtain warrant if driver refuses | Officer may request at arrest | Officer may request |
| Driver Right to Additional Test | Yes - at driver's expense by physician of choice | Same | Same |
| Driver Entitled to Results | Yes - statutory right to know test results | Same | Same |

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Lowest BAC Limit in the Nation (.05)**: Utah is the only state with a .05 BAC limit for non-commercial drivers (effective December 30, 2018). All other states use .08.

2. **Blood Test Requires Warrant**: Utah uniquely requires a search warrant for blood tests, while breath tests can be conducted under implied consent without a warrant.

3. **18-Month First Refusal**: Utah's 18-month revocation for a first refusal is longer than most states' first-refusal penalties.

4. **Right to Know Results**: Utah explicitly grants drivers the statutory right to know the results of chemical tests after they have been administered.

5. **Right to Independent Test**: Drivers may request an additional test by a physician of their own choosing at their own expense.

6. **Temporary Permit After Refusal**: Upon refusal, the driver is granted a temporary permit and may request a hearing to challenge the suspension.

7. **Poor Driving History Enhancement**: Drivers with poor driving histories who refuse may face 5- or 10-year suspensions.

## Practical Notes

- **.05 BAC = Legally Intoxicated**: Utah's .05 BAC limit means that just one or two drinks can put many drivers over the legal limit. Visitors from other states should be especially cautious.
- **Hearing to Challenge Suspension**: Drivers may request a hearing to challenge the suspension, but must demonstrate that the officer lacked reasonable grounds to believe they were driving under the influence.
- **Ignition Interlock Mandatory**: IID installation is mandatory for reinstatement after any DUI-related suspension in Utah.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Utah's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Utah roads. Out-of-state drivers face revocation of their Utah operating privilege.
- **Under 21 Penalties Are Severe**: Underage drivers face penalties even for non-driving alcohol possession, with license revocation up to 2 years possible.
- **Effective Date**: Utah Code § 41-6a-520 was effective May 6, 2026. Arrests before that date may be governed by the prior version of the statute.
