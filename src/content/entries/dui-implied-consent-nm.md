# DUI / Implied Consent - New Mexico

## Verdict

New Mexico's implied consent law (NMSA 66-8-107) deems that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing of breath or blood (or both) as determined by a law enforcement officer. A first refusal results in a **1-year license revocation**; a second refusal triggers **2 years**. New Mexico uniquely treats refusal as an aggravating factor that can elevate a DWI to "aggravated DWI," which carries enhanced criminal penalties. However, the New Mexico Supreme Court held in *State v. Vargas* (2017) that a driver **cannot be criminally punished for refusing a warrantless blood test** under the Fourth Amendment. Refusal still triggers administrative revocation. Drivers have **10 days** to request a hearing with a **$25 fee**. New Mexico also recognizes a "subsequent consent" rule allowing drivers to recant an initial refusal if done almost immediately.

> -- NMSA 66-8-107(A), [source](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-66/article-8/part-2/section-66-8-107/)

> "A driver may not be held criminally liable for refusing to submit to a warrantless blood test based on implied consent. ... blood tests bear too heavily on a subject's privacy interests to permit the state to seize warrantless samples at all DWI stops."
> -- Justia, citing State v. Vargas, 2017-NMSC-029, [source](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-66/article-8/part-2/section-66-8-107/)

> -- NCDD, New Mexico DUI Laws, [source](https://www.ncdd.com/new-mexico-dui-laws)

> "Penalties: 6 month revocation for 1st revocation under I.C.A. if .08 or higher, 21 years or older, 1 year revocation if 2nd revocation under I.C.A. or if under 21 years old with .02 or higher or anyone who refuses a chemical test."
> -- NCDD, New Mexico DUI Laws, [source](https://www.ncdd.com/new-mexico-dui-laws)

> "A subsequent change of mind can nullify a driver's initial refusal to take a blood-alcohol test and thus can cure an initial refusal. A driver will be permitted to rescind this initial refusal if the driver can prove the five elements of the test."
> -- Justia, citing In re Suazo, 1994-NMSC-070, [source](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-66/article-8/part-2/section-66-8-107/)

## Blood vs. Breath Distinctions

| Factor | Blood Test | Breath Test |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Warrant Required | **Yes** - if driver refuses, officer must obtain warrant or establish exigent circumstances | No (implied consent) |
| Criminal Penalty for Refusal | **No** - unconstitutional per *State v. Vargas* (2017) | Administrative revocation only |
| Drug Detection | Yes (all substances) | No (alcohol only) |
| Officer Choice | Officer directs; blood requires consent or warrant | Officer may request first |
| Driver Cannot Choose | Driver does not choose which test | Same |
| Subsequent Consent | Driver may recant initial refusal if done almost immediately | Same |

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Cannot Criminally Punish Blood Test Refusal**: Following *State v. Vargas* (2017-NMSC-029), New Mexico cannot criminally punish a driver for refusing a warrantless blood test. The Fourth Amendment protects against such penalties. However, administrative revocation remains valid.

2. **Refusal Elevates to Aggravated DWI**: Despite *Vargas*, refusal is still treated as an aggravating factor that elevates a DWI to "aggravated DWI," increasing minimum jail time (48 hours for 1st, 8 days for 2nd, 90 days for 3rd).

3. **Subsequent Consent Rule**: New Mexico recognizes that a driver may recant an initial refusal if done almost immediately. The driver must prove five elements to establish valid subsequent consent.

4. **10-Day Hearing Window with $25 Fee**: Drivers have just 10 calendar days to request an administrative hearing, and must include a $25 check or money order. Missing the deadline results in automatic revocation on the 20th day.

5. **Officer Chooses Test, Not Driver**: The officer determines whether to administer breath, blood, or both. The driver has no statutory right to choose.

6. **Lifetime Revocation for 4th+**: A fourth or subsequent DWI conviction results in lifetime license revocation with IID for life.

7. **55-Year Record Retention**: DWI convictions stay on a driver's criminal record for 55 years in New Mexico.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative Proceeding Is Separate**: The implied consent hearing is a totally separate case from the criminal DWI case. Resolving one does not resolve the other.
- **Blood Draw Cases Have Extended Deadline**: For blood draw cases, the 10-day hearing request period runs from receipt of the Notice of Revocation, not the arrest date.
- **Ignition Interlock License**: New Mexico offers ignition interlock licenses that permit revoked drivers to drive anytime, anywhere, for any reason, as long as the vehicle has an IID.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: New Mexico's implied consent law applies to all drivers on New Mexico roads, including out-of-state residents.
- **Under 21**: Drivers under 21 face 1-year revocation for .02+ BAC or refusal.
- **Plea Bargaining Restricted**: NMSA 66-8-102.1 prohibits plea bargaining to a lesser offense if BAC was .08+ (.04+ for commercial drivers).
