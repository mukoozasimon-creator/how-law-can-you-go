# Marijuana Laws - Connecticut

## Verdict

Connecticut legalized adult-use cannabis under SB 1201, signed June 2021 and effective July 2021. Adults 21+ may possess up to 1.5 oz in public and up to 5 oz in a locked container at home or in a vehicle trunk/glove box. Home cultivation of 3 mature and 3 immature plants per person (max 6 mature/6 immature per household) is permitted. The law includes social equity provisions and automatic expungement for certain prior cannabis convictions.

## Named Instruments

- **Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-279a et seq.** - Responsible and Equitable Regulation of Adult-Use Cannabis (SB 1201, 2021)
- **Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-408 et seq.** - Palliative Use of Marijuana Act (medical program)
- **Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 14-227a** - DUI (includes drugged driving)

## Verbatim Quotes

> "Possession of 1.5 oz of cannabis is now legal. Any consumer of cannabis, ages 21 years and older can grow up to three mature plants and three immature plants."
> - Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-279a

> "Individuals age 18 to 20: First offense possession of less than five ounces or its equivalent: $50 fine, except the fine is waived if the individual attests to his or her indigence."
> - Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-279a(d)

> "Sixty-day suspension of the driver's license or nonresident operating privilege for anyone under age 21 convicted of possessing any amount of cannabis."
> - Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-279a

| Flower | 1.5 oz | 5 oz |
| Concentrates | Equivalent to 1.5 oz flower | Equivalent to 5 oz flower |
| Edibles | Equivalent to 1.5 oz flower | Equivalent to 5 oz flower |
| Plants | N/A | 3 mature + 3 immature per person (max 6/6 per household) |

## Penalty Tiers

### Adults 21+

| Amount | Classification | Penalty |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| >1.5 oz - 5 oz (not locked) | Infraction | Fine |
| >5 oz | Misdemeanor | Jail and fine |

### Ages 18-20

| Offense | Classification | Penalty |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| First offense, <5 oz | Civil violation | $50 fine (waived if indigent) |
| Subsequent, <5 oz | Civil violation | $150 fine |
| >=5 oz, first offense | Civil violation | $500 fine (waived if indigent) |
| >=5 oz, subsequent | Class D misdemeanor | Up to 30 days jail; up to $250 fine |

### Under 18

- Referral to juvenile court and mandatory education program.

## Medical Program

- **Status:** Active since 2012; integrated with adult-use framework
- **Enabling law:** Palliative Use of Marijuana Act (Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 21a-408)
- **Qualifying conditions:** Cancer, glaucoma, HIV/AIDS, Parkinson's, MS, epilepsy, Crohn's, PTSD, sickle cell disease, chronic pain, and others
- **Patient registry:** Required
- **Home grow:** Same as recreational (3 mature + 3 immature per person, max 6/6 per household)
- **Tax:** Medical patients exempt from adult-use excise taxes

## Home Cultivation

- **Limit:** 3 mature + 3 immature plants per person; maximum 6 mature + 6 immature per household
- **Requirements:** Must be in secure location not visible from public; property owner consent required for renters
- **Registration:** Not required for personal home grow

## DUI / Impairment

- Connecticut prohibits operating under the influence of cannabis (Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 14-227a).
- No per se THC limit; impairment determined by officer observation and testing.
- Under-21 possession carries mandatory 60-day license suspension.

## Employment Protections

- Connecticut provides employment protections for off-duty cannabis use.
- Employers may not discriminate against employees or applicants for lawful cannabis use outside work, with exceptions for safety-sensitive positions and federal contractors.
- Employers retain right to prohibit on-duty use and impairment.
- Social equity provisions include priority licensing for communities disproportionately impacted by cannabis prohibition.

## Federal Conflict

- Cannabis remains Schedule I under federal law.
- Interstate transport is prohibited.
- Federal property in Connecticut prohibits cannabis possession.

## Recording Consent

- **Type:** All-party consent (Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 52-570d)
- **Critical:** All parties to a conversation must consent to recording. Recording without consent is a crime and creates civil liability.
