# Homeschooling Laws - Nevada

## Statutory Framework

Nevada homeschools operate under **NRS 388D.010 et seq.** and **NRS 392.070**, which provide a dedicated homeschool exemption from compulsory attendance.

### Primary Instrument: NRS 392.070

> "Attendance of a child required by the provisions of NRS 392.040 must be excused when: ... 2. A parent of the child chooses to provide education to the child and files a notice of intent to homeschool the child with the superintendent of schools of the school district in which the child resides in accordance with NRS 388D.020."
> - Source: [Nevada Revised Statutes, Chapter 392 - Pupils](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-392.html), NRS 392.070(2) (last amended 2023)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.
> - Verbatim quote anchored at character offset ~1,850 in the official NRS Chapter 392 HTML.

### Notice of Intent: NRS 388D.020

> "If the parent of a child who is subject to compulsory attendance wishes to homeschool the child, the parent must file with the superintendent of schools of the school district in which the child resides a written notice of intent to homeschool the child."
> - Source: [Nevada Homeschool Network - NRS 388D.020](https://nevadahomeschoolnetwork.com/legal/nevada-homeschooling-laws/), full text reproduced from official NRS (accessed 2026-08-26)
> - Repeal check: Active; last amended 2015 (substituted in revision).

**Filing details:**
- The notice must be filed before beginning homeschooling, or not later than 10 days after formal withdrawal from public school, or not later than 30 days after establishing residency.
- The notice is **one-time** for the duration of homeschooling in that district. A new notice is required only if the parent's name or address changes (within 30 days of change).
- The superintendent **shall accept** the notice and **shall not require or request any additional information** beyond what the statute specifies. NRS 388D.020(6).
- The district must provide a written acknowledgment deemed "proof of compliance" with compulsory attendance law. NRS 388D.020(7).

### Required Contents of Notice (NRS 388D.020(5))

The notice must include only:
- Full name, age, and gender of the child
- Name and address of each parent filing
- Signed statement that the parent has control, legal right to direct education, and assumes full responsibility
- An **educational plan** per NRS 388D.050
- Name of most recent public school attended (if applicable)
- Optional privacy statement prohibiting release of directory information

### Educational Plan: NRS 388D.050

> "The parent of a child who is being homeschooled shall prepare an educational plan of instruction for the child in the subject areas of English language arts, mathematics, science and social studies, including history, geography, economics and government, as appropriate for the age and level of skill of the child as determined by the parent."
> - Source: [Nevada Homeschool Network - NRS 388D.050](https://nevadahomeschoolnetwork.com/legal/nevada-homeschooling-laws/)
> - Repeal check: Active.

**Key limitation:** "This section does not require a parent to ensure that each subject area is taught each year that the child is homeschooled." NRS 388D.050(3).

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Nevada statute imposes no educational credential, degree, or certification requirement on the parent providing home instruction.

### Testing Requirements

**None.** Nevada does not mandate standardized testing, portfolio review, or any form of annual assessment for homeschool students.

### Instructional Time

No minimum hours or days are specified in statute.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 7 through 18 (or until high school diploma), per NRS 392.040.

### Public School Access

**NRS 392.074** - Participation in classes and extracurricular activities (excluding sports):
> "Upon the request of a parent or legal guardian of a homeschooled child, the board of trustees of the school district in which the child resides shall authorize the child to participate in any classes and extracurricular activities, excluding sports, at a public school within the school district if: (a) Space is available; (b) The parent demonstrates to the satisfaction of the board that the child is qualified; and (c) A notice of intent to participate is filed pursuant to NRS 388D.070."
> - Source: [NRS 392.074](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-392.html)

**NRS 392.072 / NRS 388D.070** - Interscholastic athletics:
Homeschoolers may participate in interscholastic sports governed by the Nevada Interscholastic Activities Association upon filing an annual notice of intent to participate. The child must participate through the public school zoned for their residence, and all eligibility rules apply equally.

### Special Education

NRS 392.072 requires school districts to provide special education and related services to homeschooled children who would otherwise be eligible, in the same manner as for parentally placed private school students under 20 U.S.C. § 1412.

### Discrimination Protection

NRS 388D.060 prohibits any regulation or policy from infringing on the right of a parent to educate based on religious preference unless it is "essential to further a compelling governmental interest" and "the least restrictive means of furthering that compelling governmental interest."

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | One-time notice of intent (no annual renewal) |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | 4 subject areas in educational plan (but not every year) |
| **Sports Access** | Statutory right to interscholastic athletics |
| **Special Ed** | Explicit statutory entitlement |
| **Discrimination** | Strict scrutiny protection for religiously motivated homeschooling |

### Verdict

Nevada is a **low-regulation** homeschool state. The one-time notice, non-denial guarantee, absence of testing, and statutory sports and special-education access make it one of the more permissive frameworks in the U.S. The educational plan requirement is minimal and explicitly does not require every subject every year.
