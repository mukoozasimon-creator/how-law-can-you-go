## The Short Answer

In Pennsylvania, your landlord must give you a **10-day written notice to quit** for nonpayment of rent before filing eviction. For ending a month-to-month tenancy of one year or less, the landlord must give **15 days' written notice**; for tenancies over one year, **30 days' notice** is required. The notice requirements can be **waived or modified by the lease**. Security deposits are capped at **2 months' rent** in the first year and **1 month** thereafter. Philadelphia adds additional procedural protections including eviction diversion requirements.

## Key Statutes

### Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951, Section 501 - Notice to Quit

> "A landlord desirous of repossessing real property from a tenant may notify, in writing, the tenant to remove from the same at the expiration of the time specified in the notice... In case of the expiration of a term or of a forfeiture for breach of the conditions of the lease where the lease is for any term of one year or less or for an indeterminate time, the notice shall specify that the tenant shall remove within fifteen days from the date of service thereof, and when the lease is for more than one year, then within thirty days from the date of service thereof. In case of failure of the tenant, upon demand, to satisfy any rent reserved and due, the notice shall specify that the tenant shall remove within ten days from the date of the service thereof." cite🛠web_search:1#10:~:text=A landlord desirous of repossessing real property from a tenant... the notice shall specify that the tenant shall remove within fifteen days... and when the lease is for more than one year, then within thirty days... ### Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951, Section 250.511a - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- **First year:** Maximum 2 months' rent
- **After first year:** Maximum 1 month's rent
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement
- Interest: Not required statewide (Philadelphia requires interest)

**Live link:** https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/Public/cons_index.cfm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deposit cap drops from 2 months to 1 month after first year. 30-day return.

### Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951, Section 250.512 - Retaliatory Eviction

> "A landlord may not evict a tenant, increase rent, or decrease services in retaliation for the tenant's complaint to a government agency or exercise of other legal rights." cite🛠web_search:1#7:~:text=Retaliatory eviction or rent hikes against tenants who report code violations or organize are illegal

**Live link:** https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/Public/cons_index.cfm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Retaliation is prohibited. Tenant can raise as defense in eviction proceedings.

### Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951, Section 250.205 - Self-Help Eviction

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination, <= 1 year | 15 days written | No (but lease can waive) |
| Month-to-month termination, > 1 year | 30 days written | No (but lease can waive) |
| Nonpayment of rent | 10 days written | N/A |
| Lease violation | Per lease terms / 15 days | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **Lease waiver:** Pennsylvania uniquely allows the lease to waive or modify notice requirements. Check your lease carefully.
- **Philadelphia protections:** Philadelphia requires eviction diversion (mediation before filing) for certain tenancies and has additional tenant protections beyond state law.
- **No rent control:** Pennsylvania preempts local rent control, but Philadelphia has procedural protections.
- **Service methods:** Notice must be posted on the property or hand-delivered. Regular mail is not sufficient evidence of hand delivery. Certified mail with signed return receipt, or text/email with proof of actual receipt, may suffice.
- **Recording:** Pennsylvania is a two-party consent state for recordings (18 Pa.C.S. § 5703). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Pennsylvania has shorter notice periods and no just-cause requirement (unlike NYC). Both allow lease modification of notice rules (NY does not).
- **vs. Ohio:** Both have moderate protections, but Ohio has a shorter 3-day eviction notice and stronger retaliation presumptions.
- **vs. California:** Pennsylvania is significantly more landlord-friendly with waivable notices and no just-cause requirement.
