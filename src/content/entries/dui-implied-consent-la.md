# DUI / Implied Consent - Louisiana

## Verdict

Louisiana's implied consent law (LSA-R.S. 32:661 et seq.) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if lawfully arrested for DWI. A first refusal results in a **1-year license suspension** (or 2 years if BAC is .20 or higher upon conviction). Louisiana allows drivers to elect a restricted driver's license with an ignition interlock device (IID) for the entire suspension period on first and second offenses. Refusal is admissible as evidence in criminal proceedings. Louisiana also provides that individuals measured through breath shall be afforded the timely option of a blood test.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Restricted License with IID Available on First Offense**: Louisiana allows drivers to obtain a restricted driver's license with an IID for the entire suspension period, even on a first refusal. This is more permissive than many states that impose hard suspension periods.

2. **Breath-to-Blood Option**: Louisiana uniquely provides that individuals tested by breath "shall be afforded the timely option of a blood test." This gives drivers a statutory right to request a confirmatory blood test after a breath test.

3. **Underage Record Protection**: For underage drivers with a BAC between .02 and .08, the submit shall not be included on the official driving record. This provides some protection for young drivers.

4. **Rehabilitative Court Pathway**: Drivers with 3rd or 4th offenses who are enrolled and in good standing in a rehabilitative (drrug, alcohol, or sobriety) court may be eligible for a restricted license after 45 days with IID.

5. **5-Year Lookback for Repeat Offenses**: Louisiana uses a 5-year lookback period from the date of the first offense to the date of the third offense for enhanced penalties.

6. **Pending Recording Law Change**: House Bill 410 (introduced March 2026) would change Louisiana from one-party to two-party consent for in-person conversations. As of August 2026, this bill has not become law.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative and Criminal Proceedings Separate**: License suspension for refusal is administrative and proceeds independently of the criminal DWI case.
- **Substance Abuse Evaluation Required**: All DWI offenders must undergo a substance abuse evaluation and complete any recommended treatment before license reinstatement.
- **Community Service**: First convictions require mandatory community service in addition to fines and possible jail time.
- **Vehicle Insurance**: SR-22 insurance is typically required for reinstatement after a DWI-related suspension.
- **Hardship License**: The restricted license with IID is the primary mechanism for maintaining driving privileges during suspension.
