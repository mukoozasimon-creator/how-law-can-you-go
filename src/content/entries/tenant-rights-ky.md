## The Short Answer

In Kentucky, your landlord must give you a **7-day written notice** to pay rent or quit for nonpayment (KRS § 383.660(2)). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **15-day notice** to cure or quit (§ 383.660(1)). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (§ 383.695). Kentucky has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### KRS § 383.660(1) - 15-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If there is a material noncompliance by the tenant with the rental agreement or a noncompliance with KRS 383.605 materially affecting health and safety, the landlord may deliver a written notice to the tenant specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than fifteen days after receipt of the notice, if the breach is not remedied in fifteen days." cite🛠web_search:42#3:~:text=If there is a material noncompliance by the tenant with the rental agreement... the landlord may deliver a written notice to the tenant specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate upon a date not less than fifteen days after receipt of the notice, if the breach is not remedied in fifteen days.

**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 15-day notice for lease violations. Tenant has opportunity to cure.

### KRS § 383.695 - Termination of Month-to-Month Tenancy

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least thirty days before the termination date specified in the notice." cite🛠web_search:42#3:~:text=The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least thirty days before the termination date specified in the notice.

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=35978
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### KRS § 383.580 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** (or up to 60 days if lease specifies) after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **actual damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=35978
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-60 day return. Move-in checklist required.

### KRS § 383.655 - Self-Help Eviction Prohibited

> "A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks." cite🛠web_search:42#3:~:text=A landlord may not willfully interrupt or terminate any utility service being supplied to the tenant, except in the case of an emergency or for repairs, or prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including changing the locks.

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Kentucky landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Kentucky preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery or certified/registered mail.
- **Recording:** Kentucky is a one-party consent state for recordings (KRS § 526.010). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Indiana:** Both have moderate protections, but KY has a shorter 7-day notice vs. IN's 10-day notice.
- **vs. Ohio:** Both have similar protections, but KY has longer lease violation notice (15 days vs. OH's 3 days).
- **vs. West Virginia:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but KY has stronger deposit return rules.
