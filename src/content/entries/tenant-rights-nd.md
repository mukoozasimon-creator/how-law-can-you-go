## The Short Answer

In North Dakota, your landlord must give you a **3-day written notice** of intention to evict for nonpayment of rent (NDCC 47-32-01(4)). For lease agreement violations, a **3-day notice** to vacate is also required (47-32-01(5)). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (47-32-01(7)). North Dakota has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### NDCC 47-32-01(4) - 3-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "An eviction action is maintainable once a tenant has failed to pay rent for three days after the rent is due. The landlord must provide a written 3-day notice of intention to evict."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.nd.gov/cencode/t47c32.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 3 days.

### NDCC 47-32-01(5) - 3-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "For violation of a material term of the lease agreement, the landlord must send a written 3-day notice specifying the violation and stating that the tenant has 3 days to vacate."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.nd.gov/cencode/t47c32.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice for lease violations. Unlike many states, North Dakota does not require giving the tenant an opportunity to cure lease violations.

### NDCC 47-32-01(7) - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "For termination of a month-to-month tenancy, no notice is required beyond the 30-day termination notice."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.nd.gov/cencode/t47c32.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination.

### NDCC 47-32-01 - No Notice for Irreparable Harm

> "No notice is required when the tenant does any of the following: uses the premises for an illegal purpose; causes or threatens to cause irreparable harm to the premises; or violates a no-contact order."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.nd.gov/cencode/t47c32.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No notice required for illegal use, irreparable harm, or no-contact order violations.

### NDCC 47-16-07 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **1 month's rent**.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after lease termination with itemized statement.
- Must provide written notice of deposit terms.

**Live link:** https://www.legis.nd.gov/cencode/t47c16.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. 1-month cap.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 3 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 3 days to vacate (no cure) | N/A |
| Illegal use / irreparable harm | No notice required | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** North Dakota landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** North Dakota does not have statewide rent control.
- **No cure for violations:** Unlike most states, ND does not require landlords to give tenants an opportunity to cure lease violations. The 3-day notice is to vacate, not to cure.
- **Recent changes (SB 2366):** Enhanced notice requirements, extended response times (up to 5 days in some cases), and courts must schedule hearings within 10 business days.
- **Recording:** North Dakota is a one-party consent state for recordings (NDCC 12.1-15-02). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. South Dakota:** Both have no just-cause requirement. SD repealed pre-suit notice requirement in 2024; ND still requires 3-day notice.
- **vs. Minnesota:** MN requires 14-day notice for nonpayment and has just-cause requirements in some cities; ND has 3 days and no just-cause.
- **vs. Montana:** Both have 3-day notices for nonpayment and no just-cause requirements. MT gives 14-day cure for violations; ND does not require cure.
