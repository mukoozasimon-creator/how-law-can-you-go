## The Short Answer

In Colorado, your landlord must have **just cause** to evict or refuse to renew your tenancy (C.R.S. § 13-40-104). Colorado is a **statewide just-cause state** with enumerated grounds. For nonpayment, the landlord must serve a **10-day notice to pay or quit** (5 days for exempt residential agreements). For lease violations, a **10-day notice to cure or quit** applies (5 days for exempt). For substantial violations (criminal/drug activity), a **3-day notice to quit** with no right to cure applies. For no-fault terminations (owner move-in, demolition, substantial repairs), the landlord must give **90 days' notice** and may owe **relocation assistance**. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### C.R.S. § 13-40-104 - Just Cause for No-Fault Terminations

**No-fault grounds include:**
- Demolition or conversion to nonresidential/short-term rental: **90 days**
- Substantial repairs/renovations: **90 days** (tenant has right of first refusal if repairs under 180 days)
- Owner or family member occupancy: **90 days** (owner must move in within 3 months; cannot list for rent for 90 days)

**Live link:** https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-13.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 90-day notice for no-fault terminations. Some grounds require relocation assistance.

### C.R.S. § 38-12-103 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** (or up to 60 days if lease specifies) after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **3x the amount wrongfully withheld** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-38.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-60 day return. Triple damages for wrongful withholding.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Nonpayment of rent | 10 days (5 days exempt) | Yes |
| Lease violation | 10 days cure or quit (5 days exempt) | Yes |
| Substantial violation (criminal/drugs) | 3 days (no cure) | Yes |
| Month-to-month termination | 21 days | Yes (for covered tenancies) |
| Owner move-in / demolition / repairs | 90 days | Yes |

## Important Notes

- **Just-cause coverage:** Applies to most residential tenancies. Exemptions include owner-occupied buildings with 5 or fewer units, employer-provided housing, and certain short-term rentals.
- **Relocation assistance:** Required for some no-fault terminations. Amount varies by jurisdiction.
- **HB 23-1120 (2023):** Established statewide just-cause framework, replacing the patchwork of local ordinances.
- **Eviction record suppression:** Under current law, eviction records are suppressed until landlord wins. HB 26-1047 (pending) would make suppression permanent except for substantial violations.
- **Recording:** Colorado is a one-party consent state for recordings (C.R.S. § 18-9-303). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Washington:** Both are statewide just-cause states with similar frameworks. CO has 21-day month-to-month notice; WA has 20 days.
- **vs. California:** Both require just cause, but CO's exempt residential agreement provision allows shorter notices for small landlords.
- **vs. Texas:** CO is far more tenant-friendly with just-cause requirements and relocation assistance.
