## The Short Answer

In Minnesota, your landlord must serve a **14-day written notice** with a specific accounting of amounts due before filing eviction for nonpayment (Minn. Stat. § 504B.321 subd. 1a). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **notice to cure** with reasonable time. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (or one full rental period, whichever is less) (§ 504B.135). Minnesota has no statewide just-cause requirement, no rent control (statewide preemption), and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### Minn. Stat. § 504B.178 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **21 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Interest: Not required by state law (some local ordinances may require it).
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/504B.178

| Lease violation | Reasonable notice to cure | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Minnesota landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Minnesota preempts local rent control (statewide preemption).
- **Housing Court:** Hennepin and Ramsey counties have specialized Housing Court divisions.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearing set not less than 7 nor more than 14 days from summons issuance.
- **Recording:** Minnesota is a one-party consent state for recordings (Minn. Stat. § 626A.02). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Wisconsin:** Both have similar protections, but MN has a 14-day notice vs. WI's 5-day notice for nonpayment.
- **vs. Iowa:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but MN has longer notice periods.
- **vs. Washington:** MN is far more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no rent control.
