## The Short Answer

In Virginia, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or quit for nonpayment (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(F)). For material lease breaches, the landlord must give **21 days to cure** with termination no less than **30 days** after notice. To end a month-to-month tenancy, either party must give **30 days' written notice** before the next rent due date (§ 55.1-1253(A)). **Important change:** SB 48/HB 15 extends the nonpayment notice to **14 days**, effective July 1, 2027. Virginia has no just-cause requirement and, as a Dillon Rule state, no municipal rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### Va. Code § 55.1-1226 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **45 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must be held in **escrow account** at Virginia financial institution.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **actual damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1226/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 45-day return. Escrow account required.

## Important Notes

- **Dillon Rule state:** Virginia preempts local rent control. No municipality can enact rent control.
- **No just-cause requirement:** Virginia landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery, certified/registered/first-class mail, or electronic notice if lease provides.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearing within 21 days of filing. Writ of eviction issued after judgment.
- **Recording:** Virginia is a one-party consent state for recordings (Va. Code § 19.2-62). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are landlord-friendly with no just-cause or rent control, but VA has longer notice (5 days vs. NC's 10-day demand) and a 21-day cure period for lease violations.
- **vs. Maryland:** VA has no just-cause requirement; MD has no statewide just-cause rule but has stronger local protections in some counties.
- **vs. Washington:** VA is far more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no rent control.
