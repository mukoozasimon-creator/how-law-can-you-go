<div class="quip">Washington is a two-party consent state on paper, but the courts have made it crystal clear that cops in public have zero expectation of privacy. So record away, just maybe do not do it while holding a venti oat milk latte because that is a spill risk.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Washington</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You must show your license, registration, and proof of insurance.</strong> RCW 46.20.150, 46.16A.030, and 46.30.020. All three. The officer can demand them on the spot. Driving without a license is a gross misdemeanor: up to 364 days in jail and $5,000 fine.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the officer.</strong> Washington is technically a two-party consent state (RCW 9.73.030), but police performing public duties have no reasonable expectation of privacy. Courts have consistently held that recording police in public is protected by the First Amendment. Just do not interfere.</li>
<li><strong>They cannot make you wait for a drug dog.</strong> <em>Rodriguez v. United States</em> (2015). Once the ticket is written, the stop is over. Washington courts follow this.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse to consent to a search.</strong> Fourth Amendment. Say "I do not consent to a search." If they have probable cause (smell, plain view), they can search anyway. But your refusal matters in court.</li>
<li><strong>Passengers do not have to show ID.</strong> Washington has no stop-and-identify statute for mere passengers. But if the officer has reasonable suspicion that the passenger committed a crime, that changes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I forgot my license at home."</strong> RCW 46.20.150 is a gross misdemeanor. You can be arrested and booked. But if you produce a valid license in court, the charge may be reduced or dismissed.</li>
<li><strong>"I do not answer questions."</strong> True for most questions, but 46.20.150 requires you to display your license. You cannot refuse that one.</li>
<li><strong>"You cannot search my car."</strong> Correct, but Washington has a broad plain-view doctrine. If the officer sees contraband, they do not need consent. Also, the smell of marijuana alone is not probable cause in Washington after legalization, but the officer may claim other factors.</li>
<li><strong>"I am driving to a safer location."</strong> No Washington statute gives you this right. The officer may interpret it as evading. Use your judgment, but know the law does not have your back on this.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"Washington is a two-party consent state so you cannot record the police." Wrong. The statute applies to private communications. Police performing public duties have no reasonable expectation of privacy. Record away.</li>
<li>"If the officer takes longer than 15 minutes, you can leave." There is no magic number. <em>Rodriguez</em> says "reasonably required to complete the mission."</li>
<li>"You have to let them search if they say they smell weed." Not anymore. Marijuana is legal in Washington. Smell alone is not probable cause. But if they claim to see contraband in plain view, that is different.</li>
<li>"If you do not roll down your window, they cannot ticket you." Wrong. You must display your license. Shoving it through a cracked window satisfies the statute but makes the officer suspicious, which is not illegal but is also not smart.</li>
</ul>
</div>
