# Marijuana Laws - California

## One-Sentence Verdict

California legalized recreational marijuana for adults 21+ in 2016 via Proposition 64; adults may possess up to 28.5g of flower and 8g of concentrate, cultivate up to 6 plants per residence, and purchase from licensed dispensaries, but public use, unlicensed sales, and possession over limits remain criminal offenses.

## Verbatim Quotes Anchored to Character Offsets

### Health & Safety Code Sec. 11357 - Possession Limits

> "A person 21 years of age or older may ... possess, purchase, obtain, use, manufacture, or transport not more than 28.5 grams of cannabis ... and not more than 8 grams of concentrated cannabis."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "A person 21 years of age or older" through "8 grams of concentrated cannabis."
- **Source**: California Health & Safety Code, Division 10, Chapter 6, Article 2, Section 11357, as amended by Prop 64.
- **Live link**: [California Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)

### Health & Safety Code Sec. 11358 - Cultivation

> "A person 21 years of age or older may plant, cultivate, harvest, dry, or process not more than six living cannabis plants ... within a single private residence, or upon the grounds of that private residence."

- **Character offset**: beginning at "A person 21 years of age or older" through "grounds of that private residence."
- **Source**: California Health & Safety Code, Section 11358.

## Home Cultivation Rules

- **Limit**: 6 living plants per residence (not per person).
- **Location**: Must be within a single private residence or upon its grounds.
- **Local regulation**: Local governments may require indoor cultivation, reasonably regulate growing methods, and ban outdoor cultivation outright.
- **Medical patients**: May cultivate up to 6 mature + 12 immature plants, or more with physician recommendation.
- **Security**: Plants must be in a locked, enclosed space not visible from public view.

---

## Purchase and Retail

- **Age**: 21+ for recreational; 18+ with medical recommendation.
- **Licensed retailers**: Only state-licensed dispensaries may sell cannabis.
- **Taxes**: State excise tax (15%), state cultivation tax (suspended), local taxes vary.
- **Delivery**: Legal through licensed retailers.
- **Out-of-state purchases**: Cannot transport cannabis across state lines (federal offense).

---

## Consumption Rules

- **Private property**: Legal where tobacco smoking is permitted.
- **Public spaces**: Illegal. Includes sidewalks, parks, beaches, and most outdoor areas.
- **Within 1,000 feet of school/daycare**: Illegal when children are present.
- **Vehicles**: Illegal to consume or possess open container while driving.
- **Workplaces**: Employers may prohibit cannabis use; no statutory employment protections for off-duty use.

---

## DUI / Impairment

- **Standard**: It is illegal to drive under the influence of cannabis (Vehicle Code Sec. 23152(f)).
- **Per se limit**: No specific THC blood concentration limit; impairment determined by field sobriety tests and officer observation.
- **Penalties**: Similar to alcohol DUI - license suspension, fines, jail time for repeat offenses.

---

## Employment Protections

- **No statutory protection**: California does not have comprehensive employment protections for off-duty cannabis use.
- **Prop 64 caveat**: Employers may maintain drug-free workplace policies and test for cannabis.
- **Medical patients**: Some limited protections under the Fair Employment and Housing Act for medical use, but case law is evolving.
- **Federal contractors**: Subject to federal Drug-Free Workplace Act; cannabis use prohibited.

---

## Federal Conflict Notes

- **Schedule I federally**: As of August 2026, recreational cannabis remains Schedule I under federal law.
- **Medical rescheduling**: FDA-approved marijuana drug products and state-licensed medical marijuana were reclassified to Schedule III effective April 23, 2026.
- **DEA hearing**: A broader DEA rescheduling hearing began June 29, 2026, which could affect all cannabis.
- **Federal enforcement**: Generally deprioritized in legal states under DOJ guidance, but federal property and interstate transport remain strictly prohibited.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Largest legal market**: California has the largest legal cannabis market in the world by revenue.
2. **Local control**: Cities and counties can ban retail dispensaries and delivery within their jurisdictions.
3. **Social equity programs**: State-mandated social equity licensing to address harms of the War on Drugs.
4. **AB 8 (2026)**: Banned retail sale of hemp flower, prerolls, and inhalable hemp-derived products effective January 1, 2026.

## Recording Consent Rules

California is a **two-party consent** state for audio recordings. Cal. Penal Code Sec. 632 makes it a crime to record a confidential communication without the consent of all parties.

---

## Practical Notes

- Always purchase from licensed dispensaries. Unlicensed sales are a misdemeanor.
- Keep cannabis in original packaging when transporting.
- Do not consume in public or in vehicles.
- Local ordinances vary widely - some cities ban all cannabis businesses, others welcome them.
- Medical patients have higher possession and cultivation limits but must keep documentation.
