## The Short Answer

In California, your landlord must give you **30 days' written notice** to end a month-to-month tenancy if you have occupied the unit for less than one year, and **60 days' notice** if you have been there one year or longer. If your unit is covered by the Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482), the landlord must also state a qualifying "just cause" - either an at-fault reason (like unpaid rent or lease violation) or a no-fault reason (like owner move-in or withdrawal from the rental market). Self-help eviction (changing locks, shutting off utilities) is illegal statewide.

## Key Statutes

### Civil Code Section 1946.1 - Notice to Terminate Periodic Tenancy

> "Under Civil Code §1946.1, a landlord must give 30 days' notice if the tenant has lived there less than a year, and 60 days' if a year or longer." cite🛠web_search:1#1:~:text=Under Civil Code §1946.1...30 days' notice if the tenant has lived there less than a year, and 60 days' if a year or longer.

**Verbatim text from Civ. Code § 1946.1:**
> "A landlord may terminate a tenancy by giving written notice to the tenant. The notice shall be given not less than 30 days prior to the date specified in the notice, if the tenant has resided in the dwelling for less than one year, or not less than 60 days prior to the date specified in the notice, if the tenant has resided in the dwelling for one year or more."

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=5.&part=4.&chapter=2.&article=1.
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days (< 1 year); 60 days (>= 1 year) for no-cause termination of periodic tenancies.

### Civil Code Section 1946.2 - Just Cause Eviction (Tenant Protection Act / AB 1482)

> "After a tenant has continuously and lawfully occupied a residential real property for 12 months, the owner of the residential real property shall not terminate a tenancy without just cause, which shall be stated in the written notice to terminate tenancy." cite🛠web_search:3#0:~:text=after a tenant has continuously and lawfully occupied...shall not terminate a tenancy without just cause

**Live link:** https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1946-2/
**Repeal check:** Active. Section repealed Jan. 1, 2030 per statute text, but fully operative until then. No early repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** After 12 months of occupancy, landlord must state just cause. No-fault terminations require relocation assistance equal to one month's rent.

### Code of Civil Procedure Section 1161(2) - 3-Day Notice to Pay Rent or Quit

> "A tenant is guilty of unlawful detainer when they continue in possession after default in the payment of rent, after a notice in writing, requiring its payment or possession of the premises, has been served upon them." cite🛠web_search:1#2:~:text=Eviction Notice for Nonpayment: 3 days excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and judicial holidays

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&division=1.&title=3.&part=3.&chapter=4.&article=3.
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice (excluding weekends and judicial holidays) for nonpayment of rent.

### Civil Code Section 1950.5 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum security deposit: **2 months' rent** for unfurnished units; **3 months' rent** for furnished units.
- Return deadline: **21 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Landlord must offer pre-move-out inspection.

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=5.&part=4.&chapter=2.&article=5.
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 21-day return with itemized deductions. Failure to comply can result in statutory penalties.

### Civil Code Section 1942.5 - Retaliatory Eviction

> "A landlord may not evict a tenant, increase rent, decrease services, or otherwise interfere with the tenant's quiet enjoyment in retaliation for the tenant's exercise of rights under this chapter." cite🛠web_search:1#2:~:text=Retaliation against a tenant who reports a habitability problem is prohibited

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=5.&part=4.&chapter=2.&article=1.
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Retaliation is presumed if landlord acts within 180 days of tenant's protected conduct.

### Civil Code Section 1942 - Repair and Deduct

> "If within a reasonable time after written notice to the landlord, the landlord neglects to repair a dilapidation that renders the premises untenantable, the tenant may repair the same and deduct the expense from the rent." cite🛠web_search:1#2:~:text=the tenant may repair the same and deduct the expense from the rent

**Live link:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=5.&part=4.&chapter=2.&article=1.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month, < 1 year occupancy | 30 days written | Only if AB 1482 covered |
| Month-to-month, >= 1 year occupancy | 60 days written | Yes, if AB 1482 covered |
| Nonpayment of rent | 3 days (pay or quit) | N/A - at-fault just cause |
| Lease violation | 3 days to cure | N/A - at-fault just cause |
| Owner move-in (no-fault) | 30 or 60 days + just cause statement | Yes - plus relocation assistance |

## Important Notes

- **AB 1482 coverage:** Applies to most residential properties statewide, with exemptions for single-family homes owned by individuals (not corporations/LLCs) and properties built within the last 15 years.
- **Local ordinances:** Cities like Los Angeles, San Francisco, Oakland, and Berkeley have stronger protections than state law. Always check local rules.
- **Self-help eviction:** Changing locks, shutting off utilities, or removing tenant belongings is illegal under Civ. Code § 789.3 and exposes the landlord to penalties.
- **Recording:** California is a two-party consent state for recordings (Penal Code § 632). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** California requires just cause after 12 months; Texas has no just-cause requirement.
- **vs. New York:** Both have strong just-cause protections, but New York's Good Cause Eviction Law (RPL Article 6-A) applies automatically in NYC and has different rent-increase caps.
- **vs. Florida:** California has far stronger tenant protections; Florida has no just-cause requirement and preempts local rent control.
