## The Short Answer

In Arkansas, your landlord must give you a **3-day written notice** to quit and demand for possession before filing an unlawful detainer action (ACA 18-60-304). Arkansas also has a unique **criminal failure-to-vacate** statute requiring a **10-day notice** (ACA 18-16-101). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (ACA 18-17-704). Arkansas has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### ACA 18-60-304 - 3-Day Notice to Quit (Unlawful Detainer)

> "A landlord may file an unlawful detainer action after giving the tenant a written three-day notice to quit and demand for possession. The notice must state that the tenant has three days to vacate or legal action will be commenced."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-18/subtitle-2/chapter-60/subchapter-3/section-18-60-304/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice to quit for unlawful detainer. No cure period for nonpayment under this statute.

### ACA 18-16-101 - 10-Day Criminal Failure-to-Vacate

> "If a tenant fails to pay rent and refuses to vacate after receiving a written ten-day notice, the tenant may be charged with criminal failure to vacate, a misdemeanor."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-18/subtitle-1/chapter-16/section-18-16-101/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 10-day notice under criminal failure-to-vacate statute. Unique to Arkansas. Tenant can avoid criminal charge by vacating within 10 days.

### ACA 18-17-701 - 5-Day Notice for Nonpayment (Residential Landlord-Tenant Act)

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may give the tenant a written notice that the rental agreement will terminate if rent is not paid within five days."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-18/subtitle-2/chapter-17/subchapter-7/section-18-17-701/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 5-day notice under RLTA for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 5 days.

### ACA 18-17-704 - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least 30 days before the termination date."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-18/subtitle-2/chapter-17/subchapter-7/section-18-17-704/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### ACA 18-17-901 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **2 months' rent**.
- Return deadline: **60 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide written notice of deposit terms at move-in.

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-18/subtitle-2/chapter-17/subchapter-9/section-18-17-901/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 60-day return. 2-month cap.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment (RLTA) | 5 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Unlawful detainer | 3 days to quit | N/A |
| Criminal failure-to-vacate | 10 days to vacate | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Arkansas landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Arkansas does not have statewide rent control.
- **Criminal failure-to-vacate:** Arkansas is one of the few states that criminalizes remaining after a 10-day notice for nonpayment. This is distinct from the civil unlawful detainer process.
- **Two paths:** Landlord can choose between civil unlawful detainer (3-day) or criminal failure-to-vacate (10-day).
- **Recording:** Arkansas is a one-party consent state for recordings (ACA 5-60-120). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Louisiana:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but AR has the unique criminal failure-to-vacate statute.
- **vs. Oklahoma:** OK has a 5-day notice for nonpayment similar to AR's RLTA provision.
- **vs. Texas:** TX has a 3-day notice for nonpayment; AR has both 3-day (unlawful detainer) and 5-day (RLTA) paths.
