# DUI / Implied Consent - Minnesota

## Verdict

Minnesota's implied consent law (Minn. Stat. § 169A.51) deems that any person driving on a public road has automatically agreed to chemical testing for alcohol or controlled substances if a law enforcement officer has grounds to suspect impairment. Refusal is treated as a **separate criminal offense** (gross misdemeanor for repeat/aggravated cases) in addition to administrative license revocation. A first-time refusal without aggravating factors can result in up to **90 days in jail, a $1,000 fine, and a one-year license revocation**. Minnesota uniquely criminalizes refusal as an independent offense, meaning a driver can be convicted of test refusal even if the underlying DWI is dismissed.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal Is a Separate Crime**: Minnesota uniquely treats chemical test refusal as an independent criminal offense under Minn. Stat. § 169A.20, subd. 2. A driver can be convicted of refusal even if acquitted of the underlying DWI.

2. **Implied Consent Advisory**: Officers must read an "Implied Consent Advisory" informing the driver that refusal is a crime, that Minnesota law requires submission to testing, and that there is a limited right to consult an attorney.

3. **Limited Right to Counsel**: Minnesota recognizes a limited right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit to testing, though this right is not absolute and must not unduly delay the testing process.

4. **Aggravating Factors**: Refusal is treated as an aggravating factor that can elevate penalties. Other aggravating factors include: BAC of .16 or higher, presence of a child under 16 (if more than 36 months younger than driver), and prior qualified impaired driving incidents within 10 years.

5. **Administrative and Criminal Tracks Run Separately**: Minnesota has two parallel processes: (1) the criminal DWI case in district court, and (2) the administrative implied consent action through the Department of Public Safety. Resolving one does not resolve the other.

6. **Seven-Day Temporary License**: After arrest, the driver receives a seven-day temporary license before revocation takes effect (ten days if notice is mailed).

## Practical Notes

- **Two Parallel Cases**: Every DWI arrest in Minnesota creates two separate legal matters: the criminal case and the administrative license action. A driver must address both independently.
- **60-Day Judicial Review Window**: Judicial review of an administrative revocation must be filed within 60 days of service of the notice of revocation.
- **Plate Impoundment and Vehicle Forfeiture**: In qualifying cases (e.g., BAC .16+, prior DWI, child in car), Minnesota may impose plate impoundment or even vehicle forfeiture in addition to license revocation.
- **Qualified Prior Impaired Driving Incident**: For penalty enhancement, Minnesota counts both prior convictions AND prior implied consent revocations or operating privilege losses within the preceding ten years.
- **Out-of-State Priors**: Out-of-state prior DWI convictions may count as qualified prior impaired driving incidents if they would have been violations under Minnesota law.
