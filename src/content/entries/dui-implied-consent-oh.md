<div class="quip">Ohio: where the Buckeyes are always in season and your license is suspended on the spot if you refuse to blow.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Ohio</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You already consented to chemical testing.</strong> Ohio Rev. Code 4511.191. By driving in Ohio, you are deemed to have consented to whole blood, blood serum/plasma, breath, or urine testing if lawfully arrested for OVI. The officer chooses which test.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal = Administrative License Suspension (ALS).</strong> Ohio Rev. Code 4511.191(G). The officer seizes your license immediately upon refusal. ALS lengths range from 90 days to 5 years depending on prior convictions and refusals.</li>
<li><strong>The officer must read the statutory warning.</strong> Ohio Rev. Code 4511.192. The officer must read a specific warning form informing you of the consequences of refusal and testing over the legal limit. Failure to read the warning properly can invalidate the refusal.</li>
<li><strong>Refusal is admissible in court.</strong> The prosecutor can use your refusal as evidence of consciousness of guilt.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the stop.</strong> Ohio is a one-party consent state. You may record your own interactions with police without informing them.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I want to speak to an attorney first."</strong> You have no right to an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing. The officer does not have to wait.</li>
<li><strong>"I'll take a blood test instead of breath."</strong> The officer chooses the test, not you. You can request blood, but the officer is not required to honor it.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer didn't read me the warning correctly."</strong> If the officer deviates substantially from the statutory warning form in 4511.192, the refusal may be invalid. This is a common defense at ALS hearings.</li>
<li><strong>"I wasn't driving, I was sleeping in the car."</strong> Ohio uses 'physical control' standard. If you have the keys and the ability to operate the vehicle, you can be arrested for OVI-related offenses.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"If you refuse, they have no case." Wrong. Refusal triggers immediate ALS AND can be used as evidence of guilt. The prosecutor can still convict on officer observations and field sobriety tests.</li>
<li>"They can't suspend your license without a hearing." Wrong. ALS is immediate. You have 30 days to request a hearing, but the suspension begins right away.</li>
<li>"The ALS only applies if you're convicted." Wrong. ALS is administrative and happens regardless of the criminal case outcome.</li>
<li>"You have 90 days to request an ALS hearing." Wrong. You have 30 days. Miss it and the suspension stands.</li>
</ul>
</div>
