# Homeschooling Laws - Nebraska

## Statutory Framework

Nebraska does not have a dedicated homeschool statute. Homeschools operate as **"exempt schools"** - private schools that elect not to meet state accreditation or approval requirements - under **Section 79-1601 R.R.S.** and **Rule 13**.

### Primary Instrument: Section 79-1601 R.R.S.

> "Parents, legal guardians, or educational decision makers may educate their child at home by electing not to meet State approval or accreditation requirements (Section 79-1601 R.R.S.). In Nebraska, 'home schools' are referred to as exempt schools and are considered non-approved and non-accredited private schools."
> - Source: [Nebraska Department of Education - Exempt School Program](https://www.education.ne.gov/fos/exempt-schools/) (2026-01-05)
> - Repeal check: Active. **LB1027 (2024)** removed the State Board's option to adopt rules for achievement testing and school visitations.

### Rule 13

Rule 13 governs the procedures and standards for filing for an exemption. As of 2024, the rule has been revised to reflect statutory changes from LB1027.

### Annual Filing

Parents must file two forms annually by **July 15**:
1. **Statement of Election and Assurances (Form A)**
2. **Parent Representative Form (Form B)**

> "Nebraska considers homeschools 'exempt schools' under Rule 13 - a private school framework with minimal oversight. Annual filing required by July 15 including Statement of Election and Parent Representative forms."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Nebraska](https://numaschool.com/states/nebraska/homeschool-laws) (2026-07-30)

**Key filing details:**
- Filed with the **Nebraska Department of Education (NDE)**
- Can be submitted online at https://exemptschool.education.ne.gov
- First-year filers must include a copy of the child's **birth certificate**
- The NDE notifies the resident public school district of the exemption

### 2024 Reforms (LB1027)

Significant deregulation took effect in 2024:
- Removed the State Board's authority to adopt rules for **achievement testing** of exempt school students
- Removed authority for **school visitations** (previously optional with parental consent)
- Eliminated requirement to report "instructional monitors" to NDE
- Eliminated requirement to submit **evidence of curricula** to NDE (replaced with written assurance of compliance)

> "Due to an opinion issued by the Nebraska Attorney General Robert Spire dated July 30, 1987, the State Board of Education chose not to test or visit private exempt schools. In 2024, LB1027 removed the option for state exempt school student testing or school visits."
> - Source: [NCHEA - State Requirements](https://nchea.org/homeschool-101/state-requirements/) (2024-11-05)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None formally required.** The parent must sign the Statement of Election affirming that the instructor has "basic skills" in the required subject areas. No degree, diploma, or certification is mandated.

> "The state of Nebraska doesn't set formal requirements for homeschool instructors. You must sign the Statement of Election and Assurances to confirm that you (or the homeschool instructor you choose) have basic skills in language arts, math, science, social studies, and health."
> - Source: [Student Discipline Defense - Homeschooling in Nebraska](https://www.studentdisciplinedefense.com/homeschooling-in-nebraska-rights-and-responsibilities)

### Required Subjects

Five subjects must be taught:
- Language arts
- Mathematics
- Science
- Social studies
- Health

### Instructional Time

- **1,032 hours per year** for elementary students
- **1,080 hours per year** for high school students

> "Parents are required to provide 1,032 hours of instruction each year for elementary school students and 1,080 hours for high school students."
> - Source: [Miacademy - Nebraska Homeschool Requirements](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/by-state/homeschooling-in-nebraska/) (2026-08-05)

### Testing Requirements

**None.** LB1027 (2024) explicitly removed all state testing authority over exempt schools.

### Record-Keeping

Not statutorily mandated, but maintaining records of instruction hours and subject coverage is recommended.

### Religious vs. Nonreligious Exempt Schools

When filing, parents indicate whether the basis for homeschooling is **religious** or **nonreligious**:
- **Nonreligious exempt schools:** Must comply with state vaccination requirements (medical exemption available)
- **Religious exempt schools:** Religious convictions against immunization are recognized

> "Religious exempt schools do not need to comply with immunization laws."
> - Source: [NCHEA - State Requirements](https://nchea.org/homeschool-101/state-requirements/)

### Opportunity Scholarships Act

Nebraska's **Opportunity Scholarships Act** provides ESA funding that homeschoolers can access - a significant shift for a state that historically offered no financial support for home education.

> "A significant recent change is funding. The Opportunity Scholarships Act provides ESA funds that Nebraska homeschoolers can access."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Nebraska](https://numaschool.com/states/nebraska/homeschool-laws)

### Public School Access

Homeschooled students have **unfunded access** to public school offerings like sports, courses, and extracurricular activities. Local districts set their own policies on part-time enrollment. Part-time students must typically be enrolled in at least five credit hours to play sports.

> "Homeschooled students have unfunded access to public school offerings like sports, courses, and extracurricular activities."
> - Source: [Johns Hopkins Homeschool Hub - Nebraska](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/states/nebraska/) (2026-01-06) (Authority: S)

### Special Situations

- **6-year-old exemption:** Parents may file an affidavit with the local district exempting a 6-year-old from compulsory attendance for that year
- **Early withdrawal (age 16+):** Students aged 16-17 may withdraw before graduation with an exit interview with the superintendent and proper forms signed with the Commissioner of Education
- **Completion before 18:** If a student completes graduation requirements before age 18, the parent must still notify NDE that instruction is complete

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 18.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal Classification** | Exempt school (non-accredited private school) |
| **Notification** | Annual filing by July 15 (Forms A and B) |
| **Testing** | None (LB1027 removed all testing authority) |
| **Qualifications** | None formal (affirmation of "basic skills") |
| **Required Subjects** | 5 subjects |
| **Instructional Time** | 1,032 (elem) / 1,080 (HS) hours |
| **Immunization** | Depends on religious/nonreligious designation |
| **Funding** | Opportunity Scholarships Act ESA |
| **2024 Reform** | Major deregulation via LB1027 |

### Verdict

Nebraska is a **low-regulation** state that has become increasingly homeschool-friendly. The 2024 LB1027 reforms eliminated the last vestiges of state oversight (testing and visitation authority), leaving only the annual filing and hour requirements. The "exempt school" framework provides a well-defined legal structure without intrusive oversight.
