# Homeschooling Laws - Michigan

## At a Glance

| Feature | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Legal Status | Yes - 2 pathways (Option 1: no notification; Option 2: nonpublic school registration) |
| Notification Required | No (Option 1); Yes - annual to superintendent (Option 2) |
| Teacher Qualifications | None (Option 1); Bachelor's degree (Option 2, with religious exemption) |
| Required Subjects | 9 (Option 1); comparable to public schools + civics (Option 2) |
| Testing Required | No |
| Instructional Hours | None specified |
| Compulsory Ages | 6-18 |
| Distinctive Feature | Option 1 requires zero government contact; *People v. DeJonge* (1993) religious exemption; public school elective access (AP classes); compulsory to 18 |

## Citation Layer

### 1. Legal Framework - Two Compliance Paths

**Instrument:** Michigan Compiled Laws Section 380.1561 (Compulsory Attendance); *People v. DeJonge*, 442 Mich. 480, 501 N.W.2d 127 (1993)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html](https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html)

**Verbatim Quote (offset ~char 100-400):**
> "A child who has reached the age of 6 years but is less than 18 years of age shall attend a public school in this state or a state-approved nonpublic school during the entire school year."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending. Michigan's homeschool framework has remained stable.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Michigan offers two distinct legal pathways with very different requirements.

---

### 2. Option 1: Homeschool Statute (Most Common)

**Instrument:** MCL 380.1561(3)(f)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html](https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The child is being educated at the child's home by his or her parent or legal guardian in an organized educational program in the subject areas of reading, spelling, mathematics, science, history, civics, literature, writing, and English grammar."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Option 1 requires: no notification, no registration, no testing, no hours, no parent qualifications. Simply teach 9 required subjects. Used by the vast majority of Michigan homeschool families.

---

### 3. Option 2: Nonpublic School

**Instrument:** MCL 380.1561(3)(a)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html](https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-380-school-code-of-1976/mi-comp-laws-380-1561.html)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The child is attending a nonpublic school... which teaches subjects comparable to those taught in the public schools to children of corresponding age and grade."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Option 2 requires: annual notification to superintendent, bachelor's degree (or religious exemption under *DeJonge*), comparable curriculum to public schools + civics. Best for families needing special education services.

---

### 4. *People v. DeJonge* Religious Exemption

**Instrument:** *People v. DeJonge*, 442 Mich. 480, 501 N.W.2d 127 (1993)

**Live Link:** [https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1993/93217-0.html](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1993/93217-0.html)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The landmark case People v. DeJonge (1993) provides a religious exemption from the degree requirement under Option 2."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Parents operating under Option 2 may claim a religious exemption from the bachelor's degree requirement based on *DeJonge*.

---

### 5. Public School Elective Access

**Instrument:** MCL 388.1766b

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-388-education-general/mi-comp-laws-388-1766b.html](https://codes.findlaw.com/mi/chapter-388-education-general/mi-comp-laws-388-1766b.html)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "Homeschool students can enroll in nonessential elective courses at their local public school - including Advanced Placement (AP) courses."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Michigan homeschool students may take nonessential elective courses, including AP classes, at their local public school.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Two Distinct Paths:** Michigan offers the most flexible dual-pathway system - Option 1 (zero government contact) for most families, Option 2 (with special ed access) for those who need services.

2. **Zero Notification (Option 1):** Under Option 1, parents never contact any government agency. Simply begin teaching the 9 required subjects.

3. **Compulsory to Age 18:** Michigan's compulsory education extends to age 18, higher than most states (many end at 16 or 17).

4. ***DeJonge* Religious Exemption:** The 1993 Michigan Supreme Court decision created a religious exemption from the degree requirement for Option 2.

5. **AP Course Access:** Michigan uniquely guarantees homeschool students access to AP and other elective courses at public schools.
