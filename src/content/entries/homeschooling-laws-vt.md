# Homeschooling Laws - Vermont

## Statutory Framework

Vermont homeschools operate under **16 V.S.A. 166b** (Home Study Program), with significant amendments effective July 1, 2023, that simplified the enrollment process.

### Primary Instrument: 16 V.S.A. 166b

> "A parent or legal guardian shall send the Secretary notice of intent to enroll the parent's or legal guardian's child in a home study program."
> - Source: [Vermont Legislature - 16 V.S.A. 166b](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/003/00166B) (Authority: NA)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; major amendments effective July 1, 2023.

### 2023 Legislative Changes (H-0461)

Governor Scott signed H-0461 on June 14, 2023, significantly simplifying home study requirements:

> "On Wednesday, June 14, 2023, Governor Scott signed H-0461. Within this bill is language that updates the current Home Study program rules in 16 V.S.A. 166b. The changes in this bill are significant as the process for enrolling in home study is being simplified beginning on July 1, 2023."
> - Source: [Vermont Agency of Education - Home Study](https://education.vermont.gov/schools/school-operations/home-study) (Authority: NA)

**Key 2023 changes:**
- Enrollment/Notice of Intent forms must be submitted annually at least **10 business days prior** to the intended start date
- AOE must provide written acknowledgement within 10 business days (constitutes verification of enrollment)
- Parents must notify AOE in writing within 10 business days of withdrawing
- Families are **no longer required to submit** a Minimum Course of Study (MCOS) to AOE (attestation only)
- Families are **no longer required to submit** an End of Year Assessment to AOE (maintain records privately)
- Families must attest to tracking progress and maintaining documentation
- Families must attest to **175 days** of equivalent instruction per year
- All AOE Home Study hearing procedures have been removed

> "Families are no longer required to submit an MCOS to AOE. Families must attest on enrollment/Notice of Intent forms they are tracking their student's progress and must maintain documentation of that progress using some form of assessment. Families are no longer required to submit an End of Year Assessment to AOE."
> - Source: [Vermont Agency of Education - Home Study Updates Summer 2023](https://education.vermont.gov/schools/school-operations/home-study)

### Minimum Course of Study: 16 V.S.A. 906

Home study programs must provide a minimum course of study in:
- Basic communication (reading, writing, use of numbers)
- Citizenship, history, and government in Vermont and the United States
- Physical education and comprehensive health education
- English, American, and other literature
- The natural sciences
- The fine arts

> "Home study programs in Vermont must provide a minimum course of study in the following fields: basic communication, including reading, writing, and the use of numbers; citizenship, history, and government in Vermont and the United States; physical education and comprehensive health education; English, American, and other literature; the natural sciences; and the fine arts."
> - Source: [U.S. Department of Education - Vermont State Regulations](http://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools/vermont-state-regulations-of-private-and-home-schools) (Authority: S)

**Note:** Students 13 years or older are no longer required to report physical education, comprehensive health, and fine arts.

### Annual Assessment

While no longer submitted to AOE, families must maintain documentation of annual assessment using one of these methods:
- A standardized assessment administered by the school district
- A progress review conducted by a Vermont certified teacher
- A parent report of the school year with at least four samples of student work
- Grades obtained from an online school

> "Families in home study programs are now responsible for maintaining a copy of their End-of-Year Assessment (EOYA) for their student records."
> - Source: [SVHS - Homeschooling in Vermont](https://svhs.co/homeschooling-vermont-laws/) (2025-01-10)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Vermont statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for homeschool parents.

### Qualified Home Study Program

A "qualified home study program" is one that has, for two consecutive years, not been disallowed by a hearing officer, meets all requirements, and has enabled the student to progress commensurate with age and ability. Qualified programs do not have to submit the minimum course of study to the Agency of Education.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 6 through 16.

### Public School Access: 16 V.S.A. 563(24)

School boards must have a policy on integrating home study students through enrollment in courses, participation in co-curricular and extracurricular activities, and use of facilities. They enroll their own students first; if space is available, they will enroll home study students.

> "School boards are supposed to have a policy on how to integrate home study students through enrollment in courses, participation in co-curricular and extracurricular activities, and use of facilities."
> - Source: [Vermont Home Education Network - Laws](https://vhen.org/laws) (Authority: NA)

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual notice 10 business days before start; AOE acknowledgement within 10 days |
| **Testing** | Annual assessment (maintain privately; 4 options) |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | 6 fields (MCOS) - PE/health/fine arts waived at age 13+ |
| **Instructional Time** | 175 days equivalent (attestation) |
| **2023 Reform** | Major simplification - no MCOS/EOYA submission; hearings removed |
| **Public School Access** | Districts must have integration policy; space-available basis |
| **Qualified Program** | 2-year good standing = reduced requirements |

### Verdict

Vermont is a **low-to-moderate regulation** state that became significantly more homeschool-friendly with the 2023 H-0461 reforms. The shift from submission to attestation for MCOS and EOYA dramatically reduced the bureaucratic burden. The 6-field minimum course of study and 175-day equivalent remain, but the removal of hearing procedures and submission requirements places Vermont in a much more permissive position than before 2023.
