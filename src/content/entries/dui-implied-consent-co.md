# DUI / Implied Consent - Colorado

## Verdict

Colorado operates under an **"Express Consent"** law (formerly implied consent). By driving in Colorado, you automatically agree to submit to a chemical test (blood or breath) if a law enforcement officer has probable cause to arrest you for DUI or DWAI. Refusal triggers a **12-month license revocation** for a first offense, with escalating penalties for subsequent refusals. Blood tests preserve a sample for independent retesting; breath tests do not. A refusal can also lead to designation as a Persistent Drunk Driver (PDD), requiring Level II alcohol education, SR-22 insurance, and ignition interlock for two years after reinstatement.

## Blood vs. Breath Distinctions

| Factor | Blood Test | Breath Test |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Warrant Required | No (express consent) | No (express consent) |
| Sample Preservation | **YES** - Split sample available for independent retesting | **NO** - No sample preserved |
| Drug Detection | Yes (all intoxicants) | No (alcohol only) |
| Officer Discretion | Officer may choose based on circumstances | Officer may choose based on circumstances |
| Driver Choice | Driver may request blood if officer requests breath, and vice versa, but officer has final say | Same as blood |

## Distinctive State Features

1. **"Express Consent" vs. "Implied Consent"**: Colorado uniquely rebranded its law as "express consent" to emphasize that driving constitutes an affirmative agreement to testing, not merely a passive implication.

2. **Recantation Right**: A driver who initially refuses may reconsider and recant, but must do so in sufficient time to obtain a sample within **two hours** of driving. The officer is not required to wait.

3. **Persistent Drunk Driver (PDD) Designation**: Refusal can trigger PDD status, which mandates Level II alcohol education, SR-22 insurance, and ignition interlock for two years after reinstatement - even on a first offense.

4. **Administrative-Only Process**: Refusal consequences are handled entirely through the DMV administrative process, separate from criminal court. A driver can have their license revoked for refusal even if never convicted of DUI.

5. **Cannabis Inclusion**: Colorado's express consent law explicitly includes cannabis, prescription medications, and illegal substances - not just alcohol.

## Practical Notes

- **Roadside PBTs**: The express consent law does NOT apply to preliminary breath tests (PBTs) at the roadside before arrest. Drivers may refuse roadside testing without express consent penalties, though refusal may contribute to probable cause for arrest.
- **Hearing Process**: Drivers have the right to request a DMV hearing to challenge the revocation, but must act promptly (typically within 7 days of notice).
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: The express consent law applies to all drivers in Colorado, including out-of-state residents and those without a valid Colorado license.
- **Medical Exceptions**: Drivers who cannot physically provide a sample due to injury or medical condition may have a defense, but must typically provide documentation.
