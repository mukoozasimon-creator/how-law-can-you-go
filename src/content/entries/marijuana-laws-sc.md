# Marijuana Laws - South Carolina

## Verdict

Marijuana is illegal for recreational and medical purposes in South Carolina. The state has no functioning medical marijuana program. The only exception is Julian's Law (S. 1035, 2014), which permits patients with severe epilepsy to use low-THC/high-CBD oil with a physician's recommendation. Possession of any amount of marijuana remains a criminal offense. Legislative efforts to expand access (SB 53, HB 3804) have stalled in committee as of 2026.

## Medical Program

- **Status:** No medical marijuana program exists.
- **Julian's Law exception:** Patients with Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome, Dravet's Syndrome, or other severe forms of epilepsy may use CBD oil with a physician's written recommendation.
- **Product requirements:** Must contain no more than 0.9% THC and at least 15% CBD (or 98% CBD per some statutory interpretations).
- **No dispensary system:** Products must be obtained through clinical trials or out-of-state sources (federal legal risk applies).

## Home Cultivation

- **Status:** Illegal for all purposes.
- Cultivation of any cannabis plant is treated as manufacturing and carries felony penalties.

## DUI / Impairment

- South Carolina prohibits operating a motor vehicle under the influence of cannabis.
- DUI penalties mirror alcohol DUI under S.C. Code Section 56-5-2930.
- First offense: $400 fine, 48 hours to 30 days jail, license suspension 6 months.

## Employment Protections

- No employment protections for cannabis use exist.
- Employers may drug test and terminate for any cannabis use.
- Medical CBD use under Julian's Law does not create employment protections.

## Federal Conflict

- South Carolina law aligns with federal Schedule I classification.
- No state-federal conflict exists because state law does not authorize medical or recreational marijuana beyond the narrow CBD exception.
