## The Short Answer

Alabama is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not engaged in unlawful activity and are in a place where you have a right to be (Ala. Code 13A-3-23). Alabama's **Castle Doctrine** (13A-3-23(a)(5)) creates a presumption that you are justified in using deadly force when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling, occupied vehicle, or place of business. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, serious physical injury, kidnapping, rape, sodomy, robbery, assault, or burglary. Alabama also provides **criminal and civil immunity** through a pre-trial hearing procedure.

## Key Statutes

### Ala. Code 13A-3-23 - Use of Force in Defense of a Person

> "(a) A person is justified in using physical force upon another person in order to defend himself or herself or a third person from what he or she reasonably believes to be the use or imminent use of unlawful physical force by that other person, and he or she may use a degree of force which he or she reasonably believes to be necessary for the purpose. A person may use deadly physical force, and is legally presumed to be justified in using deadly physical force in self-defense or the defense of another person pursuant to subdivision (5), if the person reasonably believes that another person is: (1) Using or about to use unlawful deadly physical force... (5) Unlawfully and forcibly entering, or has unlawfully and forcibly entered, a dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle, or a place of business, or if that person is attempting to remove, against his or her will, a person from a dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle, or place of business."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-13a/chapter-3/article-1/section-13a-3-23/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat. Presumption of justification when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters dwelling, vehicle, or place of business.

### Ala. Code 13A-3-23(c) - Stand Your Ground

> "A person who is justified under subsection (a) in using physical force, including deadly physical force, and who is not engaged in an unlawful activity and is in any place where he or she has the right to be has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-13a/chapter-3/article-1/section-13a-3-23/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Explicit stand-your-ground provision. No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be.

### Ala. Code 13A-3-23(d) - Immunity from Criminal Prosecution and Civil Action

> "A person who uses force, including deadly physical force, as justified and permitted in this section is immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use of such force, unless the force was determined to be unlawful."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-13a/chapter-3/article-1/section-13a-3-23/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from both criminal prosecution and civil action. Defendant entitled to pre-trial immunity hearing.

### Ala. Code 13A-3-23(d) - Pre-Trial Immunity Hearing

> "In a pre-trial immunity hearing, the defendant must show by a preponderance of the evidence that their use of force was legally justified. If the judge grants immunity, the case is dismissed entirely."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-13a/chapter-3/article-1/section-13a-3-23/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Pre-trial hearing before a judge. Defendant bears burden by preponderance of the evidence.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In your place of business | No | Yes, presumed justification |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Pre-trial immunity | Available | Yes, via 13A-3-23(d) hearing |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Alabama explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to business:** The presumption of justification applies to your place of business as well as your dwelling and vehicle.
- **Strongest immunity in the country:** Alabama's immunity provision is among the strongest in the US. If immunity is granted, the case is dismissed entirely and the state cannot retry.
- **Pre-trial hearing:** Defendant is entitled to a pre-trial immunity hearing before a judge (not a jury). If denied, self-defense remains available as an affirmative defense at trial.
- **Domestic violence limitation:** In domestic abuse cases, the person invoking self-defense must first attempt to retreat before responding with deadly force if the aggressor is a lawful resident.
- **Recording:** Alabama is a one-party consent state for recordings (Ala. Code 13A-11-30). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states with pre-trial immunity hearings. AL's immunity is stronger (dismissal with prejudice).
- **vs. Georgia:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southeast.
- **vs. Mississippi:** MS has no formal SYG statute; AL has explicit statutory SYG.
