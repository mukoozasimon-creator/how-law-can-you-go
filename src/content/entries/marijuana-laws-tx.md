# Marijuana Laws - Texas

## One-Sentence Verdict

Recreational marijuana remains fully illegal in Texas; possession of any amount is a criminal offense, with possession of 2 oz or less a misdemeanor punishable by up to 180 days in jail and a $2,000 fine, while the only legal cannabis is low-THC medical oil for registered patients and hemp-derived products at or below 0.3% Delta-9 THC.

### Texas Health & Safety Code Sec. 481.121 - Possession Penalties

> "(a) Except as authorized by this chapter, a person commits an offense if the person knowingly or intentionally possesses a usable quantity of marihuana. (b) An offense under Subsection (a) is: (1) a Class B misdemeanor if the amount of marihuana possessed is two ounces or less; (2) a Class A misdemeanor if the amount of marihuana possessed is four ounces or less but more than two ounces; (3) a state jail felony if the amount of marihuana possessed is five pounds or less but more than four ounces;"

## Medical Program

- **Program name**: Texas Compassionate Use Program (CUP).
- **Qualifying conditions**: Originally intractable epilepsy only; expanded to include PTSD, cancer, autism, ALS, MS, seizure disorders, incurable neurodegenerative disease, and spasticity.
- **Product type**: Low-THC cannabis oil only (no flower, no edibles, no smoking).
- **THC cap**: 1% by weight.
- **Registration**: Physician must register with the program and issue a prescription.
- **Dispensaries**: Licensed by the Department of Public Safety; limited number.
- **No home cultivation**: Patients cannot grow their own medicine.

---

## Hemp and CBD

- **Hemp**: Legal under Texas Agriculture Code Ch. 121 (2019 Hemp Farming Act).
- **Delta-9 THC limit**: 0.3% by dry weight.
- **Delta-8, Delta-10, THCP**: Banned as of July 31, 2026. Texas Supreme Court ruled DSHS could classify these as controlled substances. Possession is now a felony.
- **Hemp vapes/e-cigarettes**: Banned under SB 2024. Selling any vape containing cannabinoids is a Class A misdemeanor.
- **Smokable hemp flower**: No longer protected after June 2026 appellate ruling.

---

## Local Decriminalization

Several Texas cities have passed "cite and release" or civil penalty ordinances for small amounts:
- Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, El Paso, and others have deprioritized enforcement.
- However, local ordinances do NOT override state law. State troopers and sheriff's deputies can still arrest under state statute.
- Officer discretion to arrest remains.

---

## DUI / Impairment

- **Standard**: It is illegal to operate a motor vehicle while intoxicated by any substance, including marijuana (Texas Penal Code Sec. 49.04).
- **Per se limit**: No specific THC blood limit.
- **Penalties**: First offense DWI is a Class B misdemeanor (up to 180 days jail, $2,000 fine). Enhanced penalties for repeat offenses and high BAC (or drug equivalent).

---

## Employment Protections

- **No statutory protection**: Texas does not protect off-duty cannabis use.
- **At-will employment**: Employers may terminate for cannabis use, including medical use.
- **Federal contractors**: Subject to federal Drug-Free Workplace Act.

---

## Federal Conflict Notes

- **Schedule I federally**: All marijuana remains Schedule I under federal law.
- **Medical rescheduling**: Limited to FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical programs; Texas CUP may qualify.
- **Federal enforcement**: Active on federal property and for interstate transport.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Harsh concentrate penalties**: Texas treats concentrates as felonies at ANY amount, unlike many states that treat small amounts as misdemeanors.
2. **Limited medical program**: One of the most restrictive medical programs in the nation - no flower, no smoking, 1% THC cap.
3. **Delta-8 ban (2026)**: Texas aggressively banned hemp-derived intoxicating cannabinoids.
4. **No home cultivation**: Even medical patients cannot grow their own medicine.

## Recording Consent Rules

Texas is a **one-party consent** state for audio recordings. Texas Penal Code Sec. 16.02 makes it a felony to intercept a wire or oral communication without the consent of at least one party.

---

## Practical Notes

- Even a single joint can result in arrest and jail time in Texas.
- Concentrates (vape pens, edibles, wax) are treated far more harshly than flower.
- Do not bring cannabis from legal states into Texas - this is a felony.
- If you have a medical card from another state, it is NOT recognized in Texas.
- Local decriminalization is not a guarantee - state law enforcement can still arrest.
