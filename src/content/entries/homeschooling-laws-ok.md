## Summary

Oklahoma is the only state in the United States with homeschooling protected in its constitution. Article 13, Section 4 of the Oklahoma Constitution explicitly preserves parents' right to provide education through "other means." In practical terms, this means Oklahoma has virtually no homeschool requirements: no notification, no registration, no parent qualifications, no standardized testing, no curriculum approval, and no minimum instructional hours. The only legal standard is that instruction should be "in good faith and equivalent" to public school education. Oklahoma also offers a $1,000 per-student Parental Choice Tax Credit for qualified homeschool expenses.

## Legal Framework

### Constitutional Protection

**Oklahoma Constitution, Article 13, Section 4**

> "The Legislature shall provide for the compulsory attendance at some public or other school, unless other means of education are provided, of all the children in the State who are sound in mind and body, between the ages of eight and sixteen years, for at least three months in each year."

- **Instrument**: Oklahoma Constitution, Art. 13, § 4
- **Live Link**: https://www.oklegislature.gov/constitution/#article13
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; constitutional amendment would be required
- **Verdict**: Oklahoma is the **only state** with constitutional protection for homeschooling as "other means of education."

### Primary Statute

**70 O.S. § 10-105** - Attendance compulsory - Age limits - Exemptions

> Establishes compulsory attendance for children ages 5-18 and provides exemptions for children receiving "other means of education."

- **Instrument**: 70 O.S. § 10-105
- **Live Link**: https://www.oklegislature.gov/osstatuestitle.html
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Homeschooling is permitted as "other means of education" under constitutional and statutory authority.

## Notification

**No notification is required.** Oklahoma is a "no notice" state. Parents do not need to register with or notify the state, local school district, or any other authority. There is no form to file and no approval to seek.

**Practical Note**: If withdrawing a child from public school, sending a formal withdrawal letter to the school is recommended to prevent truancy confusion, though it is not legally required.

## Required Subjects

No subjects are legally mandated for homeschoolers. However, the "equivalency" standard suggests covering the subjects taught in Oklahoma public schools under 70 O.S. § 11-103:

- Reading and writing
- Mathematics
- Science
- Citizenship
- United States Constitution
- Health and safety
- Physical education
- Conservation

## Assessment & Testing

No standardized testing or assessments are required. Homeschoolers are explicitly exempt from all state testing mandates.

## Record-Keeping

No records are required by law. However, maintaining documentation is strongly recommended:
- Attendance logs
- Curriculum documentation
- Work samples
- Progress notes

These records protect families in custody disputes, support college applications, and provide evidence of education if ever questioned.

## Special Provisions

- **Parental Choice Tax Credit**: Up to **$1,000 per student** for qualified homeschool expenses (curriculum, textbooks, instructional materials, tutoring, etc.). Claimed directly on the state tax return.
- **Constitutional Protection**: Article 13, Section 4 makes homeschooling a constitutionally protected right, not merely a legal option
- **No Inspections**: Public school officials cannot visit or inspect home educational programs
- **No Immunization Requirements**: Homeschoolers are not subject to school immunization mandates
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Driver's License**: No specific homeschool provisions

## Distinctive Features

- **Only state with constitutional protection** for homeschooling
- **No notice, no registration, no approval** - the least restrictive framework in the U.S.
- No parent qualifications, no testing, no curriculum approval, no minimum hours
- $1,000 per-student tax credit for homeschool expenses
- "In good faith and equivalent" is the sole legal standard
- No home visits or inspections permitted
- No immunization requirements for homeschoolers
- Strongest legal protections of any state
