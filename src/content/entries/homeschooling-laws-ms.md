# Homeschooling Laws - Mississippi

## Statutory Framework

Mississippi recognizes "home study programs" as a legitimate exemption from compulsory attendance under **Miss. Code Ann. § 37-13-91**.

### Primary Instrument: Miss. Code Ann. § 37-13-91

> "A legitimate home instruction program" qualifies for exemption from Mississippi's compulsory attendance requirements.
> - Source: [Blue Folder - Mississippi Homeschool Requirements & Laws](https://bluefolder.app/states/mississippi) (2026)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

### Certificate of Enrollment

Parents must file an annual **Certificate of Enrollment** with the local school attendance officer.

> "Mississippi is a low regulation state - file a certificate of enrollment and you're done with state requirements. Annual filing by September 15 to your local school attendance officer - no approval needed."
> - Source: [Numa School - Mississippi Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/mississippi/homeschool-laws) (2026-07-30)

**Key details:**
- Filed annually by **September 15**
- Submitted to the local school attendance officer
- No approval is required; the filing itself satisfies the legal obligation
- No specific form mandated by statute; most districts provide a standard certificate

### Good Faith Requirement

The only substantive restriction is that the home study program cannot be "operated or instituted for the purpose of avoiding or circumventing the compulsory attendance law." As long as the parent is genuinely educating the child in good faith, Mississippi imposes no further oversight.

> "Mississippi's only meaningful restriction is that home study programs cannot be 'operated or instituted for the purpose of avoiding or circumventing the compulsory attendance law.'"
> - Source: [Numa School - Mississippi Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/mississippi/homeschool-laws)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** Mississippi statute does not require any degree, diploma, or teaching credential for homeschool parents.

### Testing Requirements

**None.** No standardized testing, portfolio review, or annual assessment is required.

> "No standardized testing, no required subjects, no mandatory instructional hours or days."
> - Source: [Numa School - Mississippi Homeschool Laws](https://numaschool.com/states/mississippi/homeschool-laws)

### Required Subjects

**None specified.** Mississippi does not mandate particular subjects or curriculum content.

### Instructional Time

No minimum hours or days are specified in statute.

### Record-Keeping

Not statutorily required. However, maintaining records of coursework, attendance, and academic progress is recommended for transcript purposes and potential future enrollment in public or private school.

> "Families aren't required to keep records of any kind, including attendance, academic records, portfolios, evaluations, or assessments."
> - Source: [ABC Mouse - Mississippi Homeschooling Laws](https://www.abcmouse.com/learn/homeschool/mississippi-homeschooling-laws/31092) (2024-10-04)

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Children must start school for the academic year when they turn 6 and before September 1st. They must attend until they turn **17**.

### Immunization

Mississippi law requires immunizations for public and private school students with only medical exemptions. The law does not address homeschoolers, so homeschool students are **not required** to be immunized.

> "Mississippi law requires all public and private school students to receive certain immunizations and offers only medical exemptions. However, the law does not address any required immunizations for homeschoolers."
> - Source: [ABC Mouse - Mississippi Homeschooling Laws](https://www.abcmouse.com/learn/homeschool/mississippi-homeschooling-laws/31092)

### ESA Funding

Limited ESA funding is available through the **Education Scholarship Account Program** primarily for students with special needs.

### Public School Access

Mississippi does not guarantee homeschoolers access to public school classes, sports, or extracurricular activities. District policies vary.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual Certificate of Enrollment by Sep 15 |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | None |
| **Instructional Time** | None specified |
| **Record-Keeping** | None required |

### Verdict

Mississippi is one of the **most deregulated** homeschool states in the nation. A single annual filing satisfies all state requirements. No testing, no subjects, no hours, no qualifications, and no record-keeping mandates give parents maximum autonomy. The "good faith" requirement is the only substantive limitation and is rarely enforced against genuine homeschoolers.
