## Summary

Washington operates a "trust but verify" model for homeschooling. Parents must file an annual Declaration of Intent, meet one of four parent qualification options, teach 11 required subjects, provide 1,000 hours of instruction (450 for kindergarten), and complete an annual assessment. The law explicitly states that home-based instruction requirements should be "construed liberally," recognizing that home education is "less structured and more experiential" than classroom instruction. Compulsory education does not begin until age 8.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Statute

**RCW 28A.200.010** - Home-based instruction

> "(1) All decisions relating to philosophy or doctrine, selection of books, teaching and curriculum materials, methodology, handling of controversial issues, except as for such curriculum requirements as are specifically provided for in this chapter, and other aspects of the educational program are the responsibility of the parent except for matters specifically referred to in this chapter. (2) The provisions of this chapter shall be construed liberally..."

- **Instrument**: RCW 28A.200.010
- **Live Link**: https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=28A.200.010
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Home-based instruction is legal with specific filing, qualification, subject, hour, and assessment requirements.

### Parent Qualification Statute

**RCW 28A.225.010(4)** - Compulsory school attendance

> "A parent who intends to have his or her child receive home-based instruction... shall file annually a signed declaration of intent..."

- **Instrument**: RCW 28A.225.010(4)
- **Live Link**: https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=28A.225.010
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026
- **Verdict**: Establishes parent qualification options and Declaration of Intent requirements.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Notification** | Annual Declaration of Intent by September 15 |
| **Teacher Qualifications** | Must meet 1 of 4 options (see below) |
| **Required Subjects** | 11 subjects (see below) |
| **Testing/Assessment** | Annual standardized test or certificated teacher evaluation |
| **Attendance** | 1,000 hours/year (grades 1-12); 450 hours (K) |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 8-17 |

### Parent Qualification Options (RCW 28A.225.010(4))

Parents must meet **one** of the following:

1. **45 college quarter credits** (or equivalent semester credits)
2. **Parent-qualifying course** at a Washington community college, vocational-technical institute, or approved provider
3. **Certificated-teacher supervision** - supervised by a Washington-certificated teacher with at least one contact hour per week
4. **Superintendent approval** - deemed sufficiently qualified by the local school district superintendent

## Notification

**Annual Declaration of Intent** must be filed with the resident school district superintendent by **September 15** each year, or within two weeks of the beginning of any public school quarter, trimester, or semester. The declaration must include:

- Child's name and age
- Parent's name and address
- Statement that parent meets one of the four qualifications
- If using certificated-teacher supervision, identify the supervising teacher

**Important**: No filing is required before a child turns 8.

## Required Subjects (11)

Under RCW 28A.200.020, home-based instruction must include:

1. Reading
2. Writing
3. Spelling
4. Language
5. Math
6. Science
7. Social Studies
8. History
9. Health
10. Occupational Education
11. Art and Music Appreciation

The statute explicitly states these requirements should be "construed liberally" and that home education is "less structured and more experiential."

## Assessment & Testing

Annual assessment is required for any child age 8 through 18. Parents must choose **one** of:

1. **Standardized achievement test** - state Board of Education-approved, administered by a qualified individual
2. **Written assessment** - by a Washington-certificated person currently working in education

**Test results are private** and are not reported to the state or district. They must be kept in the parent's files.

## Record-Keeping

Parents must keep:
- Declaration of Intent filings
- Annual assessment/test results
- Immunization records
- Any other records relating to instructional and educational activities

Records must be forwarded to any school to which the child transfers.

## Special Provisions

- **Age 8 Exemption**: Children under 8 are not subject to compulsory education law; no paperwork required
- **Transfer Students**: When transferring to public school, the superintendent may require a standardized achievement test and has authority to determine appropriate grade/course placement
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **ALE Programs**: Alternative Learning Experience (RCW 28A.232) and Parent Partnership Programs provide publicly-funded pathways that do not require Declaration of Intent
- **Funding**: No statewide homeschool ESA or tax credit; no state income tax

## Distinctive Features

- Compulsory education begins at **age 8** (one of the latest in the country)
- Requires **parent qualifications** (one of only ~10 states)
- 11 required subjects (above average)
- 1,000-hour annual instruction requirement
- "Construed liberally" statutory language protects flexible teaching methods
- Test results remain **private** - not reported to authorities
