# DUI / Implied Consent - Nebraska

## Verdict

Nebraska's implied consent law (Neb. Rev. Stat. 60-6,197 et seq.) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are driving under the influence. A first refusal results in a **1-year license revocation**. Nebraska uniquely advises drivers that **refusal is a separate crime** for which they may be charged. Drivers receive a **15-day temporary permit** after refusal and may apply for an **Ignition Interlock Permit (IIP)** during the revocation period. Refusal is admissible as evidence in criminal proceedings. Nebraska does NOT grant a right to consult an attorney before testing.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal Is a "Separate Crime" Advisory**: Nebraska uniquely requires officers to advise drivers that refusal to submit to testing "is a separate crime for which the person may be charged." While the actual criminal charge would be for the underlying DUI, this advisory creates significant pressure to submit.

2. **Ignition Interlock Permit (IIP)**: Nebraska offers an IIP that allows revoked drivers to continue driving during the revocation period as long as they install an IID. This is available for both refusal and failure cases.

3. **15-Day Temporary Permit**: Upon refusal, the officer confiscates the license and issues a 15-day temporary permit, giving the driver a short window to challenge the revocation.

4. **No Assistance for Independent Test**: While drivers have the right to an independent test at their own expense, Nebraska law explicitly states that law enforcement "may not hinder said request, but in no way needs to assist or facilitate in obtaining the tests."

5. **12-Year Lookback for Criminal Penalties**: Nebraska uses a 12-year lookback period for criminal DUI penalty enhancements.

6. **Administrative Hearing**: Drivers may request an administrative hearing to challenge the revocation. The hearing must be requested within the temporary permit period.

7. **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face license impoundment of 30-90 days for .02+ BAC or refusal.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative and Criminal Proceedings Separate**: The license revocation for refusal is administrative and proceeds independently of the criminal DUI case.
- **IIP Application**: Drivers should apply for the Ignition Interlock Permit promptly if they wish to maintain driving privileges during revocation.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Nebraska's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Nebraska roads. Out-of-state drivers face revocation of their Nebraska operating privilege.
- **No Plea Bargaining Statute**: Nebraska does not have a statutory restriction on plea bargaining for DUI cases.
- **SR-22 Insurance**: Typically required for reinstatement after DUI-related suspension or revocation.
