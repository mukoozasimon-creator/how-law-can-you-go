## The Short Answer

Kentucky is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you have a right to be (KRS 503.055(3)). Kentucky's **Castle Doctrine** (KRS 503.055(1)) creates a presumption that you held a reasonable fear of imminent death or serious physical injury when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your residence or vehicle. Kentucky also provides **criminal immunity** through a pre-trial hearing procedure (KRS 503.085). The prosecutor must overcome the presumption with clear and convincing evidence.

## Key Statutes

### KRS 503.050 - Use of Physical Force in Self-Protection

> "(4) A person who is not engaged in an unlawful activity and who is attacked in any place where he or she has a right to be has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground and meet force with force, including deadly force, if he or she reasonably believes it is necessary to do so to prevent death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another or to prevent the commission of a felony involving the use of force."

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=43793
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be. Deadly force justified against imminent death, great bodily harm, or forcible felony.

### KRS 503.055 - Castle Doctrine Presumption

> "(1) A person is presumed to hold a reasonable fear of imminent peril of death or great bodily harm to himself or herself or another when using defensive force that is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily harm to another if: (a) The person against whom the defensive force was used was in the process of unlawfully and forcibly entering or had unlawfully and forcibly entered a dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle, or if that person had removed or was attempting to remove another against that person's will from the dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle; and (b) The person who uses defensive force knew or had reason to believe that an unlawful and forcible entry or unlawful and forcible act was occurring or had occurred."

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=43794
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters residence or vehicle.

### KRS 503.085 - Immunity from Criminal Prosecution and Civil Action

> "(1) A person who uses force as permitted in KRS 503.050, 503.055, 503.070, 503.080, or 503.090 is justified in using such force and is immune from criminal prosecution and civil action for the use of such force, unless the person against whom the force was used is a peace officer... (2) A law enforcement agency may use standard procedures for investigating the use of force as described in subsection (1) of this section, but the agency may not arrest the person for using force unless it determines that there is probable cause that the force that was used was unlawful."

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=43798
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Immunity from criminal prosecution and civil action. Law enforcement cannot arrest without probable cause that force was unlawful.

### KRS 503.085 - Pre-Trial Immunity Hearing

> "Upon proper request, the trial court must make a pre-trial determination of whether the defendant acted in self-defense, and if so, dismiss the charge under the statutorily granted immunity."

**Live link:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=43798
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Pre-trial immunity hearing required upon request. Court must determine if defendant acted in self-defense.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your residence (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Pre-trial immunity | Available | Yes, via KRS 503.085 hearing |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Kentucky explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine requires "forcible" entry:** The presumption only applies when the entry is both unlawful AND forcible. A non-forcible entry (e.g., walking through an unlocked door without force) does not trigger the presumption (Commonwealth v. Wimsett, 2026).
- **Immunity with arrest limitation:** Kentucky uniquely limits law enforcement's ability to arrest someone who claims self-defense. Police must determine there is probable cause that the force was unlawful before making an arrest.
- **Pre-trial hearing:** Upon proper request, the trial court must hold a pre-trial hearing to determine if the defendant acted in self-defense. If so, the charge must be dismissed.
- **Recording:** Kentucky is a one-party consent state for recordings (KRS 526.010). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.
