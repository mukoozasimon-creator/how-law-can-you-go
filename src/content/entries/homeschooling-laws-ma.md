## Summary

Massachusetts is one of the highest-regulation states for homeschooling. Unlike most states where notification suffices, Massachusetts requires **prior approval** from the local school committee or superintendent before a family may legally begin homeschooling. This requirement traces back to the 1987 court case *Care and Protection of Charles*. While approval cannot be "unreasonably withheld," families must submit an education plan demonstrating that their program will provide instruction "equal to that provided in the public schools" in the required subjects. Requirements vary by district.

## Legal Framework

### Primary Regulation

**603 CMR 8.00** - Approval of Private Schools; Home Education

> "The school committee or superintendent shall approve a home education proposal if the parent or guardian can demonstrate that the home education program will provide an education that is equal in thoroughness and efficiency to that provided in the public schools."

- **Instrument**: 603 CMR 8.00
- **Live Link**: https://www.mass.gov/regulations/603-CMR-800
- **Repeal Check**: Active as of 2026; no repeal legislation pending
- **Verdict**: Prior approval is required. The standard is "equal in thoroughness and efficiency," not identical to public school.

### Key Case Law

**Care and Protection of Charles**, 399 Mass. 324 (1987)
- Established that homeschools are classified as private schools
- Required prior approval from school committee or superintendent
- Held that "equal" does not mean "identical" - homeschool programs need not mirror public school curriculum, methods, or schedule
- Established that approval cannot be unreasonably withheld

## Notification / Approval Process

**Step 1**: Submit a letter of intent / education plan to the local school committee or superintendent **before** beginning home instruction.

**Step 2**: The plan must demonstrate that the program will provide instruction "equal in thoroughness and efficiency to that provided in the public schools."

**Step 3**: If approved, proceed with homeschooling. If denied, you may appeal or revise and resubmit.

**Annual Verification**: Parents must annually verify with the local superintendent whether their child will continue to be homeschooled.

## Required Subjects

Under 603 CMR 8.00 and the *Charles* framework, instruction must be provided in:

1. Reading
2. Writing
3. English Language Arts
4. Mathematics
5. Science
6. History
7. Geography
8. Civics
9. Physical Education
10. Art
11. Music
12. Foreign Language (at the discretion of the school committee)

Note: The specific list may vary slightly by district interpretation.

## Assessment & Testing

- Districts **may** require periodic evaluations or standardized testing
- Some districts require annual portfolio reviews
- Requirements vary significantly by district

## Record-Keeping

Parents should maintain:
- Education plan and approval documentation
- Curriculum records and materials used
- Portfolio of student work samples
- Assessment/evaluation results (if required by district)
- Attendance records

## Special Provisions

- **District Variation**: Requirements vary significantly by district. Some districts are very supportive; others are more demanding
- **Umbrella Schools**: Families may homeschool under the supervision of an approved nonpublic school or bona fide church organization, which may reduce district oversight
- **Sports/Activities**: No statutory right to public school extracurricular participation
- **Funding**: No state-level financial programs for homeschoolers

## Distinctive Features

- One of only a handful of states requiring **prior approval**
- Legal framework established by court case (*Care and Protection of Charles*, 1987) rather than statute
- Requirements vary significantly by district
- "Equal in thoroughness and efficiency" standard (not identical)
- Approval cannot be unreasonably withheld
- Strong homeschool networks exist to help families navigate district requirements
