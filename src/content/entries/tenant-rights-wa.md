## The Short Answer

In Washington, your landlord must have **just cause** to evict or refuse to renew your tenancy (RCW 59.18.650). Washington is a **statewide just-cause state** with 18 enumerated grounds. For nonpayment, the landlord must serve a **14-day pay-or-vacate notice** in the statutory form (RCW 59.12.030(3)). For lease violations, a **10-day comply-or-vacate notice** applies. For waste, nuisance, or criminal activity, a **3-day notice to quit** applies. Owner move-in and sale of single-family residence require **90 days' advance notice**. Some no-fault grounds require **relocation assistance**. Self-help eviction is illegal and exposes the landlord to damages of **3x monthly rent** plus attorney's fees.

> "Under RCW 59.18.650, landlords must have one of 18 enumerated just causes to evict a tenant or refuse to renew a lease. ### RCW 59.12.030(3) - 14-Day Pay-or-Vacate Notice

> "For nonpayment in a chapter 59.18 RCW tenancy, RCW 59.12.030(3) requires a written notice that, in the alternative, demands payment of the rent or surrender of the premises, and the tenant has 14 days after service to comply." cite🛠web_search:27#4:~:text=For nonpayment in a chapter 59.18 RCW tenancy, RCW 59.12.030(3) requires a written notice that, in the alternative, demands payment of the rent or surrender of the premises, and the tenant has 14 days after service to comply.

### RCW 59.18.650 - Notice Periods by Ground

| Ground | Notice Period |
|--------|--------------|
| Nonpayment of rent | 14 days (pay or vacate) |
| Substantial breach of material lease term | 10 days (comply or vacate) |
| Waste, nuisance, criminal activity | 3 days (notice to quit) |
| Owner or family move-in | 90 days |
| Sale of single-family residence | 90 days |
| Substantial rehabilitation | 90 days |
| Other good cause | Varies |

### RCW 59.18.240 / 59.18.250 - Retaliation Prohibited

### RCW 59.18.650 - Wrongful Eviction Damages

> "A landlord who removes a tenant, or causes a tenant to be removed, in any way in violation of the section is liable to the tenant for wrongful eviction. A tenant who prevails is entitled to the greater of their economic and noneconomic damages or three times the monthly rent of the dwelling at issue, plus reasonable attorneys' fees and court costs." cite🛠web_search:27#4:~:text=A landlord who removes a tenant, or causes a tenant to be removed, in any way in violation of the section is liable to the tenant for wrongful eviction. ## Important Notes

- **Statewide just cause:** RCW 59.18.650 applies statewide. Local ordinances (like Seattle's) can add stronger protections but cannot weaken the state floor.
- **Relocation assistance:** Required for certain no-fault terminations (owner move-in, sale, substantial rehabilitation). Amount varies by jurisdiction.
- **Rent increase notice:** 30 days for increases under 10%; 60 days for 10% or more in a 12-month period. Seattle may require up to 180 days.
- **Security deposits:** No statutory cap. Return deadline: 21 days with itemized statement.
- **Recording:** Washington is a two-party consent state for recordings (RCW 9.73.030). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Both are statewide just-cause states with similar frameworks. WA has statutory form requirements for notices; CA does not.
- **vs. Oregon:** Both are just-cause states in the Pacific Northwest with similar protections.
- **vs. Texas:** WA is far more tenant-friendly with just-cause requirements and relocation assistance.
