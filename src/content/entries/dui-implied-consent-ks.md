# DUI / Implied Consent - Kansas

## Verdict

Kansas's implied consent law (K.S.A. 8-1001 et seq.) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are driving under the influence. A first refusal results in a **1-year license suspension** followed by **2 years of ignition interlock device (IID) restriction**. Each subsequent refusal adds another year to the IID period (3 years for 2nd, 4 years for 3rd, 5 years for 4th, and 10 years for 5th). Drivers are eligible for a **limited restricted driving permit with IID after 90 days** of suspension. Kansas uniquely grants drivers the right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit, though the consultation must not unreasonably delay the test. Refusal is admissible as evidence in court.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Escalating IID Periods**: Kansas uniquely extends the ignition interlock restriction period with each subsequent refusal, reaching up to 10 years for a fifth occurrence. The suspension period itself remains 1 year, but the IID restriction grows dramatically.

2. **Right to Consult Attorney**: Kansas is one of the few states that explicitly grants drivers the right to consult with an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing, provided the consultation does not unreasonably delay the test.

3. **Limited Restricted Permit After 90 Days**: Even on a refusal, drivers may apply for a limited restricted driving permit for work purposes with an IID installed after serving 90 days of the suspension.

4. **Lifetime Administrative Lookback**: The Kansas Department of Revenue goes back lifetime on administrative driver's license actions, meaning any prior refusal or failure counts regardless of how long ago it occurred.

5. **Inadequate Sample = Refusal**: Kansas explicitly states that failure to provide an adequate breath sample constitutes a refusal unless the driver can prove the failure was due to a medical condition unrelated to alcohol or drugs.

6. **Refusal Admissible in Criminal Trial**: Under K.S.A. 8-1001(n), refusal is explicitly admissible as evidence against the driver at any trial on a DUI charge.

7. **Officer May Obtain Warrant After Refusal**: If a driver refuses, the officer may obtain a warrant to authorize conducting the test by force if necessary.

## Practical Notes

- **Two Tracks**: Kansas has separate administrative (license) and criminal (DUI) proceedings. The administrative suspension proceeds regardless of the criminal case outcome.
- **14-Day Hearing Request**: Drivers have 14 days from imposition of suspension to file an administrative appeal.
- **Diversion Program**: Kansas has a diversion program for first-time offenders that can result in dismissal upon completion, but diversion counts as a prior for administrative purposes.
- **CDL Disqualification**: CDL holders face 1-year disqualification for a first refusal and lifetime disqualification for a second.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face license impoundment for 30-90 days with a .02+ BAC.
