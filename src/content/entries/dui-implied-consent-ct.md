# DUI / Implied Consent - Connecticut

## Verdict

Connecticut's implied consent law (C.G.S. § 14-227b) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state is presumed to have given permission to be tested to determine BAC if arrested for DUI/OUI/DWI. Refusal triggers a **45-day license suspension** followed by a mandatory ignition interlock device (IID) requirement. The IID period is **1 year for a first refusal**, **2 years for a second refusal**, and **3 years for a third or subsequent refusal**. This is longer than the IID period for failing the test (6 months, 1 year, and 2 years respectively). Refusal is admissible as evidence of "consciousness of guilt" in criminal proceedings. Drivers have **7 days** to request a hearing with the DMV Administrative Per Se Unit. Refusal is not a separate crime but carries automatic administrative consequences.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal = Longer IID Than Failure**: Connecticut's penalty structure uniquely punishes refusal more harshly than test failure through longer IID requirements. First refusal = 1 year IID vs. 6 months for failure. Second refusal = 2 years vs. 1 year for failure. Third+ refusal = 3 years vs. 2 years for failure.

2. **45-Day Hard Suspension for All**: Both refusal and failure trigger the same 45-day hard suspension period during which no driving is permitted. The difference lies in the post-suspension IID requirement.

3. **"Consciousness of Guilt" Jury Instruction**: Prosecutors may present refusal as proof the driver knew they were intoxicated. Judges often instruct juries they may interpret refusal as consciousness of guilt.

4. **Third Conviction = Permanent Revocation**: A third or subsequent DUI conviction results in permanent license revocation, with the possibility of requesting a hearing for reconsideration after at least 2 years.

5. **Administrative Per Se Unit**: Connecticut has a dedicated DMV Administrative Per Se Unit (860-263-5204) that handles all DUI-related license suspensions.

6. **Second Conviction Restrictions**: During the first year of the 3-year IID period for a second conviction, driving is restricted to work, school, treatment, IID service center, and probation officer appointments only.

7. **No Separate Refusal Crime**: Unlike Minnesota, Connecticut does not criminalize refusal itself. The consequences are purely administrative (license suspension and IID).

## Practical Notes

- **30-Day Delay Before Suspension Begins**: The 45-day suspension typically begins 30 days after the arrest date, giving drivers a short window to arrange alternative transportation.
- **Hearing by Phone or In Person**: DMV hearings may be conducted by telephone or in person. Drivers should request a hearing promptly within the 7-day window.
- **Ignition Interlock Required for Reinstatement**: IID installation is mandatory for license restoration after any DUI-related suspension in Connecticut.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Connecticut's implied consent law applies to all drivers in the state. Out-of-state drivers face suspension of their Connecticut operating privilege.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 are considered legally intoxicated at .02 BAC or higher.
- **SR-22 Insurance**: Typically required for reinstatement after DUI-related suspension.
