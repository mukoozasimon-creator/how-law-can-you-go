# DUI / Implied Consent - Hawaii

## Verdict

Hawaii's implied consent law (HRS 291E-11, 291E-15) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (breath, blood, or urine) if lawfully arrested for operating a vehicle under the influence of an intoxicant (OVUII). A first refusal results in a **2-year license revocation** - one of the longest first-refusal penalties in the nation. A second refusal within 10 years triggers **3 years**, and a third refusal within 10 years triggers **4 years**. By comparison, failing a test results in only 1 year for a first offense. Officers must use the **HPD 396K form** to inform arrestees of implied consent and the choice to submit or refuse. Drivers under 18 are **not eligible** for IID-restricted driving privileges. Hawaii requires IID installation during the entire revocation period for all OVUII-related suspensions.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Longest First Refusal Penalty in the US (2 Years)**: Hawaii's 2-year revocation for a first refusal is among the longest in the nation, double the penalty for failing a test (1 year).

2. **IID Required During Entire Revocation**: Hawaii requires ignition interlock device installation for the entire revocation period, not just after reinstatement. This allows revoked drivers to continue operating vehicles equipped with IID.

3. **Under 18 Not Eligible for IID Privileges**: Drivers under 18 years of age are explicitly not eligible for IID-restricted driving privileges, meaning they face a complete driving ban during revocation.

4. **HPD 396K Form**: Officers must use a standardized HPD 396K form to inform arrestees of implied consent rights and consequences. Different forms are used for drivers 21+ (HPD 396B) and under 21 (HPD 396B and HPD 396H).

5. **10-Year Lookback**: Hawaii uses a 10-year lookback period for both administrative revocations and criminal penalty enhancements.

6. **Force Permitted with Safeguards**: Officers may use force to obtain blood samples, but must submit a Use of Force Report (HPD-192E). If the qualified medical personnel determines that recovering the sample presents an imminent threat to health, the process must cease.

7. **Work-Related Restricted License**: A work-related restricted license may be available in some circumstances, but IID is mandatory for all OVUII-related revocations.

## Practical Notes

- **Revocation, Not Suspension**: Hawaii uses "revocation" rather than "suspension" for OVUII penalties, which typically requires reapplication for a license rather than automatic reinstatement.
- **Multiple Revocations Run Concurrently**: When multiple revocations are imposed for a single incident, the periods run concurrently (together) rather than consecutively (back-to-back).
- **Substance Abuse Assessment**: Two or more refusals may trigger a court-ordered substance abuse assessment and treatment.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Hawaii's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Hawaii roads. Out-of-state drivers face revocation of their Hawaii operating privilege.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face penalties at any measurable amount of alcohol, with escalating penalties for repeat offenses within 5 years.
- **Felony OVUII**: Third and subsequent OVUII convictions are felonies with 10-30 days mandatory jail and 1-5 years prison possible.
