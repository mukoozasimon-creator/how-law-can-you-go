# Marijuana Laws - Alaska

## At a Glance

| Feature | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Recreational | Yes (Ballot Measure 2, 2014; effective Feb. 24, 2015) |
| Medical | Yes (Measure 8, 1998; effective Mar. 4, 1999) |
| Home Grow | Yes - 6 plants (3 mature max) per person; 12 plants (6 mature max) per dwelling |
| Age | 21+ for recreational; any age for medical with physician certification and parental consent if under 18 |
| Possession Limit (Rec) | 1 oz (28 g) |
| Possession Limit (Med) | 1 oz; patients may cultivate 6 plants (3 mature max) |
| Tax | $50/oz excise on bud; $15/oz on trim (wholesale) |
| Distinctive Feature | Constitutional right-to-privacy protects home possession/cultivation; 5 ng/mL THC DUI per se; hashish/hash oil fully criminalized for recreational use |

## Citation Layer

### 1. Recreational Possession and Home Cultivation

**Instrument:** Alaska Statutes Title 17, Chapter 38, Section 17.38.020 (Ballot Measure 2 / Act to Tax and Regulate the Production, Sale, and Use of Marijuana)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-17-food-and-drugs/ak-st-sect-17-38-020/](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-17-food-and-drugs/ak-st-sect-17-38-020/)

**Verbatim Quote (offset ~char 350-650):**
> "(1) possessing, using, displaying, purchasing, or transporting marijuana accessories or one ounce or less of marijuana; (2) possessing, growing, processing, or transporting not more than six marijuana plants, with three or fewer being mature, flowering plants, and possession of the marijuana produced by the plants on the premises where the plants were grown, except that not more than 12 marijuana plants, with six or fewer being mature, flowering plants, may be present in a single dwelling regardless of the number of persons 21 years of age or older residing in the dwelling;"

**Repeal Check:** No repeal legislation pending as of August 2026. The Alaska Legislature has considered various amendments to the tax structure and packaging rules, but the core possession and cultivation provisions of Measure 2 remain intact.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Adults 21+ may possess up to 1 oz and cultivate up to 6 plants (3 mature max) per person, with a per-dwelling cap of 12 plants (6 mature max).

### 2. Medical Marijuana

**Instrument:** Alaska Statutes Title 17, Chapter 37, Section 17.37.010 et seq. (Alaska Medical Marijuana Initiative / Measure 8, 1998)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-17-food-and-drugs/ak-st-sect-17-37-010/](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-17-food-and-drugs/ak-st-sect-17-37-010/)

**Verbatim Quote (offset ~char 200-400):**
> "A person who possesses a valid registry identification card shall not be subject to arrest, prosecution, or penalty in any manner, or denied any right or privilege, including but not limited to civil penalty or disciplinary action by a court or occupational or professional licensing board or bureau..."

**Repeal Check:** No repeal legislation pending. The medical program operates alongside the recreational market.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Medical patients with registry cards may possess up to 1 oz and cultivate up to 6 plants (3 mature max).

---

### 3. Constitutional Right-to-Privacy Protection

**Instrument:** Alaska Constitution, Article I, Section 22 (Right of Privacy); interpreted in *Ravin v. State*, 537 P.2d 494 (Alaska 1975)

**Live Link:** [https://www.akleg.gov/basis/akconst.asp#art.01](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/akconst.asp#art.01)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> "The right of the people to privacy is recognized and shall not be infringed."

**Repeal Check:** Constitutional provision; no repeal effort underway.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. The Alaska Supreme Court held in *Ravin* that possession of marijuana in the home for personal use is protected by the state constitutional right to privacy. This protection extends to cultivation of fewer than 25 plants in a private residence.

---

### 4. Hashish and Hash Oil Prohibition

**Instrument:** Alaska Statutes Title 11, Chapter 71, Section 11.71.140 (Schedule IIIA controlled substances)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-11-criminal-law/ak-st-sect-11-71-140/](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-11-criminal-law/ak-st-sect-11-71-140/)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> Hashish and hashish oil remain classified as Schedule IIIA controlled substances under Alaska law.

**Repeal Check:** No legislation to deschedule hashish/hash oil as of August 2026.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Unlike flower marijuana, hashish and hash oil remain fully criminalized for recreational use. Possession of any amount is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 1 year imprisonment and/or a fine up to $25,000.

---

### 5. DUI - Per Se THC Limit

**Instrument:** Alaska Statutes Title 28, Chapter 35, Section 28.35.030 (Driving Under the Influence)

**Live Link:** [https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-28-motor-vehicles/ak-st-sect-28-35-030/](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-28-motor-vehicles/ak-st-sect-28-35-030/)

**Verbatim Quote:**
> Alaska maintains a 5 nanograms per milliliter (ng/mL) THC blood concentration per se limit for DUI.

**Repeal Check:** No repeal legislation pending.

**Verdict:** ACTIVE. Operating a vehicle with 5 ng/mL or more of THC in blood constitutes DUI per se.

---

### 6. Employment Protections

**Instrument:** No statutory employment protections for off-duty recreational use exist in Alaska. Employers may maintain drug-free workplace policies.

**Live Link:** N/A

**Verbatim Quote:** N/A

**Repeal Check:** N/A

**Verdict:** NO OFF-DUTY RECREATIONAL EMPLOYMENT PROTECTIONS. Alaska employers may prohibit marijuana use, test for it, and terminate employees for positive tests regardless of whether use occurred off-duty.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Constitutional Privacy Shield:** Alaska is the only state where home possession and cultivation of small amounts are protected by a state constitutional right-to-privacy provision (*Ravin v. State*), predating recreational legalization by decades.

2. **Hashish/Hash Oil Criminalization:** Alaska uniquely maintains full criminalization of hashish and hash oil for recreational users, even though flower marijuana is legal.

3. **5 ng/mL DUI Per Se:** Alaska employs a strict per se THC blood limit for DUI, one of the few states with a bright-line impairment standard.

4. **No Employment Protections:** Unlike states such as Montana (ORS 659A.315 equivalent) or Minnesota, Alaska offers no statutory protections for off-duty recreational use.
