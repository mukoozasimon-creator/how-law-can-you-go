## The Short Answer

In North Carolina, your landlord must serve a **10-day written demand for past-due rent** before filing eviction for nonpayment (G.S. 42-3). For lease violations, there is no universal statutory cure period - the landlord may file immediately for material violations. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **7 days' written notice** (G.S. 42-14). North Carolina has no rent control (statewide preemption), no just-cause requirement, and security deposits are capped at **2 months' rent** for tenancies longer than month-to-month. Self-help eviction is illegal, and retaliation is prohibited with a 12-month presumption window.

### North Carolina General Statutes Section 42-14 - Notice to Terminate Month-to-Month Tenancy

> "A tenancy from year to year may be terminated by a notice given at least one month before the end of the current year of the tenancy; a tenancy from month to month by a like notice of seven days." cite🛠web_search:2#4:~:text=A tenancy from month to month by a like notice of seven days.

**Live link:** https://www.ncleg.gov/Laws/GeneralStatutes/Chapter42

**Verdict:** 7 days written notice for month-to-month termination. 1 month for year-to-year.

### North Carolina General Statutes Section 42-51 - Security Deposit Cap

**Key rules:**
- **Month-to-month or weekly:** No statutory cap
- **Longer than month-to-month:** Maximum **2 months' rent**
- Must be held in **trust account** at licensed NC institution
- Bank notification must be provided to tenant within 30 days of lease commencement
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out (60 days if interim accounting provided within 30 days)
- Bad-faith retention: **Treble damages** under G.S. 42-55

**Live link:** https://www.ncleg.gov/Laws/GeneralStatutes/Chapter42
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 2-month cap for longer tenancies. Trust account required. Treble damages for bad-faith retention.

## Important Notes

- **Fast eviction timeline:** NC has one of the fastest eviction processes in the country. Hearings are scheduled within 7 days of summons issuance. Uncontested evictions typically take 4-6 weeks from filing to lockout.
- **No repair-and-deduct:** North Carolina does NOT allow tenants to unilaterally withhold rent or deduct repair costs. The remedy is rent abatement through court action.
- **No rent control:** State law preempts local rent control ordinances.
- **Entry notice:** Chapter 42 is silent on entry notice. Market standard is 24 hours, but lease terms control.
- **Eviction record expunction:** G.S. 42-36.4 requires clerks to seal certain eviction records, including judgments 3+ years old, dismissed proceedings, and proceedings naming respondents under 18.
- **Recording:** North Carolina is a one-party consent state for recordings (G.S. 15A-287). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Georgia:** Both are landlord-friendly Southern states, but NC has a shorter 7-day termination notice vs. Georgia's 60 days. NC also has a 10-day rent demand vs. Georgia's demand-for-possession system.
- **vs. Florida:** Both have fast eviction timelines, but Florida has stricter notice-counting rules (business days only).
- **vs. California:** NC is significantly more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and much shorter notice periods.
