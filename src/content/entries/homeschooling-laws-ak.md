# Homeschooling Laws - Alaska

## Statutory Framework

Alaska offers **four distinct legal pathways** for home education, but the overwhelming majority of families use Option 1 - the homeschool statute - which provides the most permissive framework in the United States.

### Primary Instrument: AS 14.30.010(b)(12)

> "A child between the ages of 7 and 16 is subject to compulsory school attendance. However, AS 14.30.010(b)(12) provides an exemption for a child 'being educated in the child's home by a parent or legal guardian.'"
> - Source: [Alaska Statutes Title 14, Education](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-14-education-libraries-and-museums/ak-st-sect-14-30-010/), AS 14.30.010(b)(12) (Authority: A)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

**Key point:** This single exemption clause is the entirety of Alaska's homeschool statute. No notification, no testing, no approval, no curriculum oversight.

> "Under the primary homeschool statute - AS 14.30.010(b)(12) - your child is exempt from compulsory attendance simply by 'being educated in the child's home by a parent or legal guardian.' That's it. No notification, no testing, no approval, no forms, no teacher qualifications."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Alaska](https://numaschool.com/states/alaska/homeschool-laws) (2026-07-30)

### Option 1: Homeschool Statute (Most Popular)

- No notification required
- No testing required
- No teacher qualifications
- No curriculum approval
- No forms to file
- Simply begin educating the child at home

This is the path used by the vast majority of Alaska homeschooling families.

### Option 2: Private Tutor

A child may be instructed by an **Alaska-certified teacher** acting as a private tutor. This option is rarely used because it requires hiring a certified teacher.

### Option 3: School Board Approval

Parents may submit a written request to the local school board demonstrating that the child is "equally well-served" by home instruction. The board may grant an excusal from mandatory attendance. This path involves district oversight that most families prefer to avoid.

### Option 4: State Correspondence Programs

Alaska operates unique **state-funded correspondence programs** that provide curriculum, materials, and teacher support at no cost to families. These programs (such as IDEA, Raven, and others) are publicly funded but allow families to homeschool with state support. While popular, these are optional and distinct from the standalone homeschool statute.

> "Unique state-funded correspondence programs provide curriculum, materials, and teacher support at no cost to families."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Alaska](https://numaschool.com/states/alaska/homeschool-laws)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None** for Option 1. No degree, diploma, certification, or credential is required.

### Testing Requirements

**None** for Option 1.

### Required Subjects

**None** specified in statute for Option 1.

### Instructional Time

No minimum hours or days are specified in statute for Option 1.

### Record-Keeping

**None required by law** for Option 1.

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

Ages 7 through 16 - one of the shortest ranges in the nation.

### Public School Sports Access

Alaska statute guarantees homeschoolers access to public school interscholastic athletics.

> "Homeschoolers can access public school sports under Alaska Statute § 14.30.365 - one of the stronger access laws in the country."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Alaska](https://numaschool.com/states/alaska/homeschool-laws)

### Immunization

Not required for homeschoolers operating under Option 1.

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal Options** | 4 pathways (homeschool statute, private tutor, board approval, correspondence) |
| **Notification** | None (Option 1) |
| **Testing** | None (Option 1) |
| **Qualifications** | None (Option 1) |
| **Required Subjects** | None (Option 1) |
| **Instructional Time** | None specified (Option 1) |
| **Record-Keeping** | None required (Option 1) |

### Verdict

Alaska is arguably the **most deregulated** homeschool state in America. Option 1 requires absolutely nothing from the state - no paperwork, no oversight, no qualifications. The four-path system gives families maximum flexibility, and the state-funded correspondence programs provide an unusual middle ground for families who want support without surrendering autonomy. The 7-16 compulsory age range is the narrowest in the nation.
