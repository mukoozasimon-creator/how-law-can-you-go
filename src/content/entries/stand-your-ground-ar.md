## The Short Answer

Arkansas became a **stand-your-ground state in 2021** (Act 250). You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not the initial aggressor, are lawfully present, and are not engaged in criminal activity (ACA 5-2-607). Arkansas's **Castle Doctrine** (ACA 5-2-607) presumes reasonable fear when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling or occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, serious physical injury, or the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### ACA 5-2-607 - Use of Deadly Physical Force in Defense of a Person

> "(a) A person is justified in using deadly physical force upon another person if the person reasonably believes that the other person is: (1) Committing or about to commit a felony involving force or violence; (2) Using or about to use unlawful deadly physical force; or (3) Imminently endangering the person's life or imminently about to victimize the person as the target of a crime of violence. (b) A person who is not the initial aggressor and is not engaged in criminal activity and who is in a place where the person has a right to be has no duty to retreat before using deadly physical force as described in this section."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-5/subtitle-2/chapter-2/section-5-2-607/
**Repeal check:** Active. Enacted by Act 250 of 2021, effective March 3, 2021.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat if lawfully present, not initial aggressor, and not engaged in criminal activity.

### ACA 5-2-606 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of a Person

> "(c) A person who is not the initial aggressor and is not engaged in criminal activity and who is in a place where the person has a right to be has no duty to retreat before using physical force as described in this section."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-5/subtitle-2/chapter-2/section-5-2-606/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before using any force, not just deadly force.

### ACA 5-2-608 - Use of Physical Force in Defense of Premises

> "A person in possession or control of premises or a person who is licensed or privileged to be thereon is justified in using physical force upon another person when and to the extent that the person reasonably believes the force to be necessary to prevent or terminate the commission or attempted commission of a criminal trespass by the other person in or upon the premises."

**Live link:** https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-5/subtitle-2/chapter-2/section-5-2-608/
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified to prevent criminal trespass.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/serious injury |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, same standard |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Stand-your-ground since 2021:** Arkansas enacted its stand-your-ground law in 2021, removing the previous duty to retreat.
- **No Castle Doctrine presumption statute:** While Arkansas recognizes Castle Doctrine principles, the statutory presumption framework is less formalized than in some other SYG states.
- **Criminal activity disqualifier:** You cannot claim self-defense if you are engaged in criminal activity at the time.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Arkansas is a one-party consent state for recordings (ACA 5-60-120). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the South.
- **vs. Louisiana:** Both are stand-your-ground states with similar frameworks.
- **vs. Oklahoma:** Both are stand-your-ground states. OK has broader statutory protections.
