## The Short Answer

Massachusetts is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you can do so safely (Commonwealth v. Benoit, 452 Mass. 212). Massachusetts's **Castle Doctrine** (M.G.L. c. 278, 8A) removes the duty to retreat inside your own dwelling against an unlawful intruder, but only if you reasonably believe the intruder is about to inflict great bodily injury or death, and you use reasonable means to defend yourself. The Castle Doctrine does **not** apply to persons lawfully in your home.

## Key Statutes

### M.G.L. c. 278, 8A - Castle Doctrine

> "In the prosecution of a person who is an occupant of a dwelling charged with killing or injuring one who was unlawfully in such dwelling for the purpose of committing a felony, if the occupant was in his dwelling at the time of the offense and acted in the reasonable belief that the person unlawfully in such dwelling was about to inflict great bodily injury or death upon such occupant or upon another person lawfully in such dwelling, and that such occupant used reasonable means to defend himself or such other person lawfully in such dwelling, there shall be no duty on such occupant to retreat from such person unlawfully in such dwelling."

**Live link:** https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter278/Section8A
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat inside dwelling against unlawful intruder. Must reasonably believe intruder is about to inflict great bodily injury or death.

### Commonwealth v. Benoit, 452 Mass. 212 (2008) - Duty to Retreat in Public

> "A self-defense instruction is not required unless there is some evidence that the defendant availed himself of all means, proper and reasonable in the circumstances, of retreating from the conflict before resorting to the use of deadly force. This rule does not impose an absolute duty to retreat regardless of personal safety considerations; an individual need not place himself in danger nor use every means of escape short of death before resorting to self-defense. He must, however, use every reasonable avenue of escape available to him."

**Live link:** https://www.mass.gov/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat before deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. Not an absolute duty - need not place self in danger.

### Commonwealth v. Peloquin, 437 Mass. 204 (2002) - Castle Doctrine Limits

> "Nothing in G.L. c. 278, 8A eliminates the duty on the part of the occupant of the dwelling to retreat from a confrontation with a person who is lawfully on the premises."

**Live link:** https://www.mass.gov/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine does not apply to persons lawfully in the home, even if they become violent.

### Commonwealth v. Carlino, 449 Mass. 71 (2007) - Dwelling Definition

> "The Castle Doctrine instruction is not warranted where the fatal encounter occurred in the driveway outside of the dwelling."

**Live link:** https://www.mass.gov/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling case law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Castle Doctrine applies only inside the dwelling, not in the driveway or yard.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (unlawful intruder) | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent great bodily injury/death |
| In your dwelling (lawful guest) | Yes, if safe | Same standard as public |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same standard |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** Massachusetts imposes a duty to retreat before using deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. This is a key distinction from stand-your-ground states.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to unlawful intruders:** The Castle Doctrine only applies to persons unlawfully in your dwelling. It does not apply to lawful guests, family members, or anyone with permission to be there.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to dwelling:** The instruction is not warranted for encounters outside the dwelling (driveway, porch, yard).
- **Burden on prosecution:** If you assert the Castle Doctrine, the burden falls on the prosecution to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that you were not acting in lawful self-defense.
- **Reasonable means:** The force used must be "reasonable means" to defend yourself. Proportionality still applies.
- **Recording:** Massachusetts is a two-party consent state for recordings (M.G.L. c. 272, 99). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New York:** Both impose a duty to retreat in public. NY limits Castle Doctrine to dwelling; MA does the same.
- **vs. Connecticut:** CT imposes a duty to retreat in public; similar to MA.
- **vs. Rhode Island:** RI is a stand-your-ground state; MA is not.
