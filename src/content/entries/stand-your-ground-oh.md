## The Short Answer

Ohio is a **stand-your-ground state** (since April 4, 2021). You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you lawfully have a right to be (ORC 2901.09). Ohio's **Castle Doctrine** (ORC 2901.05(B)(2)) creates a rebuttable presumption that you acted in self-defense when someone unlawfully enters your home or occupied vehicle. Since 2019, the prosecution bears the burden of disproving self-defense beyond a reasonable doubt once the defendant presents some evidence (ORC 2901.05).

## Key Statutes

### ORC 2901.09 - No Duty to Retreat in Self-Defense

> "A trier of fact shall not consider the possibility of retreat as a factor in determining whether or not a person who used force in self-defense, defense of another, or defense of that person's residence reasonably believed that the force was necessary to prevent injury, loss, or risk to life or safety."

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2901.09
**Repeal check:** Active. Enacted by Senate Bill 175, effective April 4, 2021.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before using force in self-defense. Trier of fact cannot consider failure to retreat as evidence of unjustified force.

### ORC 2901.05(B)(2) - Castle Doctrine Presumption

> "The actor's belief that force was necessary is presumed to be reasonable if the actor is a lawful occupant of the residence or vehicle and the other person is not a lawful occupant and has unlawfully entered or is in the process of unlawfully entering the residence or vehicle."

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2901.05
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable self-defense when unlawful intruder enters your home or occupied vehicle.

### ORC 2901.05 - Burden of Proof on Self-Defense

> "The burden of going forward with the evidence of an affirmative defense is on the accused. Once the accused has introduced sufficient evidence to raise an issue as to the affirmative defense, the state must disprove the affirmative defense beyond a reasonable doubt."

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2901.05
**Repeal check:** Active. Amended by House Bill 228, effective March 28, 2019.
**Verdict:** Once defendant presents some evidence of self-defense, prosecution must disprove it beyond reasonable doubt. Defendant does not bear the burden of proving self-defense.

### ORC 2901.05(B)(1) - Self-Defense Elements

> "A person is presumed to have acted in self-defense or defense of another when using defensive force that is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily harm to another if the person using the force knew or had reason to believe that the person against whom the force was used was in the process of unlawfully and without privilege to do so entering, or had unlawfully and without privilege to do so entered, the residence or vehicle occupied by the person using the defensive force."

**Live link:** https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2901.05
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption applies to both home and occupied vehicle.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable self-defense |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable self-defense |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Burden of proof | On prosecution | Once defendant raises the issue |

## Important Notes

- **Stand-your-ground since 2021:** Ohio became the 36th state to pass stand-your-ground legislation when Senate Bill 175 took effect on April 4, 2021.
- **Burden shifted to prosecution:** Since 2019 (HB 228), once the defendant presents some evidence of self-defense, the prosecution must disprove it beyond a reasonable doubt. Previously, the defendant had to prove self-defense.
- **Castle Doctrine applies to vehicle:** Unlike many states that limit Castle Doctrine to the home, Ohio extends the presumption to your occupied vehicle.
- **Lawful presence required:** You cannot claim stand-your-ground if you are trespassing or committing a crime.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you provoked the encounter, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Recording:** Ohio is a one-party consent state for recordings (ORC 2933.52). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Pennsylvania:** Both are stand-your-ground states. PA enacted in 2011; OH enacted in 2021. OH shifted burden of proof to prosecution in 2019.
- **vs. Michigan:** Both are stand-your-ground states with Castle Doctrine extending to vehicle.
- **vs. Indiana:** Both are stand-your-ground states. IN has had SYG longer; OH enacted in 2021.
