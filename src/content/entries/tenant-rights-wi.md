## The Short Answer

In Wisconsin, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or vacate for nonpayment (Wis. Stat. § 704.17(2)(a)). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **5-day notice** with right to cure (or **14-day no-cure notice** for repeat violations or imminent danger). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **28 days' written notice** (§ 704.19(3)). Wisconsin has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### Wis. Stat. § 704.28 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **21 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **double the amount wrongfully withheld** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statute/704/28
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 21-day return. Move-in checklist required. Double damages for wrongful withholding.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 28 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 5 days (pay or vacate) | N/A |
| Lease violation (curable) | 5 days (cure or vacate) | N/A |
| Lease violation (non-curable or repeat) | 14 days (no cure) | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Wisconsin landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Wisconsin preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery or certified/registered mail. Posting on door is NOT authorized for pre-suit notice.
- **Recording:** Wisconsin is a one-party consent state for recordings (Wis. Stat. § 968.31). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Illinois:** WI is more landlord-friendly with no just-cause requirement and no Chicago-style RLTO.
- **vs. Minnesota:** Both have similar protections, but MN has stronger tenant protections in some areas.
- **vs. Michigan:** Both have similar 5-day/28-day notice frameworks, but MI has stricter deposit rules.
