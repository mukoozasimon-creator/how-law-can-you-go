## The Short Answer

In Louisiana, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to vacate for nonpayment (La. Civ. Code Art. 2683). For lease violations, the landlord must give a **5-day notice** to cure or vacate. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **10 days' written notice** before the end of the rental period (La. Civ. Code Art. 2728). Louisiana has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no statewide security deposit cap. Self-help eviction is illegal.

### La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3251 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Must provide **move-in inspection checklist**.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **actual damages** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=108736
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. Move-in checklist required.

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Louisiana landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Louisiana preempts local rent control.
- **Eviction timeline:** Hearings typically within 2-3 weeks of filing. Writ of possession issued after judgment.
- **Service methods:** Personal delivery or certified/registered mail.
- **Recording:** Louisiana is a one-party consent state for recordings (La. Rev. Stat. § 15:1303). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Mississippi:** Both are landlord-friendly Southern states with similar protections.
- **vs. Texas:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but LA has shorter notices (5 days vs. TX's 3 days for nonpayment).
- **vs. Florida:** Both have fast eviction timelines, but FL has stricter notice-counting rules (business days only).
