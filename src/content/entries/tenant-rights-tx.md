## The Short Answer

In Texas, your landlord does **not** need a reason (just cause) to end a month-to-month tenancy. The landlord must give **30 days' written notice** to terminate a month-to-month lease. For nonpayment of rent, the landlord must give a **3-day notice to vacate** (unless the lease specifies a different period). Texas is generally considered landlord-friendly: there is no rent control, no security deposit cap, and no statewide just-cause requirement. Self-help eviction (changing locks, shutting off utilities) is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### Texas Property Code Section 91.001 - Notice for Month-to-Month Tenancy

> "A tenancy at will, from year to year, or for less than a year, may be terminated by either party by a notice given to the other at least one month before the date on which the party intends the termination to take effect." cite🛠web_search:1#8:~:text=To end a month-to-month tenancy in Texas, the required notice is: 30 days (one month)

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.91.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### Texas Property Code Chapter 24 - Forcible Entry and Detainer (Eviction)

> "Self-help evictions - lockouts, utility shutoffs, seizing belongings - are illegal." cite🛠web_search:1#6:~:text=Self-help evictions — lockouts, utility shutoffs, seizing belongings — are illegal

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.24.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3-day notice to vacate for nonpayment (default; lease can modify). Court filing required; only court order can remove tenant.

### Texas Property Code Section 92.056 - Landlord's Duty to Repair

> "A landlord shall make a diligent effort to repair or remedy a condition if: (1) the tenant specifies the condition in a notice to the landlord; (2) the tenant is not delinquent in rent; and (3) the condition materially affects the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant." cite🛠web_search:1#3:~:text=The landlord must make a diligent effort to repair or remedy a condition if (1) the tenant specifies the condition in a notice to repair, (2) the tenant is not delinquent in rent, and (3) the condition materially affects the physical health or safety

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.92.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Landlord has 7 days to begin repairs after written notice. Tenant can repair-and-deduct, terminate lease, or sue for damages if landlord fails.

### Texas Property Code Section 92.331 - Retaliation

> "A landlord may not retaliate against a tenant by filing an eviction, depriving the tenant of use of the premises, decreasing services, increasing rent, or terminating the tenancy because the tenant has exercised a right under this chapter." cite🛠web_search:1#6:~:text=A landlord may not retaliate against a tenant by filing an eviction, deprivation of use, materially decreasing services, increasing rent (with specific carveouts), or terminating the tenancy in response to protected conduct.

**Protected actions include:** good-faith exercise of a lease or statutory right; notice to repair; complaint to government agency; complaint to public utility; participation in tenant organization.

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.92.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 6-month protected window after tenant's protected action. Penalty: one month's rent plus $500 plus actual damages plus attorney's fees.

### Texas Property Code Section 92.101-92.110 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statutory cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **30 days** after move-out with itemized list of deductions.
- Landlord must provide written description of damages and charges.

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.92.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day return. No cap on amount. Bad-faith retention can result in triple damages plus attorney's fees under § 92.109.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 3 days to vacate (default) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 3 days to vacate (if lease specifies) | N/A |
| Retaliatory eviction defense | N/A - affirmative defense | No - but retaliation is prohibited |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Texas landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason or no reason, provided proper notice is given.
- **No rent control:** Texas preempts local rent control except under a declared housing emergency (never used).
- **Repair-and-deduct:** Under § 92.0561, tenant can deduct up to one month's rent or $500 (whichever is greater) for repairs, but must follow strict procedural requirements.
- **Late fees:** Safe harbor of 12% of monthly rent (1-4 units) or 10% (5+ units), after a 2-day grace period.
- **Recording:** Texas is a one-party consent state for recordings (Penal Code § 16.02). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Texas has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and no security deposit cap - far more landlord-friendly.
- **vs. Florida:** Both are landlord-friendly states with no just-cause requirement, but Florida has stricter notice counting rules (business days only).
- **vs. New York:** Texas has none of New York's just-cause or rent-stabilization protections.
