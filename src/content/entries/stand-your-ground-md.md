## The Short Answer

Maryland is **NOT a stand-your-ground state**. You have a **duty to retreat** before using deadly force in public if you can do so safely (Maryland common law). Maryland's **Castle Doctrine** is recognized under common law and allows you to defend yourself inside your home without retreating, but the force used must be reasonable and proportional to the threat. Deadly force is only justified if you reasonably believe you are the victim of a life-threatening action.

## Key Case Law

### Maryland Common Law - Duty to Retreat

> "Maryland has no affirmative law requiring you to retreat before using force. But under common law doctrine, if you are in public, you must retreat before using deadly force if it is safe to do so and you can retreat to a location of safety. In other words, you only must retreat if there is a safer place to go to. You are not required to avoid using force if doing so would put you at greater risk. Use of deadly force must be your last resort."

**Live link:** https://www.findlaw.com/state/maryland-law/maryland-self-defense-laws.html
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling common law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Duty to retreat in public before deadly force if safe retreat is available.

### Maryland Common Law - Castle Doctrine

> "You do not have to retreat from your own home or property. If necessary, you may use reasonable force to defend property, including deadly force. When defending your home, you must believe there is an imminent threat. Someone in the street who looks threatening is not enough."

**Live link:** https://www.findlaw.com/state/maryland-law/maryland-self-defense-laws.html
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling common law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat inside your own home. Force must be reasonable and proportional.

### Maryland Self-Defense Elements

> "For self-defense to be justified in Maryland: (1) The threat must be immediate and real. (2) The person must reasonably believe they are in imminent danger of serious bodily harm or death. (3) The use of deadly force must be a last resort - there must be no safe means of escape."

**Live link:** https://www.careylawoffice.com/2024/11/04/marylands-duty-to-retreat-law-vs-stand-your-ground-law/
**Repeal check:** Active. Controlling common law as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Three-part test: imminent threat, reasonable belief, and last resort.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home | No | Yes, if reasonable belief of imminent death/serious bodily harm |
| In public | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Only if cannot retreat safely |
| Defending another | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same standard |
| In your vehicle | Yes, if safe retreat possible | Same duty to retreat applies |

## Important Notes

- **Duty to retreat in public:** Maryland imposes a duty to retreat before using deadly force in public if safe retreat is available. This is common law, not statute.
- **Castle Doctrine by common law:** Maryland recognizes the Castle Doctrine under common law, not by statute. No duty to retreat inside your own home.
- **Reasonable and proportional force:** Even inside your home, the force used must be reasonable and proportional to the threat. Deadly force is not justified against a non-life-threatening intruder.
- **Last resort requirement:** Deadly force must be your last resort. You must have no alternative but to use force.
- **No civil immunity statute:** Maryland does not have a specific civil immunity statute for self-defense.
- **Recording:** Maryland is a two-party consent state for recordings (Md. Courts and Judicial Proceedings Code 10-402). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Virginia:** VA is a stand-your-ground state by case law (if without fault); MD imposes a duty to retreat.
- **vs. Pennsylvania:** PA is a stand-your-ground state; MD is not.
- **vs. Delaware:** DE imposes a duty to retreat in public; similar to MD.
