# Homeschooling Laws - Idaho

## Statutory Framework

Idaho has one of the most permissive homeschool environments in the United States. The entire legal framework rests on a single exemption clause within the compulsory attendance statute.

### Primary Instrument: Idaho Code § 33-202

> "The legal basis comes from Idaho Code § 33-202, which provides a compulsory attendance exemption for children who are 'otherwise comparably instructed.'"
> - Source: [Blue Folder - Idaho Homeschool Requirements & Laws](https://bluefolder.app/states/idaho) (2026-02-16)
> - Repeal check: Active as of 2026; no repeal pending.

The statute provides an exemption from compulsory attendance for children who are **"otherwise comparably instructed."** This language has been interpreted broadly to mean that parents must provide instruction in subjects commonly taught in public schools, but there is no specific list of required subjects and no mechanism for the state to verify compliance.

> "Exceptions to Attendance Requirements, Child is otherwise comparably instructed."
> - Source: [FindLaw - Idaho Compulsory Education Laws](https://www.findlaw.com/state/idaho-law/idaho-compulsory-education-laws.html) (2016-06-20)

### Notification

**None required.** Idaho requires absolutely zero notification to any state agency, school district, or other governmental entity.

> "Idaho requires absolutely zero notification to anyone. You do not need to tell the state, your school district, or any other agency that you are homeschooling. You simply begin teaching."
> - Source: [Blue Folder - Idaho Homeschool Requirements](https://bluefolder.app/states/idaho)

### Teacher Qualifications

**None.** No degree, diploma, certification, or any credential is required.

### Testing Requirements

**None.** No standardized testing, portfolio review, or annual assessment is mandated.

### Required Subjects

Statute does not list specific subjects. The "comparably instructed" standard is broadly interpreted. Some sources suggest a "sequentially progressive curriculum" in fundamental academic areas, but this is guidance, not statutory mandate.

> "The content you teach must offer a sequentially progressive homeschool curriculum for the appropriate grade level in fundamental academic areas including fine arts, language arts, math, science, and social studies."
> - Source: [SVHS - Homeschooling in Idaho](https://svhs.co/homeschooling-idaho-laws/) (2026-01-16)

Note: This "sequentially progressive" language appears in some guidance materials but is not codified in Idaho Code § 33-202 itself.

### Instructional Time

No minimum hours or days are specified in statute.

### Record-Keeping

**None required by law.**

> "Record-Keeping Requirements: No"
> - Source: [ABC Mouse - Idaho Homeschool Laws](https://www.abcmouse.com/learn/homeschool/idaho-homeschool-laws/30589) (2024-10-02)

### Compulsory Attendance Ages

**Ages 7 through 16** - one of the shortest compulsory age ranges in the nation.

> "Idaho also has one of the shortest compulsory age ranges in the nation, covering only ages 7 through 16."
> - Source: [Blue Folder - Idaho Homeschool Requirements](https://bluefolder.app/states/idaho)

There is no kindergarten requirement, and students may complete compulsory education earlier than in most other states.

### Empowering Parents Grant

Idaho provides the **Empowering Parents Grant**, which offers up to **$1,000 per student annually** for educational expenses through the Idaho State Board of Education.

> "The Empowering Parents Grant provides up to $1,000 per student annually for educational expenses."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Idaho](https://numaschool.com/states/idaho/homeschool-laws) (2026-05-19)

### Public School Sports Access

Idaho law guarantees homeschoolers access to public school athletics.

> "Homeschoolers have access to public school sports under Idaho law."
> - Source: [Numa School - Homeschooling in Idaho](https://numaschool.com/states/idaho/homeschool-laws)

### Immunization

Not required for homeschoolers.

### Starting Homeschooling

If a child is currently enrolled in public school, parents should formally withdraw them. Otherwise, parents simply begin instruction.

> "It's very easy to start homeschooling in Idaho. If your child is currently enrolled in public school, you'll need to formally withdraw them and let the school know you'll be homeschooling. You are not required to give advance notice or details about the education you will be providing."
> - Source: [ABC Mouse - Idaho Homeschool Laws](https://www.abcmouse.com/learn/homeschool/idaho-homeschool-laws/30589)

### Distinctive Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Notification** | None |
| **Testing** | None |
| **Qualifications** | None |
| **Required Subjects** | None statutorily listed ("comparably instructed") |
| **Instructional Time** | None specified |
| **Record-Keeping** | None required |
| **Compulsory Ages** | 7-16 (shortest in U.S.) |
| **Funding** | Empowering Parents Grant up to $1,000/student |
| **Sports Access** | Statutory guarantee |

### Verdict

Idaho is one of the **most deregulated** homeschool states in America. The absence of notification, testing, qualifications, subject requirements, hour minimums, and record-keeping obligations places it in the top tier of homeschool-friendly jurisdictions. The only legal requirement is to provide "comparably instructed" education, with no state mechanism to evaluate compliance. The Empowering Parents Grant and sports access add active state support to an already minimal regulatory framework.
