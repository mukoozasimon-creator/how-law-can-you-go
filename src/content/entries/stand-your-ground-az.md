## The Short Answer

Arizona is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you may legally be and are not engaged in an unlawful act (A.R.S. 13-405(B)). Arizona's **Castle Doctrine** (A.R.S. 13-418) creates a presumption that you reasonably believed deadly force was necessary when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death or serious physical injury, or to prevent certain enumerated felonies (A.R.S. 13-411).

## Key Statutes

### A.R.S. 13-404 - Justification; Self-Defense

> "A person is justified in threatening or using physical force against another when and to the extent a reasonable person would believe that physical force is immediately necessary to protect himself against the other's use or attempted use of unlawful physical force."

**Live link:** https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00404.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified when a reasonable person would believe it immediately necessary against unlawful force.

### A.R.S. 13-405 - Justification; Use of Deadly Force

> "(B) A person has no duty to retreat before threatening or using deadly physical force pursuant to this section if the person is in a place where the person may legally be and is not engaged in an unlawful act."

**Live link:** https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00405.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before deadly force if lawfully present and not engaged in unlawful act.

### A.R.S. 13-418 - Justification; Defensive Display of a Firearm; Presumption

> "A person is presumed to have reasonably feared imminent death or serious physical injury to himself or another person when the person uses deadly physical force if both of the following apply: (1) The person against whom the deadly physical force was used was in the process of unlawfully and forcefully entering, or had unlawfully and forcibly entered, a dwelling, residence or occupied vehicle, or had removed or was attempting to remove another person against the other person's will from the dwelling, residence or occupied vehicle. (2) The person who uses deadly physical force knew or had reason to believe that the person against whom the deadly physical force was used was unlawfully and forcibly entering or had unlawfully and forcibly entered the dwelling, residence or occupied vehicle or had removed or was attempting to remove another person against the other person's will from the dwelling, residence or occupied vehicle."

**Live link:** https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00418.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle.

### A.R.S. 13-411 - Justification; Use of Force in Crime Prevention

> "A person is justified in threatening or using both physical force and deadly physical force against another if and to the extent the person reasonably believes that physical force or deadly physical force is immediately necessary to prevent the other's commission of arson of an occupied structure, burglary in the first or second degree, kidnapping, manslaughter, murder, child molestation, sexual assault, aggravated assault, armed robbery or any other felony involving the use or threat of deadly physical force."

**Live link:** https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/00411.htm
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified to prevent enumerated felonies involving deadly force.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Preventing enumerated felonies | No | Yes, under 13-411 |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Arizona explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using deadly force if you are lawfully present and not engaged in an unlawful act.
- **Castle Doctrine presumption:** The presumption of reasonable fear under 13-418 is a significant legal advantage. The prosecution must overcome this presumption with evidence.
- **Dwelling includes more than home:** Arizona courts have interpreted "dwelling" broadly. A recent Arizona Supreme Court case confirmed that "dwelling" can include areas beyond the four walls of a house.
- **Civil immunity:** Arizona provides civil immunity for justified use of force under A.R.S. 13-413.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal (A.R.S. 13-404(C)).
- **Recording:** Arizona is a one-party consent state for recordings (A.R.S. 13-3005). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections. TX extends Castle Doctrine to workplace; AZ does not statutorily.
- **vs. California:** Both are stand-your-ground states. AZ has a statutory Castle Doctrine presumption; CA has a rebuttable presumption.
- **vs. New Mexico:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southwest.
