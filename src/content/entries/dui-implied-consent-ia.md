# DUI / Implied Consent - Iowa

## Verdict

Iowa's implied consent law (Iowa Code 321J.6, 321J.9) deems that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (breath, blood, or urine) if a law enforcement officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are operating while intoxicated (OWI). A first refusal results in a **1-year license revocation**, which is longer than the 180-day revocation for failing a test. Drivers are eligible for a **temporary restricted license after 90 days** if they install an ignition interlock device (IID). Refusal is admissible as evidence in criminal proceedings. Iowa does NOT grant a right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit to testing. Officers must inform drivers that refusal will result in license revocation.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Refusal Penalty Longer Than Failure**: Iowa's 1-year revocation for a first refusal is significantly longer than the 180-day revocation for failing a test. This creates a strong incentive to submit to testing.

2. **Temporary Restricted License After 90 Days**: Even after refusal, drivers can apply for a temporary restricted license after 90 days if they install an IID, allowing them to drive to work, school, and treatment.

3. **No Right to Attorney Before Testing**: Iowa does not grant drivers the right to consult an attorney before deciding whether to submit to chemical testing.

4. **12-Year Lookback for Criminal Penalties**: Iowa uses a 12-year lookback period for criminal penalty enhancements, though administrative actions may consider all prior history.

5. **Controlled Substance Zero Tolerance**: It is illegal to operate a vehicle with any amount of a controlled substance in one's body, regardless of impairment.

6. **Deferred Judgment Available**: First-time offenders may be eligible for deferred judgment with probation, which can avoid a conviction if successfully completed.

7. **Administrative and Criminal Proceedings**: Iowa has separate administrative (license) and criminal (OWI) proceedings that run independently.

## Practical Notes

- **90-Day Hard Suspension**: For a first refusal, the first 90 days are a hard suspension with no driving privileges. After 90 days, a temporary restricted license with IID may be available.
- **Deferred Judgment vs. Refusal**: Drivers facing a first OWI must weigh the option of deferred judgment (which requires test submission and conviction) against refusal (which guarantees longer revocation but may weaken the criminal case).
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Iowa's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Iowa roads. Out-of-state drivers face revocation of their Iowa operating privilege.
- **Under 21**: Drivers under 21 are subject to zero-tolerance rules for both alcohol (.02+) and controlled substances (any amount).
- **Substance Abuse Evaluation**: All OWI offenders must undergo a substance abuse evaluation and complete any recommended treatment.
