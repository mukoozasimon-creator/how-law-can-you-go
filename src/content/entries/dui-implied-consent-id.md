# DUI / Implied Consent - Idaho

## Verdict

Idaho's implied consent law (Idaho Code 18-8002) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are driving under the influence. A first refusal results in a **1-year absolute license suspension** plus a **$250 civil penalty**. A second refusal within 10 years triggers a **2-year suspension** plus the same $250 civil penalty. Idaho requires **1 year of ignition interlock device (IID)** installation on any vehicle the driver operates after the suspension period ends. Officers must inform drivers of implied consent requirements using language in **strict compliance** with the statutory wording. Drivers have **7 days** to request an administrative hearing. No restricted permit is available for first or second refusals.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Absolute Suspension - No Restricted Permit**: Idaho imposes an absolute (hard) suspension for first and second refusals with no restricted driving permit available. This is stricter than many states that offer work permits or IID-restricted licenses during suspension.

2. **$250 Civil Penalty**: In addition to license suspension, refusal carries a $250 civil penalty - a flat fee regardless of offense number.

3. **Strict Compliance Warning Requirement**: Officers must inform drivers of implied consent requirements using language in "strict compliance with statutory language." Any deviation may invalidate the suspension.

4. **Officer May Request Second Test**: If an officer has reasonable cause to believe the suspect was under the influence of a drug, they may request a second test (blood or urine) even after a breath test.

5. **10-Year Lookback**: Idaho uses a 10-year lookback period for refusal penalty escalation.

6. **IID Required After ALL DUI-Related Suspensions**: Whether the driver refused or failed, they must install an IID for 1 year on every vehicle they drive after the suspension period ends.

7. **7-Day Hearing Deadline**: Drivers have just 7 days from receipt of the notice of intended suspension to request a hearing.

## Practical Notes

- **Absolute Suspension Means No Driving**: During the 1-year (or 2-year) absolute suspension, the driver cannot legally operate any motor vehicle for any reason. This includes work, medical, and emergency purposes.
- **Administrative and Criminal Proceedings**: Idaho has separate administrative (license) and criminal (DUI) proceedings. The administrative suspension is immediate upon refusal.
- **28-Day Judicial Review**: Within 28 days of the hearing results, a driver may file a petition in district court for judicial review.
- **Out-of-State Drivers**: Idaho's implied consent law applies to all drivers on Idaho roads. Out-of-state drivers face suspension of their Idaho operating privilege.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face penalties at .02 BAC or higher.
- **Aggravated DUI**: DUI with excessive BAC, prior convictions, or with a child in the vehicle can be charged as aggravated DUI, a felony.
