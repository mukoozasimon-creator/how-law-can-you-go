## The Short Answer

In Vermont, your landlord must give you a **14-day written notice** to pay rent or the rental agreement terminates (9 V.S.A. 4467(a)). For material lease violations, a **30-day notice** to quit applies (9 V.S.A. 4467(b)(1)). For criminal activity or violence, a **14-day unconditional notice** applies (9 V.S.A. 4467(b)(2)). To end a month-to-month tenancy without cause, the landlord must give **60 days' notice** (tenants there 2 years or less) or **90 days' notice** (tenants there more than 2 years) (9 V.S.A. 4467(c)). Vermont has no just-cause requirement but has some of the longest no-cause notice periods in the country. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### 9 V.S.A. 4467(a) - 14-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may deliver a written notice specifying the amount of rent required to remedy the breach and the date on which the lease will terminate if it is not paid, not less than 14 days after receipt."

**Live link:** https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 14 days. Tenant may defeat ejectment at any time by paying all rent in arrears plus interest and costs, but cannot use this remedy more than once in 12 months.

### 9 V.S.A. 4467(b)(1) - 30-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If the tenant fails to comply with a material term of the rental agreement, the landlord may deliver a written notice specifying the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate on a date at least 30 days after receipt."

**Live link:** https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day notice for lease violations. Landlord is not required to provide an opportunity to cure.

### 9 V.S.A. 4467(b)(2) - 14-Day Notice for Criminal Activity

> "For criminal activity, illegal drug activity, or acts of violence threatening health or safety, the landlord may deliver a written 14-day notice to quit. The landlord is not required to provide an opportunity to cure."

**Live link:** https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14-day no-cure notice for criminal/violent activity.

### 9 V.S.A. 4467(c) - 60/90-Day No-Cause Termination

> "For tenants who have resided continuously for two years or less, at least 60 days after the date of actual notice. For tenants who have resided continuously for more than two years, at least 90 days after the date of actual notice."

**Live link:** https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 60 days for tenants there 2 years or less; 90 days for tenants there more than 2 years. Among the longest no-cause notice periods in the US.

### 9 V.S.A. 4467(k) - 60-Day Filing Deadline

> "A notice to terminate a tenancy shall be insufficient to support a judgment of eviction unless the proceeding is commenced not later than 60 days from the termination date set forth in the notice."

**Live link:** https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/137/04467
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Landlord must file eviction action within 60 days of the termination date in the notice.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| No-cause termination (0-2 years) | 60 days written | No |
| No-cause termination (2+ years) | 90 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 14 days (pay or quit) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 30 days to quit | N/A |
| Criminal activity | 14 days (no cure) | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Vermont landlords can terminate tenancies without cause with proper notice, but the notice periods are among the longest in the country.
- **No rent control:** Vermont does not have statewide rent control, but some municipalities have local ordinances.
- **Once-per-year cure:** Tenant can defeat ejectment for nonpayment by paying all arrears, but only once per 12-month period.
- **Filing deadline:** Landlord must file within 60 days of termination date or the notice becomes invalid.
- **Recording:** Vermont is a two-party consent state for recordings (9 V.S.A. 1192). All parties must consent to recording.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. New Hampshire:** NH requires good cause; VT does not but has longer notice periods.
- **vs. Maine:** ME has 30-day no-cause notice; VT has 60-90 days.
- **vs. Massachusetts:** MA has just-cause requirements in some cities; VT has long no-cause notice periods statewide.
