## The Short Answer

Texas is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you have a legal right to be, are not engaged in criminal activity, and did not provoke the person against whom force was used (Tex. Penal Code 9.31, 9.32). Texas's **Castle Doctrine** extends beyond the home to your occupied vehicle and workplace, creating a presumption of reasonable fear when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters (Tex. Penal Code 9.32(b)).

## Key Statutes

### Tex. Penal Code 9.31 - Self-Defense

> "A person is justified in using force against another when and to the degree the actor reasonably believes the force is immediately necessary to protect the actor against the other's use or attempted use of unlawful force."

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm#9.31
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Force justified when reasonably believed immediately necessary against unlawful force.

### Tex. Penal Code 9.32 - Deadly Force in Self-Defense

> "A person is justified in using deadly force against another: (1) if the actor would be justified in using force against the other under Section 9.31; and (2) when and to the degree the actor reasonably believes the deadly force is immediately necessary: (A) to protect the actor against the other's use or attempted use of unlawful deadly force; or (B) to prevent the other's imminent commission of aggravated kidnapping, murder, sexual assault, aggravated sexual assault, robbery, or aggravated robbery."

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm#9.32
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified against imminent deadly force or enumerated forcible felonies.

### Tex. Penal Code 9.32(b) - Castle Doctrine Presumption

> "The actor's belief under Subsection (a)(2) that the deadly force was immediately necessary as described by that subdivision is presumed to be reasonable if the actor: (1) knew or had reason to believe that the person against whom the deadly force was used: (A) unlawfully and with force entered, or was attempting to enter unlawfully and with force, the actor's occupied habitation, vehicle, or place of business or employment; (B) unlawfully and with force removed, or was attempting to remove unlawfully and with force, the actor from the actor's occupied habitation, vehicle, or place of business or employment; or (C) was committing or attempting to commit an enumerated felony."

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm#9.32
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters home, vehicle, or workplace.

### Tex. Penal Code 9.33 - Defense of Third Person

> "A person is justified in using force or deadly force against another to protect a third person if: (1) under the circumstances as the actor reasonably believes them to be, the actor would be justified under Section 9.31 or 9.32 in using force or deadly force to protect himself against the unlawful force or unlawful deadly force he reasonably believes to be threatening the third person he seeks to protect; and (2) the actor reasonably believes that his intervention is immediately necessary to protect the third person."

**Live link:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm#9.33
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Same standard as self-defense applies when defending others.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your workplace | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |
| Defending property only | No | No - deadly force not for property alone |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Texas explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force if you are lawfully present, not engaged in criminal activity, and did not provoke the encounter.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to vehicle and workplace:** Unlike many states that limit Castle Doctrine to the home, Texas extends the presumption to your occupied vehicle and place of business.
- **Civil immunity:** Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code 83.001 provides civil immunity for justified use of force or deadly force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you provoked the other's use of force, unless you abandoned the encounter and communicated that abandonment.
- **Recording:** Texas is a one-party consent state for recordings (Tex. Penal Code 16.02). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Both are stand-your-ground states. TX extends Castle Doctrine to vehicle/workplace; CA limits statutory Castle Doctrine to home.
- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections. FL has a pre-trial immunity hearing procedure; TX does not.
- **vs. New York:** NY imposes a duty to retreat in public; TX does not.
