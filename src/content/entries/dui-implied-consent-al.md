# DUI / Implied Consent - Alabama

## Verdict

Alabama's implied consent law (Code § 32-5A-192) requires that any person who operates a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or oral fluid) if lawfully arrested for DUI. A first refusal results in a **90-day license suspension**. Second and subsequent refusals within a 10-year period trigger **1-year** (2nd) and **3-year** (3rd) suspensions. Refusal is admissible as evidence in both civil and criminal proceedings. Alabama does NOT require officers to read specific warnings about the exact length of suspension for the administrative penalty to apply. Commercial drivers face 1-year disqualification for a first refusal and lifetime disqualification for a second.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **No Warning Required for Administrative Penalty**: Alabama does not require that officers read specific warnings about the exact length of suspension for the administrative refusal suspension to apply. The suspension can proceed even without proof of warnings.

2. **Oral Fluid Testing**: Alabama is one of the few states that explicitly includes oral fluid (saliva) testing in its implied consent statute, in addition to blood and breath.

3. **Refusal Adds to Interlock Term**: For offenses after July 1, 2018, a refusal adds an extra year to the ignition interlock requirement beyond what the underlying DUI would require.

4. **Independent Test Right**: Alabama explicitly grants the right to an independent test at the driver's expense by a physician or qualified technician of the driver's choosing. Failure to obtain an independent test does not preclude admission of the state's test.

5. **Actual Physical Control Standard**: Alabama uses "actual physical control" rather than "driving," meaning a person can be charged with DUI even if the vehicle is not moving or turned on.

6. **10-Year Lookback for Refusals**: Refusal penalties escalate based on refusals within a 10-year period.

7. **Court Referral Program**: All DUI convictions require court referral program evaluation and completion of DUI education or treatment before ALEA will reissue a license.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative Suspension Proceeds Regardless of Criminal Case**: Even if the DUI charge is dropped or the driver is acquitted, the administrative refusal suspension can remain or be reduced only at ALEA's discretion.
- **Ignition Interlock as Bond Condition**: Judges may require ignition interlock as a bond condition while the criminal case is pending, which can pause some license penalties.
- **90-Day Suspension Can Be Stayed**: For first convictions, the 90-day suspension can be stayed if the driver elects a 90-day ignition interlock and obtains a restricted license.
- **Felony DUI Threshold**: A fourth DUI conviction or any DUI after a prior felony DUI becomes a Class C felony with 1 year and 1 day to 10 years imprisonment.
- **Chemical Test Validity**: Chemical analyses must be performed according to methods approved by the Alabama Department of Forensic Sciences by an individual with a valid permit.
