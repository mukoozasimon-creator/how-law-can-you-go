## The Short Answer

In Florida, your landlord must give you a **3-business-day notice** to pay rent or vacate for nonpayment. For lease violations, the landlord must give a **7-day notice** - either to cure the violation or to vacate unconditionally for serious or repeated violations. To end a month-to-month tenancy, either party must give **30 days' written notice**. Florida has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and state law preempts all local landlord-tenant ordinances (HB 1417, 2023). Self-help eviction is illegal and exposes the landlord to penalties of at least 3 months' rent plus attorney's fees.

## Key Statutes

### Florida Statutes Section 83.56 - Termination of Rental Agreement

> "If the tenant fails to pay rent when due and the default continues for 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays, after delivery of written demand by the landlord for payment of the rent or possession of the premises, the landlord may terminate the rental agreement." cite🛠web_search:1#11:~:text=Non-payment of rent: 3 business days (excludes weekends and legal holidays)

**Verbatim text from F.S. 83.56(2)(a) for non-curable violations:**
> "If such noncompliance is of a nature that the tenant should not be given an opportunity to cure it or if the noncompliance constitutes a subsequent or continuing noncompliance within 12 months of a written warning by the landlord of a similar violation, deliver a written notice to the tenant specifying the noncompliance and the landlord's intent to terminate the rental agreement by reason thereof... the tenant shall have 7 days from the date that the notice is delivered to vacate the premises." cite🛠web_search:1#12:~:text=If such noncompliance is of a nature that the tenant should not be given an opportunity to cure it... the tenant shall have 7 days from the date that the notice is delivered to vacate the premises.

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 3 business days for nonpayment; 7 days for lease violations (curable or non-curable depending on severity).

### Florida Statutes Section 83.57 - Termination of Tenancy Without Specific Term

> "A tenancy at will, from year to year, or for less than a year, may be terminated by either party by a notice given to the other at least one month before the date on which the party intends the termination to take effect." cite🛠web_search:1#11:~:text=Month-to-month termination, either party: 30 days written notice

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### Florida Statutes Section 83.49 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- No statutory cap on security deposit amount.
- Return deadline: **15 days** if no claim; **30 days** with written notice of intent to claim by certified mail or consented email.
- Missing the 30-day claim notice deadline **forfeits all deductions** regardless of actual damages.

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-day absolute deadline for claim notice. Forfeiture rule is strict.

### Florida Statutes Section 83.64 - Retaliatory Conduct

> "A landlord may not discriminatorily increase a tenant's rent or decrease services, or bring or threaten to bring an action for possession, primarily because the landlord is retaliating against the tenant for protected conduct done in good faith." cite🛠web_search:1#11:~:text=Retaliation is presumed if a landlord takes action (eviction, rent increase, reduction of services) within one year of the protected activity.

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Retaliation is presumed if landlord acts within 1 year of protected activity. Burden shifts to landlord to rebut.

### Florida Statutes Section 83.67 - Prohibited Practices (Self-Help Eviction)

> "A landlord may not directly or indirectly shut off utilities, lock out a tenant, remove doors, remove locks, or otherwise prevent reasonable access to the dwelling unit except through lawful process." cite🛠web_search:1#11:~:text=A landlord may not directly or indirectly shut off utilities, lock out a tenant, remove doors, remove locks, or otherwise prevent reasonable access to the dwelling unit except through lawful process.

**Penalty:** Actual damages plus **at least 3 months' rent** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Self-help eviction is strictly prohibited. Minimum penalty is 3 months' rent.

### Florida Statutes Section 83.53 - Landlord's Access to Dwelling Unit

> "The landlord may enter the dwelling unit: (a) In case of emergency; (b) When the tenant unreasonably withholds consent; (c) For the purpose of inspecting the premises; (d) For the purpose of making necessary repairs; (e) For the purpose of showing the dwelling unit to prospective tenants, purchasers, or lenders. Entry shall be at a reasonable time and after reasonable notice." cite🛠web_search:1#11:~:text=Entry for repairs, inspection, or showing the unit requires 12 hours' notice and must be made at a reasonable time statutorily defined as between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m.

**Live link:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter83/All
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 12 hours' notice required for non-emergency entry, between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 3 business days (pay or vacate) | N/A |
| Curable lease violation | 7 days to cure | N/A |
| Non-curable lease violation | 7 days to vacate (no cure) | N/A |
| Self-help eviction penalty | N/A | N/A - 3 months' rent minimum |

## Important Notes

- **HB 1417 (2023):** Florida preempted all local landlord-tenant ordinances that conflict with Chapter 83. Local rent control, source-of-income protections, and additional notice requirements are no longer enforceable.
- **Business days matter:** The 3-day nonpayment notice excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. Miscalculating days is a common reason for eviction dismissal.
- **Security deposit forfeiture:** If the landlord misses the 30-day claim notice deadline, they forfeit ALL deductions - even if the tenant destroyed the unit.
- **Partial rent:** If landlord accepts partial rent after posting nonpayment notice, specific additional steps are required before filing eviction (F.S. 83.56(5)).
- **Recording:** Florida is a two-party consent state for recordings (F.S. 934.03). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Florida has no just-cause requirement, no rent control, and much weaker tenant protections overall.
- **vs. Texas:** Both are landlord-friendly states with no just-cause requirement, but Florida has stricter notice-counting rules (business days only) and stricter security deposit claim deadlines.
- **vs. New York:** Florida has none of New York's just-cause or rent-stabilization framework.
