## The Short Answer

Nevada is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not the initial aggressor, are not committing a crime, and are in a place where you have a legal right to be (NRS 200.120). Nevada's **Castle Doctrine** (NRS 200.120, 200.130) presumes reasonable fear of death or great bodily harm when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your occupied dwelling or vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, serious bodily injury, or a violent felony. Nevada also provides **civil immunity** under NRS 41.095.

## Key Statutes

### NRS 200.120 - Justifiable Homicide

> "The killing of one person by another is justifiable homicide when committed: 1. In self-defense, or in defense of habitation or property, against a person who manifestly intends or endeavors, by violence or surprise, to commit a felony, or against a person who manifestly intends and endeavors, in a violent, riotous or tumultuous manner, to enter the habitation of another for the purpose of offering violence to any person therein."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-200.html#NRS200Sec120
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified to prevent felony or violent entry into habitation.

### NRS 200.130 - Defense of Habitation or Property

> "If a person kills another in self-defense, it must appear that the danger was so urgent and pressing that, in order to save the person's own life, or to prevent the person from receiving great bodily harm, the killing of the other was absolutely necessary."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-200.html#NRS200Sec130
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force must be absolutely necessary to prevent death or great bodily harm.

### NRS 200.200 - Killing in Self-Defense

> "If a person kills another in self-defense, it must appear that the danger was so urgent and pressing that, in order to save the person's own life, or to prevent the person from receiving great bodily harm, the killing of the other was absolutely necessary."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-200.html#NRS200Sec200
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Same standard as NRS 200.130.

### NRS 41.095 - Civil Immunity

> "In any civil action brought by a person who was subjected to the use of force, the person who used the force is immune from civil liability if the use of force was justified under NRS 200.120 or 200.200."

**Live link:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-041.html#NRS41Sec095
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Civil immunity for justified use of force.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your occupied dwelling | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Nevada explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force in self-defense.
- **Castle Doctrine limited to occupied dwelling/vehicle:** The presumption applies only to occupied dwellings and vehicles.
- **Civil immunity:** Nevada provides civil immunity for justified use of force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Nevada is a one-party consent state for recordings (NRS 200.620). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Arizona:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southwest.
- **vs. California:** Both are stand-your-ground states. CA has no formal SYG statute; NV has statutory SYG.
- **vs. Utah:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Mountain West.
