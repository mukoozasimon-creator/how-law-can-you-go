## The Short Answer

In Alaska, your landlord must give you a **7-day written notice** to pay rent or vacate for nonpayment (AS 34.03.220). For material lease violations, a **10-day notice** to cure or vacate applies (34.03.220). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (34.03.290). Alaska has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### AS 34.03.220 - 7-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may give the tenant a written seven-day notice to pay or vacate. If the tenant pays the rent in full within seven days, the notice is void."

**Live link:** http://www.legis.state.ak.us/basis/statutes.asp#34.03.220
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 7 days.

### AS 34.03.220 - 10-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If the tenant violates a material term of the rental agreement, the landlord may give a written notice specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and that the rental agreement will terminate in 10 days if the breach is not remedied."

**Live link:** http://www.legis.state.ak.us/basis/statutes.asp#34.03.220
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 10-day cure-or-vacate for lease violations.

### AS 34.03.290 - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least 30 days before the termination date."

**Live link:** http://www.legis.state.ak.us/basis/statutes.asp#34.03.290
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination by either party.

### AS 34.03.210 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **2 months' rent**.
- Return deadline: **14 days** if no deductions; **30 days** with itemized statement.
- Must provide written notice of deposit terms.

**Live link:** http://www.legis.state.ak.us/basis/statutes.asp#34.03.210
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14-30 day return. 2-month cap.

### AS 34.03.350 - Self-Help Eviction Prohibited

> "A landlord may not willfully exclude a tenant from possession by changing locks, shutting off utilities, or removing doors. Violations subject the landlord to actual damages plus up to 1.5 months' periodic rent and attorney's fees."

**Live link:** http://www.legis.state.ak.us/basis/statutes.asp#34.03.350
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Self-help eviction is illegal. Tenant can recover damages plus statutory penalty.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 7 days (pay or vacate) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 10 days (cure or vacate) | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Alaska landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Alaska does not have statewide rent control.
- **Entry notice:** Landlords must provide at least 24 hours' notice before entry (AS 34.03.300).
- **Recording:** Alaska is a one-party consent state for recordings (AS 42.20.310). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Hawaii:** Both have no just-cause requirement, but HI has longer notice periods (45-day month-to-month, 10-day nonpayment) vs. AK's 30-day/7-day.
- **vs. Washington:** WA has just-cause requirements in some cities; AK does not statewide.
- **vs. Oregon:** OR has statewide just-cause requirements; AK does not.
