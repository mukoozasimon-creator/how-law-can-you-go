# Marijuana Laws - Georgia

## One-Sentence Verdict

Recreational marijuana remains fully illegal in Georgia; possession of 1 oz or less is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 12 months in jail and a $1,000 fine, while the only legal cannabis is low-THC oil for registered patients under the Hope Act, and several cities have enacted local civil-fine ordinances that do not override state law.

## Possession Penalties (Recreational)

| Amount | Classification | Penalty |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| 1 oz or less | Misdemeanor (high and aggravated) | Up to 12 months jail, $1,000 fine |
| More than 1 oz | Felony | 1-10 years prison |
| 10+ lbs | Trafficking | Mandatory minimum prison time |

### Concentrates (THC Oil, Wax, Edibles, Vape Cartridges)

| Activity | Classification | Penalty |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| Possession of any amount | Felony (Schedule I) | 1-15 years prison depending on amount |
| Manufacture/delivery | Felony | Enhanced penalties |

**Critical note**: THC oil, vape cartridges, wax, and edibles are often charged as felony possession of a Schedule I controlled substance at ANY amount in Georgia, unlike flower which has a 1-oz misdemeanor threshold.

### Trafficking Thresholds

| Amount | Mandatory Minimum |
|--------|-------------------|
| 10-2,000 lbs | 5 years + $100,000 fine |
| 2,000-10,000 lbs | 7 years + $250,000 fine |
| 10,000+ lbs | 15 years + $500,000 fine |

## Medical Program

- **Program name**: Georgia Low-THC Oil Patient Registry (Hope Act).
- **SB 220 update (effective July 1, 2026)**: Expanded program renamed "Medical Cannabis"; 18 qualifying conditions; 12,000 mg THC possession limit; vaporization authorized for patients 21+.
- **Qualifying conditions**: Cancer, ALS, seizure disorders, MS, Crohn's, mitochondrial disease, Parkinson's, sickle cell, Tourette's, autism, PTSD, intractable pain, and others.
- **Product type**: Low-THC oil only (THC cap: historically 5%, now 12,000 mg total possession under SB 220). Raw flower remains prohibited.
- **Vaporization**: Authorized for patients 21+ under SB 220; leaf marijuana for home vaporization permitted.
- **Registration**: Georgia-licensed physician registered with DPH submits certification; patient pays $30 state fee; 5-year registry card issued.
- **No reciprocity**: Out-of-state medical cards are NOT recognized.
- **No home cultivation**: Patients cannot grow their own medicine.
- **Dispensaries**: Licensed by Georgia Medical Cannabis Commission (GMCC).

## Hemp and CBD

- **Hemp**: Legal under Georgia Hemp Farming Act (0.3% or less Delta-9 THC).
- **Delta-8, Delta-10, THCP**: In legal gray area; no explicit state ban as of August 2026, but federal changes may affect availability.
- **Hemp vapes**: No explicit state ban, but federal restrictions apply.

---

## Local Decriminalization

Several Georgia cities have enacted civil-citation ordinances for low-level possession:
- **Atlanta** (City Council Ordinance 17-O-1152): $75 civil fine for possession of less than 1 oz.
- **Savannah, Athens-Clarke County, South Fulton, Macon-Bibb County**: Similar civil fine ordinances.
- **Important**: Local ordinances do NOT change state law. State troopers, sheriff's deputies, and officers in other jurisdictions can still arrest under O.C.G.A. Sec. 16-13-30. Officer discretion to arrest is preserved.

---

## DUI / Impairment

- **Standard**: It is illegal to operate a motor vehicle while under the influence of marijuana (O.C.G.A. Sec. 40-6-391).
- **Per se limit**: No specific THC blood limit; impairment determined by field sobriety tests and officer observation.
- **Penalties**: First offense - up to 12 months jail, $300-$1,000 fine, license suspension, DUI school, community service.

---

## Employment Protections

- **No statutory protection**: Georgia does not protect off-duty cannabis use.
- **At-will employment**: Employers may terminate for cannabis use, including medical use.
- **Federal contractors**: Subject to federal Drug-Free Workplace Act.

---

## Federal Conflict Notes

- **Schedule I federally**: All marijuana remains Schedule I under federal law.
- **Medical rescheduling**: Limited to FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical programs.
- **Federal property**: Illegal on federal land, including national parks and military bases.
- **Federal housing**: Public housing may prohibit medical marijuana use.

---

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Harsh concentrate penalties**: Any amount of THC concentrate is a felony in Georgia - there is no misdemeanor threshold.
2. **SB 220 expansion (2026)**: Significantly expanded the medical program with 18 conditions, 12,000 mg possession limit, and vaporization for adults.
3. **No home cultivation**: Even medical patients cannot grow their own medicine.
4. **School zone enhancements**: Drug offenses within 1,000 feet of schools carry enhanced penalties of up to 20 years for first conviction.
5. **Local civil fines patchwork**: Multiple cities have decriminalized, but state law enforcement can still arrest anywhere.

## Recording Consent Rules

Georgia is a **one-party consent** state for audio recordings. O.C.G.A. Sec. 16-11-62 makes it a felony to record a private communication without the consent of at least one party.

---

## Practical Notes

- Even a single joint can result in arrest and jail time in most of Georgia.
- Vape pens, edibles, and wax are felonies at ANY amount - this is the most common way Georgians face felony charges.
- If you have a medical card from another state, it is NOT recognized in Georgia.
- The Atlanta $75 civil fine only applies within Atlanta city limits and does not bind state or county officers.
- SB 220 (effective July 1, 2026) expanded medical access but raw flower remains prohibited.
