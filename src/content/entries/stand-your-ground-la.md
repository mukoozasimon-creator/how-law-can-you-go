## The Short Answer

Louisiana is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are in a place where you have a right to be and are not engaged in illegal activity (La. R.S. 14:19(C)). Louisiana's **Castle Doctrine** (La. R.S. 14:20) creates a presumption that you reasonably believed deadly force was necessary when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your home, business, or vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, great bodily harm, or the commission of a forcible felony.

## Key Statutes

### La. R.S. 14:19 - Use of Force or Violence in Defense

> "(C) A person who is not engaged in unlawful activity and who is in a place where he or she has a right to be shall have no duty to retreat before using force or violence as provided for in this Section and may stand his or her ground and meet force with force."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=78391
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat before using force if lawfully present and not engaged in unlawful activity.

### La. R.S. 14:20 - Justifiable Homicide

> "(A) A homicide is justifiable: (1) When committed in self-defense by one who reasonably believes that he is in imminent danger of losing his life or receiving great bodily harm and that the killing is necessary to save himself from that danger. (2) When committed for the purpose of preventing a violent or forcible felony involving danger to life or of great bodily harm by one who reasonably believes that such an offense is about to be committed and that such action is necessary for its prevention."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=78392
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Deadly force justified against imminent death, great bodily harm, or to prevent violent/forcible felony.

### La. R.S. 14:20 - Castle Doctrine Presumption

> "There is a legal presumption that you held a reasonable belief that deadly force was necessary if: (1) The person you used force against was in the process of unlawfully and forcibly entering your home, business, or vehicle. (2) You knew or had reason to believe an unlawful entry was occurring."

**Live link:** https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=78392
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters home, business, or vehicle.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your home (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your business | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** Louisiana explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force if you are lawfully present and not engaged in illegal activity.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to business and vehicle:** The presumption of reasonable fear applies to your home, business, and vehicle.
- **Civil immunity:** Louisiana provides civil immunity for justified use of force.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor, unless you withdrew and communicated withdrawal.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** Louisiana is a one-party consent state for recordings (La. R.S. 15:1303). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Texas:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections in the South.
- **vs. Mississippi:** MS has no formal SYG statute; LA has explicit statutory SYG.
- **vs. Alabama:** Both are stand-your-ground states with Castle Doctrine protections.
