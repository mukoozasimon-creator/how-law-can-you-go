<div class="quip">New Jersey is a one-party consent state, which means you can record the officer while they explain why the Turnpike toll was $47. The recording will not lower the toll, but it might keep you out of jail.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in New Jersey</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You must show your license, registration, and proof of insurance.</strong> N.J.S.A. 39:3-29. All three. The officer can demand them on the spot. Failure to exhibit is a fine of $150-$500. No jail for first offense, but you can be arrested and held until identity is verified.</li>
<li><strong>You can record the officer.</strong> New Jersey is a one-party consent state (N.J.S.A. 2A:156A-3). You are legally permitted to record your own interactions with police. Courts have consistently held that recording police in public is protected by the First Amendment. Just do not interfere.</li>
<li><strong>They cannot make you wait for a drug dog.</strong> <em>Rodriguez v. United States</em> (2015). Once the ticket is written, the stop is over. New Jersey courts follow this.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse to consent to a search.</strong> Fourth Amendment. Say "I do not consent to a search." If they have probable cause (smell, plain view), they can search anyway. But your refusal matters in court.</li>
<li><strong>Passengers do not have to show ID.</strong> New Jersey has no stop-and-identify statute for mere passengers. But if the officer has reasonable suspicion that the passenger committed a crime, that changes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I forgot my license at home."</strong> N.J.S.A. 39:3-29 is a fine-only offense for first-timers, but the officer can arrest you to verify your identity. You will sit in a cell until someone brings your license or they run your info.</li>
<li><strong>"I do not answer questions."</strong> True for most questions, but 39:3-29 requires you to exhibit your license. You cannot refuse that one.</li>
<li><strong>"You cannot search my car."</strong> Correct, but New Jersey has a broad plain-view doctrine. If the officer sees contraband, they do not need consent. Also, the smell of marijuana alone is no longer probable cause in New Jersey after legalization, but the officer may claim other factors.</li>
<li><strong>"I am driving to a safer location."</strong> No New Jersey statute gives you this right. The officer may interpret it as evading. Use your judgment, but know the law does not have your back on this.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"New Jersey is a sanctuary state so they cannot ask for ID." Wrong. N.J.S.A. 39:3-29 applies to everyone driving a motor vehicle. Immigration status is irrelevant to a traffic stop.</li>
<li>"If the officer takes longer than 10 minutes, you can leave." There is no magic number. <em>Rodriguez</em> says "reasonably required to complete the mission."</li>
<li>"You have to let them search if they say they smell weed." Not anymore. Marijuana is legal in New Jersey. Smell alone is not probable cause. But if they claim to see contraband in plain view, that is different.</li>
<li>"If you do not roll down your window, they cannot ticket you." Wrong. You must exhibit your documents. Shoving them through a cracked window satisfies the statute but makes the officer suspicious, which is not illegal but is also not smart.</li>
</ul>
</div>
