## The Short Answer

South Carolina is a **stand-your-ground state**. You have **no duty to retreat** before using force in self-defense if you are not the initial aggressor and are in a place where you have a legal right to be (S.C. Code 16-11-440). South Carolina's **Castle Doctrine** (S.C. Code 16-11-440(A)) creates a presumption of reasonable fear of great bodily harm when someone unlawfully and forcibly enters your home, dwelling, or occupied vehicle. You may use deadly force to prevent imminent death, great bodily injury, or the commission of a violent felony.

## Key Statutes

### S.C. Code 16-11-440(A) - Protection of Persons and Property Act (Castle Doctrine)

> "A person is presumed to have a reasonable fear of imminent peril of death or great bodily injury to himself or another person when using deadly force that is intended or likely to cause death or great bodily injury to another person if the person: (1) against whom the deadly force is used is in the process of unlawfully and forcefully entering, or has unlawfully and forcibly entered a dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle, or if he or she removes or is attempting to remove another person against his or her will from the dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle; and (2) who uses deadly force knows or has reason to believe that an unlawful and forcible entry or unlawful and forcible act is occurring or has occurred."

**Live link:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c011.php
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Presumption of reasonable fear when intruder unlawfully and forcibly enters dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle.

### S.C. Code 16-11-440(C) - Stand Your Ground

> "A person who is not engaged in an unlawful activity and who is attacked in any other place where he or she has a right to be, including, but not limited to, his or her place of business, has no duty to retreat and has the right to stand his or her ground and meet force with force, including deadly force, if he or she reasonably believes it is necessary to prevent death or great bodily injury to himself or herself or another person or to prevent the commission of a violent crime as defined in Section 16-1-60."

**Live link:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c011.php
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** No duty to retreat anywhere you have a right to be, including place of business.

### S.C. Code 16-11-450 - Exceptions to Castle Doctrine

> "The presumption set forth in Section 16-11-440(A) does not apply if: (1) the person against whom the defensive force is used has the right to be in or is a lawful resident of the dwelling, residence, or vehicle... (2) the person or persons sought to be removed are children or grandchildren, or are otherwise in the lawful custody or under the lawful guardianship of, the person against whom the defensive force is used... (3) the person who uses defensive force is engaged in an unlawful activity or is using the dwelling, residence, or occupied vehicle to further an unlawful activity."

**Live link:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c011.php
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Three statutory exceptions to the Castle Doctrine presumption.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Duty to Retreat? | Deadly Force Allowed? |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| In your dwelling (intruder) | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your occupied vehicle | No | Yes, presumed reasonable fear |
| In your place of business | No | Yes, under stand-your-ground |
| In public (lawfully present) | No | Yes, if imminent deadly threat |
| Defending another | No | Yes, same standard |

## Important Notes

- **No duty to retreat:** South Carolina explicitly removes any duty to retreat before using force anywhere you have a legal right to be.
- **Castle Doctrine extends to vehicle:** The presumption of reasonable fear applies to your occupied vehicle as well as your dwelling.
- **Place of business included:** Stand-your-ground protections explicitly extend to your place of business.
- **Civil immunity:** South Carolina provides civil immunity for justified use of force under the Protection of Persons and Property Act.
- **Initial aggressor exception:** You cannot claim self-defense if you were the initial aggressor.
- **Proportionality:** Force must be proportional to the threat.
- **Recording:** South Carolina is a one-party consent state for recordings (S.C. Code 17-30-30). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. North Carolina:** Both are stand-your-ground states in the Southeast with similar Castle Doctrine frameworks.
- **vs. Georgia:** Both are stand-your-ground states with broad protections.
- **vs. Florida:** Both are stand-your-ground states. FL has a pre-trial immunity hearing; SC does not.
