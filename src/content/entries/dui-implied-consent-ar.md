# DUI / Implied Consent - Arkansas

## Verdict

Arkansas's implied consent law (Ark. Code Ann. 5-65-202 et seq.) requires that any person operating a motor vehicle in the state automatically consents to chemical testing (blood, breath, or urine) if lawfully arrested for DWI. The arresting officer decides which test to administer; the driver does not choose. A first refusal results in a **180-day license suspension** and a **$100 fine**. Second and subsequent refusals within 5 years trigger escalating penalties: **2 years** (2nd), **3 years** (3rd), and **lifetime revocation** (4th). Arkansas uniquely requires that officers advise drivers of their right to an independent test **in writing** and must assist in obtaining it. If the driver is found not guilty, the state must reimburse the cost of the independent test. Blood tests require consent; officers cannot force a blood draw without a warrant.

## Distinctive State Features

1. **Blood Test Requires Consent**: Arkansas uniquely requires actual consent for blood tests. Officers cannot force a blood draw under implied consent alone; they must obtain a warrant or the driver's consent.

2. **Written Advisory for Independent Test**: Officers must advise drivers of their right to an independent test **in writing** and must assist in obtaining it. This is a higher procedural standard than most states.

3. **State Reimburses Independent Test if Not Guilty**: If a driver obtains an independent test and is subsequently found not guilty, Arkansas law requires the state to reimburse the cost of that independent test.

4. **Urine Test Last Resort**: Urine tests are explicitly not to be used unless blood and breath tests are unavailable.

5. **Lifetime Revocation for 4th Refusal**: A fourth refusal within 5 years triggers lifetime license revocation - one of the harshest repeat-refusal penalties in the country.

6. **No Restricted Permit for 2nd+ Refusal**: While a first refusal may qualify for an IID-restricted license (for alcohol-only arrests), second and subsequent refusals offer no restricted permit option.

7. **5-Year Lookback**: Arkansas uses a 5-year lookback period for refusal penalty escalation.

## Practical Notes

- **Administrative and Criminal Proceedings Separate**: License suspension for refusal is administrative and proceeds independently of the criminal DWI case.
- **Field Sobriety Tests Are Voluntary**: Drivers can refuse roadside sobriety tests in Arkansas without automatic penalties, though refusal may contribute to probable cause for arrest.
- **Statutory Presumptions**: BAC of .04 or less creates a presumption of non-impairment; .08 or more creates a presumption of impairment.
- **Under 21 Zero Tolerance**: Drivers under 21 face penalties at .02 BAC or higher.
- **MASEP Requirement**: All DWI offenders must complete the Mississippi Alcohol Safety Education Program (MASEP) or equivalent Arkansas program.
