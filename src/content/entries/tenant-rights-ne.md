## The Short Answer

In Nebraska, your landlord must give you a **7-day written notice** to pay rent or the rental agreement terminates (Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(2)). For material lease violations, a **14-day notice** to cure is required, with lease termination in **30 days** if uncured (76-1431(1)). For criminal activity threatening health/safety, a **5-day notice** to quit applies (76-1431(4)). To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice** (76-1437). Nebraska has no just-cause requirement and no rent control. Self-help eviction is illegal.

## Key Statutes

### Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(2) - 7-Day Notice for Nonpayment

> "If rent is unpaid when due, the landlord may give the tenant a written seven-day notice to pay rent. The notice must inform the tenant that the tenancy will terminate if rent is not paid within seven days."

**Live link:** https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=76-1431
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 7-day notice for nonpayment. Tenant can cure by paying within 7 days.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(1) - 14/30-Day Notice for Lease Violations

> "If the tenant materially violates the rental agreement, the landlord may give written notice specifying the breach and stating the rental agreement will terminate in 30 days unless the breach is remedied within 14 days."

**Live link:** https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=76-1431
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14 days to cure, 30 days to quit. Second same violation within 6 months = 14-day unconditional notice.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1431(4) - 5-Day Notice for Criminal Activity

> "If the tenant or any person on the premises with the tenant's consent engages in violent criminal activity or certain illegal drug-related activities, the landlord may terminate by giving a written five-day notice to quit."

**Live link:** https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=76-1431
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 5-day no-cure notice for violent criminal or drug activity.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1437 - 30-Day Month-to-Month Termination

> "The landlord or the tenant may terminate a month-to-month tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least 30 days before the periodic rental date."

**Live link:** https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=76-1437
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30 days written notice for month-to-month termination. Week-to-week requires 7 days.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1416 - Security Deposits

**Key rules:**
- Maximum: **1 month's rent** (no separate pet deposit allowed beyond this cap).
- Return deadline: **14 days** if no deductions; **30 days** with itemized statement.
- Must provide written notice of deposit terms.

**Live link:** https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=76-1416
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14-30 day return depending on deductions. 1-month cap.

## What This Means for You

| Situation | Notice Required | Just Cause Required? |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Month-to-month termination | 30 days written | No |
| Nonpayment of rent | 7 days (pay or vacate) | N/A |
| Lease violation | 14 days cure / 30 days quit | N/A |
| Criminal activity | 5 days (no cure) | N/A |

## Important Notes

- **No just-cause requirement:** Nebraska landlords can terminate month-to-month tenancies for any reason with proper notice.
- **No rent control:** Nebraska does not have statewide rent control.
- **Self-help prohibited:** Landlords cannot change locks, shut off utilities, or remove tenant belongings without a court order.
- **Retaliation protection:** Neb. Rev. Stat. 76-1439 prohibits retaliatory eviction.
- **Recording:** Nebraska is a one-party consent state for recordings (Neb. Rev. Stat. 86-290). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. Kansas:** Both have similar 14/30-day violation structures. KS has 3-day nonpayment vs. NE's 7-day.
- **vs. Iowa:** IA has 3-day nonpayment vs. NE's 7-day. Both have 7/30-day or 14/30-day violation structures.
- **vs. Missouri:** MO has 10-day nonpayment vs. NE's 7-day.
