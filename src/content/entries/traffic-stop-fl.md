<div class="quip">Florida will let you record the cop, but only from 25 feet away. So bring a zoom lens and maybe a snack, because you are going to be standing back there a while.</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>The stuff that is actually true in Florida</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>You must show your license and proof of insurance on demand.</strong> Fla. Stat. 322.15 and 316.646. Noncriminal traffic infraction. But refusing can escalate to resisting without violence (843.02), which is a misdemeanor.</li>
<li><strong>You can record, but stay 25 feet back.</strong> Fla. Stat. 843.31 (HB 11, 2025). New law as of July 2025. Misdemeanor to approach within 25 feet of a first responder after being warned. This includes recording. Stand back. Use zoom.</li>
<li><strong>They cannot make you wait for a drug dog.</strong> <em>Rodriguez v. United States</em> (2015). Once the ticket is written, the stop is over. Florida courts follow this.</li>
<li><strong>You can refuse to consent to a search.</strong> Fourth Amendment. Say "I do not consent to a search." But Florida has a broad plain-view doctrine and officers can frisk for weapons under 901.151(5) if they have probable cause you are armed.</li>
<li><strong>Passengers cannot leave without permission.</strong> <em>Presley v. State</em> (2017). Florida Supreme Court. You are detained for the duration of the stop. Do not try to walk away.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="varies">
<b>The grey area where people get themselves arrested</b>
<ul>
<li><strong>"I do not answer questions."</strong> True for most questions, but 322.15 requires you to display your license. You cannot refuse that.</li>
<li><strong>"You cannot search my car."</strong> Correct, but Florida officers can order you out of the car (<em>Maryland v. Wilson</em>), and if they see contraband in plain view, they do not need consent.</li>
<li><strong>"I am driving to a safer location."</strong> Fla. Stat. 316.1935 makes willful failure to stop a third-degree felony. Florida does not play around with this. There is no statutory right to continue driving.</li>
<li><strong>"The officer did not read me my rights."</strong> <em>Berkemer v. McCarty</em> says roadside questioning is not custodial for Miranda. They do not have to read you your rights during a routine traffic stop.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>Things TikTok told you that are wrong</b>
<ul>
<li>"Florida is a stand your ground state so I do not have to comply." Wrong. Stand your ground applies to self-defense, not traffic stops. Refusing to comply with a lawful order during a traffic stop is resisting without violence (843.02).</li>
<li>"If I do not roll down my window, they cannot ticket me." Wrong. You must display your license. Shoving it through a cracked window satisfies the statute but makes the officer suspicious, which is not illegal but is also not smart.</li>
<li>"The 25-foot rule only applies to protests." Wrong. HB 11 applies to any approach within 25 feet of a first responder after a verbal warning. Traffic stops count.</li>
<li>"If the officer takes longer than 15 minutes, you can leave." There is no magic number. <em>Rodriguez</em> says "reasonably required to complete the mission."</li>
</ul>
</div>
