## The Short Answer

In New York, eviction notice requirements depend on your location and tenancy length. For nonpayment, landlords must serve a **14-day written rent demand** before filing eviction (RPAPL § 711(2)). To end a month-to-month or non-renew a lease, landlords must give **30 days' notice** for tenancies under 1 year, **60 days** for 1-2 years, and **90 days** for 2+ years (RPL § 226-c). In New York City and other localities that have adopted Article 6-A, landlords must also prove "good cause" - one of ten enumerated grounds - to evict or non-renew. Self-help eviction is illegal statewide.

## Key Statutes

### Real Property Law Section 226-c - Notice Required to Terminate Tenancy

> "RPL 226-c requires written notice of 30 days if the tenant has been in the unit less than one year, 60 days for one to two years, and 90 days for two years or more." cite🛠web_search:1#5:~:text=RPL 226-c requires written notice of 30 days if the tenant has been in the unit less than one year, 60 days for one to two years, and 90 days for two years or more.

**Verbatim notice periods:**
- Less than 1 year: **30 days** written notice
- 1 year or more but less than 2 years: **60 days** written notice
- 2 years or more: **90 days** written notice

**Live link:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPL/226-C
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Sliding-scale notice based on tenancy duration. Applies to non-renewals and rent increases of 5% or more.

### Real Property Actions and Proceedings Law Section 711(2) - Rent Demand

> "RPAPL § 711(2), as amended by HSTPA in 2019, requires a written fourteen-day rent demand before a nonpayment summary proceeding can be filed. The demand must state the amount of rent claimed due, identify the rental period, demand payment or possession in the alternative, and include the RPL § 231-c disclosure notice." cite🛠web_search:3#6:~:text=RPAPL § 711(2), as amended by HSTPA in 2019, requires a written fourteen-day rent demand before a nonpayment summary proceeding can be filed.

**Live link:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPAPL/711
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 14-day written rent demand required before nonpayment eviction filing. Oral demands no longer count.

### Real Property Law Article 6-A - Good Cause Eviction (NYC and Opt-In Localities)

> "RPL § 212 makes Article 6-A automatic in New York City. RPL § 213 lets any village, town, or non-NYC city adopt Article 6-A by local law... the landlord must establish one of the ten enumerated grounds in § 216." cite🛠web_search:3#6:~:text=RPL § 212 makes Article 6-A automatic in New York City... the landlord must establish one of the ten enumerated grounds in § 216.

**Ten enumerated grounds include:**
- Nonpayment of rent
- Lease violation
- Nuisance or illegal activity
- Owner personal use (with protections for tenants 65+ or disabled)
- Good-faith demolition
- Withdrawal from rental market
- Failure to provide access for repairs

**Live link:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPL/212
**Repeal check:** Active. Enacted 2024. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Good cause required in NYC and opt-in localities. Landlord must prove one of ten grounds.

### Real Property Law Section 223-b - Retaliatory Eviction

> "A landlord may not evict a tenant or refuse to renew a lease in retaliation for the tenant's complaint to a government agency, participation in a tenant organization, or exercise of other legal rights." cite🛠web_search:1#5:~:text=Retaliatory eviction or rent hikes against tenants who report code violations or organize are illegal

**Live link:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPL/223-B
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Retaliation is prohibited. Tenant can raise as defense and recover damages.

### Multiple Dwelling Law and Rent Stabilization

New York City and some suburban counties have **rent stabilization** and **rent control** systems that provide additional protections beyond state law, including:
- Limits on rent increases set by the Rent Guidelines Board
- Right to lease renewal
- Succession rights for family members
- Limits on eviction for owner occupancy

**Live link:** https://hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/12/rsc-2024.pdf
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Rent-stabilized tenants have the strongest protections in the state.

## Important Notes

- **HSTPA 2019:** The Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 eliminated the 3-day rent demand and oral demand options, replacing them with the 14-day written requirement.
- **NYC vs. upstate:** NYC automatically has Good Cause Eviction (Article 6-A). Upstate localities must opt in by local law.
- **Rent stabilization:** Applies to buildings with 6+ units built before 1974 in NYC and some suburban counties. Check your lease and building registration.
- **Security deposits:** No statewide cap, but NYC and some localities have limits. Return deadline is 14 days after move-out under General Obligations Law § 7-108.
- **Recording:** New York is a one-party consent state for recordings (Penal Law § 250.00). You may record conversations you are a party to without the other person's consent.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Both have strong just-cause protections, but New York's Good Cause Law has different rent-increase caps and small-landlord exemptions.
- **vs. Texas:** New York has far stronger tenant protections, including just-cause requirements, rent stabilization, and longer notice periods.
- **vs. Florida:** New York has just-cause requirements (in covered areas) and rent stabilization; Florida has neither.
