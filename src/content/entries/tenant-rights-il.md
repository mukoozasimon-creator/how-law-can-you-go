## The Short Answer

In Illinois, your landlord must give you a **5-day written notice** to pay rent or quit for nonpayment, and a **10-day written notice** for lease violations before filing eviction. To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give **30 days' written notice**. Chicago and suburban Cook County have significantly stronger protections through the Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO), including just-cause requirements, rent increase notice rules, and stronger retaliation protections. Self-help eviction is illegal statewide.

### 765 ILCS 710 - Security Deposit Return Act

**Key rules:**
- No statewide cap on security deposit amount (Chicago caps at amount set by ordinance).
- Return deadline: **30 to 45 days** after move-out with itemized statement.
- Wrongful withholding: Tenant can recover **2x the deposit** plus attorney's fees.

**Live link:** https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs5.asp?ActID=2367
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** 30-45 day return. Double damages for wrongful withholding.

**Live link:** https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs5.asp?ActID=2367

### 765 ILCS 742 - Residential Tenants' Right to Repair Act

> "If a landlord fails to maintain the premises in a fit and habitable condition, the tenant may make necessary repairs and deduct the cost from rent, not exceeding the lesser of $500 or one-half month's rent." cite🛠web_search:2#0:~:text=the right to repair under 765 ILCS 742

**Live link:** https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs5.asp?ActID=2367
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Repair-and-deduct up to $500 or half month's rent, whichever is less, after written notice and 14-day waiting period.

### Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO)

Chicago adds significant protections:
- **Just-cause requirement** for non-renewal in some circumstances
- **Rent increase notice:** 30 days for increases under 10%; 60 days for 10-25%; 120 days for over 25%
- **Security deposit cap:** Amount set by ordinance
- **Entry notice:** 2 days written notice required
- **Relocation assistance** for certain no-fault terminations

**Live link:** https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bldgs/provdrs/standards_and_policy.html
**Repeal check:** Active. No repeal pending as of August 2026.
**Verdict:** Chicago tenants have significantly stronger protections than state law alone.

## Important Notes

- **Chicago RLTO:** Applies to most residential rental units in Chicago. Exemptions include owner-occupied buildings with 6 or fewer units and certain subsidized housing.
- **Cook County protections:** Suburban Cook County has its own Just Cause for Eviction Ordinance with protections similar to but distinct from Chicago's RLTO.
- **Forcible entry and detainer:** Illinois evictions are filed in Circuit Court as forcible entry and detainer actions.
- **Recording:** Illinois is a two-party consent state for recordings (720 ILCS 5/14-2). All parties must consent to recording conversations.

## Cross-State Notes

- **vs. California:** Illinois state law is more landlord-friendly, but Chicago's RLTO is comparable to California's AB 1482.
- **vs. Texas:** Illinois has stronger retaliation protections and repair rights, but no statewide just-cause requirement (except in Chicago/Cook County).
- **vs. Ohio:** Both have similar state-level protections, but Chicago's local ordinances are far stronger than anything in Ohio.
