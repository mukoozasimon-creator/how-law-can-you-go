<div class="quip">The Fifth Amendment still exists in a moving vehicle. But not the way TikTok thinks.</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>What people say</b>
<p>"You don't have to answer any questions as a passenger. Just say 'I don't answer questions.'"</p>
</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>What the cases actually say</b>
<p><em>Berkemer v. McCarty</em> (1984) held that roadside questioning during a routine traffic stop is <strong>not a custodial interrogation</strong> for Miranda purposes. That means the officer does not have to read you Miranda rights before asking questions. It does <em>not</em> mean you are required to answer.</p>
<p><em>Hiibel</em> (2004) held that a state <em>may</em> require you to disclose your name if there is reasonable suspicion that you have committed a crime. It said explicitly that <strong>no physical ID document is required</strong> — only a name, and only where reasonable suspicion exists as to <em>you</em>.</p>
<p>The result: you generally do not have to answer questions, but the officer generally does not have to tell you that, and in some states refusing to identify yourself is itself a crime if there is reasonable suspicion.</p>
</div>
