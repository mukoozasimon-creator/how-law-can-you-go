<div class="quip">That thing you have been telling people at parties for ten years is not real.</div>

<div class="wrongbox">
<b>What people say</b>
<p>"Killing a swan is treason because the Crown owns all swans."</p>
</div>

<div class="settled">
<b>What the UK Law Commission found</b>
<p>The Crown does hold the right of swan ownership on certain stretches of the Thames, but <strong>killing one has never been an act of treason.</strong> The Law Commission's Statute Law Repeals team published "Legal Curiosities: Fact or Fable?" in March 2013 and traced this claim to its origin: a confusion between the Crown's ceremonial right of ownership and the actual statutory offence of treason, which has never included swans.</p>
<p>The treason statutes (Treason Act 1351, Treason Act 1702, and others) list the acts that constitute treason. Killing a bird is not among them. The claim is a myth, and the debunk is better content than the myth ever was.</p>
</div>
